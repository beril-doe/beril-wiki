# Carbon Source Utilization Predicts Ecology and Lifestyle in Pseudomonas

This report asks whether carbon-source utilization predicts ecology and lifestyle in [[entities/pseudomonas|Pseudomonas]]. Its main answer is that the deep split between Pseudomonas sensu stricto (the P. aeruginosa group) and Pseudomonas_E (the P. fluorescens/putida group) dominates the pattern. The split is driven by loss of plant-derived sugar catabolism. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] Carbon profiles also carry a real but weak signal of isolation environment. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] The report rates the pathway-loss hypothesis (H1b) strongly supported and the ecology-prediction hypothesis (H1a) partially supported. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] The evidence comes from one genus and from genome predictions, with isolation metadata available for only part of the genomes. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

## Key Findings

### Sugar pathway loss separates the subgenera

Pseudomonas sensu stricto shows near-complete loss of plant-derived sugar catabolism compared with Pseudomonas_E. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] Of 62 carbon pathways, 43 differed significantly between 7 Pseudomonas s.s. species and 189 Pseudomonas_E species. These species each had at least 5 genomes. The tests were Mann-Whitney U tests with Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) correction (q < 0.05). [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] All 7 pathways that differed by more than 50 percentage points involved plant-derived sugars or sugar alcohols. These were absent from P. aeruginosa but prevalent in the P. fluorescens group. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] The report states that differences between host-associated and free-living subgenera exceed 50 pp for 7 pathways and exceed 10 pp for 15 pathways. It calls this the most comprehensive evidence to date for metabolic streamlining in host-adapted Pseudomonas. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

Pathway completeness (P. aeruginosa group, P. fluorescens group, difference):

| Ribose | 27.9% | 92.0% | +64.2 pp [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] |
| Arabinose | 0.0% | 62.6% | +62.6 pp [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] |
| Galacturonate | 28.6% | 88.4% | +59.8 pp [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] |
| Myo-inositol | 0.0% | 58.8% | +58.8 pp [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] |
| Mannitol | 25.9% | 77.5% | +51.6 pp [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] |
| Sorbitol | 25.9% | 77.4% | +51.5 pp [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] |

The report describes near-complete loss of xylose, arabinose and myo-inositol catabolism in the P. aeruginosa group (0% vs. 59-74% in the P. fluorescens group). [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] It also reports dramatic reductions in ribose, galacturonate, mannitol and sorbitol pathways. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] The P. fluorescens/putida group has greater carbon pathway breadth (mean richness 56.1) than P. aeruginosa (mean richness ~50), particularly for sugar and sugar alcohol catabolism. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

Amino acid catabolism (arginine, histidine, serine, glutamate and others) stays near-universal (>99%) in both subgenera. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] Arginine, histidine, serine and glutamate pathways remain >99.5% complete in P. aeruginosa despite loss of plant sugars. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] The same holds for the citrate, succinate and pyruvate pathways. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] Nineteen pathways showed no significant subgenus difference. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] The report lists a shared core of conserved central carbon metabolism: acetate, pyruvate, fructose, L-lactate, D-lactate, glycerol, alanine, sucrose, 2-oxoglutarate, propionate, phenylacetate, deoxyribonate and glucose-6-phosphate. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

The report interprets this pattern as consistent with P. aeruginosa keeping amino acid catabolism for growth in host environments, such as cystic fibrosis (CF) sputum amino acids. In this reading it loses degradation of plant-derived carbon sources it no longer encounters. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] The report calls the lost pathways plant-derived carbon sources predicted to be irrelevant where amino acids and organic acids dominate. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] It reads the loss of xylose, arabinose, myo-inositol and galacturonate pathways as release from selection for plant-associated metabolism during the move to animal hosts. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] These are interpretations, not tested results.

The report places this in the literature as follows.
- It says La Rosa et al. (2018) tracked P. aeruginosa adaptation in CF lungs and found convergent loss of carbon catabolism with retained amino acid utilization. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] It says its genome-scale results quantify the pattern across the whole genus rather than within one species during infection. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- It cites Palmer et al. (2007), who showed that CF sputum carbon sources are dominated by amino acids, especially aromatic amino acids. The report takes this as the explanation for retained amino acid pathways with lost sugar catabolism. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- It cites Flynn et al. (2016), who showed that P. aeruginosa relies on amino acids and short-chain fatty acids from anaerobic mucin-fermenting bacteria in CF airways. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- It cites Rossi et al. (2021), who documented progressive loss of metabolic versatility in chronic P. aeruginosa infections. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] The report suggests that much of this loss reflects ancestral streamlining of the lineage, because the pathways were already absent at species level across thousands of isolates. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- It cites Mathee et al. (2008) for a mosaic P. aeruginosa genome with strain-specific regions of genomic plasticity. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] It finds the sugar pathway losses conserved at the species level, not strain-specific. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- It cites Silby et al. (2011) and Loper et al. (2012) for P. fluorescens group metabolic diversity, with ~54% of the pangenome encoding variable metabolic capabilities. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- It cites Belda et al. (2016), who documented 92 catabolic pathways in the re-annotated P. putida KT2440 genome. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] It cites Nikel & de Lorenzo (2018) for broad P. putida use of sugars, organic acids and aromatics. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

Rhamnose and fucose go the other way, but the result is a null. Rhamnose was more complete in P. aeruginosa (66.8%) than in the P. fluorescens group (41.3%). [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] Fucose was 66.8% against 45.1%. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] Neither difference was significant after FDR correction. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] The report suggests the P. aeruginosa values may reflect its use of rhamnolipids as virulence factors. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

### Profiles carry a modest ecological signal

Principal component analysis (PCA) of all species' profiles puts the primary axis between Pseudomonas s.s. and Pseudomonas_E, driven by sugar pathway loss. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] The first 5 components of the 62-pathway PCA captured 74.9% of variance, and principal component 1 (PC1) explained 31.2%. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] Among 54 free-living and plant-associated species, the first 5 components captured 74.9% of variance, with PC1 at 31.2% and PC2 at 17.9%. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] Within Pseudomonas_E, free-living, host-associated and plant-associated species overlap substantially. Lifestyle variation there is subtler than the deep phylogenetic divide. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

Among 54 free-living and plant-associated species, a PERMANOVA-like permutation test (999 permutations) linked carbon profiles to isolation environment (p = 0.006). Between-group mean distance was 2.054 and within-group mean distance was 1.890. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] The between/within ratio was 1.087. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] The report reads this as metabolic niche partitioning, since composition is non-randomly associated with isolation environment even among free-living species. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] It says its result extends Saati-Santamaria et al. (2022), who analyzed 3,274 Pseudomonas genomes and found niche-dependent functional divergence with environment-specific pathway sets. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] The extension is the use of standardized GapMind predictions rather than KEGG-based annotations. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

A Random Forest on 4 environment classes (soil, freshwater, plant_surface, rhizosphere; 51 species) reached balanced accuracy of 0.408 +/- 0.169 against 0.250 chance. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] The report calls this not strongly predictive, though it confirms an ecological signal. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] Carbon profiles carry real ecological signal but are insufficient alone to discriminate fine-grained environment types among free-living species. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] The report attributes the moderate accuracy to small class sizes, overlap between related environments such as soil and rhizosphere, and the coarse resolution of 62 GapMind pathways. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] It calls the modest accuracy consistent with Guo et al. (2026), who found hydrocarbon degradation genes concentrated in the accessory genome with strain-level rather than species-level variation. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

The top discriminating pathways, with importance, were:
- D-serine, 0.132, associated with rhizosphere niches. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- Arabinose, 0.094, a plant-derived pentose sugar. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- Rhamnose, 0.086, a plant cell wall component. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- Fucose, 0.085, a plant/animal glycan sugar. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- Xylose, 0.070, a hemicellulose-derived sugar. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

The report calls D-serine, arabinose and rhamnose the top discriminating pathways. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] It calls this consistent with Ghirardi et al. (2012), who linked rhizosphere competence in fluorescent pseudomonads to catabolic profile diversity. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

### Richness by lifestyle

Across species with at least 5 genomes, free-living and plant-associated species had median richness of 57 pathways complete in >50% of genomes. Host-associated species had median 55. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] Within Pseudomonas_E alone, the same trend holds. Plant-associated species averaged 56.7 pathways, free-living 56.1 and host-associated 55.2. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] Across all species, richness ranged from 27 to 61 (mean 54.6). [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] Mean pathway completeness was 0.882. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

### Scope of the claim

The report states that this is the first study to systematically quantify standardized carbon pathway profiles across the full breadth of Pseudomonas (433 species, 12,732 genomes). [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] It notes that several species, including P. fluorescens and P. putida, are isolated from multiple environments. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

## Data and Methods

The report extracted [[entities/gapmind|GapMind]] carbon pathway predictions from the KBase Data Lakehouse `kbase_ke_pangenome` collection. They cover 12,732 genomes in 433 Pseudomonas species clades from GTDB r214. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] The study used the pangenome, gapmind_pathways, genome, ncbi_env, sample, gtdb_metadata and gtdb_taxonomy_r214v1 tables. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] Species-level completeness is the fraction of genomes scoring "complete" or "likely_complete" for each of 62 pathways. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] The genus spans 5 subgenera. Pseudomonas s.s. has 19 species and 6,905 genomes, mainly P. aeruginosa. Pseudomonas_E has 398 species and 5,687 genomes from the P. fluorescens, P. putida and P. syringae groups. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

Keyword-based classification assigned genomes to 10 environment categories. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] Genome counts were:
- Clinical: 4,197 (33.0%). [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- Human (other): 1,659 (13.0%). [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- Freshwater: 566 (4.4%). [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- Soil: 551 (4.3%). [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- Plant surface: 503 (4.0%). [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- Rhizosphere: 275 (2.2%). [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- Food/dairy: 141 (1.1%). [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- Animal: 136 (1.1%). [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- Industrial: 80 (0.6%). [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- Marine: 63 (0.5%). [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

Of 433 species, 387 had at least one classifiable genome. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] Majority-lifestyle assignment gave 204 free-living species [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology], 109 host-associated [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology], 59 plant-associated [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] and 15 food-associated. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

## Caveats and Open Work

- Classifiable isolation metadata covered only 64.2% of genomes (8,171/12,732). [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] Clinical and other human isolates made up 33.0% and 13.0% of genomes. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] Keyword classification left ~6.7% "unknown" and ~29.1% "other", and misclassification could attenuate ecological signal. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- P. aeruginosa comprises 53% of all genomes (6,760/12,732) because of clinical importance. Many environmental species have <10 sequenced genomes, which affects the power of species-level comparisons. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- The environment classifier is only modestly predictive. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] GapMind's 62 pathways may be too coarse for strain-level niche differences within environmental species. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- The 62 pathways miss genus-specific capabilities, notably toluene, naphthalene and benzoate degradation central to P. putida ecology. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- The dominant PCA signal separates subgenera rather than lifestyles. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- The subgenus comparison used only 7 Pseudomonas s.s. species against 189 Pseudomonas_E species. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- Within Pseudomonas_E, lifestyle categories overlap substantially. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] Differences there are subtle, and the analyses do not explicitly control for phylogenetic non-independence. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- Majority-vote assignment of one environment per species obscures generalist species that inhabit multiple environments. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

The report proposes these next analyses.
- Extend to aromatic degradation using KEGG modules for toluene, benzoate and naphthalene. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] The report hypothesizes these likely carry stronger environmental signal. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- Use phylogenetic generalized least squares (PGLS) or phylogenetic logistic regression with the GTDB species tree to test whether lifestyle-pathway associations persist after controlling for relatedness. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- Analyze within-species pathway variation to look for metabolic ecotypes, meaning subpopulations adapted to different niches. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- Cross-reference pathway predictions with random barcode transposon sequencing (RB-TnSeq) fitness data from kescience_fitnessbrowser to validate functionally important pathways. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- Move to genome-level prediction using the full 789K genome-pathway matrix with genome-specific isolation sources, to gain statistical power. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
