# Lignin Enrichment and Ecological Memory in Microbial Communities

This report asks how lignin enrichment, with and without added labile carbon, reshapes bacterial and fungal communities, and whether a community's first-round history still shapes it in a second round. Its main answer is that a single lignin round almost completely replaces the base bacterial community, and that Round 1 history explains more of Round 2 composition than the Round 2 carbon source does. The bacterial evidence is strong, with large effect sizes and consistent replicates. The fungal evidence is weaker because replicates disagree, and tests on single OTUs are limited by n=3 per group. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

## Key Findings

### Lignin enrichment restructures the bacterial community

The unenriched base community is dominated by unclassified taxa (Incertae Sedis, 44.3%), with Pseudomonas below 0.1%. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] After one lignin round, Pseudomonas (39.3%) and Acinetobacter (25.2%) together make up more than 64% of 16S reads. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] The report calls the shift consistent across replicates and a near-complete turnover from the unenriched state. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Shannon diversity falls from 6.46 (Base) to 3.16 (L), and observed operational taxonomic units (OTUs) fall from 1,594 to 163, a 90% reduction. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Permutational multivariate analysis of variance (PERMANOVA) attributes 97.9% of community variance to treatment (R²=0.979, p=0.001). [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

### Labile carbon shifts the lignin community

Adding labile carbon alongside lignin (group LC) moves the bacterial community away from the lignin-only profile. Acinetobacter rises to 41.7% in LC versus 25.2% in L. Pseudomonas falls to 23.0% versus 39.3%. Aeromonas reaches 20.2% versus 0.1%. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Shannon diversity is 2.41 in LC versus 3.16 in L, and Pielou's evenness is 0.49 versus 0.62. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] The report suggests, as an interpretation and not a test result, that labile carbon lets copiotrophic taxa co-dominate with lignin degraders and so makes the community less even. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

### Ecological memory in Round 2

Round 2 bacterial communities group by their Round 1 history. Groups with lignin history (L-L, L-LC) are Pseudomonas-dominated (52–53%), with low Acinetobacter (<1–20%). [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Groups with lignin-plus-labile history (LC-L, LC-LC) are Acinetobacter-dominant (31–34%), with lower Pseudomonas (24–26%) and elevated Enterobacter (2.7–18.1%). [src: lignin_community_enrichment] On the 12 Round 2 samples, PERMANOVA assigns 58.9% of variance to Round 1 history (F=14.31, R²=0.589, p=0.002). Current Round 2 carbon source explains 32.7% (F=4.85, R²=0.327, p=0.018). [src: lignin_community_enrichment] The history/R2 distance ratio is 1.15–1.59, and values above 1 indicate that history matters more than current conditions. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] The report concludes that communities with different histories do not converge under identical Round 2 conditions, and presents this as quantitative evidence for ecological memory. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

### Fungal communities respond more extremely but less reliably

The report finds more extreme treatment-specific responses in fungi than in bacteria. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] The base fungal community is Fusarium-dominated (26.5%), with 253 OTUs and Shannon=3.50. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Under lignin alone, Fusarium persists (43.4%) and Fusicolla emerges (30.3%). [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Lignin plus labile carbon replaces the community completely, with Chrysosporium at 61.4% and Aspergillus at 27.2%. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] In Round 2, Malassezia dominates L-L (63.4%) and LC-L (50.0%). [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Pleurotus, described as a known lignin-degrading white-rot fungus, emerges in LC-LC at 50.0%. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Internal transcribed spacer (ITS) diversity collapses from 253 OTUs in Base to 3–8 OTUs per group after enrichment. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

### Taxa that respond to lignin

Pseudomonas is absent in base (<0.1%) and dominant in all enriched conditions (23–53%). The report notes it is known for the beta-ketoadipate pathway and protocatechuate catabolism. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

- Flavobacterium rises from 0.1% in base to 14.1% in L, with a centered log-ratio (CLR) effect of +11.2 in the Base vs L comparison. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] The report describes Flavobacterium species as documented aromatic-compound degraders, with several strains carrying ring-hydroxylating dioxygenases and isolated from PAH-contaminated environments. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Its fall to 1.4% in LC suggests a specialist selected by lignin-derived aromatics, not a generalist copiotrophic responder. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]
- Comamonas is 0.1% in base and is enriched 17–51x in Round 2 groups (3.6–5.1%). It is described as known for degrading aromatic compounds including phenylpropanoids. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]
- Aminobacter is absent in base and Round 1 and appears in Round 2 L-L (7.6%) and LC-L (6.4%). It is described as known for aromatic amine metabolism. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]
- Rhodococcus, a well-characterized lignin degrader, stays at 0.03–0.67% across conditions and is highest in L (0.67%). [src: lignin_community_enrichment]
- Sphingomonas is higher in base (1.74%) than in enriched conditions. The report reads this as no selection by lignin enrichment despite its known aromatic catabolism. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

One round of lignin enrichment replaced most of the bacterial base community, and adding labile carbon shifted the result again. Round 2 communities kept the imprint of their Round 1 history, which the report presents as ecological memory. Fungi responded even more sharply, but poor replicate consistency limits what the ITS data can support. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

### Round 2 and ecological memory

Round 2 communities separated by their Round 1 history. Groups with lignin history (L-L, L-LC) were Pseudomonas-dominated (52–53%) with low Acinetobacter (<1–20%). [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Groups with lignin-plus-labile history (LC-L, LC-LC) were Acinetobacter-dominant (31–34%), had lower Pseudomonas (24–26%) and had elevated Enterobacter (2.7–18.1%). [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

PERMANOVA on the 12 Round 2 samples gave the following results:
- R1 history explained 58.9% of variance (F=14.31, R²=0.589, p=0.002). [src: lignin_community_enrichment]
- Current R2 carbon source explained 32.7% (F=4.85, R²=0.327, p=0.018). [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

The history/R2 distance ratio was 1.15–1.59, and values above 1 mean history matters more than current conditions. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Communities with different R1 histories did not converge under identical R2 conditions. The report presents this as quantitative evidence for ecological memory. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] See [[concepts/ecological-memory-and-historical-contingency-in-serially-passaged-communities]].

### Fungal community

The fungal community showed more extreme treatment-specific responses than the bacterial one. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]
- Base: Fusarium-dominated (26.5%) with diverse secondary taxa, 253 OTUs and Shannon=3.50. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]
- L: Fusarium persisted (43.4%) and Fusicolla emerged (30.3%). [src: lignin_community_enrichment]
- LC: the community was completely replaced, with Chrysosporium at 61.4% and Aspergillus at 27.2%. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]
- Round 2: Malassezia dominated L-L (63.4%) and LC-L (50.0%). [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Pleurotus emerged in LC-LC (50.0%). [src: lignin_community_enrichment] The report describes Pleurotus as a known lignin-degrading white-rot fungus. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

ITS (internal transcribed spacer) diversity collapsed from 253 OTUs in Base to 3–8 OTUs per group after enrichment. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] This has a caveat. Within-group Bray-Curtis distances reached 0.99–1.00 for several Round 2 groups, versus 0.09 for 16S. That limits statistical inference for ITS. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] See [[concepts/fungal-community-responses-to-lignin-and-labile-carbon]].

### Statistical limits

With n=3 per group, pairwise Mann-Whitney U tests have a minimum achievable p-value of 0.10, because only 10 permutations are possible for 3 vs 3. This makes FDR-corrected significance impossible for individual OTUs. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Pairwise PERMANOVA with 3 vs 3 samples has the same permutation floor of p~0.10. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Global tests, namely Kruskal-Wallis across all 7 groups and global PERMANOVA, reach significance because they pool more samples. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] The report calls the Round 2 factorial PERMANOVA (M2, n=12) its most statistically powered test. It gave significant results for both R1 history (p=0.002) and R2 carbon source (p=0.018). [src: lignin_community_enrichment] The report holds that CLR differences and R² effect sizes stay interpretable even when p-values are constrained. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] See [[concepts/small-sample-limits-in-community-amplicon-statistics]].

## Results: Beta Diversity and PERMANOVA

This part of the report covers read processing, the diversity and ordination results, and the differential-abundance analysis. The data are high quality and most reads survive processing. Enrichment sharply cuts diversity, and PERMANOVA separates the groups. Dispersion differences and n=3 per group limit the inference. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

### Sequencing and read processing

The dataset has 21 samples (7 groups x 3 replicates). Each was sequenced for 16S V3-V4 and ITS2, giving 84 paired-end FASTQ libraries. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] All libraries have Q30 > 0.94. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Reads went through vsearch with 97% OTU clustering and de novo chimera removal. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] 16S taxonomy came from SILVA 138.2 (vsearch sintax at 80% confidence). [src: lignin_community_enrichment] ITS taxonomy came from NCBI ITS_RefSeq_Fungi using BLASTn. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

### Ordination and PERMANOVA

PCoA (principal coordinates analysis) separated all treatment groups clearly. PC1 and PC2 together explain 81.1% of variance for 16S and 34.0% for ITS. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] The report used a 3-tier PERMANOVA (permutational multivariate analysis of variance) design with Bray-Curtis distances and 999 permutations. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] See also [[entities/permanova]].

Group explained most bacterial variance and a smaller share of fungal variance in every model. The R1 history and R2 labile C factors were significant for bacteria only. For fungi both were non-significant (p=0.090), so this is a null result. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

[src: lignin_community_enrichment] The report therefore says PERMANOVA should be read alongside PERMDISP. Group separation is real, but dispersion differences contribute to the signal. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

### Differential abundance

With n=3 per group, no individual OTU reached FDR (false discovery rate) significance. The minimum p was 0.10 for Mann-Whitney with 3 vs 3. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Effect sizes are CLR (centered log-ratio) differences. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] The genus-level results below are therefore descriptive. See [[concepts/small-sample-limits-in-community-amplicon-statistics]].

In Base versus Lignin, three genera rose from <0.1% in Base. Acinetobacter reached 17.0% (CLR +12.3), [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Pseudomonas reached 7.5% (CLR +11.5), [src: lignin_community_enrichment] and Flavobacterium reached 9.0% (CLR +11.2). [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

In the bacterial L-versus-LC comparison, Aeromonas (CLR +6.4) [src: lignin_community_enrichment] was described as copiotrophic and fast-growing. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Shewanella (CLR +6.6) [src: lignin_community_enrichment] was described as a facultative anaerobe. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Both were enriched in LC. Peredibacter (CLR -7.3) [src: lignin_community_enrichment] was described as a bacterial predator, [src: lignin_community_enrichment] and Comamonas (CLR -6.8) [src: lignin_community_enrichment] as an aromatic compound degrader. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Both were enriched in L.

## Results; Interpretation

This part of the report shows that lignin enrichment sharply restructured bacterial communities and that enrichment history persisted into Round 2 (R2). The bacterial evidence is strong. Fungal (ITS, internal transcribed spacer) data are too variable to support the same inference. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

### Selective filtering and the labile-carbon shift

The report reads the turnover from the base community, dominated by unclassified taxa, to a Pseudomonas/Acinetobacter-dominated community after one lignin passage as strong support for lignin acting as a selective filter. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Observed OTUs (operational taxonomic units) fell by 90%, from 1,594 to 163, which the report reads as strong selection for a few taxa able to use lignin-derived aromatics. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] See [[concepts/selective-filtering-of-communities-by-lignin-enrichment]].

Adding labile carbon (LC) changed the outcome. Relative to lignin alone (L), LC had less Pseudomonas, more Acinetobacter, and Aeromonas and Enterobacter appeared. The report takes this as support for a labile-carbon-driven copiotrophic shift. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] It proposes that labile carbon relaxes selection for specialist lignin degraders by giving fast-growing copiotrophs another energy source. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Shannon diversity was lower in LC (2.41) than in L (3.16), which the report reads as stronger dominance by fewer taxa. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] See [[concepts/labile-carbon-effects-on-lignin-degrading-community-structure]].

### Ecological memory in bacteria

Communities with different Round 1 (R1) histories did not converge under identical R2 conditions. The report treats this as direct evidence of ecological memory. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] See [[concepts/ecological-memory-and-historical-contingency-in-serially-passaged-communities]].

| L-LC vs LC-LC (different history, R2=LC) | 0.486 | History signal persists | [src: lignin_community_enrichment] |
| L-L vs L-LC (history L, different R2) | 0.441 | R2 condition effect | [src: lignin_community_enrichment] |
| LC-L vs LC-LC (history LC, different R2) | 0.306 | R2 condition effect | [src: lignin_community_enrichment] |

The history/R2 ratios were 1.15 (R2=L) and 1.59 (R2=LC). Values above 1 mean history affects composition more than the current carbon source. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] The memory index was ~0.50. Communities with different R1 histories kept about half of the maximum possible divergence under identical R2 conditions. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

In the factorial PERMANOVA (permutational multivariate analysis of variance), R1 history explained 58.9% of R2 community variance (F=14.31, p=0.002). Current R2 carbon source explained 32.7% (F=4.85, p=0.018). [src: lignin_community_enrichment] See [[entities/permanova]]. The report reads the non-convergence as consistent with priority effects and historical contingency. In this reading, a copiotrophic community established by labile carbon in R1 resists converging with lignin-only histories. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

Taxa were largely carried over between rounds. 81% of R1 OTUs were retained in L → L-L and 91% in LC → LC-LC. Core taxa were maintained throughout the passage series. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

### Fungal limits and the unfinished concordance test

Bacterial 16S communities had excellent replicate consistency (within-group BC = 0.09). ITS communities were extremely variable (within-group BC = 0.65, reaching 0.99–1.00 for R2 groups). [src: lignin_community_enrichment] This limits ITS inference. The ITS PERMANOVA R² was lower than the bacterial one (0.582 vs 0.979). The memory signal was not statistically detectable in ITS (R1 history p=0.090). [src: lignin_community_enrichment] The fungal memory result is therefore a null. It does not show that fungi lack memory. See [[concepts/small-sample-limits-in-community-amplicon-statistics]].

The research plan pre-registered a per-group Procrustes M² analysis to test 16S–ITS concordance. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] It was not completed. Within-group ITS BC near 1.0 in R2 would give poor fit whatever the biological concordance. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] The report concludes that bacterial communities show robust, reproducible structure. Fungal communities are dominated by stochastic variation at this sample size and sequencing depth, so formal concordance testing is uninformative. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

## Interpretation; Data

This part of the report interprets the lignin-enrichment results against the literature and lists the limits on them. It also describes the data the study used. The study analysed user-provided amplicon data, not KBase Data Lakehouse queries. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] The main limits are small groups (n=3) and poor replicate consistency in the fungal (ITS, internal transcribed spacer) data. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

### Links to the literature

The report ties its bacterial and fungal findings to earlier work. It calls the dominance of Pseudomonas and Acinetobacter consistent with Hossain et al. (2024), who found these genera had the highest diversity of aromatic-degrading enzymes across 26 soil bacterial genomes. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] It says the later-passage enrichment of Comamonas aligns with Kanokratana et al. (2018, PMID: 29169786, PubMed identifier), who found Comamonas to be a major genus in Napier-grass-enriched lignocellulose-degrading consortia. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] It calls Aspergillus, Fusarium and Chrysosporium as dominant ITS taxa consistent with their known saprotrophic capabilities (Bouws et al. 2008, PMID: 18636256). [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

### Ecological memory and priming

The report claims controlled experimental evidence for ecological memory in lignin-degrading communities, using a sequential passaging design. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] It relates this to Hawkes & Keitt (2015), who showed that community composition depends on the order and timing of environmental changes. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] See [[concepts/ecological-memory-and-historical-contingency-in-serially-passaged-communities]].

Labile-carbon priming of lignin degradation is well established in soil ecology (Waldrop & Firestone 2004, PMID: 14614618). The report suggests that here it may act through community composition, not only through metabolic activation. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] It states that labile carbon restructures the community toward copiotrophic taxa that persist through later passages, rather than simply enhancing lignin degradation. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] See [[concepts/labile-carbon-effects-on-lignin-degrading-community-structure]].

### Bacteria versus fungi

Bacteria show reproducible treatment effects, while fungi show extreme stochasticity at the same sample size. The report proposes that this points to different community-assembly mechanisms. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] This is a proposal, and the ITS limits below qualify it. See [[concepts/fungal-community-responses-to-lignin-and-labile-carbon]].

### Limits: fungal (ITS) data

ITS findings should be treated as preliminary. Within-group Bray-Curtis distances near 1.0 for Round 2 groups limit the reliability of ITS-based tests. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] The report gives these likely causes of the poor replicate consistency:

- ITS was rarefied to 5,900 reads, against 24,000 for 16S. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]
- Enriched fungal groups have very low diversity (3–8 OTUs, operational taxonomic units), so their composition is highly sensitive to stochastic variation. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]
- Sample LL_1 was excluded (74 reads after filtering), which reduces L-L to n=2. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]
- ITS depth varies from 6,953–200,902 reads per sample, which adds replicate variability even after rarefaction. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]
- The NCBI ITS_RefSeq_Fungi database (19,375 sequences) may underrepresent environmental taxa. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

### Limits: taxonomy and resolution

ITS taxonomy came from BLAST against NCBI ITS_RefSeq_Fungi (19,375 reference sequences). Some assignments may be imprecise, and genus level is more reliable. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] The standard UNITE database could not be downloaded. The NCBI approach gives reliable genus-level classification but may miss rare or novel taxa. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Clustering used 97% OTUs with vsearch, not ASV-level (amplicon sequence variant) DADA2, because of environment constraints. This may merge closely related species. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] See [[concepts/taxonomic-resolution-and-reference-database-limits-in-amplicon-profiling]].

### Limits: statistics

With n=3 per group, pairwise Mann-Whitney and pairwise PERMANOVA (permutational multivariate analysis of variance) tests cannot reach significance in 3 vs 3 comparisons, because of the permutation floor. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] The factorial PERMANOVA (M2, n=12) is the most powerful test available. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] PERMDISP (permutational analysis of multivariate dispersions) is significant for both markers. Dispersion therefore differs between groups, and PERMANOVA captures both location and dispersion effects. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] No extraction or library-preparation batch metadata exist, so batch effects cannot be formally assessed. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] See [[concepts/small-sample-limits-in-community-amplicon-statistics]].

### Statistical limits and recommended fixes

The report names the n=3 permutation floor as the primary statistical limitation. It recommends n>=5 per group in future experiments so that pairwise significance testing is possible. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] See [[concepts/small-sample-limits-in-community-amplicon-statistics]].

For sequencing, the report makes three recommendations:
- Re-sequence the internal transcribed spacer (ITS) libraries at higher depth to improve replicate consistency in Round 2 groups. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]
- Install DADA2 (R) for exact sequence variant analysis, which would give finer taxonomic resolution than 97% OTUs. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]
- Obtain the UNITE database to improve fungal taxonomy, particularly for environmental taxa missed by the NCBI RefSeq approach. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

See [[concepts/taxonomic-resolution-and-reference-database-limits-in-amplicon-profiling]].

### Proposed extensions

- Build a 16S phylogenetic tree so that Faith's PD and UniFrac distances (weighted and unweighted) can be computed. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]
- Map the lignin-enriched genera Pseudomonas, Acinetobacter and Comamonas to `kbase_ke_pangenome` in the KBase Data Lakehouse. This would give lignin-degradation gene annotations and show whether aromatic catabolism pathways are present. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] See [[entities/kbase-ke-pangenome]] and [[concepts/taxonomic-distribution-of-aromatic-compound-catabolism]].
- Use PICRUSt2 or Tax4Fun to predict functional profiles from the 16S data. This would test whether the lignin catabolism pathways beta-ketoadipate and protocatechuate are enriched. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]
- Add intermediate time points within each enrichment round to track the kinetics of community restructuring. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

## Caveats and Open Work

Fungal replicates agree poorly. Within-group Bray-Curtis distances reach 0.99–1.00 for several Round 2 groups, against 0.09 for 16S, which limits statistical inference for ITS. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] This tempers the fungal results above, which are best read as descriptive.

With n=3 per group, pairwise Mann-Whitney U tests cannot go below p=0.10, because only 10 permutations exist for 3 vs 3. Individual OTUs therefore cannot reach FDR-corrected significance. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Pairwise PERMANOVA on 3 vs 3 samples has the same floor of about p=0.10. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Global tests, meaning Kruskal-Wallis across all 7 groups and global PERMANOVA, reach significance because they pool more samples. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] The report names the Round 2 factorial PERMANOVA (n=12) as its most powered test. It gives p=0.002 for Round 1 history and p=0.018 for Round 2 carbon source. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Effect sizes (CLR differences and R²) stay interpretable when p-values are constrained. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]
