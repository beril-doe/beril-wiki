# The Pan-Bacterial Essential Genome

This page summarises The Pan-Bacterial Essential Genome. The project asked which genes are essential across many bacteria, using random barcode transposon sequencing (RB-TnSeq) data from the Fitness Browser (FB). Only 859 of 17,222 ortholog families (5.0%) are universally essential across 48 bacteria. [src: essential_genome] A larger group of 4,799 families (27.9%) is essential in some organisms and not in others, and 11,564 families (67.1%) are never essential. [src: essential_genome] The analysis covers 221,005 genes across 48 organisms. [src: essential_genome] The Fitness Browser data were generated by Price et al. (2018). [src: essential_genome] The report adds a cross-organism essentiality dimension to that fitness data, to enable function prediction for essential genes that fitness profiling cannot reach. [src: essential_genome] The evidence is limited by the caveats listed below. See also [[concepts/universal-versus-variable-gene-essentiality-across-bacteria]] and [[entities/fitness-browser]].

## Key Findings

### Dataset scale
The Fitness Browser analysis includes 221,005 genes across 48 organisms, with 41,059 essential genes (18.6%). [src: essential_genome] Essentiality rates range from 12.2% in Pedo557 to 29.7% in Magneto. [src: essential_genome] In the report, 2,838,750 BBH (bidirectional best hit) pairs yield 17,222 ortholog groups spanning all 48 organisms. [src: essential_genome] Essential genes are shorter than non-essential genes, with median lengths of 675 bp versus 885 bp. [src: essential_genome] In the report, 17.8% of essential genes are <300 bp. [src: essential_genome]

### Universal, variable and never-essential families
The report defines universally essential families as essential in every organism where the family has members. [src: essential_genome] Across 48 bacteria, 859 (5.0%) of 17,222 ortholog families are universally essential. [src: essential_genome] Of these, 839 are strict single-copy families with copy ratio <=1.5 and no non-essential paralogs. [src: essential_genome] Twenty contain paralogs. [src: essential_genome]

Across the Fitness Browser organisms, 4,799 families (27.9%) are variably essential, and 11,564 ortholog families (67.1%) are never essential. [src: essential_genome] The interpretation describes variably essential families as the largest category. [src: essential_genome] Variably essential families have median essentiality penetrance of 33%. [src: essential_genome] Among them, 813 families are >50% essential and 704 are <10% essential. [src: essential_genome] In the Fitness Browser dataset, 7,084 essential genes have no orthologs in any other organism. [src: essential_genome]

In the Fitness Browser dataset, 15 gene families are essential in all 48 organisms with no exceptions. [src: essential_genome] The report identifies these ribosomal protein families in the absolute essential core: rpsC, rplW, rplK, rplB, rplA, rplF, rps11, rpsJ, rpsI and rpsM. [src: essential_genome] It also identifies groEL as a chaperonin [src: essential_genome], pyrG as CTP synthase [src: essential_genome], fusA as translation elongation factor G [src: essential_genome], valS as valyl-tRNA synthetase [src: essential_genome] and SelGGPS as geranyltranstransferase. [src: essential_genome] See [[entities/ribosomal-protein-families]].

### Conservation and annotation
Universally essential genes are only 8.2% hypothetical. [src: essential_genome] They are 91.7% core compared with 80.7% for non-essential genes. [src: essential_genome] In the report, 71% of universally essential families are 100% core across all genomes in their species. [src: essential_genome]

The conservation hierarchy gives these core fractions:

| Category | % core | Genes |
|---|---|---|
| Universally essential | 91.7% | 6,963 [src: essential_genome] |
| Variably essential | 88.9% | 90,844 [src: essential_genome] |
| Never essential | 81.7% | 47,140 [src: essential_genome] |
| Orphan essential | 49.5% | 4,683 [src: essential_genome] |

Essentiality penetrance and core fraction have a weak positive correlation (rho=0.123, p=1.6e-17). [src: essential_genome] Core fraction is 97.1% for families with >80% penetrance versus 92.8% for families with <20% penetrance. [src: essential_genome] As a null result, clade size does not predict essentiality rate (rho=-0.13, p=0.36). [src: essential_genome]

Orphan essential genes are 58.7% hypothetical, the highest uncharacterized fraction of any category. [src: essential_genome] The report suggests they may include recently acquired genes, rapidly evolving proteins, or lineage-specific innovations. [src: essential_genome] It interprets their 49.5% core genome rate as suggesting that many are not conserved even within their own species. [src: essential_genome] It reads its 7,084 orphan essentials (58.7% hypothetical) as showing that unknown essential gene functions are universal across bacteria, not specific to Mycoplasma. [src: essential_genome]

### Function prediction for hypothetical essential genes
The report identifies 8,297 hypothetical essential genes across 48 organisms, representing 20.2% of all essential genes. [src: essential_genome] Of these, 3,912 have orthologs and are predictable by module transfer. [src: essential_genome] Another 4,385 are orphans and are not predictable by that method. [src: essential_genome] The method uses non-essential orthologs with fitness data in other organisms to assign functional context to essential genes that lack fitness data and are invisible to independent component analysis (ICA). [src: essential_genome] See [[entities/ortholog-transfer-function-prediction]].

Module transfer via non-essential orthologs in ICA fitness modules generated 1,382 function predictions for hypothetical essential genes, covering 35.3% of predictable targets. [src: essential_genome] All of these predictions are family-backed. [src: essential_genome] Predictions span 48 organisms, from 90 predictions in pseudo1_N1B4 to 1 in Caulo. [src: essential_genome] Top predicted functions include TIGR00254 (signal transduction) [src: essential_genome], PF00460 (flagellar basal body rod) [src: essential_genome] and PF00356 (lactoylglutathione lyase). [src: essential_genome]

### Interpretation and literature context
The report interprets variable essentiality as implying dependence on genomic context, including paralogs, alternative pathways, or compensatory accessory-genome functions. [src: essential_genome] It states that metabolic context can make the same gene essential in Methanococcus but dispensable in Pseudomonas. [src: essential_genome] It asserts that context-dependent essentiality holds across 48 phylogenetically diverse species spanning Proteobacteria, Bacteroidetes, Firmicutes, and Archaea. [src: essential_genome]

- The report cites Rosconi et al. (2022) as demonstrating in S. pneumoniae that the pan-genome makes essentiality strain-dependent and evolvable. [src: essential_genome] It cites their work across 36 S. pneumoniae strains as within-species precedent for its cross-species analysis. [src: essential_genome]
- The report cites Koonin (2003) as estimating ~60 proteins common to all cellular life, and Gil et al. (2004) as proposing a minimal gene set of ~206 genes. [src: essential_genome] It relates its 15 pan-bacterial families to Koonin's estimate. [src: essential_genome] It suggests that cell wall biosynthesis and nucleotide metabolism genes among them may be bacteria-specific essentials. [src: essential_genome]
- The report attributes its 859 universally essential families exceeding Gil et al.'s ~206-gene set to inclusion of free-living bacteria with larger genomes rather than only obligate symbionts. [src: essential_genome]
- The report cites Hutchison et al. (2016) as finding unknown functions for 149 of 473 genes (31%) in a minimal Mycoplasma genome. [src: essential_genome] It describes these as 149 essential genes of unknown function in a synthetic minimal genome. [src: essential_genome]
- The report contrasts its experimental RB-TnSeq data across 48 organisms with Duffield et al. (2010)'s computational identification of 52 conserved essential proteins across 14 bacterial genomes as drug targets. [src: essential_genome]
- The report asserts that only the 859 universally essential families are reliable broad-spectrum antibiotic targets. [src: essential_genome]

## Data and Methods
The report uses the Fitness Browser gene table from Price et al. (2018), with 221,005 genes across 48 organisms. [src: essential_genome] Bidirectional best BLAST hit pairs come from the Fitness Browser besthit table. [src: essential_genome] Conservation mapping uses the KBase pangenome link table (conservation_vs_fitness/data/fb_pangenome_link.tsv). [src: essential_genome] Co-regulated gene modules are ICA fitness modules (fitness_modules/data/modules/) [src: essential_genome], and cross-organism module families come from fitness_modules/data/module_families/. [src: essential_genome] Functional categories use SEED annotations (conservation_vs_fitness/data/seed_annotations.tsv). [src: essential_genome]

The outputs include essential status for 221,005 genes [src: essential_genome], 2.84M BBH pairs [src: essential_genome], 179,237 gene-ortholog-group assignments across 17,222 ortholog groups [src: essential_genome], per-family essentiality classifications for 17,222 families [src: essential_genome], and per-family conservation metrics for 16,758 families with links. [src: essential_genome] Ortholog families, essentiality classification and functional characterization are built in one analysis stage. [src: essential_genome] Module-transfer prediction [src: essential_genome] and the link to pangenome conservation [src: essential_genome] are separate stages.

## Caveats and Open Work
- The report attributes its smaller experimentally defined universally essential set to restricting it to genes whose transposon disruption is lethal across all 48 tested organisms, a more stringent criterion than computational conservation. [src: essential_genome]
- The 4,385 orphan hypothetical essential genes are not predictable by the module-transfer method. [src: essential_genome]
- Predictions cover 35.3% of predictable targets. [src: essential_genome]
- The essential gene definition is an upper bound, because missing insertions can reflect small gene size, AT-rich sequence, or scaffold edge effects rather than true essentiality. [src: essential_genome]
- BBH orthology is conservative: it misses paralogs, gene fusions and distant homologs, so some orphan essentials may have undetected diverged orthologs. [src: essential_genome]
- Connected components of the BBH graph can over-merge unrelated genes through transitive connections, particularly for multi-domain proteins. [src: essential_genome]
- Essentiality is defined under library construction conditions, typically rich media, so genes essential only under stress are missed. [src: essential_genome]
- The 48 organisms are biased toward culturable Proteobacteria, and uncultured lineages, Actinobacteria and Firmicutes are underrepresented. [src: essential_genome]
- Module-transfer predictions are indirect, because they come from non-essential orthologs in other organisms and the essential gene's function may have diverged. [src: essential_genome]

Next analyses proposed by the report:
- Characterize the 7,084 orphan essentials: mobile-element association, recent acquisition, rapid evolution and functional categories. [src: essential_genome]
- Test the 1,382 module-transfer predictions by CRISPRi (CRISPR interference) knockdown under conditions predicted by module context. [src: essential_genome]
- Test whether essentiality of variably essential families correlates with metabolic pathway completeness, including absence of an alternative pathway. [src: essential_genome]
- Rerun the analysis as Fitness Browser taxonomic coverage grows, to test whether the 15 pan-essential families remain universal. [src: essential_genome]
- Cross-reference the Database of Essential Genes to find families essential in organisms outside the Fitness Browser. [src: essential_genome]
