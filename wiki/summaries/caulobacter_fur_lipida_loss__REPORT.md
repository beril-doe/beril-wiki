# Regulatory and proteomic architecture of Δfur-permitted lipid A loss in *Caulobacter crescentus*

## Overview

This report analyzes how Δ*fur* Δ*sspB* permits Δ*lpxc* viability in *Caulobacter crescentus* by integrating transcriptomics, single-replicate outer-membrane proteomics, Caulobacter RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing, a pooled genome-wide transposon-mutant fitness assay) fitness data, published ChvI regulons, and comparative species annotation. The evidence supports Fur derepression of transport and iron-uptake systems, a two-phase ChvI response, constitutive rather than induced sphingolipid biosynthesis, transcript–protein discordance in the Lpt apparatus, and peptidoglycan remodeling; the mechanistic importance of the SspB-buffered respiratory program and the CtpA hypothesis remains unresolved or unsupported at the preregistered thresholds. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

## Key findings

### Fur derepression and the ΔsspB-buffered respiratory program

The 4584-vs-4580 contrast (Δ*fur* Δ*sspB* Δ*rsaA* vs Δ*rsaA*) correlated with Leaden 2018's Δ*fur* signal across 93 DEGs with Spearman ρ = 0.315, p = 2.08e-03, and 71% sign concordance. Of those 93 DEGs, 53 were buffered in the present data, with logFC approximately 0 despite Leaden values of -5 to -9; the buffered set was dominated by the cbb3 / cyd / *fix*-NOPQ micro-aerobic respiratory operon, including CCNA_01466-01476, *ccoNOPQ*, *cydCDA*, and *fixG/H/I*. Fur derepression is therefore statistically supported, whereas the mechanistic importance of the Δ*sspB*-buffered respiratory program for Δ*lpxc* rescue is only a hypothesis. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

[src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

Caulobacter RB-TnSeq data from `kescience_fitnessbrowser` covered 198 experiments, including 22 envelope-stress experiments but zero pure iron-limitation experiments. The compendium covers 95 stress experiments (envelope-, drug- and metal-related stresses), 46 nitrogen-source, 42 carbon-source, 10 PYE control, plus 5 others. The genome-wide envelope-stress background, with phenotype-bearing defined as |fitness *t*| > 4 in ≥ 2 envelope-stress experiments, contained 1311 phenotype-bearing genes among 3943 genes, or 33.25%. Path A, the 32-gene concordant-strong Fur signature, contained 17 phenotype-bearing genes, or 53.1%, corresponding to 1.60× enrichment with hypergeometric p = 0.016, described in the report as marginally enriched. Path B, the 26-gene SspB-buffered set used for the enrichment test, contained 9 phenotype-bearing genes, or 34.6%, corresponding to 1.04× enrichment with p = 0.515; it was indistinguishable from background. A per-experiment condition classification of those 198 Caulobacter Fitness Browser experiments is reported as envelope=22, oxidative=4, carbon=48, iron=0 — a carbon count that differs from the 42 carbon-source figure given above, and the report does not reconcile the two. The `kescience_paperblast` collection supplied the Caulobacter SigU literature scout and the cross-species presence/absence screen. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

ChvT (CCNA_03108) had envelope-stress |*t*| = 43.7, while other Fur-derepressed TBDTs included CCNA_02910, CCNA_00210, CCNA_02048, and CCNA_00028 with |*t*| values of 9-28. The pre-registered ≥10% phenotype-bearing threshold was below the 33.25% genome background and was therefore not informative without background correction: sitting below background, it is essentially a presence test rather than an enrichment test, and hypergeometric enrichment against background is the formal H2 verdict in the revised report. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The top hits ChvT, HutA and the FrpB cluster encode outer-membrane transport machinery whose normal job is iron acquisition; in the rescued state that machinery is plausibly available to participate in sphingolipid (CPG) trafficking through the shared Lpt apparatus of the Uchendu 2026 model, although that participation is not directly demonstrated in this project. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

### ChvI phase structure

Published ChvI-induced regulons from Stein 2021 and Quintero-Yanes 2022 contained 488 genes in the tested universe. They partitioned into 20 unique-to-early genes, 10 genes induced in both phases, and 49 late-consequence genes, for a disjoint total of 79 ChvI-induced genes. The unique-to-early group included the lasso peptide cyclase CCNA_02794 at +8.6, the ApbE iron-sulfur cluster repair protein at +3.8, and *imuB* at +1.5. The project's gene-by-gene early/both/late phase-assignment table carries 89 rows, a count the report does not reconcile with its 79-gene disjoint partition. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The both-phases group — the middle cohort of the 20 unique-to-early / 10 both-phase / 49 late partition — included ChvI itself, CCNA_00237, with logFC +1.45 in 4584-vs-4580, supporting autoregulation, as well as SIMPL-family CCNA_02378, which changed from +1.9 to +3.9, and amelogenin/CpxP-related CCNA_03997. The late-consequence group included LolA-family CCNA_03820 at +2.89, Pal-like CCNA_00784 at +2.08, a *zot*-like membrane perturber, *osrP*, and multiple TBDTs. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The phase-structure threshold of at least 10 genes per cohort passed. The late cohort's envelope/transport/regulator enrichment was 24.5%, below the relaxed 50% criterion, and the late-versus-early Fisher test was p = 0.243. Caulobacter SigU, CCNA_02977, is uncharacterized in the published literature; a PaperBLAST scout returned zero substantive snippets. Thus the phase structure is supported, while SigU as the driver of the late cohort remains only partially supported. The late-versus-early envelope/transport/regulator shift was not statistically discriminated (Fisher exact p = 0.243), so the proposed "regulator → structural" progression is suggestive only. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The reported theme distribution shifts from a regulator-rich early cohort (6.7% regulator/two-component-system genes in the 20 unique-to-early genes) to an envelope-structural late cohort (10.2% envelope/OM and 12.2% TBDT among the 49 late genes), with lipid/membrane and PG/cell-wall categories at 0% in the early cohort. The 6.7% early figure cannot represent an integer count out of 20 genes, so its denominator is unresolved in the report. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

### Sphingolipid biosynthesis, CtpA, and Lpt components

Orientation analysis motivated three reframings before formal hypothesis testing: the sphingolipid biosynthesis pathway is *not induced*, which rejected the initial "Δ*fur* derepresses sphingolipid biosynthesis" framing; the canonical Lpt apparatus is maintained or up, consistent with the Uchendu 2026 shared-component model; and SdpA at +4.8 log2 in the outer-membrane proteome surfaced peptidoglycan remodeling as a fourth hypothesis (H4) that was absent from the v1 plan. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

![Sphingolipid locus heatmap from orientation notebook — neither induced nor strongly suppressed across the strain series](figures/00_sphingolipid_locus_heatmap.png)
*Orientation heatmap of the sphingolipid locus, plotted as log2(CPM+1) across libraries: neither induced nor strongly suppressed across the strain series.* [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The sphingolipid biosynthesis pathway was constitutive rather than induced: 0/6 biosynthesis genes were UP, *spt* was DOWN by -0.64 with FDR (false discovery rate, the multiple-testing-adjusted significance level) 0.002, and *sphk* was DOWN by -0.40 with FDR 0.02; the cell therefore does not respond to lipid-A loss by making more CPG-biosynthesis transcript, which rejects the "Δ*fur* derepresses sphingolipid biosynthesis" framing and leaves flux-driven or post-transcriptional rescue as a proposed alternative that these data do not establish. CtpA/CCNA_03113 showed logFC +0.58 with pvalue = 0.048 and FDR = 0.109 in 4599-vs-4584, was not detected in the outer-membrane proteome, and therefore failed both the PASS and BORDERLINE preregistered criteria. Its verdict was REJECTED at the preregistered bar. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

At the transcript level, no canonical Lpt component was DOWN; the MsbA-like CCNA_00307 was UP by +0.89 with FDR 0.01, and LptC-related CCNA_03716 was UP by +0.56 with FDR 0.005. At the protein level, the detected canonical components moved in the opposite direction: LptD changed by log2(4672/4659) = -0.47 and by log2(4672/4580) = -0.62, while LptE changed by -0.78 and -0.68, respectively. CCNA_00307 was detected only in the rescued strain, at abundance 300 in 4672 and NaN in the other strains, so its direction relative to wild-type baseline was uncomputable. The transcript upregulation of MsbA-like and LptC-related is consistent with the Uchendu 2026 shared-component model, but the two Lpt proteins directly measured go in the opposite direction, and the single-replicate outer-membrane proteome cannot resolve this: whether the canonical Lpt apparatus is functionally maintained, replaced, or downregulated at the protein level in the Δ*lpxc* state remains undetermined and requires replicated proteomics. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The Caulobacter-specific sphingolipid inner-membrane transporter CCNA_01226 (*lptC2*) showed transcript -0.60 with FDR 0.034 but protein log2(4672/4659) = +1.08. Because its protein level was already DOWN by -0.42 log2 in 4659/4580, its net protein change versus wild-type baseline was +0.66 log2, reported as approximately 1.58×. CCNA_01217 protein changed by +0.77 in 4672 versus 4659, or +0.74 versus wild type. These single-replicate observations are consistent with post-transcriptional stabilization of sphingolipid-transport machinery but do not establish it statistically; the report states that establishing it requires the replicated proteomics scheduled for summer 2026. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

### Peptidoglycan remodeling

The preregistered peptidoglycan-remodeling set contained 53 loci, of which 28 unique loci met the H4 threshold: 25 were transcript-significant in 4599-vs-4584 at FDR<0.05 and 6 had OM-proteome |log2|>1. The response was predominantly downregulatory, with 20 DOWN loci and specific inductions comprising 5 UP transcript hits plus 3 UP protein hits. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

![PG-remodeling enzymes meeting H4 threshold — coordinated up/down pattern across the strain series](figures/NB05_pg_remodeling_heatmap.png)
*Peptidoglycan-remodeling enzymes meeting the H4 threshold show a coordinated pattern of increases and decreases across the strain series.* [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

Against a backdrop in which shutdown dominates — 20 of the 28 H4 hits are DOWN — specific induced activities included the soluble lytic murein transglycosylase SdpA/CCNA_01252 at +4.8 log2 protein, Pal/CCNA_00784 at +2.08 transcript and +2.84 protein, PleA, PbpX, and CCNA_01754. Basal division and elongation machinery was broadly reduced, including FtsI, PbpZ at -1.08 transcript, PbpC at -1.15 protein, a D,D-transpeptidase at -1.27 protein, *murD*, *mviN/murJ*, multiple LdpD/E/F M23-family endopeptidases, *amiC*, *ripA*, and membrane-bound transglycosylase A at -2.47 log2 protein. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

Pal (CCNA_00784) was induced at both transcript (+2.08) and protein (+2.84) levels in the Δ*lpxc* state and was also among the late-cohort ChvI genes. The report reframes its interpretation using the primary role of Tol-Pal in retrograde phospholipid transport and outer-membrane lipid homeostasis: Pal-Tol upregulation is consistent with increased lipid-remodeling demand after loss of outer-leaflet lipid A, while direct Pal–peptidoglycan contacts and the Caulobacter division-associated role remain additional context rather than direct measurements in this project. An earlier draft instead explained the induction by Mg²⁺-bridged OM-LPS-Pal stacking, with Pal upregulated to compensate through direct protein anchoring once LPS is absent; the report explicitly identifies that explanation as uncited and replaces it with the tentative retrograde-phospholipid-transport reading. The Pal induction itself is measured, but neither compensatory mechanism is directly demonstrated here. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

### Comparative species analysis

NCBI annotation confirmed that *spt* and *cerR* were Caulobacter-unique across *C. crescentus*, *A. baumannii*, *N. meningitidis*, and *M. catarrhalis*, with patterns 1000. Hit counts across those four species were *spt* PaperBLAST (7,0,0,0) and NCBI (7,0,0,0), while *cerR* differed by method — PaperBLAST (1,1,0,0) versus NCBI (6,0,0,0) — and NCBI returned ChvG (5,0,0,0) and ChvI (6,0,0,0). The comparative PaperBLAST presence/absence screen likewise found the sphingolipid biosynthesis pathway Caulobacter-unique, with *spt* (serine palmitoyltransferase), *bcerS* (bacterial ceramide synthase), and sphingosine kinase absent in the other three species. ChvG and ChvI also showed pattern 1000 under both PaperBLAST and NCBI annotation, supporting Caulobacter restriction of the ChvG-ChvI circuit among the tested species; the *bcerS* query failed in *C. crescentus*, so that marker contributes nothing and the pathway call rests on the other markers. The comparator species therefore lack the Caulobacter sphingolipid substitute pathway and ChvG-ChvI regulatory circuit, and the report treats this headline absence as robust because it is independently supported by NCBI annotation in addition to PaperBLAST text matching and the published literature (Olea-Ozuna 2020/2024; Greenwich 2023). [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

![Comparative species presence/absence matrix across the four compared species](figures/NB06_comparative_heatmap.png)
*Comparative species presence/absence matrix.* [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The original PaperBLAST screen produced false negatives for known Caulobacter lipid A genes: LpxA returned 0 PaperBLAST hits versus 11 NCBI hits, LpxC 0 versus 15, LpxD 0 versus 15, LpxB 1 versus 18, and LpxK 0 versus 18. The report characterizes this as an approximately 80% false-negative rate for the tested Caulobacter lipid A genes, while retaining the cross-species absence claims because those were independently supported by NCBI annotation and literature. Its scorecard line states that LpxA/C/D/B/K were all 0 in PaperBLAST for *C. crescentus*, which contradicts the detailed table: LpxB was under-counted at 1 PaperBLAST hit rather than being a zero-hit false negative, whereas the other four genes had 0 PaperBLAST hits against 11-18 NCBI hits. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The original comparative notebook used PaperBLAST description-text matching; the project's adversarial review flagged an approximately 50% false-negative rate for known Caulobacter essential genes, and a follow-up notebook re-ran the comparative panel against NCBI's protein database through Biopython Entrez, which uses curated genome annotation rather than text matching. That re-test re-confirmed the headline biological claims — sphingolipid pathway absent in *A. baumannii*, *N. meningitidis* and *M. catarrhalis*, and ChvG-ChvI alphaproteobacterial-restricted — so the report instructs that the original screen be read as a screen and the NCBI re-test as the authoritative comparative measurement. Note that the report gives the false-negative rate as approximately 50% for known Caulobacter essential genes in this passage and as approximately 80% for the tested lipid A genes in its limitations, and does not reconcile the two figures. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

NCBI annotation also has its own false negatives in *C. crescentus* for genes annotated under non-canonical names: MsbA returns an NCBI *C. crescentus* count of 0 although it is present as CCNA_00307, a phospholipid/LPS ABC transporter, and LptA is present but not annotated with the *lptA* symbol in Caulobacter NA1000, because that genome is annotated primarily with CCNA_* locus tags rather than canonical Gram-negative gene symbols; these are naming-convention false negatives, not true absences. The sphingolipid and ChvI comparative findings are less exposed to this failure mode because the corresponding *C. crescentus* genes are annotated with the canonical symbols (NCBI *C. crescentus* counts of 7 for *spt*, 5 for ChvG, and 6 for *cerR*/ChvI). [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The alternative lipid A-loss routes identified in the comparison were species-specific: *A. baumannii* has PBP1A and Ld-transpeptidases, *N. meningitidis* has 9 capsule-biosynthesis PaperBLAST hits versus 2-3 in the others, and the late acyltransferase *lpxX/lpxL* family is present in *A. baumannii* (8 hits) and *N. meningitidis* (3 hits) but absent in *C. crescentus* (0). The report states that Caulobacter lacks these alternatives and instead uses its sphingolipid substitution route. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

Each of those routes has a published precedent that the report aligns with its own counts: Kang et al. 2021 (PMID 33402533) report that septal class A penicillin-binding-protein activity and ld-transpeptidases mediate selection of colistin-resistant, LOS-deficient *A. baumannii* — Δ*lpxc* viability via PBP1A loss plus LdtJ/LdtK — which the report calls mechanistically distinct from but biologically analogous to the Caulobacter SdpA/PleA and Pal-Tol peptidoglycan remodeling seen here — both organisms reorganize peptidoglycan when LPS or LOS is removed, but through different enzyme families; Steeghs et al. 2001 (PMID 11742971), a study of the outer-membrane composition of an LPS-deficient *N. meningitidis* mutant whose title alone does not name the substituting component, is read by the report as *N. meningitidis* Δ*lpxA* viability via capsule substitution, consistent with the 9 capsule-biosynthesis hits versus 2-3 in the others; and Gao et al. 2008 (PMID 18795947) identify two late acyltransferase genes responsible for lipid A biosynthesis in *M. catarrhalis* and report late-acyltransferase truncation, consistent with the *lpxX/lpxL* pattern of 8 in *A. baumannii*, 3 in *N. meningitidis* and 0 in *C. crescentus*. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

### Literature anchors

The foundational study for this system is Zik et al. 2022 (PMID 35649364), which establishes that Δ*lpxc* viability in *C. crescentus* requires both Δ*fur* and the anionic sphingolipid CPG, and that "Fur-regulated processes (not iron status per se)" underlie viability. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

Uchendu, Isom and Klein 2026 — a bioRxiv preprint rather than a peer-reviewed paper (10.1101/2026.04.12.717747) — identify the Caulobacter sphingolipid inner-membrane transporters CCNA_01213/01214/01226 (*lptG2*/*lptF2*/*lptC2*) and show that they share the canonical LptB ATPase with LPS transport; this is the shared-component model against which the project's Lpt transcript and protein measurements are compared. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The Δ*fur* reference signature comes from Leaden et al. 2018 (PMID 30210482), published Caulobacter Δ*fur* RNA-seq whose supplementary Table 2 was re-analyzed here to give a clean Fur-only differentially expressed gene signature; the Spearman ρ = 0.315 concordance with the 4584-vs-4580 contrast is what the report treats as confirming Fur derepression as a major component of its signal. That study itself reports that iron deficiency generates oxidative stress and activation of the SOS response in *C. crescentus*. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The ChvI reference regulons used for the H1 partition test are Stein et al. 2021 (PMID 34124942), which reports that the ChvG-ChvI and NtrY-NtrX two-component systems coordinately regulate growth of *C. crescentus*, and Quintero-Yanes et al. 2022 (PMID 36480504), which identifies ChvGI as the system maintaining cell-envelope homeostasis in *C. crescentus*; Greenwich et al. 2023 (PMID 37040790) supplies the alphaproteobacterial ChvG-ChvI conserved-circuit review that the cross-species absence result is read against. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

Independent cross-validation of the Fur target set comes from da Silva Neto et al. 2009 (PMID 19520766), an earlier Caulobacter Fur ChIP (chromatin immunoprecipitation) and microarray study that the report says cross-validates HutA, the FrpB-like cluster and bacterioferritin in the concordant-strong set as canonical Fur targets; that study reports Fur as the controller of iron homeostasis and oxidative-stress defense in the oligotrophic alphaproteobacterium *C. crescentus*. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

Olea-Ozuna et al. 2020/2024 (PMIDs 33063925, 39093898) are cited as independent confirmation that Caulobacter encodes a sphingolipid biosynthesis pathway absent in the other three Gram-negatives: the 2020 paper reports five structural genes required for ceramide synthesis in *Caulobacter* and for bacterial survival, and the 2024 paper reports that genes required for phosphosphingolipid formation in *C. crescentus* contribute to bacterial virulence. Two further pathway papers are cited: Dhakephalkar, Guan and Klein 2025 (PMID 39829823) identify CpgD as a phosphoglycerate cytidylyltransferase required for ceramide diphosphoglycerate synthesis, and Dhakephalkar et al. 2023 (PMID 37286040) characterize an evolutionarily distinct bacterial ceramide kinase from *C. crescentus*. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The Δ*sspB* arm is read against two proteolysis papers: Bhat et al. 2013 (PMID 23647068) identify ClpP substrates in *C. crescentus* and reveal a role for regulated proteolysis in bacterial development, and Flynn et al. 2004 (*Genes Dev* 18(18):2292) show that the SspB adaptor delivers a regulator of the extracytoplasmic-stress response to the AAA+ protease ClpXP for degradation. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

Comparative context for lipid A loss includes Moffatt et al. 2010 (PMID 20855724), which reports that colistin resistance in *A. baumannii* is mediated by complete loss of lipopolysaccharide production. Two resource papers are also cited: a 2018 *Nature* paper, "Mutant phenotypes for thousands of bacterial genes of unknown function" (557(7706):503, PMID 29769718), and the PaperBLAST paper, "PaperBLAST: Text Mining Papers for Information about Homologs" (*mSystems* 6(1):e00185-19, PMID 33531404), which describes the literature-mining resource used here for information about homologs. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The Pal interpretation rests on Tan & Chng 2025 (PMID 40055349, *Nat Commun* 16:2293), which establishes retrograde phospholipid transport for outer-membrane lipid homeostasis as the primary function of the Tol-Pal complex, rather than the LPS-Pal structural anchoring that an earlier draft of this report supposed; the project's adversarial review identified this citation as missing from that draft. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

Yeh et al. 2010 (PMID 20693330, *J Bacteriol* 192:4847) supplies the further context that the Caulobacter Tol-Pal complex is essential for outer-membrane constriction at cell division, unlike *E. coli* Tol-Pal, and that it is essential for outer-membrane integrity and for the positioning of a polar localization factor; this is cited literature context for the Pal upregulation finding rather than a measurement made in this project, and is relevant because the rescued strain must complete division with no LPS. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

## Caveats and unresolved questions

The Δ*sspB*-buffered cbb3/*fix* respiratory program is a clean transcript-level observation, but its fitness phenotype-bearing rate was 34.6%, versus 33.25% for the genome background, with hypergeometric p = 0.515 and fold = 1.04×. Consequently, the claim that respiratory ATP is required for envelope remodeling remains a working hypothesis, not an established mechanism, and the fitness data provide no independent enrichment support for the SspB-buffered respiratory set. Read as a "dual-release switch", the Δ*fur* arm is supported, whereas the Δ*sspB*-arm respiratory protection is a plausible hypothesis that is not statistically distinguished from genome background. The pre-registered ≥10% threshold sat below the 33.25% background rate — a methodological miscalibration that the adversarial review surfaced and that the report records as a learned pattern: pre-registered fitness-phenotype thresholds must be calibrated against the genome-wide background rate using fold-enrichment rather than a fixed percentage, since a ≥10% threshold against a 33% background passes any random gene set. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The fitness compendium contained zero iron-limitation experiments among 198 Caulobacter experiments, so the iron-limitation arm of H2 was descoped to envelope-only analysis. Testing that arm requires additional RB-TnSeq experiments under bipyridyl chelation, ferric supplementation, and hemin, or a cross-walk to published Δ*fur* phenotypes. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

CtpA was rejected at the preregistered bar: the BORDERLINE criterion required *both* 0.05 < pvalue < 0.15 and protein detection, but the observed pvalue = 0.048 failed the lower bound, FDR = 0.109 failed the PASS criterion, and CtpA was not detected in the outer-membrane proteome. The cumulative 4599-vs-4580 result (logFC +0.76, FDR 0.035) is significant but cannot isolate the Δ*lpxc*-specific response because it conflates Δ*fur*, Δ*sspB*, and Δ*lpxc* effects; it is reported as a supplemental observation consistent with CtpA being Fur-regulated, not as evidence for lipid-A-loss-driven CtpA induction. CtpA's specific role as the LpxF-equivalent processing step, which Zik 2022 predicted, is therefore not supported at the pre-registered bar, and that LpxF-substitute hypothesis remains untested. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The OM proteome had a single replicate per strain, so no per-protein statistics were available. The *lptC2* protein induction, Pal-Tol upregulation, LptD/LptE decline, and CCNA_01217 increase require replicated proteomics before publication-level claims can be made. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The transcriptome used a single PYE rich-medium growth condition, and the observed Fur signal represents constitutive Δ*fur* derepression rather than a direct iron-limitation response. Caulobacter SigU lacks a characterized published regulon, and the late ChvI cohort did not pass the relaxed coherence criterion. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The comparative PaperBLAST analysis was vulnerable to naming-convention false negatives, and *M. catarrhalis* was under-annotated in PaperBLAST with 162 genes total, so the report directs that its presence/absence rows be treated as advisory only. NCBI annotation strengthened the headline absence claims, but annotation-based search misses unannotated paralogs, so a deeper Pfam (protein-family hidden Markov model) search is still needed: the report specifies hmmsearch or pyhmmer queries with PF00155 serine palmitoyltransferase, PF03331 LpxC and PF01475 Fur against the named RefSeq proteomes GCF_000196795.1 (*A. baumannii* ATCC 17978), GCF_000008805.1 (*N. meningitidis* MC58) and GCF_000092265.1 (*M. catarrhalis* BBH18), which would close the residual concern that an unannotated *spt* paralog in one of the comparators was missed. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]
