# Plant Microbiome Ecotypes — Compartment-Specific Functional Guilds and Their Genetic Architecture

## Overview

This report analyzes plant association across 293,059 GTDB r214 genomes, identifying 1,136 plant-associated species and 7,995 plant-associated genomes across root, rhizosphere, phyllosphere, endophyte, and other plant contexts. It combines pangenome gene-cluster architecture, marker-gene surveys, eggNOG enrichment, GapMind pathway predictions, NMDC community data, MGnify metagenomes, and within-species phylogenetic analyses to test compartment effects, beneficial-versus-pathogenic genomic architecture, horizontal gene transfer, functional complementarity, plant-enriched gene families, dual-nature classifications, and host-specific ecotypes. Phase 2b adversarial review substantially revised several conclusions: compartment effects and complementarity effects are small, categorical cohort labels are not species-level discriminators, most marker associations are genus-scale, and phylogenetic-tree coverage limits subclade inference. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

## Key Findings

### Compartment effects are statistically significant but small

Using the refined 17-marker panel and Jaccard distances across 607 root, rhizosphere, and phyllosphere species, PERMANOVA gave pseudo-F = 23.2, p = 0.001, and R² = 0.071 with 999 permutations. PERMDISP detected dispersion heterogeneity with F = 15.6 and p = 0.001: root species were tightest around their centroid (mean distance 0.452), phyllosphere species were intermediate (0.503), and rhizosphere species were most variable (0.528). Distance-based redundancy analysis (db-RDA), which isolates centroid or location shifts, gave location-only R² = 0.060, p = 0.001; this represents 0.060 / 0.071 = 84% of the total PERMANOVA R², with 16% attributable to dispersion. Thus, compartments have real but weak functional separation of approximately 6% of variance. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The earlier Phase 1 PERMANOVA on a 25-marker panel reported R² = 0.527 and pseudo-F = 235.1, p = 0.001, but excluding the three genome-rich species per compartment reduced R² to 0.072, an 86% loss. The report attributes most of the original headline effect to taxonomic sampling and dominant rhizobial or Pseudomonas clades rather than a community-wide signal. Per-marker patterns remained directional: in the original framework, root species showed ACC deaminase enrichment with OR = 69.3, T3SS enrichment with OR = 65.6, nitrogen-fixation enrichment with OR = 14.5, and quorum-sensing enrichment with OR = 24.1; 69 of 96 marker-by-compartment tests were significant. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The genome census classified 292 species and 2,446 genomes as root-associated, 160 species and 941 genomes as rhizosphere-associated, 157 species and 1,334 genomes as phyllosphere-associated, 29 species and 291 genomes as endophytes, and 498 species and 2,983 genomes as plant-other; the total was 1,136 plant-associated species and 7,995 genomes, compared with 25,375 known non-plant species and 285,064 genomes. Root communities were dominated by Rhizobium, Mesorhizobium, and Bradyrhizobium, whereas Sphingomonas and Methylobacterium dominated the phyllosphere; Pseudomonas_E was the only genus with significant representation across all compartments. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

### Beneficial functions are more core-encoded than pathogenic functions

Beneficial plant-growth-promotion (PGP) gene clusters had a 64.6% core fraction, compared with 45.2% for pathogenic clusters and 46.8% for the genome-wide baseline. The per-species Mann–Whitney U comparison gave U = 83,567,419 and p = 3.38e-125, with a bootstrap 95% confidence interval of [0.089, 0.106] for the beneficial-minus-pathogenic difference. Biofilm genes had an 83.3% core fraction, IAA biosynthesis 78.1%, nitrogen fixation 72.3%, and phosphate solubilization 70.9%; T4SS was 48.5% singleton, coronatine toxin 45.2% singleton, and effectors 39.8% singleton. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

This architecture supports a model in which beneficial functions are more stably inherited after acquisition, while pathogenic functions remain more accessory and dynamic. The report notes a taxonomic-scope tension with a study of ten Pseudomonas strains that found biocontrol traits to be largely accessory. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

### HGT signals are mixed and scale-dependent

Among 1,136 plant-associated species, species carrying singleton marker clusters were 16 times more likely to carry transposase or integrase singletons, with Fisher OR = 15.95 and p = 8.8e-20. However, marker singletons had an overall enrichment ratio of 0.78 relative to the genomic average, indicating lower rather than higher mobility overall, with Wilcoxon p < 1e-300. Context variation was higher for pathogenic markers than beneficial markers, 0.534 versus 0.450, but the difference was not significant by Kruskal–Wallis testing, p = 0.122. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The MGnify genus-level analysis found a higher mobilome burden in plant-associated genera than in non-plant genera, with median 3.7 versus 2.8 mobile elements per genome and Mann–Whitney p = 1.49×10⁻⁵. The report explicitly contrasts this with genome-level studies reporting fewer mobile elements in plant microbiota genomes and interprets the discrepancy as potentially arising from genus-versus-genome scale: accessory elements may be diverse across plant-associated genera while individual adapted strains stabilize useful functions into the core genome. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

### Co-occurring genera show weak redundancy rather than complementarity

The corrected complementarity analysis found that co-occurring plant-associated genus pairs were slightly less complementary than random pairs, with Cohen’s d ≈ −0.4 and permutation p < 0.001. Recomputing the original max-aggregated analysis with the correct raw pair-level standard deviation gave d ≈ −0.43, while prevalence-weighted aggregation gave d = −0.39. The original NB06 value of d = −7.54 was caused mainly by dividing by the standard deviation of permutation means rather than the raw pair-level standard deviation: approximately 90% of the apparent 20-fold reduction came from the formula correction and approximately 10% from the aggregation change. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

BacDive metabolite-utilization data agreed with GapMind predictions at 83.1% consistency, supporting the reliability of the pathway completeness scores, although the GapMind core-level completeness score was 0% across compartments and may be too stringent for broad taxonomic comparisons. The refined C-score test remained structurally underpowered: among 69 NMDC co-occurring genera, there were 0 PGP-dominant, 3 pathogen-dominant, and 66 dual-or-mixed genera. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

### Plant-enriched gene families retain signal after phylum and genome-size controls

Of 5,671 eggNOG ortholog groups passing the 5% prevalence filter, 5,341, or 94.2%, were significant in raw plant-versus-non-plant tests, and 3,840 had OR > 2. The report treats this near-saturation as evidence of broad taxonomic compositional differences rather than plant-specific enrichment. After phylum-level control, 50 enriched gene families retained significance; COG3569 had Fisher OR = 8.92 and phylogenetically controlled OR = 6.01, with q = 7.7e-242, and occurred in 54% of plant-associated species versus 12% of non-plant species. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

All 50 enriched gene families were annotated: 50/50 had eggNOG descriptions and InterProScan domains, 48/50 had GO terms, and 39/50 had MetaCyc pathways. Their dominant themes were unknown-function COG category S, 19/50 or 38%; electron transport and energy metabolism, 7/50 or 14%; inorganic ion transport, 6/50 or 12%; carbohydrate metabolism, 4/50 or 8%; and secondary metabolite biosynthesis, 3/50 or 6%. The report highlights cytochrome c oxidase subunits, NADH dehydrogenase, ubiquinol–cytochrome c reductase, and IscA iron-sulfur cluster biosynthesis as candidate contributors to rhizosphere or microaerobic competitiveness. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

A real per-species logistic regression including plant status, log₁₀ genome size, and top-10 phylum dummies converged for 48/50 gene families. All 48 retained positive plant-association coefficients with absolute coefficient > 0.2 and BH-FDR q < 0.05; controlled odds ratios for the top ten ranged from 4.4 to 6.6, and q-values reached 10⁻⁹⁶. The two non-convergent cases, COG2351 and COG5343, had strong raw prevalence gaps and singular design matrices, so the report interprets them as numerical failures rather than biological rejections. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

### Refined markers confirm widespread dual-nature genomes but not a useful categorical classifier

The initial 91-marker panel was reduced to 17 plant-specific markers, and KEGG module gating required at least three genes for T3SS, at least two genes for nitrogen fixation, and three genes for T4SS. T3SS-positive species decreased from 10,300 to 1,661, a reduction of 8,855 species or 86%; nitrogen-fixation positives decreased by 346, or 14%; T4SS changed minimally. Despite stricter marker definitions, 878 of 1,115 plant-associated species, or 78.7%, were dual-nature, with 108 pathogenic, or 9.7%; 94 beneficial, or 8.4%; and 35 neutral, or 3.1%. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The broader NB02 binary-presence scheme classified 15,474 of 25,660 species, or 60.3%, as dual-nature because they carried at least one PGP and one pathogenic marker. The more assumption-laden NB07 composite scoring scheme classified 38.0% as neutral, 29.5% as pathogenic, 25.2% as dual-nature, and 7.3% as beneficial. The difference reflects the distinction between carrying both marker types and scoring highly on both genomic and metabolic axes. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

In a curated validation panel of 18 model organisms, all 14 known beneficial or pathogenic species were assigned to the dual-nature class, while all four neutral controls were misclassified: Escherichia coli K-12 and Mycobacterium tuberculosis as dual-nature, Deinococcus radiodurans as beneficial, and Thermus thermophilus as pathogenic. The continuous pathogenicity ratio, n_pathogen / (n_pgp + n_pathogen), was lower in known beneficial species than in known pathogens, with medians of 0.50 and 0.60, respectively; Mann–Whitney U = 9 and p = 0.027 for N = 7 versus 7. The report therefore treats categorical cohorts as a coarse screen and the continuous pathogen ratio as the discriminating signal. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The marker association results were also scale-dependent. Cluster-robust GLM with genus clustering across 7,555 genus clusters and 24,554 species found 8/14 markers significant at BH-FDR q < 0.05 with absolute coefficient > 0.2, including ACC deaminase OR = 8.01, T3SS OR = 2.71, nitrogen fixation OR = 2.71, phenazine OR = 2.12, CWDE cellulase OR = 1.87, phosphate solubilization OR = 1.68, CWDE pectinase OR = 1.59, and effector OR = 1.35. Within-genus label shuffling retained only nitrogen fixation, ACC deaminase, and T3SS; no multiple-testing correction was applied across the 15 markers, and Bonferroni α = 0.0033 would demote T3SS while nitrogen fixation and ACC deaminase remain significant. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

### Host and subclade structure is concentrated in a minority of species

Host extraction identified 1,307 species across 19 testable hosts with at least 10 species each. The most represented hosts were rice with 286 species, Arabidopsis thaliana with 184, wheat with 122, and maize with 98. MGnify detected 17 genera across all three crop rhizospheres, while 117 genera were unique to tomato, 54 to maize, and 5 to barley. Only 11.7% Jaccard overlap existed between 487 pangenome-derived plant genera and 438 MGnify rhizosphere genera, reflecting different isolation-metadata and metagenomic-detection processes. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

Among 65 plant-associated species with at least 20 genomes, only 18 had any phylogenetic-tree distance data; 47 lacked coverage, including Bradyrhizobium japonicum, Bradyrhizobium diazoefficiens, Mesorhizobium ciceri, Sinorhizobium medicae, Burkholderia glumae, Methylobacterium extorquens, Streptomyces scabiei, Xylella taiwanensis, and Clavibacter michiganensis. Of the 17 species meeting the testability criteria, 5, or 29%, passed Bonferroni-corrected Fisher tests at α = 0.05/17 = 0.0029: Xanthomonas vasicola, p = 1.6×10⁻⁴; Mesorhizobium sp002294985, p = 1.1×10⁻⁴; Agrobacterium pusense, p = 4.5×10⁻⁴; Pseudomonas_E avellanae, p = 7.3×10⁻⁴; and Xanthomonas campestris, p = 1.0×10⁻³. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

Three of those five species—Xanthomonas vasicola, Pseudomonas_E avellanae, and Xanthomonas campestris—also satisfied Cochran’s rule for chi-square validity. A host-by-subclade analysis found two species passing both within-species and across-species Bonferroni corrections: Xanthomonas campestris segregated toward Brassica hosts, with 46/47 genomes in one subclade and p = 2.7×10⁻¹² raw, 3.3×10⁻¹¹ after correction; Xanthomonas vasicola segregated toward Zea mays, with 47/52 genomes in one subclade and p = 1.4×10⁻¹¹ raw, 1.7×10⁻¹⁰ after correction. Ralstonia solanacearum passed BH-FDR but not across-species Bonferroni. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The report attributes the original null subclade result to a genome-ID mismatch: phylogenetic tables used bare NCBI accessions, while environmental metadata used GTDB-prefixed identifiers. Adding GB_ to GCA_ accessions and RS_ to GCF_ accessions recovered 1,306/1,306 genomes and 599 plant-associated genomes. The final interpretation is weak support for within-species plant-association segregation in 5/17 testable species, with strongest evidence in Xanthomonas and Pseudomonas, but the true effect across all 65 candidates cannot be estimated until KBase Data Lakehouse phylogenetic-tree coverage improves. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

### Annotation-table limitations were identified and operationally resolved

An audit of 22 marker Pfams found that 12 were absent from bakta_pfam_domains despite being abundant in interproscan_domains. The missing set was dominated by T3SS, T4SS, and T6SS components; the ten non-zero bakta Pfams still returned only 10–35% of the InterProScan hit counts. Versioned Pfam identifiers were recovered with LIKE patterns, yielding 19,364 hits across 7,962 species for ten queried marker Pfams. The refined cohort pipeline used InterProScan for secretion-system and CWDE detection, so its biological assignments were unaffected; the report recommends treating bakta Pfam data as a supplement rather than a substitute for InterProScan. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

## Caveats and Limitations

Plant-compartment labels were inferred primarily from NCBI isolation_source metadata, whose quality and coverage vary; only 7,995 of 293,059 genomes, or 2.7%, had plant-associated annotations, and the endophyte category contained only 29 species. BacDive identified 2,482 plant-related strains but contributed zero additional genome upgrades beyond ncbi_env. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The marker set is literature-curated and gene presence does not establish expression or phenotype. T3SS, T6SS, flagella, chemotaxis, biofilm, quorum sensing, and secretion functions can support colonization, interbacterial competition, beneficial symbiosis, or pathogenicity, and negative controls such as Escherichia, Salmonella, and Clostridioides were frequently classified as dual-nature. RNA-seq or experimental validation is required to determine whether PGP and pathogenic marker sets are co-expressed under the same conditions. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

Genome size correlates with total marker count at r = 0.44, p < 1e-300. Among plant-associated species, the dual-nature rate was 54% in the lowest genome-size quartile versus 87% in the top three quartiles; approximately 33% of dual-nature species shifted to neutral after genome-size normalization in an earlier sensitivity analysis. The 78.7% refined dual-nature value therefore contains a real genome-size gradient and remains dependent on marker definitions and scoring assumptions. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

Phylogenetic control remains incomplete. The original genus-level fixed-effect logistic regression failed to converge for 0/14 markers; the cluster-robust GLM is a practical genus-level analogue rather than a full phylogenetic generalized linear mixed model. A full GTDB-tree covariance analysis was not feasible because a dense 25K × 25K covariance matrix would be intractable in memory. The within-genus shuffle used only 200 permutations, which is under-resolved for p ≤ 0.005, and a full permulation-style analysis remains future work. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The raw OG enrichment was saturated at 5,341/5,671 significant tests, and the report cautions that this mostly reflects taxonomic divergence. Although 48/50 gene families survived phylum and genome-size control, two models were singular, and the earlier phylum-level models had convergence warnings. The causal direction between plant association and enriched functions therefore remains unresolved. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The complementarity analysis was limited by 80 GapMind pathways, a potentially over-stringent core-level completeness threshold, and loss of 62/322 genera, or 19.3%, in the NMDC taxonomy bridge. The corrected effect is small, with Cohen’s d = −0.39 for prevalence weighting, but unmatched genera could include complementary partners. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

HGT evidence is indirect because GeNomad mobile-element annotations were unavailable. The transposase/integrase co-occurrence signal, lower overall marker singleton ratio, and MGnify genus-level mobilome enrichment do not provide a single genome-level estimate of horizontal transfer. MGnify BGC, mobilome, and defense data were available only for the soil biome, so those comparisons do not directly contrast rhizosphere with bulk soil. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The MGnify and pangenome classifications have only 11.7% Jaccard overlap because they measure different phenomena: isolation metadata versus metagenomic detection. The subclade analysis is additionally limited by phylogenetic-tree coverage, with only 18/65 candidate species represented. Expanded tree coverage, accessory-gene profiling, gene-content trees, and direct mobile-element annotations are needed to resolve these uncertainties. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

## Future Directions

The report prioritizes a sparse or subsampled full PGLMM using GTDB-tree covariance; transcriptomic validation of dual-nature organisms; AlphaFold and pathway-based mechanistic analysis of COG3569, COG1845, COG0843, and COG0316; oxygen-tolerance experiments targeting high-affinity cytochrome oxidases; accessory-genome and gene-content-tree analyses of subclade adaptation; expanded KBase Data Lakehouse phylogenetic-tree coverage; finer-grained compartment sampling; reaction-level complementarity; GeNomad integration; SynCom design; and crop-specific biocontrol studies focused on host-specialized lineages. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
