## Evidence from ibd_phage_targeting

In a two-modality canonical correlation analysis (CCA), a statistical method for identifying correlated patterns across two data modalities, cadaverine loaded positively on the first canonical component (CC1). [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

The CCA pilot used 106 paired HMP2 subjects and produced four canonical correlations of 0.964, 0.928, 0.911, and 0.889. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

CC1 had a canonical correlation of r = 0.964 and a Crohn's disease-versus-non-IBD Cliff's delta of +0.498, with Mann–Whitney U-test p = 4e-4. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

Cadaverine was among the metabolites loading positively on CC1, alongside polyamines, long-chain polyunsaturated fatty acids, and fatty-acid amides. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

In paired HMP2 metabolomics, cadaverine was positively associated with Escherichia coli, with Spearman ρ = +0.45. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

The same analysis found that Escherichia coli was associated with choline at +0.25 and tryptophan at +0.25. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

The report interprets the joint positive loading of cadaverine and E. coli on CC1 as part of a dominant Crohn's disease-associated species–metabolite axis, but identifies the CCA as a pilot because the pathway modality was unavailable. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]
