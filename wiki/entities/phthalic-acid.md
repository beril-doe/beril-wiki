## Key facts

Phthalic acid was one of the 8 compounds callable through ENIGMA-isolate utilizer predictions in the ENIGMA Carbon Census. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The census identified 36 ENIGMA-isolate utilizer prediction rows for phthalic acid. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

Phthalic acid was sourced from necromass, and it was one of only 2 of the 8 ENIGMA-isolate-callable compounds with a necromass source. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The 2 necromass-sourced ENIGMA-isolate-callable compounds, phthalic acid and [[entities/terephthalic-acid|terephthalic acid]], were phthalate-class aromatics with Actinomycetota-heavy utilizer sets. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

Phthalic acid was included in the census finding that only 25 of 3109 genomes, or 0.8%, carried both an aromatic and a non-aromatic callable capacity. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The strongest reported cross-block association involving phthalic acid was its pairing with [[entities/phenylethylamine|phenylethylamine]], with Haldane-corrected odds ratio 3.37, 95% confidence interval 1.46–7.75, q=0.071, and n11=6; the corresponding Jaccard index remained approximately 0.04. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The phthalic-acid evidence is part of a specialist-dominated callable phenotype: 675 genomes carried exactly 1 of the 8 aromatic/alkaloid capacities, whereas 18 carried at least 3 and the maximum was 4. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The document cautions that the environmental atlas associated with implicated genera measures organismal abundance or occurrence rather than phthalic-acid degradation or activity, because the environmental datasets did not measure the census compounds. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]
