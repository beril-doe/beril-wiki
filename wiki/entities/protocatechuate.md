# Protocatechuate

## Identity

**Canonical name:** Protocatechuate. [^aromatic_catabolism_network]

**Known aliases:** None reported in the source document. [^aromatic_catabolism_network]

**Stable external identifier:** None reported in the source document. [^aromatic_catabolism_network]

Protocatechuate is an aromatic intermediate in *Acinetobacter baylyi* ADP1 quinate catabolism. [^aromatic_catabolism_network]

## Role in ADP1 Quinate Catabolism

Protocatechuate is processed by the [beta-ketoadipate-pathway](beta-ketoadipate-pathway.md), which converts aromatic substrates through β-ketoadipate to succinyl-CoA and acetyl-CoA for entry into the TCA cycle. [^aromatic_catabolism_network]

The [quinate-degradation-pathway](quinate-degradation-pathway.md) includes protocatechuate 3,4-dioxygenase, encoded by pcaGH, which cleaves the protocatechuate aromatic ring. [^aromatic_catabolism_network]

Protocatechuate 3,4-dioxygenase is a non-heme Fe²⁺-dependent intradiol ring-cleavage enzyme, linking protocatechuate catabolism to iron acquisition in the reported 51-gene support network. [^aromatic_catabolism_network]

The pca/qui region forms a 12-gene operon spanning pcaIJFBDCHG-quiABC and transport genes. [^aromatic_catabolism_network]

## Relation to the Support Network

Protocatechuate is part of the aromatic-pathway core surrounded by the 51-gene quinate-specific support network. [^aromatic_catabolism_network]

The network assigns 44/51 genes (86%) to four functional subsystems: 8 aromatic-pathway genes, 21 Complex I genes, 7 iron-acquisition genes, and 2 PQQ-biosynthesis genes. [^aromatic_catabolism_network]

The source links protocatechuate cleavage to the network’s iron-acquisition subsystem because pcaGH requires non-heme Fe²⁺. [^aromatic_catabolism_network]
