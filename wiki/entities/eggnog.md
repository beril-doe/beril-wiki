# eggNOG

## What it is

**Canonical name:** eggNOG. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]

**Known aliases:** eggNOG; no additional alias is reported in these documents. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas, bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

**Stable external identifier:** Not reported in these documents. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas, bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

eggNOG is a functional annotation dataset used to characterize antimicrobial-resistance (AMR) clusters, compare orthologous-group (OG) enrichment, assess metabolic functions in lineage- and environment-associated genomes, identify plant-enriched gene families, and classify prophage-associated gene modules. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas, bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory, clay_confined_subsurface, plant_microbiome_ecotypes, prophage_ecology]

## Key facts

- The AMR pangenome analysis compared eggNOG annotations for 77K AMR clusters with an 86M-cluster pangenome baseline. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]
- Among AMR clusters with eggNOG annotations, COG V (defense mechanisms) was enriched 7.05-fold, comprising 14.9% of AMR clusters versus 2.1% of the baseline; COG P (inorganic ion transport) was enriched 1.93-fold, comprising 10.7% versus 5.6%; and COG J (translation) was enriched 1.50-fold. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]
- COG L (replication), COG I (lipid metabolism), and COG N (cell motility) were depleted to 0.12-fold, 0.10-fold, and 0.08-fold, respectively. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]
- Bakta product annotations and eggNOG hits were present for 93.0% of AMR clusters, while 7.0% had Bakta annotations only; among sparsely annotated one-source clusters, 55.4% were singletons versus 34.6% of two-source clusters. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]
- A 32-species, 357,623-gene comparison **supports** the broader interpretation that conserved and novel gene classes have distinct functional profiles: novel or singleton genes were enriched in COG L (mobile elements) by +10.88%, COG V (defense mechanisms) by +2.83%, and COG S (unknown function) by +1.64%, while core genes were depleted in COG J by -4.65%, COG F by -2.09%, COG H by -2.06%, COG E by -1.81%, and COG C by -1.75%. [src: cog_analysis]
- This cross-species result **refines** the AMR-specific COG L result: COG L was depleted to 0.12-fold in AMR clusters but enriched among novel or singleton genes by +10.88%, indicating that functional distributions are dependent on the comparison class rather than universally directional. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas, cog_analysis]
- The cross-species analysis found the COG L enrichment consistent across species and interpreted it as evidence that mobile elements contribute substantially to genomic novelty; however, approximately 70% of genes had COG annotations, so unassigned genes may skew the distributions. [src: cog_analysis]
- Composite COG assignments were counted once per gene rather than split among component letters. The LV composite showed +0.34% enrichment with 76% consistency and was interpreted as a possible multifunctional mobile-defense module. This **qualifies** category-level interpretation because eggNOG v6 annotations may differ from original COG assignments. [src: cog_analysis]
- A 132.5M-gene-cluster comparison **supports** using eggNOG alongside Bakta rather than as a replacement: eggNOG had higher COG coverage (51% versus 8.2%), KEGG coverage (38.5% versus 17.3%), and Pfam coverage (63% versus 7.7%), whereas Bakta had higher GO coverage (15% versus 7.4%), product descriptions (71.2% versus 70.4%), and unique UniRef50 links covering 79.2%. [src: discoveries]
- The eggNOG–Bakta union increased any-functional-annotation coverage to 77.3%, and Bakta rescued 11.2M clusters among 39.2M missed by eggNOG; only 33.3% of Bakta’s 17.6M distinct UniRef50 IDs existed in the BERDL UniProt identifier table. This **refines** the earlier AMR result in which 93.0% of clusters had either annotation source, showing that apparent coverage depends on the annotation dimension and corpus. [src: discoveries, amr_pangenome_atlas]
- eggNOG annotation joins must respect cluster scope: `eggnog_mapper_annotations.query_name` joins to `gene_cluster.gene_cluster_id`, not `gene.gene_id`; cross-species gene-cluster identifiers are species-specific and should instead be compared through shared representations such as COG categories, KEGG orthologs, or Pfam domains. This **refines** cross-species COG interpretation by identifying the required integration keys. [src: pitfalls]
- In the Bacillota_B comparison, per-OG Fisher’s exact tests across 14,109 Firmicutes-level OGs compared 10 deep-clay anchor genomes with 62 soil-baseline genomes using Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate (FDR) correction, a fold-difference threshold of ≥3, and a minimum of ≥3 anchor genomes positive. This identified 547 OGs enriched at q<0.05, strongly supporting the preregistered prediction of at least 10 enriched OGs. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]
- This application **refines** the AMR workflow’s broad eggNOG functional categories: keyword scans classified 42 enriched OGs as anaerobic-respiration functions, 24 as sporulation-revival functions, and 12 as mineral-attachment or exopolysaccharide functions, while manual inspection indicated that the 462-OG “other or unannotated” category contained additional electron-transfer and anaerobic-respiration signal. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]
- eggNOG cluster-level annotation propagation within ≥90% average-nucleotide-identity (AAI) clusters can miss strain-level marker variants, including a single non-functional dsrA in an otherwise complete operon. This **qualifies** the interpretation of OG- and keyword-level functional counts in the deep-clay analysis. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]
- The deep-clay report proposes testing all standard amino-acid-biosynthesis EC numbers in eggNOG as a finer-grained sensitivity analysis of the 18-pathway GapMind completeness metric. This **extends** eggNOG’s use from OG categorization toward resolving pathway-completeness ceiling effects. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]
- Annotation-proxy results **qualify** eggNOG-based marker interpretation: `Preferred_name='lanM'` produced 505 additional hits with zero overlap with 62 Bakta-validated Lanmodulin genomes, eggNOG KO K02030 produced 46,369 nonspecific hits, and only 418 of 5,092 genomes with any xoxF marker hit both eggNOG K00114 and Bakta lanthanide-dependent methanol-dehydrogenase products. Marker definitions should therefore be reported explicitly rather than treating generic KOs or stale preferred names as definitive. [src: discoveries]
- The lanthanide methylotrophy atlas **refines** this calibration: among 134,578 hit-bearing rows, `Preferred_name='lanM'` had 505 eggNOG-only calls versus 62 Bakta-only Lanmodulin calls, while eggNOG K02030 had 46,369 eggNOG-only calls; the report therefore uses Bakta as the source of truth for Lanmodulin and xoxJ, eggNOG K00114 as primary for xoxF with Bakta as a union source, and eggNOG K14028 as primary for mxaF. [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]
- The same atlas **supports** using eggNOG for broad marker discovery but **qualifies** functional interpretation: K00114 identified 3,690 xoxF genomes and K14028 identified 195 mxaF genomes, whereas 2,320 xoxF-bearing genomes initially lacked eggNOG PQQ-biosynthesis annotations; 1,472 had partial eggNOG pqq calls, 33 had complete pqqA-E calls, and 897 had no PQQ evidence from either eggNOG or Bakta. [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]
- In 108 ENIGMA samples, eggNOG-derived COG-fraction proxies were used to estimate site defense and stress scores from pangenome-linked community data. This **extends** eggNOG’s environmental use but **qualifies** broad-category interpretation: confirmatory genus-level contamination–defense tests were null, while coverage-adjusted exploratory defense associations were sensitive to mapping mode, covariates, taxonomic resolution, and FDR. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]
- The ENIGMA analysis therefore **refines** the earlier evidence that eggNOG categories can reveal defense-enriched content: broad COG proxies did not establish a robust contamination-linked functional shift, and the report recommends curated metal-stress gene sets and pathway-level summaries for follow-up. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]
- In the pan-bacterial defense analysis, eggNOG description-based CRISPR-Cas detection produced 96% species prevalence, whereas the specific Cas1 Pfam marker PF01867 produced approximately 55% prevalence on the same pangenome. This **refines** the earlier marker-calibration caveats: the 96% value is treated as an upper bound, and CRISPR-Cas comparisons must specify their detection method. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]
- The combined EggNOG-or-Pfam defense-presence set prioritized recall for syndrome analysis; this **qualifies** interpretation of CRISPR-Cas syndrome specificity even though the report did not expect the significance results to be invalidated. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]
- The PHB-granule analysis **supports** eggNOG’s use for pathway-scale pangenome censuses: 118,513 PHB-related gene clusters were identified across 19,496 species, including 86,318 phaA clusters, 11,792 phaC clusters, 9,617 phaB clusters, 6,130 phaP clusters, and 4,656 phaZ clusters; no phaR clusters were identified. [src: phb_granule_ecology]
- This result **qualifies** interpretation of partial pathway calls: the 46.5% “precursors-only” category likely overestimates PHB capability because phaA and phaB also participate in general fatty-acid and thiolase metabolism, and the apparent absence of phaR may reflect incomplete annotation or misassignment of K18080. [src: phb_granule_ecology]
- The PHB analysis also **qualifies** domain-based interpretation: all 11,792 phaC clusters were classified as “other_pfam” because eggNOG domain fields contained names such as Abhydrolase_1 and PhaC_N rather than the expected Pfam accession IDs PF00561 and PF07167; mapping those names to the accessions is required for PHA synthase class analysis. [src: phb_granule_ecology]
- In the plant microbiome analysis, eggNOG was used with pangenome architecture, InterProScan, GapMind, NMDC, MGnify, and host metadata to test plant-associated gene-family enrichment across 293,059 GTDB r214 genomes. Of 5,671 eggNOG ortholog groups passing the 5% prevalence filter, 5,341 (94.2%) were significant in raw plant-versus-non-plant tests and 3,840 had OR > 2; this **qualifies** raw enrichment as largely taxonomic rather than necessarily plant-specific. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
- After phylum-level control, 50 enriched gene families retained significance; COG3569 had Fisher OR = 8.92 and phylogenetically controlled OR = 6.01, with q = 7.7e-242, and occurred in 54% of plant-associated species versus 12% of non-plant species. A per-species logistic regression controlling for plant status, log₁₀ genome size, and top-10 phylum dummies converged for 48/50 families; all 48 retained positive plant-association coefficients with absolute coefficient > 0.2 and BH-FDR q < 0.05, with controlled odds ratios for the top ten ranging from 4.4 to 6.6 and q-values reaching 10⁻⁹⁶. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
- All 50 enriched families had eggNOG descriptions and InterProScan domains, 48/50 had GO terms, and 39/50 had MetaCyc pathways. Their dominant themes were unknown-function COG category S (19/50, or 38%), electron transport and energy metabolism (7/50, or 14%), inorganic ion transport (6/50, or 12%), carbohydrate metabolism (4/50, or 8%), and secondary metabolite biosynthesis (3/50, or 6%). This **supports** eggNOG’s use for controlled discovery of plant-associated functions while leaving causal direction unresolved. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
- The plant analysis **refines** the earlier coverage and category caveats: COG3569 and other retained families may contribute to rhizosphere or microaerobic competitiveness, but the raw 5,341/5,671 signal, two singular models (COG2351 and COG5343), and incomplete phylogenetic control prevent treating these enrichments as definitive plant adaptations. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
- In a prophage ecology analysis, eggNOG annotations identified prophage-associated modules across 27,702 bacterial species and 4,005,537 annotated gene clusters: packaging and lysogenic regulation occurred in 100.0% of species, lysis in 99.9%, integration in 99.1%, anti-defense in 64.3%, head morphogenesis in 56.1%, tail in 55.6%, and all 7 modules in 34.9%. This **extends** eggNOG-based pangenome annotation from broad functional categories to module-level viral ecology. [src: prophage_ecology]
- The prophage analysis **supports** environmental and structural interpretation of eggNOG module calls: packaging, lysis, and lysogenic-regulation genes were more consistently co-localized, whereas integration and anti-defense genes were more dispersed; environmental enrichment was concentrated in tail, head morphogenesis, and anti-defense modules. [src: prophage_ecology]
- This result **qualifies** the apparent near-universal prophage prevalence because the calls were based on eggNOG functional annotations rather than dedicated prophage detectors; domesticated remnants, bacterial homologs, and bacterial integrases may therefore contribute, and the false-positive rate was uncharacterized. [src: prophage_ecology]
- The same study **supports** using eggNOG annotations in cross-collection analyses: pangenome module enrichments agreed with NMDC metagenomic correlations for head morphogenesis, tail, and anti-defense, although NMDC prophage burden was inferred indirectly and had not been independently validated for prophage genes. [src: prophage_ecology]

Together, these results **support** [[concepts/pangenome-integration]]: eggNOG categories distinguish conserved functions from mobile, defense, unknown-function, pathway-associated, and prophage-associated genome content, while AMR-, lineage-, environment-, methylotrophy-, PHB-, phage-defense-, plant-association-, and prophage-specific analyses show that broad categories, cluster propagation, annotation coverage, marker definitions, detection method, comparison class, phylogenetic control, and environmental mapping resolution can affect the apparent signal. The pitfalls report **refines** this integration guidance by requiring live schema inspection and cluster-level join validation before interpreting eggNOG annotations. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas, bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory, clay_confined_subsurface, cog_analysis, discoveries, enigma_contamination_functional_potential, lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas, phage_defense_arsenal, phb_granule_ecology, plant_microbiome_ecotypes, pitfalls, prophage_ecology]
