# Fisher's exact test with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR

This page covers per-feature Fisher's exact tests with Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (BH-FDR) correction, as used to compare cohorts in several reports. Features include markers, KEGG orthologs (KOs), ortholog groups (OGs), COG (clusters of orthologous groups) categories and metabolites. The reports below apply the test to different features and correct over different sets of tests. One Oak Ridge comparison found 8,482 of 10,845 KOs significant. [src: oak_ridge_cultivation_gap] At Harvard Forest, no individual RNA-pool KO survived FDR, and no individual metabolite passed BH-FDR. [src: harvard_forest_warming] In the plant microbiome work, 94.2% of tested OGs were significant. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The soil metal report calls its 60% discovery rate high and warns that BH-FDR is anti-conservative when tests are correlated. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]

## Key facts

Some reports add fold-change and minimum-support filters. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

## Use in genome-cohort comparisons

- Subsurface Bacillota_B: Fisher's exact tests on 14,109 Firmicutes-level eggNOG OGs compared 10 deep-clay anchor genomes with 62 baseline genomes. Filters were q<0.05, fold-difference≥3 and ≥3 positive anchor genomes. They gave 547 enriched OGs. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] The report calls this robust for large effects. It says marginal effects with anchor counts of 3–5 should be treated descriptively. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] OGs came from Firmicutes-level eggNOG orthology (tax 1239) where present, with a fallback to Bacteria/root. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]
- Clay-confined subsurface: per-marker Fisher tests compared anchor_deep with soil_baseline genomes. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] Wood–Ljungdahl complete was in 5 of 9 versus 15 of 140 genomes (OR=10.4, p_BH=0.004). [src: clay_confined_subsurface] NiFe complete was in 7 of 9 versus 35 of 140 (OR=10.5, p_BH=0.004). [src: clay_confined_subsurface] Nif complete was in 4 of 9 versus 18 of 140 (OR=5.4, p_BH=0.035). [src: clay_confined_subsurface] Sulfate-reduction markers had OR=33.8, p_BH=2×10⁻⁴. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] Within Bacillota_B, SR_complete was in 5 of 5 deep genomes versus 4 of 19 baseline genomes (OR=∞, p=0.003, BH-adjusted p=0.044). [src: clay_confined_subsurface]
- The same report's corrected iron-reduction markers gave a null. Deep clay was slightly higher than shallow, but no cohort comparison was significant (all Fisher p ≥ 0.46). [src: clay_confined_subsurface]
- Oak Ridge cultivation gap: of 10,845 KOs tested (the union of both cohorts), 8,482 (78.2%) differed in prevalence (Fisher's exact, BH-FDR < 0.05) between 3,102 cultured ENIGMA Genome Depot isolates and 2,279 subsurface/groundwater MAGs. [src: oak_ridge_cultivation_gap]
- Ecotype differentiation: 257 chi-square/Fisher tests ran across 12 species × 23 COG categories. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] 170 of 257 (66.1%) were significant after BH-FDR (q < 0.05). [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] The same report found adaptive COG differentiation magnitude 2.1x larger than housekeeping, using a one-sided Mann-Whitney U test (p = 2.53 x 10^-6). [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- PGP pangenome: across 11,272 species with at least one plant-growth-promoting gene, 8 of 10 focal-gene pairs were significant after BH-FDR. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology] Comparing 1,039 soil/rhizosphere species with 10,233 species from other environments, three of five focal genes were enriched in soil at BH-FDR q < 0.05. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology] ACC (1-aminocyclopropane-1-carboxylate) deaminase was reported as 7× more prevalent in soil/rhizosphere species, and the enrichment survived phylum-level fixed effects in logistic regression (OR = 6.98, p = 4.8e-61). [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology] Tryptophan-only logistic Model 1 predicted ipdC presence (OR = 2.81, 95% CI 1.97–4.01, p = 1.4e-08, n = 11,272). [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]
- Plant microbiome ecotypes: Fisher's exact tests with BH-FDR found 94.2% of tested OGs (5,341/5,671) significantly associated with plant status. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Across 96 marker×compartment combinations, 69 were significant (q<0.05). [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
- Prophage and AMR (antimicrobial resistance): AMR genes within 10 genes of a prophage marker were 67.6% accessory versus 65.5% for distal AMR genes, a modest effect (Fisher's exact OR=1.10, p=0.005, bootstrap 95% CI [1.024, 1.185]). [src: prophage_amr_comobilization] The report used a one-sided Fisher's exact test with a bootstrap confidence interval for this comparison. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]
- Metal specificity: metal-specific genes matched metal-resistance keywords more often than general sick genes (12.2% vs 7.8%, OR=1.64, p=2.4e-8). [src: metal_specificity] Novel metal candidate families were less often metal-specific than annotated ones (45.6% vs 58.2%, OR=0.60, p=0.003). [src: metal_specificity]
- Soil metal functional genomics: 2,355 significant COG-metal associations (FDR < 0.05) were found across 9 metals (Cu, Co, Cr, Ni, Zn, Pb, As, Cd, Hg) in 51,748 soil samples. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]

## Use in warming and field studies

The Harvard Forest report states that its omics sample sizes (n=28 metagenome, n=39 metatranscriptome) limit per-KO FDR power across 12-14K KOs. [src: harvard_forest_warming]

- A curated 62-KO C-cycling list was tested for enrichment among heated-up KOs (q<0.10, log2 FC > 0). [src: harvard_forest_warming] DNA × organic gave 5 / 57 versus 428 / 12,806 hits (OR=2.78, one-sided p=0.042), a significant enrichment. [src: harvard_forest_warming] DNA × mineral gave 0 / 57 versus 2 / 12,806 (p=1.0). [src: harvard_forest_warming] RNA × organic and RNA × mineral each had 0 / 57 versus 0 / 14,245 hits (p=1.0). [src: harvard_forest_warming]
- No individual RNA-pool KO survived FDR (q<0.10) at n=11+11. [src: harvard_forest_warming] The carbon-cycling RNA-pool signals have only nominal p-values. [src: harvard_forest_warming]
- Per-phylum Welch t-tests used n=14 control + 14 heated samples, with BH-FDR across phyla × horizon. [src: harvard_forest_warming] Actinobacteria in organic soil rose from 0.249 to 0.315 (log2 FC +0.341, q 0.049). [src: harvard_forest_warming]
- No individual metabolite passed BH-FDR. [src: harvard_forest_warming] Nominal signals included ChEBI:71028 (11/11 vs 6/11, p=0.035), [src: harvard_forest_warming] ChEBI:30918 (0/11 vs 6/11, p=0.012) [src: harvard_forest_warming] and ChEBI:27967 (OR=12, 10/11 vs 5/11, p=0.063). [src: harvard_forest_warming]

## Use as a guard against false positives

- ENIGMA contamination: high-coverage defense-score associations stayed non-significant after global FDR (q = 0.301 relaxed, 0.189 strict). [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] All confirmatory index variants, including uranium-only, were non-significant (q = 0.546 across 8 tests). [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] Exploratory coverage-aware defense associations kept positive effects but were attenuated under global control. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] The diagnostics table reports global BH-FDR q-values across all p-values. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]
- Oak Ridge lab–field work: Azospirillum's uranium association was marginal (rho=+0.20, p=0.042, q=0.077). [src: lab_field_ecology] Of 14 Fitness Browser genera detected, 11 met the prevalence threshold of ≥10 sites. [src: lab_field_ecology] After BH-FDR, 5 genera correlated significantly with uranium. [src: lab_field_ecology] Lab metal tolerance and field abundance ratio were suggestive but not significant (rho=0.503, p=0.095, n=12 genera). [src: lab_field_ecology]
- MicrobeAtlas metal ecology: BH-FDR across 47 tests left 23 significant at q < 0.05. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] The bacterial metal type diversity effect in simple B_std PGLS (phylogenetic generalized least squares) had q = 0.0028. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] The primary result survived Bonferroni (α/47=0.0011) and FDR (q=0.003). [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]
- Limits of BH correction: the soil metal report calls 2,355 significant associations from 9 metals × 435 COGs = 3,915 tests (51,748 samples) a high 60% discovery rate. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics] It also notes that metals co-contaminate, so tests are not independent. BH-FDR assumes independent tests and is anti-conservative under positive correlation, so the true FDR is likely higher than reported. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]

## Related paired tests

Paired Wilcoxon tests of copy-number rates are a different test, listed for contrast. Adaptive COG categories L, V, M, K had higher multi-copy rates than F, H in 24 of 24 species (median ratio 8.14×, paired Wilcoxon p = 5.96 × 10⁻⁸). [src: gene_copy_number_variation] All 8 pairwise comparisons were significant at BH-FDR < 0.01. [src: gene_copy_number_variation] All four one-sided Wilcoxon core-versus-accessory tests were significant. [src: gene_copy_number_variation]

## Open Directions

- Re-run the small-cohort tests (for example 9 deep-clay genomes) with exact-test power estimates.
- Pool Harvard Forest RNA data or add replicates to test whether the C-cycling signal survives FDR.
- Compare filtered and unfiltered Fisher results to show how fold and minimum-support filters change the OG list.
- Re-test the COG-metal associations with a correction that allows for correlated tests, or per-metal conditional models, to estimate the true FDR.
