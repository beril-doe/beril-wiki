## Evidence from ecotype functional differentiation

*Enterobacter kobei* was one of the 12 species in which valid gene-content ecotypes were detected in the study’s 15-species sample. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]

The analysis included **252 genomes** of *Enterobacter kobei*, identified **2 ecotypes**, and assigned **252 genomes** to those ecotypes. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]

The species met the study’s validity criteria for ecotype clustering: at least 2 ecotypes, at least 10 genomes per ecotype, and at least 20 assigned genomes in total. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]

The ecotype assignment was generated by applying principal-component analysis (PCA) to gene-content profiles, followed by KMeans clustering with candidate cluster counts from 2 through 6 and selection by silhouette score. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]

COG (Clusters of Orthologous Groups) profiles were compared between the two ecotypes using chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests with Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]

The source provides species-level aggregate results across the 12 successful species but does not report a *K. kobei*-specific silhouette score or category-by-category significance results. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]
