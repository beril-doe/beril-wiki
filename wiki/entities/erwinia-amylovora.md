## Evidence from ecotype functional differentiation

*Erwinia amylovora* was one of the 12 species in which valid within-species gene-content ecotypes were identified from the 15-species sample. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] The analysis assigned **231 genomes** to **2 ecotypes** for this species. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]

Among the species analyzed, *Erwinia amylovora* showed the clearest ecotype separation, with a silhouette score of **0.468**. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] Clustering used principal-component analysis (PCA), a dimensionality-reduction method, followed by KMeans clustering. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]

The ecotype comparison used COG (Clusters of Orthologous Groups) profiles and chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests with Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] The species-level result supports the study’s broader finding that gene-content ecotypes can be functionally differentiated, although the report cautions that phylogenetic structure, annotation coverage, sampling, and clustering assumptions were not fully controlled. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]
