# nifH

## What this entity is

**Canonical name:** nifH. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

**Known aliases:** No additional aliases were reported in this document. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

**Stable external identifier:** None was reported in this document. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

nifH is treated in this analysis as a marker associated with diazotrophic plant-growth-promoting bacteria and a guild ecologically separated from the non-diazotrophic [pqqc](pqqc.md)–[acds](acds.md) group. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

## Evidence from PGP pangenome ecology

Among the 27,702 species analyzed, nifH was one of the 13 focal plant-growth-promoting gene markers. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

nifH was negatively associated with [hcnc](hcnc.md) (OR = 0.23, q = 5.8e-29) and [pqqc](pqqc.md) (OR = 0.57, q = 2.9e-19), while its association with [ipdc](ipdc.md) was not significant (OR = 1.13, q = 0.54). [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

These associations support ecological separation between nifH-bearing diazotrophs and pqqC/acdS-bearing rhizobacteria, although co-occurrence patterns do not by themselves establish mechanism. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

Only 157 species (1.4%) carried at least three focal traits; the most common multi-trait genotype was pqqC + acdS (n = 153), followed by nifH + pqqC (n = 225), despite the overall negative nifH–pqqC association. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

In the environment comparison, nifH was depleted in soil-classified species: prevalence was 12.9% in 1,039 soil/rhizosphere species versus 19.7% in 10,233 species from other environments (OR = 0.60, q = 5.5e-08). [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

[bacillota-b](bacillota-b.md) was an exception, showing nifH enrichment in soil (OR = ∞, q = 2.5e-4). [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

## Pangenome distribution

nifH had a core fraction of 63.8%, an auxiliary fraction of 15.1%, and a singleton fraction of 21.0%; its core fraction was significantly higher than the 46.8% genome-wide baseline (q = 4.3e-71). [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

The nifH distribution therefore contributes to the report’s finding that PGP genes were generally more core-associated than the genome-wide background, while nifH retained a larger singleton fraction than several highlighted PGP genes. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

The nifH count was 2,756 rather than the approximately 1,913 estimated in the research plan because the GTDB r214 pangenome had grown incrementally; the report states that this discrepancy did not affect the analyses. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

## Interpretation and limitations

The results support nifH as a marker of a PGP ecological guild distinct from the soil-enriched, non-diazotrophic pqqC + acdS module, but the conclusion is based on gene co-occurrence and environment labels rather than direct physiological measurements. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

The report recommends deeper ecological stratification of nifH as follow-up work because the aggregate soil comparison may conceal lineage-specific distributions, including the [bacillota-b](bacillota-b.md) exception. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

PGP detection used exact Bakta gene-name matches, so variant names or product-only annotations may have been missed. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

Gene-cluster annotations were not functionally validated, and truncations, frameshifts, and pseudogenization were not filtered; consequently, a nifH annotation was not necessarily evidence of a functional gene. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]
