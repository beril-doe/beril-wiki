# Cystic fibrosis sputum

This page is about cystic fibrosis (CF) sputum, the amino acid-dominated airway environment where *P. aeruginosa* grows, which is invoked to explain retained amino acid catabolism. Two reports invoke it, and their evidence is mostly literature-based interpretation plus one assay profile that agrees with it. [src: cf_formulation_design, pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] See [[entities/pseudomonas-aeruginosa]] and [[entities/pseudomonas-aeruginosa-pa14]].

## Key facts

- Palmer et al. (2005, 2007) are cited as establishing that amino acids are *P. aeruginosa*'s primary carbon sources in CF sputum. [src: cf_formulation_design]
- Palmer et al. (2007) is also cited as showing that CF sputum is dominated by amino acids, especially aromatic amino acids. The report offers this as the ecological explanation for why *P. aeruginosa* keeps amino acid pathways while losing sugar catabolism. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- Flynn et al. (2016) is cited as showing that *P. aeruginosa* relies on amino acids and short-chain fatty acids from anaerobic mucin-fermenting bacteria in CF airways. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- The abundant amino acids named in the working theory are proline, histidine, ornithine, glutamate, aspartate and arginine. [src: cf_formulation_design]

## Use in the competitive exclusion theory

The cf_formulation_design report's working theory is that commensal communities can exclude *P. aeruginosa* by consuming the carbon sources it depends on. These are primarily the amino acids listed above. [src: cf_formulation_design] See also [[concepts/community-level-niche-coverage-and-resource-depletion-for-competitive-exclusion]] and [[summaries/cf_formulation_design__REPORT]].

The report's PA14 carbon utilization ranking was proline > histidine > ornithine > glutamate. The report says this mirrors the SCFM composition and validates its assay system. [src: cf_formulation_design] It reads the profile as consistent with amino acid-rich sputum and as showing that the assay conditions capture the relevant competitive landscape. [src: cf_formulation_design]

Two caveats limit this. The report tested 22 carbon sources. These covered major amino acids and glucose/lactate but left out mucins, lipids, iron, polyamines and the sugar alcohols its genomic analysis predicts to matter. [src: cf_formulation_design] The CF lung also holds nutrients beyond amino acids. Iron limitation and acidic pH may reveal further competitive advantages or vulnerabilities. [src: cf_formulation_design] See [[concepts/generalizability-limits-of-planktonic-single-strain-inhibition-assays]].

## Use in explaining pathway loss

The pseudomonas_carbon_ecology report calls its Pseudomonas subgenus contrast consistent with *P. aeruginosa* retaining amino acid catabolism for host growth, such as on CF sputum amino acids. In this reading the species loses degradation of plant-derived carbon sources it no longer encounters. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] The report says the loss of plant sugar catabolism agrees with La Rosa et al. (2018). That study tracked *P. aeruginosa* adaptation in CF lungs and found convergent loss of carbon catabolism with retained amino acid utilization. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] See [[concepts/host-adaptation-pathway-loss-in-pseudomonas-carbon-catabolism]], [[concepts/pathogen-metabolic-adaptation-in-the-lung-and-stability-of-competition-targets]] and [[summaries/pseudomonas_carbon_ecology__REPORT]].
