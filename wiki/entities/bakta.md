# Bakta

## What this entity is

**Bakta** is the canonical name for a bacterial genome annotation method used in these reports for antimicrobial-resistance (AMR) gene classification. [src: amr_fitness_cost] The pan-bacterial atlas **supports** this role, applying uniform Bakta and AMRFinderPlus annotations across 27,690 pangenome species before integrating conservation, function, environmental, and fitness data. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] The lanthanide methylotrophy atlas **supports and broadens** Bakta's role as a product-level annotation source for metabolic-marker detection across a 293,059-genome collection. [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]

**Known aliases:** Bakta bacterial genome annotation; `bakta_amr`; `bakta_annotations`. [src: amr_fitness_cost]

**Stable external identifier:** No stable external identifier is specified in these reports. [src: amr_fitness_cost]

## Annotation data and integration

The fitness-cost analysis used the [[entities/kbase-ke-pangenome]] tables `bakta_amr` and `bakta_annotations` to identify and classify AMR genes before linking them to [[entities/kescience-fitnessbrowser]] fitness measurements. [src: amr_fitness_cost] The broader Bakta-based assembly contained **178 Tier 1** AMR genes and **1,174 Tier 2** keyword-annotated genes; the fitness-linked analysis included **1,352 AMR genes** across **43 organisms**. [src: amr_fitness_cost] Tier 1 comprised **110 genes** in the fitness-linked analysis, while Tier 2 comprised **691 genes**. [src: amr_fitness_cost] Tier 2 represented **86%** of the AMR genes and could include non-AMR genes such as general efflux transporters; a Tier 1-versus-Tier 2 sensitivity comparison found indistinguishable fitness distributions (**KS p = 0.17**). [src: amr_fitness_cost]

The atlas **refines** this workflow at larger scale: Bakta product annotations and eggNOG hits were present for **93.0%** of AMR clusters, while **7.0%** had Bakta annotations only. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] It identified **83,008 AMRFinderPlus hits** on gene-cluster representatives, spanning **82,908 distinct clusters**, **1,939 AMR gene families**, **2,079 AMR products**, and **14,723 species**. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] Its mechanism categories were assigned by keyword matching against AMRFinderPlus product descriptions rather than the CARD Antibiotic Resistance Ontology (ARO), making this a related but distinct classification layer from the Bakta-derived classifier used in the fitness-cost analysis. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]

The new prophage analysis **refines** interpretation of Bakta annotation products: keyword and Pfam matching in `bakta_annotations` was used to identify prophage markers, but may include false positives such as phage-defense systems and miss divergent prophages because dedicated prophage predictors were not used. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization] Across the GTDB pangenome, this annotation-derived inventory contained **3,465,244 broad prophage marker clusters** and **1,261,929 strict prophage-marker clusters**; **83.8%** of broad markers were accessory and **53.8%** singleton. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization] Of **27,702 species**, **14,669 (52.9%)** carried both AMR and prophage markers. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization] In a sampled set of **36,041 AMR gene instances** from **1,953 genomes** across **100 species**, **20,073 (55.7%)** were on contigs carrying strict prophage markers, although only **1,991 (5.5%)** were within 5 genes and distances were based on ordinal gene positions rather than base-pair coordinates. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization] This **supports** Bakta's utility for large-scale candidate discovery but **does not establish** prophage-mediated AMR mobilization; the species-level association between prophage-marker density and AMR repertoire breadth was Spearman rho **0.572** across **4,770 species** (p<10^-300), with partial rho **0.464** after controlling for genome count. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]

The new pitfalls guidance **refines** interpretation and reproducibility of these annotation tables: Bakta and InterProScan annotations in the pangenome are joined at the gene-cluster level, with `eggnog_mapper_annotations.query_name` joining to `gene_cluster.gene_cluster_id`, not `gene.gene_id`. [src: pitfalls] Because BERDL schemas and namespace addresses can change during Delta-to-Iceberg migration, live catalog and schema discovery should precede reuse of historical Bakta queries. [src: pitfalls] Numeric fields in relevant pangenome metadata may be strings and must be explicitly cast before arithmetic, ordering, comparisons, or aggregation. [src: pitfalls]

In the annotation-gap study, Bakta was repurposed beyond AMR classification: its annotations were queried for alternative EC numbers and product-name matches against gapfilled reactions. [src: annotation_gap_discovery] This **extends** the method's role to recovering metabolic gene candidates; the Bakta stream produced **1,459** EC candidate entries and added **22** newly resolved reaction-organism pairs (**10.9%** of 201 pairs). [src: annotation_gap_discovery] These assignments were integrated with [[entities/diamond]]/BLAST homology, fitness, and pangenome evidence rather than treated as sufficient alone. [src: annotation_gap_discovery]

The lanthanide atlas **refines** this evidence-integration principle through marker-specific calibration: Bakta was preferred for lanmodulin because it identified **62** product-level Lanmodulin calls, whereas eggNOG produced **505** non-specific or false-positive `lanM` calls; Bakta was also preferred for xoxJ, eggNOG K00114 was primary for xoxF with Bakta used as a union source, and eggNOG K14028 was primary for mxaF. [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas] Among **2,185** xoxF genomes lacking eggNOG PQQ annotations, **1,288 (59%)** had at least one Bakta PQQ product, while **897** lacked PQQ evidence from either source. [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas] Thus Bakta can recover metabolic-marker evidence missed by eggNOG, but product calls do not establish complete pathways or enzyme activity. [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]

The PGP pangenome analysis **extends** Bakta's metabolic-marker use to plant-growth-promoting genes: exact Bakta gene-name matches detected 13 markers across 27,702 species, including 11,272 species carrying at least one marker. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology] Soil/rhizosphere enrichment was found for `acdS`, `pqqC`, and `hcnC`, while `nifH` was depleted; these are gene-distribution results rather than functional validation. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology] PGP genes were more core than the 46.8% genome-wide baseline: `pqqC` was 81.5% core and `acdS` 70.4% core, while `pqqD` was a partial exception at 55.5% core and 27.5% singleton. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology] Product-only or variant names may be missed, and truncations, frameshifts, and pseudogenization were not filtered. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]

## Pan-bacterial annotation and dark genes

The discoveries log **supports** Bakta's broader annotation utility but **refines** its coverage interpretation: across **132.5M gene clusters**, eggNOG had higher COG, KEGG, and Pfam coverage (**51%**, **38.5%**, and **63%**) than Bakta (**8.2%**, **17.3%**, and **7.7%**), whereas Bakta had higher GO coverage (**15%** versus **7.4%**), product descriptions (**71.2%** versus **70.4%**), and unique UniRef50 links covering **79.2%**. [src: discoveries] Their union increased any-functional-annotation coverage to **77.3%**, and Bakta rescued **11.2M** clusters among **39.2M** missed by eggNOG. [src: discoveries] Only **33.3%** of Bakta's **17.6M** distinct UniRef50 IDs existed in the BERDL UniProt identifier table, so downstream identifier integration remains incomplete. [src: discoveries]

Bakta v1.12.0 **supports and sharpens** dark-gene reclassification: among **39,532** Fitness Browser dark genes with pangenome links, **33,105 (83.7%)** were reclassified and **6,427** remained truly dark. [src: discoveries] The functional-dark-matter analysis used Bakta v1.12.0 with DB v6.0 and represented **58.1%** of all dark genes. [src: functional_dark_matter] All **100** top-ranked dark-gene candidates received Bakta product descriptions, and **5** dark genes also received AMRFinderPlus annotations. [src: functional_dark_matter] Among the **6,427** genes remaining hypothetical in both Fitness Browser and Bakta, UniRef50 links covered **79.4%**, UniParc/UniRef100 links **69.1%**, and RefSeq links **62.4%**. [src: functional_dark_matter]

The new truly-dark-gene analysis **refines** this result by separating the **33,105** annotation-lag genes from **6,427** genes hypothetical in both pipelines and **17,479** dark genes without pangenome links, whose Bakta status could not be assessed. [src: truly_dark_genes] Among truly dark genes, only **4.0%** had Pfam hits, **4.6%** had KEGG KOs, and **43.5%** had partial eggNOG-mapper signal; nevertheless, **79.4%** had UniRef50 links and **84.7%** had database cross-references. [src: truly_dark_genes] Only **246 (3.8%)** had zero annotation clues, while **2,314** had partial functional or phenotype clues suitable for prioritization. [src: truly_dark_genes] Thus the new analysis **supports** the existing conclusion that dark status often reflects incomplete or insufficiently integrated annotation rather than complete absence of evidence. [src: truly_dark_genes]

Truly dark genes were shorter (**121 versus 194 aa**), less conserved (**43% versus 73% core**), had fewer orthologs (**29% versus 64%**), and had higher GC deviation (**d=0.247**); **41%** of their neighbors were hypothetical and **12%** were within 2 genes of mobile elements. [src: discoveries] The new analysis **refines** the interpretation of these differences: the corresponding core-genome fractions were **43.1% versus 72.7%**, ortholog presence was **29.3% versus 63.7%**, and median ortholog breadth was **1 versus 4 organisms**; truly dark genes also had an essential fraction of **18.0% versus 13.4%**. [src: truly_dark_genes] These patterns are consistent with biological novelty, accessory-genome localization, or horizontal-transfer-associated evolution, but GC deviation and mobile-element proximity do not establish horizontal gene transfer, and short genes are intrinsically harder to annotate and measure by transposon insertion. [src: truly_dark_genes]

These results **refine** the AMR, gapfilling, metabolic-marker, and prophage-marker uses: Bakta annotations are valuable candidate-recovery evidence, but are strongest when combined with eggNOG, homology, pangenome, dedicated mobile-element prediction, and fitness evidence rather than used as standalone functional assignments. [src: discoveries] [src: functional_dark_matter] [src: prophage_amr_comobilization] The lanthanide atlas supports this caution because its Bakta-validated lanmodulin calls were clade-restricted and only partly co-occurred with xoxF, while sequence-level evidence for pseudogenes, truncated ORFs, or assembly fragmentation was out of scope. [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas] The truly-dark-gene report likewise notes that Bakta may produce false-negative functional calls when a known function is absent from the tested PSC database. [src: truly_dark_genes]

## Mechanism classification and conservation

The fitness-cost report classified resistance mechanisms using Bakta-derived AMR annotations, including efflux, enzymatic inactivation, metal resistance, and unknown categories. [src: amr_fitness_cost] The baseline-fitness comparison included **254 efflux**, **304 enzymatic-inactivation**, **144 metal-resistance**, and **74 unknown** genes. [src: amr_fitness_cost] Mechanism assignments were strongly associated with pangenome conservation status (**χ² = 69.3, p = 1.4×10⁻¹³**): **44%** of metal-resistance genes were accessory, compared with **13%** of efflux genes and **16%** of enzymatic-inactivation genes. [src: amr_fitness_cost]

The atlas **supports and broadens** this conservation analysis: AMR genes were **30.3%** core versus **46.8%** for the pangenome baseline, while the auxiliary genome was **2.2x** enriched for AMR (**33.6%** versus **15.3%**). [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] Its mechanism-level results showed **54.9%** core beta-lactamases and **6.5%** core regulatory genes, although these categories came from AMRFinderPlus product keywords rather than the earlier Bakta-derived scheme. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]

The prophage analysis **refines** the interpretation of accessory status: AMR genes within 10 genes of a prophage marker were **67.6%** accessory versus **65.5%** for distal AMR genes (odds ratio **1.10**, one-sided p=**0.005**, bootstrap 95% CI **[1.024, 1.185]**), a modest **2.1 percentage point** difference. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization] The threshold dependence and heterogeneity—odds ratios **0.78**, **0.92**, **1.10**, **1.19**, and **1.28** at 3, 5, 10, 15, and 50 genes, respectively; only **33 of 74** testable species above 1 and median species-level odds ratio **0.85**—**contradict** any simple claim that immediate prophage proximity uniformly marks recently acquired AMR cargo. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization] The proposed reversal at very close range and broader genomic-island interpretation are hypotheses, not direct mobilization tests. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]

The product classifier did not handle fosfomycin or tellurite resistance annotations, leaving approximately **25 genes** in the unknown mechanism category; reclassification would move these genes to enzymatic inactivation and metal resistance, respectively. [src: amr_fitness_cost] The atlas similarly found **22.2%** of hits Other/Unclassified and proposed systematic CARD ARO mapping. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]
