# Cytochrome bo3 oxidase

This page covers cytochrome bo3 oxidase in ADP1, also called cytochrome bo3. In ADP1 it is required on acetate and lactate and dispensable on quinate. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] See [[concepts/condition-dependent-respiratory-chain-configuration]] for the wider pattern.

## Key facts

- On quinate, only Complex I is required. Cytochrome bo3, cytochrome bd, SDH and all other respiratory components are dispensable. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]
- On acetate, Complex I, cytochrome bo3, ACIAD3522 and additional respiratory components are required. This makes acetate the most demanding carbon source. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]
- On lactate, cytochrome bo3 is required. Complex I has a mild effect, and cytochrome bd is dispensable. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]
- The top lactate-specific genes in the deletion-phenotype report are lldR and cyoC/cyoD, which are associated with lactate regulation and cytochrome oxidase. [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes]

## Interpretation

The respiratory-chain report attributes the dispensability of cytochrome bo3 and cytochrome bd on quinate to NADH (reduced nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) reoxidation being the bottleneck, not terminal oxidation. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] Its acetate model has direct entry into the TCA (tricarboxylic acid) cycle. This gives continuous NADH production that needs all available dehydrogenases, with cytochrome bo3 as the primary terminal oxidase. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] These mechanisms are the report's proposed explanations, not separate measurements.
