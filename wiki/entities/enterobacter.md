# Enterobacter

Enterobacter is a bacterial genus elevated in lignin-plus-labile-history Round 2 communities. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] It also appears in the antimicrobial resistance (AMR) atlas with 93 AMR clusters per species. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] The evidence is descriptive mean relative abundances and cluster counts. See [[summaries/lignin_community_enrichment__REPORT]] and [[summaries/amr_pangenome_atlas__REPORT]].

## Key facts

- In the AMR atlas, Klebsiella leads with 206 AMR clusters per species, followed by Salmonella (198), Citrobacter (134) and Enterobacter (93). [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]
- Round 2 groups with lignin-plus-labile history (LC-L and LC-LC) show Acinetobacter dominance (31–34%), lower Pseudomonas (24–26%) and elevated Enterobacter (2.7–18.1%). [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

## Abundance in lignin-enrichment groups

Enterobacter's mean relative abundance varied across the four groups, from 2.7% in LC-L to 18.1% in LC-LC. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] See also [[concepts/labile-carbon-effects-on-lignin-degrading-community-structure]].

| Group | Enterobacter mean relative abundance |
|---|---|
| LC | 7.5% [src: lignin_community_enrichment] |
| LC-L | 2.7% [src: lignin_community_enrichment] |
| L-LC | 8.7% [src: lignin_community_enrichment] |
| LC-LC | 18.1% [src: lignin_community_enrichment] |

For comparison, Pseudomonas in L-LC was 52.2%, and Acinetobacter in LC-LC was 34.3%. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]
