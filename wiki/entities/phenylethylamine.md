## Evidence from the ENIGMA Carbon Census

Phenylethylamine was one of the 8 compounds callable through an ENIGMA-isolate utilizer prediction. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

Phenylethylamine was classified in the Alkaloids chemical class, which contained 26 census compounds; 2 of those 26 compounds were ENIGMA-isolate-callable. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The census reported 28 ENIGMA-isolate utilizer prediction rows for Phenylethylamine. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

Phenylethylamine formed the strongest reported cross-block association with 4-hydroxybenzaldehyde, with a Haldane-corrected odds ratio of 2.84, a 95% confidence interval of 1.49–5.40, q=0.035, and n11=11. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

Phenylethylamine also formed a reported cross-block association with phthalic acid, with an odds ratio of 3.37, a 95% confidence interval of 1.46–7.75, q=0.071, and n11=6. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

Despite these associations, the reported Jaccard index for the Phenylethylamine pairings remained approximately 0.04, and only 25 of 3109 genomes, or 0.8%, carried both an aromatic and a non-aromatic capacity. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The census interpreted this pattern as supporting phylogenetic concentration rather than a portable, broadly co-assembled module set. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

Phenylethylamine is therefore relevant to [[concepts/pangenome-integration]], [[concepts/metabolic-model-gapfilling]], and [[concepts/multi-omics-integration]], because its evidence combines genome-scale capacity calls with chemical-class annotation and cross-compound co-occurrence analysis.
