## Evidence from cf_formulation_design

*Gemella sanguinis* ASMA-3044 was identified as a candidate dual-mechanism inhibitor because its observed inhibition was 62.2% higher than predicted by the metabolic model, a positive residual interpreted as evidence for inhibition beyond metabolic competition alone. [src: cf_formulation_design]

The metabolic model explained R² = 0.274 of inhibition variance before genus-level taxonomy was added, and genus-level information increased the fit to R² = 0.360; the report attributes the additional explained variance partly to species-specific direct-antagonism mechanisms. [src: cf_formulation_design]

*G. sanguinis* was included in the five-species strict-safety formulation core with [[entities/neisseria-mucosa]], [[entities/streptococcus-salivarius]], [[entities/micrococcus-luteus]], and [[entities/rothia-dentocariosa]]. [src: cf_formulation_design]

Its core-species profile reported 85% best inhibition, engraftability of 0.202, 7 pangenome genomes, 7/18 conserved amino-acid pathways, and 1 lung genome. [src: cf_formulation_design]

The report predicted that xylose and arabinose could support *G. sanguinis* as selective prebiotic substrates, but stated that these predictions require experimental validation. [src: cf_formulation_design]

GapMind pathway comparison found 7/18 amino-acid pathways and 37/39 carbon pathways conserved in the available *G. sanguinis* genomes. [src: cf_formulation_design]

## Interpretation and limitations

The positive inhibition-model residual supports the hypothesis that *G. sanguinis* may combine metabolic competition with direct antagonism against *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, rather than establishing that mechanism conclusively. [src: cf_formulation_design]

The formulation evidence remains provisional because the pairwise interaction analysis included only 8 comparisons across 5 unique pairs, and the complete 10-pair interaction matrix for the five-species core had not been measured. [src: cf_formulation_design]
