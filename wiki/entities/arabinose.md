# Arabinose

## Identity

Arabinose is a pentose proposed as a selective prebiotic substrate for supporting [neisseria-mucosa](neisseria-mucosa.md) and [gemella-sanguinis](gemella-sanguinis.md) in cystic-fibrosis airway microbiome formulations. [^cf_formulation_design]

- **Canonical name:** Arabinose. [^cf_formulation_design]
- **Known aliases:** None reported in the source document. [^cf_formulation_design]
- **Stable external identifier:** None reported in the source document. [^cf_formulation_design]
- **Entity type:** Compound. [^cf_formulation_design]

## Evidence from cf_formulation_design

The report identified arabinose as one of six pathways complete in at least one core commensal species but absent or nearly absent in PA14. [^cf_formulation_design]

PA14 pathway completeness for arabinose was 0%, and the corresponding selectivity value was 1.00. [^cf_formulation_design]

The proposed formulation strategy predicts that arabinose could support [neisseria-mucosa](neisseria-mucosa.md) and [gemella-sanguinis](gemella-sanguinis.md) while limiting direct nutritional support for PA14. [^cf_formulation_design]

This arabinose-based prebiotic prediction requires experimental validation before use. [^cf_formulation_design]

## Comparative evidence from pseudomonas_carbon_ecology

A comparative analysis of 7 [pseudomonas-aeruginosa](pseudomonas-aeruginosa.md)-group species and 189 [pseudomonas-e](pseudomonas-e.md) species found arabinose completeness of 0.0% versus 62.6%, respectively; this difference was among 43 of 62 pathways significant after Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction at q < 0.05. [^pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

This broader comparison **supports** the existing observation that arabinose availability can distinguish the PA14-associated host-pathogen context from candidate commensals, while **refining** it by showing that arabinose loss is also characteristic of the wider *Pseudomonas* s.s. group relative to *Pseudomonas_E*, rather than evidence specific to PA14 alone. [^pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

The comparative result is consistent with pathway loss in host-associated clades, but it does not directly test the proposed cystic-fibrosis formulation or establish selective growth of either commensal. [^pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
