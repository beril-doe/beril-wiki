# Arabinose

## Identity

Arabinose is a pentose proposed as a selective prebiotic substrate for supporting [[entities/neisseria-mucosa]] and [[entities/gemella-sanguinis]] in cystic-fibrosis airway microbiome formulations. [src: cf_formulation_design]

- **Canonical name:** Arabinose. [src: cf_formulation_design]
- **Known aliases:** None reported in the source document. [src: cf_formulation_design]
- **Stable external identifier:** None reported in the source document. [src: cf_formulation_design]
- **Entity type:** Compound. [src: cf_formulation_design]

## Evidence from cf_formulation_design

The report identified arabinose as one of six pathways complete in at least one core commensal species but absent or nearly absent in PA14. [src: cf_formulation_design]

PA14 pathway completeness for arabinose was 0%, and the corresponding selectivity value was 1.00. [src: cf_formulation_design]

The proposed formulation strategy predicts that arabinose could support [[entities/neisseria-mucosa]] and [[entities/gemella-sanguinis]] while limiting direct nutritional support for PA14. [src: cf_formulation_design]

This arabinose-based prebiotic prediction requires experimental validation before use. [src: cf_formulation_design]

## Comparative evidence from pseudomonas_carbon_ecology

A comparative analysis of 7 [[entities/pseudomonas-aeruginosa]]-group species and 189 [[entities/pseudomonas-e]] species found arabinose completeness of 0.0% versus 62.6%, respectively; this difference was among 43 of 62 pathways significant after Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction at q < 0.05. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

This broader comparison **supports** the existing observation that arabinose availability can distinguish the PA14-associated host-pathogen context from candidate commensals, while **refining** it by showing that arabinose loss is also characteristic of the wider *Pseudomonas* s.s. group relative to *Pseudomonas_E*, rather than evidence specific to PA14 alone. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

The comparative result is consistent with pathway loss in host-associated clades, but it does not directly test the proposed cystic-fibrosis formulation or establish selective growth of either commensal. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
