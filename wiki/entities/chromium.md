# Chromium

## What this entity is

**Canonical name:** Chromium. [src: metal_specificity]

**Known alias:** Cr. [src: metal_specificity]

**Stable external identifier:** Not specified in the source report. [src: metal_specificity]

## Key facts

Chromium was one of the metals evaluated in a comparison of metal-important genes with fitness defects across metal and non-metal experiments. [src: metal_specificity]

Of the chromium-associated metal-important gene records analyzed, 132 of 268 (49.3%) were classified as metal-specific, meaning that they showed significant fitness defects under metal stress but a sick rate below 5% across 5,945 non-metal experiments. [src: metal_specificity]

This chromium result contributes to the broader distinction between metal-specific determinants and general stress genes in [[concepts/metal-cross-resistance]]. [src: metal_specificity]

A separate soil metagenomic analysis found chromium among nine metals associated with 2,355 significant COG–metal associations at FDR < 0.05 across 51,748 samples; chromium and lead produced the strongest signals, with transporters and biosynthesis genes dominating the top hits. These observational results **support** chromium’s relevance to environmental functional-gene distributions but **refine** the fitness-based specificity claims: co-varying chromium, copper, lead, and zinc leave it unresolved whether individual associations are chromium-specific or reflect multi-metal contamination. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]

A separate genus-level PGLS analysis found that the association between metal-type diversity and inferred niche breadth remained positive after chromium was excluded (β = +0.0088, p = 0.173). This **refines** the cross-metal resistance interpretation: the overall signal was directionally distributed across metals, but exclusion of any one metal reduced significance and also compressed the maximum diversity score from 7 to 6, reducing predictor variance and power. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

Chromium was also among the recommended stresses for follow-up microcosm experiments testing candidate broad-niche, metal-diverse OTUs. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

The cross-condition classification of chromium-associated genes is part of the analysis summarized in [[summaries/metal_specificity__REPORT]] and relates to [[concepts/condition-specific-fitness]]. [src: metal_specificity]

The ecological chromium analysis is summarized in [[summaries/microbeatlas_metal_ecology__REPORT]] and connects to [[concepts/environmental-resistome]] and [[concepts/environment-embedding-geography]]. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

The soil functional-genomics analysis is summarized in [[summaries/soil_metal_functional_genomics__REPORT]]. Its planned partial-correlation analysis and effect-size audit are needed before the chromium-associated COG signals can be interpreted as metal-specific or biologically substantial. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]
