# Flavonifractor plautii

## What this entity is

**Canonical name:** Flavonifractor plautii. [^ibd_phage_targeting]

**Known alias:** F. plautii. [^ibd_phage_targeting]

**Stable external identifier:** No stable external identifier was reported in this document. [^ibd_phage_targeting]

## Key facts from ibd_phage_targeting

Flavonifractor plautii was one of six actionable Tier-A pathobiont targets, receiving a total prioritization score of 3.3. [^ibd_phage_targeting]

It was detected in 18 of 23 UC Davis Crohn's disease patients, corresponding to 78% of the cohort. [^ibd_phage_targeting]

In paired HMP2 metabolomics, F. plautii correlated negatively with cholate (ρ = −0.26) and positively with lithocholate (ρ = +0.15). [^ibd_phage_targeting]

The bile-acid association pattern supported a bile-acid coupling cost for targeting F. plautii, and the report assigned it the highest cost among the analyzed target species. [^ibd_phage_targeting]

F. plautii had 0 FDR-passing strain-adaptation genes among 3,245 tested genes in the Kumbhari strain-adaptation analysis. [^ibd_phage_targeting]

This result supported a species-abundance-mediated rather than strain-content-mediated association for F. plautii. [^ibd_phage_targeting]

The paired metabolomics analysis found an inverse lactate association for F. plautii (ρ = −0.23), while identifying only 7 strict cross-feeding triangles overall; the report therefore did not support the proposed cross-feeding interpretation. [^ibd_phage_targeting]

F. plautii was placed in a phage coverage gap: the available evidence did not establish a direct lytic-phage option for this organism. [^ibd_phage_targeting]

Because F. plautii combined the highest bile-acid coupling cost with a phage gap, it was deprioritized despite its Tier-A score. [^ibd_phage_targeting]

In the proposed state-dependent workflow, F. plautii would be dropped after an E1-to-E3 ecotype transition, although this dosing rule remains a hypothesis requiring prospective validation. [^ibd_phage_targeting]
