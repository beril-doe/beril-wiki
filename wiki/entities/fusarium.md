# Fusarium

## What this entity is

**Canonical name:** Fusarium. [^lignin_community_enrichment]

**Known aliases:** No aliases were reported in the source document. [^lignin_community_enrichment]

**Stable external identifier:** No stable external identifier was reported in the source document. [^lignin_community_enrichment]

Fusarium is a fungal genus profiled through ITS2 amplicon sequencing in the lignin-community enrichment experiment. [^lignin_community_enrichment] It is related here to [its-amplicon-sequencing](its-amplicon-sequencing.md), [lignin](lignin.md), and the cross-marker interpretation discussed in [multi-omics-integration](../concepts/multi-omics-integration.md). [^lignin_community_enrichment]

## Evidence from lignin community enrichment

Fusarium represented 26.5% of the base fungal community. [^lignin_community_enrichment]

After one round of lignin-only enrichment, Fusarium represented 43.4% of the fungal community, while the community contained 8 observed OTUs and had Shannon diversity of 1.37. [^lignin_community_enrichment]

The increase from 26.5% in the base community to 43.4% after lignin-only enrichment is consistent with Fusarium being retained and relatively enriched under this treatment, although the study did not directly measure Fusarium lignin-degradation genes or pathway activity. [^lignin_community_enrichment]

The source reported strong fungal restructuring under enrichment but cautioned that fungal replicate consistency was poor: within-group Bray–Curtis distances reached 0.99–1.00 for several Round-2 groups, compared with 0.09 for bacterial 16S profiles. [^lignin_community_enrichment]

Fusarium should therefore be interpreted as a taxonomic indicator associated with the observed lignin-enriched community rather than as direct evidence of a measured lignin-degradation function. [^lignin_community_enrichment]

## Related findings

Fusarium co-occurred with treatment-dependent dominance by other fungal taxa, including Fusicolla at 30.3% in lignin-only communities, Chrysosporium at 61.4% in lignin plus labile-carbon communities, and Aspergillus at 27.2% in lignin plus labile-carbon communities. [^lignin_community_enrichment] These results connect Fusarium to the broader [condition-specific-fitness](../concepts/condition-specific-fitness.md) pattern in which carbon regime restructures community composition. [^lignin_community_enrichment]

The fungal memory analysis was preliminary: the Round-2 fungal history effect was not statistically detectable, with R²=0.142 and p=0.090. [^lignin_community_enrichment] This limits inference about whether Fusarium specifically persists across sequential passages as part of [ecological-memory](../concepts/ecological-memory.md). [^lignin_community_enrichment]
