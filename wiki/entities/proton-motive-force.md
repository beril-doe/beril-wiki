# Proton motive force

Proton motive force (PMF) is the energy source that efflux pumps and flagella are said to share. [src: amr_cofitness_networks] It is proposed as the reason efflux genes show cofitness with flagellar genes. [src: amr_cofitness_networks] The support is interpretive, and shared dispensability could produce the same signal. [src: amr_cofitness_networks]

## Key facts

- The amr_cofitness_networks report cites Olivares Pacheco et al. (2017) as showing that efflux pump costs in *P. aeruginosa* are metabolic (PMF drain) and are compensated by metabolic rewiring. [src: amr_cofitness_networks]
- The discoveries digest suggests that the co-regulation of AMR (antimicrobial resistance) with motility and biosynthesis reflects competition for the proton motive force as a source of AMR costs. [src: discoveries]

## Proposed role in efflux–flagellar cofitness

The PMF competition model would explain why efflux genes correlate with flagellar genes, since both use PMF. [src: amr_cofitness_networks] This links [[entities/efflux-pumps]] to [[entities/flagellar-motility-genes]]. [src: amr_cofitness_networks]

Caveat: shared dispensability could produce the same signal, so the cofitness pattern does not by itself favour the PMF model. [src: amr_cofitness_networks] See [[concepts/shared-dispensability-as-a-confound-in-cofitness-enrichment]].
