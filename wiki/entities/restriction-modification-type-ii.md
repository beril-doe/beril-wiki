# Restriction-modification Type II

## What this entity is

**Canonical name:** Restriction-modification Type II. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

**Known aliases:** R-M Type II. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

**Stable external identifier:** None was provided in the source document. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

Restriction-modification Type II is an anti-phage defense-system family that uses sequence-specific cleavage of double-stranded DNA. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] It is part of the coordinated defense architecture discussed in [[concepts/cofitness-network-architecture]] and [[concepts/phage-defense-syndromes-and-arms-race]]. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

## Evidence from the phage-defense arsenal

Restriction-modification Type II was detected in 38.9% of species-level pangenomes in the quality-filtered analysis. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

In the analysis of 7,323 species-level pangenomes containing at least five genomes, Restriction-modification Type II showed positive co-occurrence with most of the other defense-system families. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

The strongest observed defense-system pairing was Restriction-modification Type II × Gabija, with 2,429 observed co-occurrences versus a null mean of 1,555, z = 46.1, and odds ratio = 24.0. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] This pairing was presented as a novel pan-bacterial defense syndrome at this scale. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

Restriction-modification Type II also showed significant positive co-occurrence with DISARM, with 3,424 observed co-occurrences, a null mean of 2,981, z = 24.4, and odds ratio = 4.1. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] It co-occurred with BREX in 3,929 species, compared with a null mean of 3,637, z = 23.0, and odds ratio = 6.4. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] Its pairing with R-M Type I comprised 3,948 observed co-occurrences versus a null mean of 3,665, z = 22.5, and odds ratio = 7.6. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

Restriction-modification Type II markers were enriched in the accessory pangenome: 9.4% of associated gene clusters were core and 68.2% were singleton clusters, compared with a background of 46.8% core and 37.9% singleton gene clusters. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] This pattern supports a connection between Restriction-modification Type II loci and mobile or accessory gene-content acquisition, while the source does not establish the transfer mechanism for individual loci. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] This finding relates to [[concepts/gene-function-acquisition-depth]]. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

## Interpretation and limitations

The Restriction-modification Type II × Gabija association is a statistical co-occurrence result rather than a direct demonstration that the two systems act synergistically during phage challenge. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] The source recommends mechanistic phage-challenge experiments comparing species with Restriction-modification Type II alone against species with Restriction-modification Type II plus Gabija. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

The source’s arms-race analysis controlled for phylum categorically but did not use a phylogenetically corrected regression, so its results are consistent with, but do not formally establish, a phylogenetically independent defense arms race. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]
