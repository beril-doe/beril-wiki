# Bagnoud et al. 2016 Opalinus porewater food web

Bagnoud et al. 2016 is the Mont Terri Opalinus porewater study that the clay_confined_subsurface report uses as the porewater paradigm. It reported a food web with three MAGs (metagenome assembled genomes) recurring across seven independent boreholes. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]

## Key facts

- Bagnoud reported a minimalistic Opalinus food web. In it, Desulfobulbaceae c16a expressed the complete Wood–Ljungdahl pathway, group 1 [NiFe]-hydrogenase, and Sat–AprAB–DsrAB. Three MAGs recurred across seven independent boreholes. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]
- Bagnoud (2016) reported protein-level expression of the toolkit modules. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]

## Relation to the clay_confined_subsurface report

The report's anchor_deep genera include the lineages Bagnoud identified as recurrent across 7 Mont Terri boreholes: Desulfosporosinus (×2), BRH-c8a (×2), BRH-c4a, Lutibacter, BRH-c54, Roseovarius, and Stenotrophomonas. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] See [[entities/desulfosporosinus]] and [[entities/dissimilatory-sulfate-reduction-pathway]]. The report interprets its H3 result as directly replicating the Bagnoud (2016) Mont Terri porewater paradigm at population-genome level. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] See [[summaries/clay_confined_subsurface__REPORT]].

## Porewater versus rock-attached paradigm

The Bagnoud paradigm describes porewater-cultured Mont Terri organisms as rich in sulfate reducers. The Mitzscherling (2023) paradigm describes rock-attached communities as rich in iron reducers. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] The central discoveries digest states the same contrast. Porewater is dominated by sulfate reducers, and rock surfaces are dominated by Geobacter/Geothrix iron reducers. It describes these as qualitatively different communities at the same clay site.
