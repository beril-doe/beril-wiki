# COG3569

## What this entity is

**Canonical name:** COG3569. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

**Entity type:** gene_or_pathway. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

**Known aliases:** No aliases were reported in the source document. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

**Stable external identifier:** No stable external identifier was reported in the source document. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

COG3569 is an eggNOG ortholog group identified as enriched among plant-associated species after controls for phylum and genome size. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

## Evidence from plant microbiome ecotypes

COG3569 had a raw Fisher odds ratio of 8.92 for plant association and a phylogenetically controlled odds ratio of 6.01. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

Its association remained highly significant after phylogenetic control, with q = 7.7e-242. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

COG3569 occurred in 54% of plant-associated species compared with 12% of non-plant species. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The finding is part of the report’s analysis of [ecotype-environment-gene-content](../concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content.md), which tests how plant-associated environments relate to gene content. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

COG3569 was one of 50 gene families that retained enrichment significance after phylum-level control. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

All 50 retained gene families, including COG3569, had eggNOG descriptions and InterProScan domains; 48 of 50 had Gene Ontology terms, and 39 of 50 had MetaCyc pathways. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The broader enriched-family set was dominated by unknown-function COG category S, which accounted for 19 of 50 families, or 38%; electron transport and energy metabolism accounted for 7 of 50, or 14%; inorganic ion transport for 6 of 50, or 12%; carbohydrate metabolism for 4 of 50, or 8%; and secondary metabolite biosynthesis for 3 of 50, or 6%. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The source highlights electron-transport and energy-metabolism functions, including cytochrome c oxidase subunits, NADH dehydrogenase, ubiquinol–cytochrome c reductase, and IscA iron-sulfur cluster biosynthesis, as candidate contributors to rhizosphere or microaerobic competitiveness; it does not assign COG3569 specifically to one of these functions. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

## Statistical context and limitations

The plant-versus-non-plant analysis tested 5,671 eggNOG ortholog groups after a 5% prevalence filter, and 5,341, or 94.2%, were significant in raw tests; the report interprets this near-saturation primarily as broad taxonomic compositional divergence rather than evidence that every significant family is plant-specific. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The controlled association for COG3569 strengthens its interpretation relative to an uncontrolled taxonomic comparison, but the report does not establish that COG3569 causes plant association or that it is expressed in plant-associated environments. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The report states that causal direction between plant association and enriched functions remains unresolved. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
