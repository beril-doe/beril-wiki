# Klebsiella michiganensis

## Identity

**Canonical name:** *Klebsiella michiganensis* [src: annotation_gap_discovery]

**Known alias:** Koxy [src: annotation_gap_discovery]

**Stable external identifier:** Not specified in this study [src: annotation_gap_discovery]

## Key facts across studies

*Klebsiella michiganensis* had the highest reported annotation-gap resolution rate in the study, with 5 of 7 gapfilled reaction-organism pairs resolved, corresponding to exactly 71.4%. [src: annotation_gap_discovery]

The study evaluated 14 organisms and found that resolution rates ranged from 20% in *Bacteroides thetaiotaomicron* to 71% in *K. michiganensis*. [src: annotation_gap_discovery]

The result places *K. michiganensis* above the other reported organisms: *Marinobacter* resolved 8 of 12 pairs (66.7%), *Azospirillum brasilense* resolved 13 of 21 (61.9%), *Herbaspirillum seropedicae* resolved 10 of 17 (58.8%), *Escherichia coli* Keio resolved 4 of 7 (57.1%), and *Bacteroides thetaiotaomicron* resolved 3 of 15 (20.0%). [src: annotation_gap_discovery]

The report associated higher resolution with better-annotated reference genomes and stronger Fitness Browser coverage, while noting that this association does not by itself establish causation. [src: annotation_gap_discovery]

A separate metabolic capability–dependency analysis identified *K. michiganensis* among the organisms with the highest reported proportions of latent capabilities—complete pathways with low measured fitness dependence—**refining** the annotation-gap result by showing that strong annotation resolution does not imply that every encoded pathway is an active dependency. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]
