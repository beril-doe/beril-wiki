# Sphingomonas

## What this entity is

**Canonical name:** Sphingomonas. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma]

**Known aliases:** No aliases were reported for this entity in the source document. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma]

**Stable external identifier:** No stable external identifier was reported for this genus in the source document. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma]

Sphingomonas is an environmental bacterial genus included in the Oak Ridge co-occurrence analysis and in the pH-associated Cluster A. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma]

## Environmental and plant-associated ecology

Sphingomonas belonged to Cluster A, which also comprised Brevundimonas, Caulobacter, Variovorax, and Sphingobium. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma] Cluster A was one of two anti-correlated genus clusters identified across 587 Oak Ridge 100-Well-Survey communities. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma] The analysis identified 47 significant genus-pair associations with absolute Spearman correlation greater than 0.2 and p < 0.01 across those 587 communities. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma]

Across 464,000 globally sampled 16S environmental profiles, Cluster A had an average pH of 6.78 and an average temperature of 15.7°C. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma] Cluster A differed from the opposing Cluster B by 1.35 pH units and 6.9°C, with Cluster B averaging pH 5.43 and 22.6°C. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma] These results support an association between Sphingomonas membership in Cluster A and the comparatively less acidic, cooler environmental niche identified in the analysis; the co-occurrence evidence is correlational rather than causal. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma]

The plant-microbiome analysis **refines** this environmental picture by identifying Sphingomonas as one of the genera dominating the phyllosphere, the above-ground plant compartment, rather than limiting its ecological association to the Oak Ridge pH–temperature pattern. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] This plant-compartment result is a genus-level distributional association and does not establish that all Sphingomonas lineages share the same host or functional phenotype. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

## Oak Ridge field ecology

The Oak Ridge field study **supports** the genus-level environmental association above: Sphingomonas was detected at 93% of 108 groundwater sites characterized by 16S amplicon sequencing. [src: lab_field_ecology]

It also **refines** the earlier ecological picture by showing a significant negative association with uranium concentration (Spearman rho=-0.382, p=4.5e-5, FDR q=2.5e-4), rather than a uniformly favorable response to metal contamination. [src: lab_field_ecology]

Sphingomonas was one of five genera whose uranium associations remained significant after Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction, while the aggregate laboratory metal-tolerance score across genera was only suggestively related to field abundance. [src: lab_field_ecology]

The field result is genus-level and correlational, so it does not establish that uranium directly inhibits Sphingomonas or identify which strains account for the association. [src: lab_field_ecology]

## Lignin-community enrichment

The lignin enrichment experiment **refines** the broader environmental association by showing that Sphingomonas was higher in the base community at 1.74% than in enriched conditions; this genus therefore did not increase under the tested lignin treatments. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

This result is conditional on the enrichment design and does not establish a mechanism for the observed decrease. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]
