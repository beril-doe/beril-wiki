# Lauric acid

## Identity

**Canonical name:** Lauric acid. [^enigma_carbon_census_1]

**Known aliases:** No aliases were specified in the source report. [^enigma_carbon_census_1]

**Stable external identifier:** No stable external identifier was reported in the source report. [^enigma_carbon_census_1]

Lauric acid was one of the 83 enrichment compounds included in the ENIGMA Carbon Census. [^enigma_carbon_census_1]

## Evidence in the ENIGMA Carbon Census

Lauric acid was the additional callable compound identified through a Tier-1 measured RB-TnSeq carbon-source fitness experiment, rather than through an ENIGMA-isolate utilizer call. [^enigma_carbon_census_1]

The census reported **0 ENIGMA-isolate utilizer rows** for lauric acid because its call was based only on measured fitness in a reference bacterium. [^enigma_carbon_census_1]

Lauric acid was sourced from necromass. [^enigma_carbon_census_1]

Lauric acid had no field data in the source-tracking analysis. [^enigma_carbon_census_1]

Because lauric acid had only a reference-bacterium measured-fitness call, it was not included among the 8 compounds with ENIGMA-isolate utilizer predictions used for the main specialization and source-tracking analyses. [^enigma_carbon_census_1]

The source report treated lauric acid as independently callable through measured fitness and therefore not subject to the genome-prevalence-<10% signature-reaction filter used for the ENIGMA-isolate calls. [^enigma_carbon_census_1]

## Significance and next steps

Lauric acid is an example of a compound whose utilization evidence comes from direct fitness measurement rather than pathway-linked ENIGMA-isolate predictions, making it relevant to [metabolic-model-gapfilling](../concepts/metabolic-model-gapfilling.md) and [multi-omics-integration](../concepts/multi-omics-integration.md). [^enigma_carbon_census_1]

Its necromass origin, lack of ENIGMA-isolate rows, and absence of field data limited its use for testing groundwater-versus-necromass source tracking. [^enigma_carbon_census_1]

The source report recommended prioritizing dark compounds for wet-lab enrichment; lauric acid instead illustrates the value of measured-fitness evidence for expanding compound callability beyond genome-annotation resources. [^enigma_carbon_census_1]
