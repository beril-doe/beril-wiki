# ipdC

## Identity

**Canonical name:** ipdC. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

**Known aliases:** No aliases were reported in this document. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

**Stable external identifier:** No stable external identifier was reported in this document. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

ipdC is a plant-growth-promoting gene marker analyzed in the [pgp_pangenome_ecology__REPORT](../summaries/pgp_pangenome_ecology__REPORT.md) pangenome study. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

## Evidence from pgp_pangenome_ecology

- ipdC was detected in 214 of 11,272 species, corresponding to 1.9% of the analyzed species. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]
- ipdC occurred in 2.5% of species with a complete tryptophan-biosynthesis pathway, defined by a GapMind score of at least 0.9, versus 0.9% of species with an incomplete pathway. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]
- The association between tryptophan-pathway completeness and ipdC presence had Fisher OR = 2.81 and p = 6.3e-10. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]
- A logistic model using tryptophan completeness alone gave OR = 2.81, 95% CI 1.97–4.01, p = 1.4e-08, with n = 11,272 species. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]
- Adding soil status to the logistic model gave OR = 2.87 and p = 7.0e-09 for the tryptophan-completeness association. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]
- Tyrosine-pathway completeness also predicted ipdC presence, with OR = 3.62 and p = 2.3e-11, so the result did not support a tryptophan-specific mechanism. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]
- The report interprets these associations as consistent with TyrR regulation of ipdC in Enterobacter cloacae, because TyrR responds to tryptophan, tyrosine, and phenylalanine. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]
- Within 1,039 soil/rhizosphere species, the association between tryptophan-pathway completeness and ipdC presence reversed, with OR = 0.30 and p = 0.02. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]
- Among 10,233 non-soil species, the same association remained positive, with OR = 3.56 and p = 7.7e-13. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]
- The soil-stratified reversal is hypothesis-generating rather than conclusive because ipdC was rare and occurred in only 214 of 11,272 species. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]
- The report suggests the hypothesis that soil plant-growth-promoting bacteria may obtain aromatic amino acids from plant exudates while retaining ipdC for indole-3-acetic-acid production from plant-supplied substrate. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]
- A phylum-plus-soil logistic model failed because quasi-complete separation resulted from the rarity of ipdC. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]
- ipdC was not significantly enriched in the raw soil comparison, with prevalence of 1.5% in soil/rhizosphere species versus 1.9% in other species, OR = 0.79, and q = 0.47. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]
- ipdC had a core fraction of 76.5%, an auxiliary fraction of 9.7%, and a singleton fraction of 13.7%, with BH-FDR q = 3.4e-19. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]
- The core/accessory distribution places ipdC among the PGP genes that were more core-associated than the genome-wide baseline of 46.8%. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

## Interpretation and open work

The ipdC findings connect [ecotype-environment-gene-content](../concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content.md) to aromatic-pathway completeness, while its core/accessory distribution contributes to [pangenome-integration](../concepts/pangenome-integration.md) and [gene-function-acquisition-depth](../concepts/gene-function-acquisition-depth.md). [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

The report recommends a controlled ipdC model that adjusts for genome size, COG coverage, and total pathway count to distinguish aromatic-pathway-specific effects from general metabolic capacity. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]
