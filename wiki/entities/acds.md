# acdS

## Identity

**Canonical name:** acdS, a plant-growth-promoting (PGP) gene marker. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

**Known aliases:** No aliases are reported in the source document. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

**Stable external identifier:** No stable external identifier is reported in the source document. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

## Key facts

In the BERDL pangenome analysis, acdS was significantly enriched among 1,039 soil/rhizosphere species compared with 10,233 species from other environments. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

acdS occurred in 15.8% of soil/rhizosphere species and 2.6% of other species, corresponding to an odds ratio of 7.02 and a Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate adjusted q-value of 5.1e-62. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

The acdS enrichment remained strong after phylum-level fixed effects in logistic regression, with an odds ratio of 6.98 and p = 4.8e-61. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

In a strict rhizosphere-only sensitivity analysis using genomes whose isolation source contained “rhizosphere” or “root nodule,” acdS enrichment had an odds ratio of 10.6 and q = 7.6e-38. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

acdS showed strong positive co-occurrence with [pqqc](pqqc.md), with OR = 7.24, n = 286 co-occurring species, and q = 1.2e-83. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

The report places acdS and [pqqc](pqqc.md) in a putative non-diazotrophic rhizosphere-effectiveness module, with additional positive co-occurrence between pqqC and [hcnc](hcnc.md) and [ipdc](ipdc.md). [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

Among the highlighted PGP genes, acdS was 70.4% core, 13.4% auxiliary, and 16.2% singleton, with q = 7.9e-25 for its higher core fraction relative to the genome-wide baseline. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

The genome-wide baseline was 46.8% core, while the mean accessory fraction across all PGP genes was 29.7% compared with 53.2% genome-wide. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

These results support the interpretation that acdS is predominantly core rather than an accessory gene in the analyzed pangenome, while the report notes that gene-cluster annotations were not functionally validated and did not filter truncations, frameshifts, or pseudogenization. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

The report cautions that environment classification was conservative and noisy, and that sampling bias toward clinical and host-associated genomes may cause the observed acdS and pqqC enrichment effects to underestimate true rhizosphere enrichment. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]
