# Streptococcus pneumoniae

## What this entity is

**Canonical name:** *Streptococcus pneumoniae* [src: amr_environmental_resistome]

**Known aliases:** *S. pneumoniae* [src: amr_environmental_resistome]

**Stable external identifier:** Not reported in the source document. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]

## Key facts from the environmental resistome analysis

The analysis included 7,944 *Streptococcus pneumoniae* genomes. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]

The species had 59 AMR gene clusters, of which 1 was core and 58 were accessory. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]

Accessory AMR represented 98% of the species’ AMR gene clusters. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]

*Streptococcus pneumoniae* was clinical-dominant, with 99.6% of its environmental classification attributed to clinical sources. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]

This case study contributes to the report’s finding that deeply sampled, clinically represented species can have highly accessory AMR repertoires, although the analysis is observational and the species-level classification does not constitute a per-genome within-species comparison. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]

The pan-bacterial atlas **supports** this clinical-versus-environmental interpretation at aggregate scale: human/Clinical species averaged 10.6 AMR clusters per species, compared with 4.6 for Soil/Terrestrial species, and clinical AMR was 30.8% core versus 58.1% for soil AMR. These atlas values are not species-specific measurements for *S. pneumoniae*. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]
