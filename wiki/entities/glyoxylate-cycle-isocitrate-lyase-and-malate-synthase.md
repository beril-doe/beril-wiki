# Glyoxylate cycle (isocitrate lyase and malate synthase)

This page covers the glyoxylate cycle (isocitrate lyase and malate synthase). Its marker enzymes are isocitrate lyase (aceA/icl, K01637) and malate synthase (aceB/glcB, K01638). [src: harvard_forest_warming] The evidence here is modest. It comes from one deletion-phenotype screen in ADP1 and from nominal-significance RNA results in one soil warming study.

## Key facts

- Aliyu et al. (2016), as cited in the Harvard Forest report, describe coordinated icl/aceA + aceB/glcB upregulation as a published signature of stress or C2-substrate utilization in Actinobacteria. [src: harvard_forest_warming]

## Acetate-specific phenotypes in ADP1

Top acetate-specific ADP1 genes include fadB, malate synthase G and citB. They are associated with fatty acid β-oxidation and the glyoxylate shunt. [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes] See [[summaries/adp1_deletion_phenotypes__REPORT]] and [[concepts/condition-specific-gene-phenotypes-and-functional-annotation-yield]].

## Response to warming at Harvard Forest

In Harvard Forest mineral soil RNA, both glyoxylate genes were higher in heated than in control plots. Isocitrate lyase (K01637) had a log2 fold change (FC) of +0.460 with nominal p=0.037. Malate synthase (K01638) had a log2 FC of +0.268 with nominal p=0.037. [src: harvard_forest_warming] The report ranks the glyoxylate cycle (icl + ms) and methanotrophy (pmoA/pmoB) as its strongest mechanistic RNA signals. It notes that these are not the canonical carbohydrate-active enzymes (CAZymes) that the published 5°C-respiration narrative would predict. [src: harvard_forest_warming]

The methanotrophy signals are directional and consistent across horizons. The glyoxylate signals rest on p=0.037 in heated mineral soil. [src: harvard_forest_warming]

The report calls the heated-mineral glyoxylate signature, together with Actinobacteria enrichment at Harvard Forest, mechanistically self-consistent. [src: harvard_forest_warming] That is an interpretation, not a direct test.
