# TerL

## What this entity is

**Canonical name:** TerL. [src: prophage_ecology]

**Known alias:** terminase large subunit. [src: prophage_ecology]

**Stable external identifier:** None reported in this document. [src: prophage_ecology]

TerL is a prophage-associated terminase marker used to cluster sequences into putative prophage lineages. [src: prophage_ecology]

## Findings in prophage ecology

The study used [[entities/mmseqs2]] clustering at 70% AAI, where AAI means amino-acid identity, to group 38,085 TerL sequences from 11,789 bacterial species into 10,991 lineages. [src: prophage_ecology]

The largest TerL lineage contained 1,094 members across 869 species, while 6,921 lineages, or 63%, were singletons. [src: prophage_ecology]

Threshold sensitivity produced 4,001 lineages at 50% AAI and 16,283 lineages at 80% AAI. [src: prophage_ecology]

No individual TerL lineage showed significant environment-specific enrichment after false-discovery-rate correction in 0/500 tests. [src: prophage_ecology]

Among 824 TerL lineages with at least 5 species, 325 were classified as specialists using Shannon entropy below 1.0 or a dominant environment above 80%, while 499 were classified as generalists distributed across 3 or more environments. [src: prophage_ecology]

Specialist TerL lineages were concentrated in animal-associated, freshwater, and marine environments. [src: prophage_ecology]

These results indicate that module-level prophage ecology and TerL-lineage ecology are decoupled: prophage modules showed environmental effects beyond host phylogeny, whereas individual TerL lineages did not exceed the constrained host-phylogeny expectation. [src: prophage_ecology]

The report therefore supports a model in which modular exchange contributes to environmental differences without requiring independent whole-lineage adaptation, although this is an ecological interpretation rather than a direct mechanistic demonstration. [src: prophage_ecology]
