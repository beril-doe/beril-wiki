# Black Queen Hypothesis

The Black Queen Hypothesis (BQH) was proposed by Morris et al. (2012). It holds that costly genes can be lost when community members provide their functions as public goods. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] Three BERIL projects report analyses framed by it, at the clade, pathway and community scales. A fourth proposes a future community test. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] The clade-level association is described as support [src: metabolic_capability_dependency], the community-scale soil signal as weak but consistent [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology], and the pathway-level conservation test gave a null result. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]

## Key facts

- Morris et al. (2012) proposed that biosynthetic gene loss is selectively favoured when the environment reliably supplies the metabolite. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]
- One report cites D'Souza et al. (2014) and Morris et al. (2012) as demonstrating gene loss in experimental and natural populations, respectively. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]
- D'Souza et al. (2014) is cited as showing a ~13% fitness benefit from biosynthetic gene loss when metabolites are available from neighbors. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]
- Schakel et al. (2025) is cited as showing that cross-feeding-based gene loss requires spatial structuring. It is not straightforwardly predicted by the fitness cost of retaining genes alone. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]

## Evidence at the species and clade level

Across Fitness Browser species clades, the latent capability fraction correlated positively with pangenome openness (Spearman ρ = 0.69, p = 0.0004, n = 22 clades). [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] The report treats this as clade-level support for the Black Queen framework. In its words, clades with more fitness-neutral complete pathways also have more dynamic pangenomes, consistent with ongoing streamlining. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] See [[entities/pangenome-openness]] and [[concepts/latent-metabolic-capability-versus-fitness-dependency]].

A separate pan-bacterial analysis covered 2,810 species with 10+ genomes. Species with more variable pathways had significantly more open pangenomes (partial Spearman rho=0.530, p=2.83e-203, controlling for genome count). The report calls this consistent with the BQH prediction that pathway gene loss is ongoing. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

The same report interprets strain differences in biosynthetic capability within a species as a precondition for Black Queen dynamics, in which metabolic functions become community-level public goods. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] It reads accessory-dependent amino acid synthesis as direct evidence for Black Queen dynamics. Under that reading, synthesis capacity is distributed across strains rather than universally maintained. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] See [[concepts/black-queen-dynamics-in-amino-acid-biosynthesis]].

It also reframes the hypothesis as a continuum of metabolic pathway dependency that varies with environmental context. It sets this against a binary "needed vs genomic baggage" framework. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] It proposes that pathway gene loss may be constrained by the breadth of environmental conditions a species encounters, not just mean fitness under one condition. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

## Null result at the pathway level

The pathway-level test (H2a) was not supported. Conservation, defined as the fraction of species genomes carrying a complete pathway, did not differ between latent capabilities and active dependencies. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] The report suggests that the Black Queen signal operates at the level of genome dynamics and community context. It does not appear in per-pathway cross-genome conservation ratios. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] It reads this null result as aligned with Schakel et al. (2025). [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]

## Community-scale evidence in soil

In NMDC soil microbiomes, 11 of 13 tested pathways trend in the BQH-predicted direction (binomial test p = 0.011). [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] The report calls this a weak but consistent community-scale signal. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] The result is unchanged when restricted to Soil-ecosystem samples (11/13 negative, p = 0.011; leu q = 0.022). [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] The leucine and arginine results are read as consistent with the prediction that costly functions are most likely lost when environmental supply is reliable. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] The report describes this as extending the hypothesis to community-weighted scores at landscape scale. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] See [[entities/leucine-biosynthesis]].

## Costly and dispensable genes

Costly+dispensable genes are proposed as candidates for this process. Caveat: they are predominantly selfish elements rather than metabolic genes. They may represent an earlier stage, before the gene has been lost despite its cost. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] The report proposes testing this with ENIGMA CORAL community composition data. The question is whether organisms with more costly+dispensable genes co-occur with organisms that could provide the lost functions. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]
