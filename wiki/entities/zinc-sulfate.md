# Zinc sulfate

Zinc sulfate (ZnSO4) is a chloride-free zinc salt. The counter_ion_effects report used it as a natural control for counter-ion effects, and it reports a definitive rejection of counter-ion confounding on that basis. [src: counter_ion_effects] The central discoveries digest repeats the zinc sulfate finding. [src: discoveries] Related pages: [[concepts/counter-ion-confounding-in-metal-fitness-assays]], zinc, [[entities/cobalt]], [[summaries/counter_ion_effects__REPORT]].

## Key facts

- In the per-metal NaCl-overlap analysis (NaCl is sodium chloride), zinc supplied as sulfate had mean overlap of 44.6% across 12 organisms, with mean maximum Cl⁻ of 0 mM. [src: counter_ion_effects]
- Zinc sulfate ranked 4th in NaCl overlap, above 10 of 14 metals. [src: counter_ion_effects]
- Zinc delivered as ZnSO₄ had the highest NaCl correlation (r=0.715 in DvH). [src: counter_ion_effects, discoveries]
- If chloride were the confound, zinc should show minimal overlap. [src: counter_ion_effects]

## Comparison with chloride-delivered metals

Chloride-free zinc overlapped with NaCl more than most metals delivered as chlorides. [src: counter_ion_effects, discoveries] Its 44.6% exceeded cobalt at 41.3%, copper at 41.0% and nickel at 39.3%. [src: counter_ion_effects] Cobalt chloride delivers up to 500 mM Cl⁻ yet has lower overlap than zinc sulfate. [src: counter_ion_effects]

## Hypothesis test

The report rejects H1b, which predicted that overlap scales with Cl⁻ concentration, because ZnSO₄ with 0 Cl⁻ exceeds most chloride metals. [src: counter_ion_effects]

## Proposed follow-up

The report proposes comparing ZnCl₂ 0.2 mM with ZnSO₄ 0.2 mM in MR-1. Zinc was already tested as sulfate, so adding the chloride form would directly test the null. [src: counter_ion_effects]
