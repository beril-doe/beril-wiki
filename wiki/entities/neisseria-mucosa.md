# Neisseria mucosa

This page covers Neisseria mucosa (N. mucosa), a lung-adapted commensal that serves as the formulation anchor species. The evidence comes mainly from one report, [[summaries/cf_formulation_design__REPORT]], with a supporting pangenome observation in [[summaries/discoveries]]. In that work the species is the anchor of the cystic fibrosis (CF) formulations. [src: cf_formulation_design]

## Key facts

- Among inhibition-tested species it has the highest engraftability (1.595), combining high prevalence with strong transcriptional activity. [src: cf_formulation_design]
- In the pangenome, 33% of its genomes come from respiratory sources, which makes it disproportionately lung-associated compared with other CF formulation species. [src: discoveries]
- Of 21 lung/respiratory genomes identified, it contributes 5 (33%). [src: cf_formulation_design]
- Weyand (2017) reviewed Neisseria colonization models in the upper respiratory tract and noted its prevalence in oral tissue. [src: cf_formulation_design]
- The report treats its engraftment as an assumption, with ecological context from respiratory colonization literature. [src: cf_formulation_design]

## Role in formulations

The report calls N. mucosa the indispensable anchor at every formulation size, with near-additive pairwise interactions. [src: cf_formulation_design] The recommended strict-safe k=1 formulation is N. mucosa ASMA-3643, with composite 0.753, 18% PA14 niche coverage, 88% inhibition and engraftability 1.595. [src: cf_formulation_design] It is the best single organism, but low niche coverage limits monotherapy. [src: cf_formulation_design] See [[concepts/multi-criterion-design-of-protective-microbiome-formulations]] and [[concepts/engraftability-and-lung-adaptation-of-candidate-commensals]].

### Pairwise interactions

Pairwise testing finds near-additive Pseudomonas aeruginosa PA14 inhibition for N. mucosa combinations. [src: cf_formulation_design] The two N. mucosa pairs were +5.3% and −2.2%, which supports its role as an anchor that does not interfere with partners. [src: cf_formulation_design] The report notes modest antagonism in some other pairs. [src: cf_formulation_design] See [[concepts/pairwise-interaction-among-commensals-in-competition-assays]].

### Inhibition residual

N. mucosa ASMA-3643 has a top positive PA14 inhibition residual of +57.2% relative to metabolic predictions. [src: cf_formulation_design] See [[concepts/metabolic-competition-versus-direct-antagonism-in-pathogen-inhibition]].

## Pangenome and pathway conservation

The pangenome has two clades. NB07 uses s__Neisseria_mucosa_A (15 genomes). The PROTECT reference genome GCA_003028315.1 maps to s__Neisseria_mucosa (8 genomes). [src: cf_formulation_design]

For the 15 genomes, 16/16 (100%) amino acid pathways and 27/27 (100%) carbon pathways were conserved at >95%. [src: cf_formulation_design] The core-species profile gives 16/18 for the amino acid pathways in the same clade. The report gives both numbers without explaining the different denominators. [src: cf_formulation_design]

A sensitivity check on the 8-genome clade showed stronger conservation. It found 18/18 amino acid pathways at >95%, versus 16/18 for the 15-genome clade. [src: cf_formulation_design] It also found 37/62 carbon pathways versus 27/62. [src: cf_formulation_design] That clade has 1 respiratory genome. [src: cf_formulation_design] The report concludes that the primary analysis understates conservation for this anchor species. [src: cf_formulation_design] See [[concepts/species-level-conservation-of-metabolic-pathways-across-pangenomes]].
