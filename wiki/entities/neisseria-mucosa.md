# Neisseria mucosa

## Identity

**Canonical name:** *Neisseria mucosa* [src: cf_formulation_design]

**Known alias:** *N. mucosa* [src: cf_formulation_design]

**Stable external identifier:** No stable external identifier is reported in the source document. [src: cf_formulation_design]

## Evidence from the formulation-design study

*Neisseria mucosa* was the leading candidate by inferred engraftability, with an engraftability score of **1.595**, defined as prevalence multiplied by the logarithm of the activity ratio. [src: cf_formulation_design]

It was also the strongest-performing core candidate by best inhibition, with **88%** inhibition. [src: cf_formulation_design]

The positive inhibition-model residual for *N. mucosa* isolate ASMA-3643 was **+57.2%**, identifying it as a candidate for combined metabolic-competition and direct-antagonism effects. [src: cf_formulation_design]

The strict-safe k=1 formulation containing only *N. mucosa* provided **18%** niche coverage, **88%** inhibition, and engraftability **1.595**. [src: cf_formulation_design]

The recommended primary clinical candidate was the k=2 formulation combining *N. mucosa* with [[entities/rothia-dentocariosa]], which provided **18%** niche coverage, **84%** mean inhibition, and combined engraftability **0.820**. [src: cf_formulation_design]

The study characterized *N. mucosa* using **15 pangenome genomes**, found **16/18** conserved amino-acid pathways, and identified **5 lung genomes**, representing **33%** of its analyzed genomes. [src: cf_formulation_design]

A sensitivity analysis using an **8-genome** *N. mucosa* clade found stronger conservation: **18/18** amino-acid pathways at greater than **95%**, **37/62** carbon pathways, and **1** respiratory genome. [src: cf_formulation_design]

The proposed prebiotic strategy predicts that xylose and arabinose could support *N. mucosa*, but this prediction requires experimental validation before use. [src: cf_formulation_design]

## Interpretation and limitations

The evidence supports *N. mucosa* as a high-priority formulation candidate because it combines strong planktonic inhibition with the highest inferred engraftability among the tested candidates. [src: cf_formulation_design]

Engraftability was inferred from patient prevalence and transcriptional activity rather than measured after administration, so the **1.595** score does not establish in vivo engraftment. [src: cf_formulation_design]

The inhibition evidence comes from planktonic assays using PA14, whereas cystic-fibrosis airway populations primarily occupy structured biofilms. [src: cf_formulation_design]

The report recommends validating the candidate against PAO1 and **3–5** mucoid clinical *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* isolates. [src: cf_formulation_design]
