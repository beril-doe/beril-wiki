# Bray-Curtis dissimilarity

## What it is

**Canonical name:** Bray-Curtis dissimilarity. [src: prophage_ecology]

Bray-Curtis dissimilarity is a compositional distance measure used in this study to compare prophage-module composition among bacterial species. [src: prophage_ecology]

**Known aliases:** No aliases were reported in the source document. [src: prophage_ecology]

**Stable external identifier:** None was reported in the source document. [src: prophage_ecology]

## Use in prophage ecology

Bray-Curtis dissimilarity was used for PERMANOVA, a permutation-based multivariate analysis of variance, on prophage-module composition across a 1,773-species subsample. [src: prophage_ecology]

The PERMANOVA identified significant effects of genome-size quartile, environment, and family-level phylogeny, each with p=0.01. [src: prophage_ecology]

Genome size had the strongest reported effect on Bray-Curtis prophage-module composition, with F=212.99, followed by environment with F=30.04 and phylogeny with F=6.17. [src: prophage_ecology]

These results were used to support the conclusion that environmental conditions explain prophage-module composition beyond host phylogeny, while also showing that genome size is the dominant predictor in the reported comparison. [src: prophage_ecology]
