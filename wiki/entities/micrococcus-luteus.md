## Evidence from the formulation study

*Micrococcus luteus* was one of the five species in the strict-safety core proposed for competitive exclusion of *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*. [src: cf_formulation_design]

It was included in the best three-species formulation, *Neisseria mucosa* + *Micrococcus luteus* + *Streptococcus salivarius*, which achieved 100% coverage of the *P. aeruginosa* niche and 75% inhibition. [src: cf_formulation_design]

The inclusion of *M. luteus* was critical for complete niche coverage because it grew on 9 of *P. aeruginosa* PA14's 11 preferred substrates. [src: cf_formulation_design]

Its best inhibition value was 38%, and its engraftability score was 0.000. [src: cf_formulation_design]

The pangenome analysis included 295 *M. luteus* genomes, with 18/18 amino-acid pathways and 18/18 carbon pathways conserved at the reported threshold. [src: cf_formulation_design]

GapMind pathway analysis found 18/18 amino-acid pathways and 39/39 carbon pathways conserved in *M. luteus* at greater than 95%. [src: cf_formulation_design]

No *M. luteus* lung genomes were available, and no patient engraftability was detected for this species. [src: cf_formulation_design]

Because its broad substrate coverage enabled complete PA14 niche coverage but its engraftment has not been observed, the report treats the three-species formulation containing *M. luteus* as an aspirational second-line candidate contingent on demonstrating engraftment in vivo. [src: cf_formulation_design]

## Evidence gap

The source provides broad in vitro substrate coverage and pangenome conservation evidence, but it does not provide measured in vivo engraftment for *M. luteus*. [src: cf_formulation_design]

Testing *M. luteus* administration in appropriate cystic-fibrosis airway models would determine whether its substrate-coverage benefit can be realized despite the absence of observed lung engraftability. [src: cf_formulation_design]
