# Toolik palsa peat site

Toolik palsa peat site (also NEON Toolik) is the source of the K14731-carrying MAGs (metagenome assembled genomes) in a biocatalysis proposal. [src: neon_mag_functional_discovery] The page draws on two reports: one on MAG functional discovery and one on eukaryotic DNA in soil metagenomes. See also [[entities/nmdc-neon-metagenome-studies]].

## Key facts

- Location and chemistry: a NEON Toolik biosample (`nmdc:bsm-11-cypgnq21`) is described at 68.62°N, -149.63°W, pH 5.24, O horizon. It is physically archived at the NEON Biorepository at Arizona State University. [src: neon_mag_functional_discovery]
- Habitat: the report describes the palsa source habitat as having ~−10 °C mean annual temperature and pH ≈ 5.2. [src: neon_mag_functional_discovery]
- Eukaryotic fraction: within the NEON soil study, Toolik has a eukaryotic fraction of 17%. The same study lists Utqiaġvik 30% and Caribou-Poker Creeks 22%, against 2–8% for temperate forests. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] See [[concepts/within-study-environmental-drivers-of-eukaryotic-dna-in-soil-metagenomes]] and [[summaries/euk_in_prok_correlates__REPORT]].

## K14731-carrying MAGs

The neon_mag_functional_discovery report anchors its biotech proposal on 15 LATE_ONLY K14731-carrying MAGs from this site. [src: neon_mag_functional_discovery] It describes the sub-Arctic ε-lactone hydrolases as cold-active by virtue of their source habitat. It proposes them as candidates for broad lactone-biocatalysis applications. [src: neon_mag_functional_discovery] See [[concepts/biocatalyst-potential-of-uncultured-soil-bacterial-enzymes]] and [[summaries/neon_mag_functional_discovery__REPORT]].
