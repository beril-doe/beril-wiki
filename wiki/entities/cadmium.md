# Cadmium

Cadmium (Cd) is a toxic metal tested in the Metal Fitness Atlas. It shows no significant core-genome enrichment of its important genes, and the data come from only one organism, so the evidence is weak. [src: metal_fitness_atlas]

## Key facts

- In the atlas conservation analysis, cadmium (toxic) has 92 important genes, core fraction 0.522, delta -0.010, odds ratio (OR) 0.96 and p-value 9.2e-01. [src: metal_fitness_atlas]
- The atlas treats this as a null result, likely due to limited organism coverage (1 organism). [src: metal_fitness_atlas]
- See [[concepts/core-genome-enrichment-of-metal-stress-fitness-genes]], [[entities/metal-fitness-atlas]] and [[concepts/organism-coverage-and-attrition-in-cross-organism-fitness-conservation-analyses]].

## Counter-ion correction

In the per-metal NaCl-overlap analysis, cadmium (chloride) had mean overlap 50.5% across 1 organism, and mean maximum chloride was not reported. [src: counter_ion_effects] After correction, cadmium was the only metal whose core-genome enrichment reversed, with delta going from -0.008 to -0.108. [src: counter_ion_effects] The core fraction for cadmium-important genes changed from 0.522 to 0.422 against a baseline of 0.530 (n=92, 1 organism). [src: counter_ion_effects] This result has very low statistical power because it rests on 92 original genes from 1 organism. [src: counter_ion_effects] The report calls the corrected deltas for cadmium (n=92) and iron (n=9), each from a single organism, statistically unreliable. [src: counter_ion_effects] See [[concepts/counter-ion-confounding-in-metal-fitness-assays]] and [[entities/iron]].

## Metal specificity

For cadmium, categorized as toxic, 52/93 metal-important gene records were metal-specific (55.9%). [src: metal_specificity] See [[concepts/metal-specific-versus-pleiotropic-stress-gene-classification]].
