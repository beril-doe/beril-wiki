# Cadmium

## Identity

Cadmium is a metal evaluated in experiments assessing metal-specific versus general-stress fitness determinants. [^metal_specificity]

- **Canonical name:** Cadmium. [^metal_specificity]
- **Known aliases:** None reported in the source. [^metal_specificity]
- **Stable external identifier:** Not reported in the source. [^metal_specificity]

## Evidence from metal_specificity

Among 93 cadmium-important gene records, 52 were classified as metal-specific, corresponding to 55.9%. [^metal_specificity]

Metal-specific genes showed significant fitness defects under metal stress but a sick rate below 5% across the non-metal experiments used for classification. [^metal_specificity]

The cadmium result contributes to the report's distinction between metal-specific fitness determinants and broadly pleiotropic stress genes. [^metal_specificity]

## Evidence from microbeatlas_metal_ecology

A cross-genus phylogenetic generalized least-squares (PGLS), a regression method accounting for phylogenetic relationships, analysis found that the association between metal-type diversity and inferred niche breadth remained positive when cadmium was excluded, but was not significant (β = +0.0084, p = 0.186). This **refines** the cadmium-specific result: cadmium contributes to a distributed cross-metal signal, but the analysis does not establish that cadmium alone drives ecological breadth. [^microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

Cadmium was also among the recommended stresses for proposed microcosm validation experiments targeting organisms with both broad inferred niches and diverse metal-resistance repertoires. [^microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

## Evidence from soil_metal_functional_genomics

A soil metagenomic analysis included cadmium among nine metals measured across 51,748 samples and identified 2,355 significant COG–metal associations at FDR < 0.05; COG means Cluster of Orthologous Groups functional category and FDR means false discovery rate. [^soil_metal_functional_genomics] This **supports** cadmium’s relevance to environmental functional-gene distributions but does not provide a cadmium-specific association count or establish that cadmium caused the observed shifts. [^soil_metal_functional_genomics]

The environmental result **refines** the fitness-based evidence: chromium and lead produced the strongest signals overall, while co-varying metals—including cadmium in the nine-metal design—leave it unresolved whether individual associations are cadmium-specific or reflect multi-metal contamination. [^soil_metal_functional_genomics]
