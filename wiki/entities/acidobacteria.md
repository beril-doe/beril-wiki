# Acidobacteria

Acidobacteria (also called Acidobacteriota) is the phylum this page covers. It was depleted in heated Harvard Forest organic soil. [src: harvard_forest_warming] Both results come from single reports.

## Key facts

- Harvard Forest organic soil: relative abundance was 0.035 in control and 0.024 in heated samples, log2 fold change (FC) −0.549, q (Benjamini-Hochberg, BH) 0.049. [src: harvard_forest_warming]
- xoxF per-genome rate in Acidobacteriota is 28 % in the KBase Data Lakehouse pangenome. [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]

## Response to warming

The report states that its organic-horizon signal (Actinobacteria up, Acidobacteria down, q=0.049) directly reproduces the warming signature that the same Harvard Forest team reported from 16S amplicon and metagenome data. [src: harvard_forest_warming] It cites DeAngelis et al. (2015, Front. Microbiol.), who found Actinobacteria, Alphaproteobacteria and Acidobacteria to be the phyla with the strongest warming responses across 5/8/20-year warmed plots. Those shifts were most pronounced in the organic horizon. [src: harvard_forest_warming] See [[concepts/warming-response-of-soil-microbial-community-composition]], [[entities/actinomycetota]] and [[summaries/harvard_forest_warming__REPORT]].

## xoxF carriage

The lanthanide methylotrophy atlas reports the highest per-genome xoxF rates in Acidobacteriota (28 %) and Gemmatimonadota (25 %). The report notes that the methylotrophy literature rarely cites these phyla. [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas] It lists Acidobacteriota as a high-rate xoxF carrier outside the methylotroph canon. [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]

In the GTDB-r214 atlas, Acidobacteriota has 285 xoxF calls and 3 mxaF calls across 1,006 genomes. That gives an xoxF rate of 28.3 % and an xoxF:mxaF ratio of 95 : 1, with BH false discovery rate (BH-FDR) p_BH 1.2 × 10⁻⁷⁹. [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]

Within Acidobacteriota, soil-sediment xoxF enrichment persists (within-phylum odds ratio (OR)=2.16, p_BH=2.2 × 10⁻⁵). The report suggests that at least part of the broader soil signal is therefore not pure phylogenetic confounding. [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]
