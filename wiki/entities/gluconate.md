# Gluconate

**Type:** compound (carbon source)

Gluconate appears in this corpus as a carbon source. One project tracks its utilization pathway in *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* genomes from the lung, and another records gaps in gluconate utilization across fitness-profiled organisms [src: cf_formulation_design, functional_dark_matter].

## Reduced predicted utilization in lung *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* genomes

The cystic fibrosis formulation-design comparison reports "PA lung metabolic streamlining", in which lung [[entities/pseudomonas-aeruginosa]] genomes show reduced predicted sugar-utilization pathway scores for [[entities/sorbitol]], mannitol and gluconate, while amino acid catabolic pathways remain comparatively conserved — the same project reports amino acid pathways as "97.4% conserved across 1,796 lung PA genomes" and "CF vs non-CF PA show zero amino acid pathway differences" [src: cf_formulation_design]. These are pathway-completeness predictions from genome comparison rather than measured loss of growth on gluconate, so neither complete pathway loss nor absolute invariance of amino acid catabolism is established by them [src: cf_formulation_design]. The report presents the pattern as confirming that competitive-exclusion targets are evolutionarily stable; because that durability is inferred from pathway scores rather than measured, it is best stated as the hypothesis that amino-acid-based exclusion targets stay more durable than sugar-based ones as lung strains evolve [src: cf_formulation_design]. See [[concepts/competitive-exclusion-consortium-design]].

## Gluconate-utilization gaps in functional dark matter

The functional dark-matter analysis lists gluconate utilization as a carbon-category gap present in 26 organisms, with [[entities/marinobacter]] and *D. desulfuricans* given as examples [src: functional_dark_matter]. See [[concepts/experimental-prioritization-of-functional-dark-matter]].
