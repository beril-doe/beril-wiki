# GapMind

GapMind (also GapMind carbon pathways, GapMind pathway completeness, GapMind pathways, gapmind_pathways) predicts pathway completeness for about 80 carbon and amino acid pathways. [src: cf_formulation_design, annotation_gap_discovery] Its predictions are computational and cover only part of metabolism, so the evidence is useful but bounded. [src: essential_metabolome, functional_dark_matter] In the cited reports it agrees well with growth data for one strain and for ENIGMA pairs, with limits in coverage and in how the data are queried. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency, genotype_to_phenotype_enigma] Reports also flag that its pathway resolution may be too coarse for strain-level or complementarity analyses, and that linking it to metabolite data needs a lookup table. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes, pseudomonas_carbon_ecology, webofmicrobes_explorer]

## Key facts

- Coverage: it covers amino acid biosynthesis and carbon utilization, not all metabolic functions. [src: functional_dark_matter] Many ModelSEED gapfilled reactions fall outside its coverage. [src: annotation_gap_discovery]
- Pathway set: GapMind covers 80 pathways, 18 amino acid biosynthesis and 62 carbon utilization. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] It has exactly 80 pathways and multiple rows per genome-pathway pair, one per step or component. [src: pitfalls]
- Data: the pathway table `gapmind_pathways` in the `kbase_ke_pangenome` collection gives completeness predictions, reported as 305M predictions over 293K genomes. [src: essential_metabolome, metabolic_capability_dependency] The table has 305M rows. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] See [[entities/kbase-ke-pangenome]] and [[entities/kbase-data-lakehouse]]. The webofmicrobes_explorer report used the `pangenome` and `gapmind_pathways` tables for species clade counts and pathway predictions. [src: webofmicrobes_explorer]
- Granularity: the available data give pathway step counts, not individual step identities. [src: annotation_gap_discovery]
- Categories: one analysis used only "complete" and "likely_complete" and may miss partial pathways. [src: essential_metabolome] The score categories are complete, likely_complete, steps_missing_low, steps_missing_medium and not_present, not a binary present flag. [src: pitfalls]
- Derived files: one project generated data/gapmind_genome_pathways.csv with 23,424,480 rows of per-genome completeness [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] and data/gapmind_species_summary.csv with 27,690 rows of per-species statistics. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] The pseudomonas_carbon_ecology report provides data/carbon_pathway_scores.csv with 789,012 rows of genome-level best GapMind scores for 62 Pseudomonas carbon pathways. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

## Comparison with gapfilling

Of 104 GapMind-gapfill pathway pairings, GapMind partially corroborated the gapfilling. [src: annotation_gap_discovery] It called pathways incomplete (`not_present` or `steps_missing`) for many carbon sources where ModelSEED needed gapfilling. [src: annotation_gap_discovery] Exact concordance was limited by the step-count granularity. [src: annotation_gap_discovery] See [[summaries/annotation_gap_discovery__REPORT]].

## Pseudomonas carbon catabolism and prebiotics

The pseudomonas_carbon_ecology report extracted GapMind carbon pathway predictions from the KBase Data Lakehouse kbase_ke_pangenome collection for 12,732 genomes across 433 Pseudomonas species clades defined by GTDB r214. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] Species-level completeness was the fraction of genomes scoring complete or likely_complete for each of 62 carbon pathways. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] Mean completeness across all species was 0.882. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] Pathway richness, defined as pathways complete in >50% of genomes, ranged from 27 to 61 with mean = 54.6. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] The report used standardized GapMind predictions rather than KEGG-based annotations to assess ecological differentiation. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

GapMind carbon predictions for these species showed near-complete loss of plant-derived sugar catabolism in the P. aeruginosa group relative to the P. fluorescens/putida group. [src: discoveries] Of 62 carbon pathways, 43 differ significantly (FDR, false discovery rate, < 0.05). The largest effects were xylose (+74 pp), ribose (+64 pp), arabinose (+63 pp), galacturonate (+60 pp) and myo-inositol (+59 pp). [src: discoveries] Amino acid catabolism stayed near-universal (>99.5%) in both groups. [src: discoveries] The report cautions that the 62 pathways (Price et al. 2022) may be too coarse to capture niche-specific differences at the strain level within environmental species. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] See [[concepts/host-adaptation-pathway-loss-in-pseudomonas-carbon-catabolism]].

In the CF (cystic fibrosis) formulation work, GapMind pathway conservation across 499 pangenome genomes of 5 core commensal species tested whether isolate carbon-utilization profiles generalize between strains. [src: cf_formulation_design] The report compared pathway completeness between the 5 core commensals and P. aeruginosa across the pangenome to seek selective prebiotics. [src: cf_formulation_design] Six pathways were complete in at least one commensal but absent in PA14. Each was carried by only 1–2 of the 5 core species. [src: cf_formulation_design] One summary lists them as xylitol, myoinositol, xylose, arabinose, fucose and rhamnose, with 100% completeness in formulation commensals and 0% in PA14. [src: discoveries] Seven pathways differed significantly (FDR < 0.05) between lung and non-lung P. aeruginosa. All were carbon pathways lost in lung isolates, including sorbitol (−0.165), mannitol (−0.204) and gluconate (−0.185). [src: cf_formulation_design] See [[concepts/selective-prebiotics-for-commensal-consortia]].

## Capability versus fitness

The pathway_capability_dependency report compares GapMind completeness predictions with RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing) fitness data from the Fitness Browser, classifying 161 organism-pathway combinations across 7 model bacteria into four categories. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] Across 7 Fitness Browser organisms × 23 GapMind pathways, 35.4% were Active Dependencies (complete and fitness-important). 41.0% were Latent Capabilities (complete but fitness-neutral in rich media). 14.9% were Incomplete but Important, and 8.7% were Missing. [src: discoveries] All Latent Capabilities became fitness-important under condition-specific analysis (nitrogen limitation, stress, carbon source shifts). [src: discoveries] Only 7 of 48 Fitness Browser organisms have GapMind data, which limits this tier to a small set of model organisms whose near-complete core genomes compress the conservation validation signal. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] See [[concepts/metabolic-capability-versus-fitness-dependency]].

The metabolic_capability_dependency report proposes using GapMind per-step gene assignments instead of SEED proxies to improve pathway-to-fitness mapping precision and reduce false positives. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] Its SEED-based mapping left some pathways out: deoxyribonate and myoinositol had no matching SEED subsystem role descriptions and were absent from all analyses. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] Full-name phenylalanine and tyrosine entries also lacked SEED coverage, but their phe and tyr equivalents were included through abbreviation-based matching. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] Alanine appeared in fitness metrics but was excluded from classification for all organisms because fewer than 3 SEED-annotated genes met the minimum coverage threshold. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]

For P. fluorescens FW300-N2E3, GapMind predicted a complete tryptophan biosynthesis pathway. [src: discoveries] All 13 Web of Microbes-produced metabolites mapped to GapMind pathways (lactate, valine, alanine, arginine, aspartate, glutamic acid, glycine, phenylalanine, proline, trehalose, tryptophan, malate, lysine) had complete predictions. [src: discoveries, fw300_metabolic_consistency] All 13 also showed growth in Fitness Browser experiments. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] These 13 were 13/58 (22%) of the Web of Microbes metabolites. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] The report reads the concordance as validating GapMind for this strain. [src: discoveries, fw300_metabolic_consistency] That is one strain. It cites Price et al. (2022) as reporting high accuracy against fitness data [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] and states that GapMind 2024 improved amino acid biosynthesis annotations and that pangenome-level predictions in the KBase Data Lakehouse inherit this accuracy. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] See [[entities/pseudomonas-fluorescens-fw300-n2e3]].

### Linking to Web of Microbes

Linking Web of Microbes (WoM) metabolites to GapMind pathways by simple name matching failed. [src: webofmicrobes_explorer] The link is blocked because GapMind internal pathway names do not contain simple metabolite names. [src: webofmicrobes_explorer] The report states that a dedicated pathway-to-metabolite mapping table would enable the link. [src: webofmicrobes_explorer] It proposes building one by mapping each GapMind pathway to its substrate and product metabolites using GapMind documentation. [src: webofmicrobes_explorer]

## Core versus all-gene completeness

Comparing sequence_scope = 'core' (core pangenome genes only) with 'all' (all genes) shows which pathways depend on the accessory genome. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] The Tier 2 analysis covers 2,810 GTDB species with at least 10 genomes and all 80 pathways. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] The pathways with greatest accessory dependence are all amino acid biosynthesis routes. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] These pathways consistently show the largest gaps, so their completeness depends on genes not universally present within a species. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] Carbon utilization pathways tend to show smaller gaps, perhaps because carbon catabolism genes are universally present or universally absent. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] The report's completeness criterion is a GapMind prediction of completeness in >50% of conspecific genomes. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] The plant_microbiome_ecotypes report leaves the appropriate core-level completeness threshold as an open question. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

## Prediction of growth phenotypes

In preliminary ENIGMA binary-growth prediction, the GapMind pathway-completeness baseline for amino acids and carbon had AUC (area under curve) 0.646, accuracy 78.8% and coverage 24.3%. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma] On 118 testable pairs it reached 96.5% recall and 79% precision, so it almost never misses a grower but sometimes predicts growth when the pathway is present but unused. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma] About 76% of ENIGMA conditions (metals, antibiotics, nitrogen, stress) have neither GapMind pathway coverage nor CSP (carbon source phenotypes) training data. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma] The report considers its complementarity hypothesis supported: GapMind (78.8% accuracy, 24% coverage, mechanistic) and a GBDT (gradient boosted decision tree) model (AUC 0.78 on amino acids, broader coverage, data-driven) complement each other. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma] See [[concepts/predictability-of-binary-growth-phenotypes-from-gene-content]].

## Dark genes

Across 44 Fitness Browser-linked species, GapMind found 1,256 organism-pathway pairs with nearly-complete pathways (`steps_missing_low`) where dark genes with strong fitness effects co-occur. [src: functional_dark_matter] Carbon source utilization pathways are the most frequently gapped. [src: functional_dark_matter] The analysis is organism-level co-occurrence, not direct gene-to-step assignment. [src: functional_dark_matter] It also misses dark genes with signaling, regulatory or structural roles. [src: functional_dark_matter]

## Conservation across organisms

In a 7-organism sample, 17 of 18 amino acid biosynthesis pathways were present in all 7. [src: essential_metabolome] Serine biosynthesis was the exception, in 6 of 7 (85.7%). [src: essential_metabolome] Fumarate and succinate were present in all 7 organisms (87.5%, as reported). [src: essential_metabolome] Acetate, propionate and L-lactate were also among the most widely conserved carbon sources. [src: essential_metabolome] The same list included deoxyribose and deoxyribonate [src: essential_metabolome] and putrescine. [src: essential_metabolome] The report says all amino acids can be catabolized. [src: essential_metabolome] Ethanol and deoxyinosine occurred in 6 of 7 organisms (75%). [src: essential_metabolome] The report reads this as shared central catabolic capacity. [src: essential_metabolome]

## Ecology and niche breadth

GapMind carbon breadth was positively associated with niche breadth (β = +0.142, SE (standard error) = 0.033, p = 1.9 × 10⁻⁵). [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] The report calls GapMind completeness a qualitative repertoire metric that grows with generalism. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] It states that specialists have high carbohydrate KO (KEGG ortholog) density per Mb but low GapMind breadth, and generalists the converse. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] It explains this by genome streamlining, which raises per-Mb density while shrinking the absolute gene complement. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] This reconciliation is exploratory, since the metrics were computed independently across studies. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] See [[concepts/genome-streamlining-and-genome-size-in-ecological-specialists]].

A community analysis correlated community-weighted GapMind amino acid biosynthesis completeness with ambient amino acid metabolomics across 131 NMDC soil samples (Spearman). [src: discoveries] The NMDC report defined community completeness as frac_complete, the fraction of taxa with GapMind score ≥ 5 and no missing steps. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] It chose score 5 over frac_likely_complete (score ≥ 4) because the unambiguous complete call matches the Black Queen Hypothesis prediction that taxa without a complete pathway depend on environmental supply. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] frac_likely_complete was computed for sensitivity comparisons. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] The approach uses pathway-level completeness rather than flux-based or PICRUSt-style models, yet reaches comparable conclusions. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] The report cites Ramoneda et al. (2023) as using GapMind to show non-random amino acid auxotrophy across bacterial phyla and environments. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] It integrates 305M GapMind records (27,690 GTDB species × 80 pathways) with NMDC multi-omics for 220 samples spanning two habitat types. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] Its inputs came from `gapmind_pathways`, `gtdb_species_clade` and `pangenome`. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

In plant-microbiome work, BacDive metabolite utilization cross-validated GapMind predictions at 83.1% consistency, which the report reads as confirming reliable completeness scores. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] All 69 genera in 348 soil/rhizosphere samples had GapMind data across 80 pathways. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The complementarity analysis there (NB06) used max-aggregated species-to-genus GapMind scores, which inflated Cohen's d to -7.54. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The report also warns that the 80-pathway resolution may be too coarse for metabolic complementarity analysis. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The pgp_pangenome_ecology report took tryptophan and tyrosine completeness scores from the `gapmind_pathways` table. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology] In it, species with complete tryptophan biosynthesis (GapMind score ≥ 0.9) carried ipdC at 2.5% versus 0.9% in incomplete species (Fisher OR (odds ratio) = 2.81, p = 6.3e-10). [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology] That analysis had 27,690 species with tryptophan completeness scores [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology] and a file of trp and tyr completeness per species with 27,690 rows. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology] The report cautions that trp and tyr scores are both proxies for overall pathway completeness, so genome size or total pathway count would need control to isolate specific biochemical coupling. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology] See [[concepts/aromatic-amino-acid-biosynthesis-and-auxin-pathway-gene-coupling]].

## Deep clay self-sufficiency

Deep-clay genomes show no GapMind amino-acid advantage. Unfiltered, anchor_deep averaged 16.2/18 versus 16.7/18 for soil baseline (Mann–Whitney p=0.15, Cohen's d=−0.17). [src: clay_confined_subsurface] The table gives n=9 versus 150, means 16.22 versus 16.66, p=0.153. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] After CheckM filtering (≥80% complete, ≤5% contamination), deep clay was lower: 15.5/18 versus 17.1/18 (p=0.009, d=−0.84). [src: clay_confined_subsurface] The table gives n=6 versus 137, 15.50 versus 17.14. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] Within Bacillota_B the difference was not significant: 16.5 versus 16.8 (p=0.07, d=−0.13). [src: clay_confined_subsurface] The table gives n=4 versus 19, 16.50 versus 16.79, p=0.073. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]

Shallow clay was higher. Unfiltered, n=30 versus 150, means 17.87 versus 16.66, d=+0.52, p=0.006. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] After CheckM filtering, n=30 versus 137, 17.87 versus 17.14, d=+0.43, p=0.029. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] The report attributes this almost certainly to cultivation-quality selection among agricultural isolates. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]

The 18-pathway universe saturates near 18 for most cultivable bacteria. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] Most cultured organisms reach 17–18/18 regardless of habitat, which limits detection of a positive signal. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] See [[concepts/biosynthetic-self-sufficiency-as-a-deep-subsurface-adaptation]].

## Data handling pitfalls

Several query mistakes produced empty or wrong results. See [[concepts/join-key-validity-and-identifier-format-mismatches]].

- With `sequence_scope = 'core'`, `score_simplified` holds only 0.0 (incomplete) or 1.0 (complete), not a continuous confidence score. [src: pitfalls]
- The table is genome-level, with one row per genome-pathway pair; aggregate to species with MAX(score_simplified) grouped by clade_name and pathway. [src: pitfalls] The best score per genome-pathway pair is taken before species aggregation. [src: pitfalls]
- `clade_name` stores the full gtdb_species_clade_id with the representative genome accession suffix, not the short GTDB_species name. [src: pitfalls] In nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology, joining to a temporary view built from GTDB_species returned 0 rows for this reason. [src: pitfalls]
- `metabolic_category` stores 'aa' and 'carbon'. [src: pitfalls] Filtering on 'amino_acid' returns 0 rows. [src: pitfalls]
- In pangenome_pathway_geography, searching for the nonexistent score_category 'present' counted exactly 80 pathways for every species with 0% present. [src: pitfalls]
- GapMind genome IDs omit the GTDB RS_ or GB_ prefixes used by the pangenome genome table. [src: pitfalls] A direct equality match returns zero rows. [src: pitfalls] The fix is to strip the prefix or use a fallback chain [src: pitfalls] that tries the original ID, then the stripped ID, then an accession substring. [src: pitfalls]
- In plant_microbiome_ecotypes, core-level scoring gave 0% completeness across all compartments in broad taxonomic comparisons. [src: pitfalls, plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The report says core-level scoring is too stringent for genus-level or cross-species comparisons where pathway genes may not be universal [src: pitfalls] and recommends genus-level max-aggregation. [src: pitfalls]

## FW300-N2E3 data

The FW300-N2E3 report used the `gapmind_pathways` table of `kbase_ke_pangenome` for pathway completeness predictions. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] It also provides a file, data/gapmind_pathways.tsv, with 3,200 rows of GapMind pathway-genome pairs for the clade. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]

## Caveats

- Predictions are computational, not experimental. [src: essential_metabolome]
- The dataset may miss organisms with divergent metabolic genes. [src: essential_metabolome]
- Non-canonical or poorly characterized pathways are not detected. [src: essential_metabolome]
- GapMind predicts ~80 pathways, many outside the report's 22 tested substrates. [src: cf_formulation_design]
- Coverage of ENIGMA conditions is low. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma]
- The 62 carbon pathways may be too coarse for strain-level niche differences. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- Max-aggregation across species to genus inflated an effect size. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

See [[concepts/reliability-and-coverage-limits-of-gapmind-pathway-predictions]].
