# Glycoside hydrolases

Glycoside hydrolases (GH) are a CAZy class of enzymes that degrade carbohydrates. In one pan-bacterial analysis, GH gene counts per genome differed strongly between biomes, and the difference was statistically very strong. [src: cazyme_pangenome_ecology] See also [[concepts/biome-level-carbohydrate-active-enzyme-repertoire-differentiation]] and [[summaries/cazyme_pangenome_ecology__REPORT]].

## Key facts

- Bacterial GH genes were 3–4× more abundant in fiber-rich environments than in marine ones. Mean GH genes were 28.9 in sheep-rumen, 31.9 in tomato-rhizosphere, 37.7 in barley-rhizosphere and 23.5 in soil, against 9.0 in marine. [src: cazyme_pangenome_ecology]
- The bacterial biome-effect Kruskal-Wallis test for GH gave H = 7,793 and BH-adjusted p ≈ 0. [src: cazyme_pangenome_ecology] (See [[entities/kruskal-wallis-test-with-eta-squared-effect-size]].)
- The report states that fiber-rich rumen, soil and rhizosphere environments select for genomes with extensive GH repertoires for polysaccharide degradation. [src: cazyme_pangenome_ecology] This is the report's interpretation of the pattern, not a separate test.

## Phylum patterns

[[entities/bacteroidota]] had the most GH-skewed CAZy profiles, while Pseudomonadota were relatively enriched in glycosyl transferases (GT; see [[entities/glycosyl-transferases]]). [src: cazyme_pangenome_ecology] Soil Bacteroidota carried a mean of 65.3 GH genes. This was the highest phylum–biome combination and 2.8× the Bacteroidota average across biomes. [src: cazyme_pangenome_ecology] The report claims this quantifies their specialization at pan-bacterial scale for the first time, and that the magnitude had not been quantified at genome-resolved scale. [src: cazyme_pangenome_ecology]

## Link to horizontal transfer

In environmental GT2 neighbourhoods, GH23 (murein lytic transglycosylase) was the second most common CAZy family, with 106 occurrences. The report suggests that cell wall remodelling genes cluster with GT2–T4SS syntenic loci. [src: t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt] See [[concepts/horizontal-transfer-of-carbohydrate-active-enzymes-via-conjugative-machinery]] and [[summaries/t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt__REPORT]].
