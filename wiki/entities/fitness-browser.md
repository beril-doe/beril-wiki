# Fitness Browser

The Fitness Browser (fit.genomics.lbl.gov; KBase Data Lakehouse collection `kescience_fitnessbrowser`) is a collection of RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing) mutant fitness data. Its primary citation is Price et al. (2018), "Mutant phenotypes for thousands of bacterial genes of unknown function". [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] Many projects use it, and its reported size varies with the subset analysed (48 organisms in the gene table, 43 in the fitness-conservation analyses). It does not include Acinetobacter baylyi ADP1. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] It is also heavily skewed toward Gammaproteobacteria and Pseudomonadota. [src: functional_dark_matter] Its tables have many schema quirks (string-typed columns, case-sensitive orgId, composite keys) that cause silent errors. [src: pitfalls] See [[entities/random-barcode-transposon-sequencing]] and [[entities/kbase-data-lakehouse]].

## Key facts

- The collection holds 27,410,721 per-gene fitness measurements across 7,552 experiments and 228,709 genes. [src: berdl_data_atlas] One report states the same total as 27.4M measurements. [src: berdl_data_atlas]
- Another report describes the dataset (feba.db; Price et al. 2018) as 48 organisms, 228K genes, 27M fitness scores and 7,552 experiments. [src: snipe_defense_system] It calls it the Deutschbauer/Price Fitness Browser database. [src: snipe_defense_system]
- The Fitness Browser database grew from the initial RB-TnSeq work and now covers 48 organisms. [src: annotation_gap_discovery] That initial work was Wetmore et al. (2015, mBio). By the report's PubMed account, it ran 387 genome-wide fitness assays across 5 bacteria and found 5,196 genes with significant phenotypes. [src: annotation_gap_discovery]
- Other reports describe Price et al. (2018) as demonstrating genome-wide mutant fitness across 32 bacteria [src: conservation_vs_fitness], as the source of the data they used [src: essential_genome, lab_field_ecology], as providing genome-wide fitness measurements that reveal condition-dependent costs and benefits [src: core_gene_tradeoffs], as the largest resource of genome-wide bacterial mutant fitness data [src: fitness_effects_conservation], and as showing that mutant fitness can assign phenotypes to genes of unknown function. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] The functional_dark_matter report extends this fitness-based function prediction to 48 organisms and 7,552 conditions. [src: functional_dark_matter] The module_conservation report credits Price et al. (2018) with generating the data and developing the ICA (independent component analysis) decomposition used to identify co-regulated fitness modules. [src: module_conservation]
- The core_gene_tradeoffs report uses the collection as its RB-TnSeq mutant fitness source. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]
- Gene-level sources differ by report. The gene table holds ~221K genes across 48 bacteria [src: conservation_vs_fitness], or 221,005 genes across 48 organisms. [src: essential_genome] The fitness-conservation work used RB-TnSeq fitness for ~194K genes across 43 bacteria. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis, fitness_effects_conservation] The gradient dataset spans 194,216 protein-coding genes across 43 bacteria, including Methanococcus, Ralstonia and Bacteroides. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] The Oak Ridge report used per-gene fitness across 43 organisms from fitness_effects_conservation/data/fitness_stats.tsv. [src: lab_field_ecology]
- The lakehouse tables used include organism, experiment, genefitness and gene. [src: annotation_gap_discovery] Others are besthitswissprot [src: berdl_data_atlas], fitbyexp_caulo [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] and gene_phenotypes. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] The AMR (antimicrobial resistance) studies used genefitness, gene and experiment for fitness measurements, gene metadata and experiment classification. [src: amr_fitness_cost] They used organism, gene and genefitness for AMR fitness effects. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] Other reports used genefitness, gene and exps for fitness values, gene metadata and experiment descriptions [src: costly_dispensable_genes], and genefitness and experiment via cached matrices. [src: counter_ion_effects]
- Further table uses: fitness_modules used genefitness, gene, exps and ortholog for fitness scores, gene metadata, experiment conditions and ortholog mappings. [src: fitness_modules] functional_dark_matter used gene, genefitness, specificphenotype, experiment, cofit, ortholog, genedomain, seedannotation, organism and specog. [src: functional_dark_matter] fw300_metabolic_consistency used genefitness, experiment, gene and seedannotation. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] metabolic_capability_dependency used genefitness, gene and seedannotation for gene-level fitness, essentiality inference and SEED subsystem proxies for pathway membership. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] metal_cross_resistance used experiment, genefitness, gene and organism. [src: metal_cross_resistance] metal_fitness_atlas used genefitness, expcondition, experiment and ortholog. [src: metal_fitness_atlas] The ENIGMA study used organism, experiment, genefitness and gene for RB-TnSeq data on 7 anchor strains. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma] The pathway_capability_dependency report used genefitness (27M rows), experiment (7.5K experiments) and besthitkegg (KEGG annotations). [src: pathway_capability_dependency] The respiratory_chain_wiring report used experiment, gene and genefitness for cross-species aromatic experiments and Complex I/NDH-2 fitness. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] Its outputs include 14 rows of Complex I aromatic fitness across Fitness Browser organisms [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] and 33 rows of Fitness Browser experiments with aromatic substrates [src: respiratory_chain_wiring], from Spark queries for aromatic experiments and the NDH-2 compensation test. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] The prophage_amr_comobilization report used genefitness, gene and organism for its attempted H3 fitness-cost comparison. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]
- Per-gene fitness summary statistics come from conservation_vs_fitness/data/ and fitness_effects_conservation/data/. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] Organism-specific co-fitness pairs are in data/cofit/{org}_cofit.tsv. [src: cofitness_coinheritance] Top-20 co-fitness partners per gene come from the cofit table via Spark. [src: cofitness_coinheritance]
- fitness_modules stored per-organism gene-fitness matrices extracted from Spark [src: fitness_modules], gene and experiment metadata with KEGG, SEED, TIGRFam and PFam annotations [src: fitness_modules], and BBH (bidirectional best hit) ortholog pairs and ortholog groups. [src: fitness_modules] The ICA fitness modules dataset holds 1,116 co-regulated modules across 32 organisms. [src: module_conservation]
- For lab phenotype work, one report recommends the collection for gene-level fitness across hundreds of conditions. It joins to ke_pangenome on genome_id. [src: berdl_data_atlas]
- The kbase × kescience bridge is the most-used realized bridge, with 36 BERIL projects. It builds on the Fitness Browser pangenome cross-reference of Price et al. (2018). [src: berdl_data_atlas] See [[concepts/linking-fitness-assay-genes-to-pangenome-clusters]].
- The DvH analysis used RB-TnSeq fitness data for 757 experiments and 2,741 genes [src: field_vs_lab_fitness], generated by Price et al. (2018). [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]
- The essential-genome report took BBH pairs from the besthit table. [src: essential_genome] The discoveries digest notes BBH coverage for ~30 organisms. [src: discoveries]
- The SNIPE defense-system report queried a local SQLite copy, feba.db (7.4 GB, from Figshare). [src: snipe_defense_system] It used the GeneDomain and GeneFitness tables, which are also available as kescience_fitnessbrowser in the lakehouse. [src: snipe_defense_system] Its scores are log2 ratios from RB-TnSeq, and it identified carbon-source conditions by condition_1 and expGroup = 'carbon source'. [src: snipe_defense_system] Among the 48 organisms, ManXYZ was present only in E. coli K-12 (Keio collection), with full fitness data for 168 conditions. [src: snipe_defense_system]
- The module_conservation analysis covers a 29/32 organism subset, because 3 module organisms (Cola, Kang, SB2B) lack pangenome links when their species had too few GTDB genomes for pangenome construction. [src: module_conservation]
- The paperblast_explorer report found 129,823 VIMSS cross-references linking PaperBLAST to the Fitness Browser, a path from literature coverage to experimental fitness phenotypes. [src: paperblast_explorer] It proposes using the ~130K links to find fitness-important genes lacking literature as "high-impact understudied genes". [src: paperblast_explorer]
- The truly_dark_genes report found that of 39,532 Fitness Browser dark genes with pangenome links, bakta v1.12.0 reclassifies 33,105 (83.7%), leaving 6,427 truly dark genes called hypothetical by both pipelines. [src: truly_dark_genes]

## Schema notes and annotation chains

Several tables differ from their documentation. keggmember uses keggOrg/keggId rather than orgId/locusId and must be joined through besthitkegg. [src: discoveries, pitfalls] kgroupec uses ecnum, not ec. [src: discoveries, pitfalls] seedclass has orgId, locusId, type, num, and stores EC numbers, not a subsystem/category hierarchy. [src: discoveries, pitfalls] The fitbyexp_* tables are long format (expName, locusId, fit, t), not pre-pivoted, and the pitfalls digest advises using genefitness with an orgId filter instead. [src: discoveries, pitfalls] A separate pitfalls note describes per-organism fitbyexp_{orgId} tables (e.g. fitbyexp_keio, fitbyexp_dvh) as pre-pivoted convenience tables with non-standard schemas that need per-organism discovery. [src: pitfalls]

### Typing, case, size and keys

- Every column is stored as a string, so numeric columns (fit, t, begin, end, cofit, rank, ratio, protein_length) need casting before comparison or sorting. [src: pitfalls] In particular, t must be cast to DOUBLE before ABS. [src: pitfalls]
- orgId is case-sensitive: use 'Keio', not 'keio'. [src: pitfalls]
- genefitness has ~27M rows and cofit ~13.6M, so filter by orgId and ideally locusId. [src: pitfalls]
- Joins need the composite (orgId, locusId) key, because locusId alone collides across organisms. [src: pitfalls]
- kgroupdesc uses the column desc, not description. [src: pitfalls]
- The experiments table is experiment, not exps, with columns expName, expGroup and condition_1. [src: pitfalls] Selecting Group fails with UNRESOLVED_COLUMN. [src: pitfalls] expGroup gives clean condition categories (carbon source, nitrogen source, stress, pH, temperature), and the digest recommends it over pattern matching on condition_1. [src: pitfalls]
- gene.type encodes feature type (1=CDS, 5=pseudo, 7=rRNA, 2=tRNA), not essentiality. [src: pitfalls] Across all 45 tables in kescience_fitnessbrowser, there is no explicit essentiality column, insertion count or has-fitness-data flag. [src: pitfalls]

### KEGG and SEED mapping

There is no single (orgId, locusId)-to-KO table. [src: pitfalls] besthitkegg maps each locus to a KEGG organism and gene (keggOrg, keggId) by best hit. [src: pitfalls] keggmember maps each (keggOrg, keggId) pair to its KEGG ortholog group kgroup, and both joins are required. [src: pitfalls] The pitfalls query joins besthitkegg to keggmember on keggOrg and keggId, then left-joins kgroupdesc on kgroup. [src: pitfalls]

For SEED, the chain is seedannotation (orgId, locusId, seed_desc) to seedannotationtoroles (seed_desc to seedrole) to seedroles. [src: pitfalls] seedannotationtoroles has only seed_desc and seedrole. [src: pitfalls] seedroles has toplevel, category, subsystem and seedrole columns. [src: pitfalls] For functional descriptions, the digest recommends seedannotation. [src: pitfalls]

The native KEGG tables (besthitkegg, keggmember, kgroupec) give direct gene-to-KEGG KO-to-EC-to-pathway mapping for all 48 organisms. [src: discoveries] The report calls this much simpler than the DIAMOND blastp link-table pipeline of conservation_vs_fitness and says it covers more organisms. [src: discoveries] The chain is besthitkegg → keggmember → kgroupec → keggconf → GapMind pathway. [src: discoveries] The pathway_capability_dependency report states this FB-native route is used instead of the pangenome link table. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] An earlier statement of it gives besthitkegg to EC to KEGG map. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

### Locus-tag mismatch

The aaseqs download (fit.genomics.lbl.gov/cgi_data/aaseqs) uses RefSeq-style locus tags (e.g., ABZR86_RS*) for some organisms, while the gene table uses original tags (e.g., N515DRAFT_*). [src: discoveries] This caused a complete join failure for Dyella79 (0% merge rate), one of 34 organisms, and motivates locus-tag consistency checks. [src: discoveries] The pitfalls digest reports that Dyella79 had 0% overlap, while other organisms had >94%. [src: pitfalls] Joining DIAMOND hits built from aaseqs back to the gene table can silently produce zero matches. [src: pitfalls] See [[concepts/join-key-validity-and-identifier-format-mismatches]].

## Joining to structure and pangenomes

The Fitness Browser uses a composite (orgId, locusId) key and does not expose protein_id, so a proposed structural-fitness join had to be corrected. [src: berdl_data_atlas] SQL (structured query language) probing found the working path. It runs from genefitness to besthitswissprot through (orgId, locusId), then to alphafold_entries through sprotAccession = uniprot_accession. [src: berdl_data_atlas] The corrected join gives 55,454 genes across 48 organisms with both fitness data and an AlphaFold model. [src: berdl_data_atlas]

In the pangenome mapping, 44 of 48 organisms mapped to pangenome species clades. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] It produced 177,863 gene-to-cluster links at 100.0% median protein identity and 94.2% median gene coverage. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] A DIAMOND-based link table with 177,863 links at 100% sequence identity found 178 AMR genes across 37 organisms. These gave 29,386 fitness measurements. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] A data file lists the same 29,386 measurements as covering 162 AMR genes in 36 organisms. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] The reports give different gene and organism counts for these measurements. The 100% threshold avoids paralog confusion but may undercount effects for close variants. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] A 90% identity DIAMOND threshold may also miss recently acquired genes with low sequence similarity. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] Adding pangenome conservation to the phenotypic data distinguishes evolutionarily stable from transient phenotypic genes. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

In the P4-D1 audit of 18,989 species, Fitness Browser BBH covered only 0.18% (35 species) and was judged suitable only for point validation. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] The fb_pangenome_link.tsv file supplies that 35-species cross-walk. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] The gene_copy_number_variation report proposes using ~30 Fitness Browser–pangenome-linked species to test whether paralog copy count correlates with fitness in relevant conditions. [src: gene_copy_number_variation] It says this would connect its copy-number-variation observations to the dosage-selection story of Elliott, Cuff & Neidle 2013. [src: gene_copy_number_variation]

The prophage_amr_comobilization report notes that the 48 model organisms overlap poorly with the GTDB pangenome species it analysed. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization] Because of this limited overlap, it could not test its fitness-cost hypothesis H3 [src: prophage_amr_comobilization], which remains open for expanded fitness datasets. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]

## Fitness, conservation and essentiality

Fitness importance and pangenome conservation are positively correlated across ~194,000 genes in 43 bacteria, with a 16-percentage-point gradient between essential and always-neutral genes. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] That report adds a pangenome conservation dimension to the Price et al. fitness measurements. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] Genes tagged in the specificphenotype table for strong condition-specific effects are 77.3% core versus 70.3% for genes without specific phenotypes (OR=1.78, p=1.8e-97). [src: fitness_effects_conservation] That table provides the report's condition-specific fitness annotations. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] The metal_specificity report proposed independent validation against the specificphenotype table's built-in annotations [src: metal_specificity], but did not complete it. [src: metal_specificity] See [[concepts/calibration-of-fitness-phenotype-thresholds-against-genome-background]].

In the trade-off analysis, 25,271 genes (17.8%) are true trade-off genes, important (fit < -1) in some conditions and burdensome (fit > 1) in others. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] The selection signature matrix has 28,017 costly, conserved genes, which the report interprets as maintained by selection because they are essential in natural environments absent from lab experiments. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] See [[concepts/costly-but-conserved-genes-laboratory-burden-versus-conservation]].

The essential-genome analysis covers 221,005 genes across 48 organisms, 41,059 essential (18.6%). [src: essential_genome] Rates range from 12.2% in Pedo557 to 29.7% in Magneto. [src: essential_genome] See [[concepts/universal-versus-variable-gene-essentiality-across-bacteria]].

## Functional dark matter

The functional_dark_matter census integrates fitness_modules, essential_genome, conservation_vs_fitness and module_conservation with direct Fitness Browser queries across all 48 organisms. [src: functional_dark_matter] It identifies 57,011 dark genes, 24.9% of 228,709 total genes. [src: functional_dark_matter] Of these, 39,532 (69.3%) link to the pangenome, 6,142 (10.8%) belong to ICA fitness modules with function predictions, 7,787 (13.7%) show strong fitness effects (|fit| ≥ 2), and 9,557 (16.8%) are essential. [src: functional_dark_matter] See [[concepts/census-and-evidence-depth-classification-of-functionally-dark-genes]].

The collection is heavily skewed toward Gammaproteobacteria, with 21/48 organisms and 78% of top-500 dark genes. [src: functional_dark_matter] Of the 48 organisms, 37 are Pseudomonadota (77%), and Bacteroidota (4), Cyanobacteriota (1) and Halobacteriota (2) are the only other phyla. [src: functional_dark_matter] The fitness ICA decomposition in fitness_modules yielded 1,116 stable modules across 32 organisms, all with >=100 experiments. [src: fitness_modules]

## Co-fitness

Co-fitness identifies genes in the same pathway or complex. The cofitness_coinheritance report found this lab-measured coupling weakly but significantly predicts co-inheritance, with a small effect size. [src: cofitness_coinheritance] Ralstonia UW163 and GMI1000 have zero co-fitness data despite appearing in the organism table, so they were excluded from the primary analysis. [src: cofitness_coinheritance]

## Use in antimicrobial-resistance studies

- One meta-analysis used 27M fitness measurements across 25 diverse bacteria. [src: amr_fitness_cost] It classified 6,804 experiments: 2,868 carbon/nitrogen, 1,862 stress, 727 standard, 447 metal and 443 antibiotic. [src: amr_fitness_cost] In that analysis, AMR gene knockouts had systematically higher fitness than non-AMR knockouts under non-antibiotic conditions. The report reads this as a metabolic burden. [src: amr_fitness_cost] See [[concepts/fitness-cost-of-antimicrobial-resistance-genes]].
- The pangenome atlas finds a different pattern. AMR genes show slightly less fitness cost than the non-AMR baseline, with median fitness -0.007 versus -0.012 (Mann-Whitney p=3.7e-6). [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] Only 37 of 48 organisms had AMR genes, mostly environmental strains. That analysis therefore misses the cost of recently acquired mobile resistance in pathogens. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]
- The cofitness study used cached fitness matrices from the collection. [src: amr_cofitness_networks] It used the full intersection of 28 organisms with AMR genes, fitness matrices and ICA modules. [src: amr_cofitness_networks] See [[entities/cofitness-analysis]].

## Metals

The collection contains 559 metal-related experiments, 8.2% of 6,804 total, covering 16 metals. [src: metal_fitness_atlas] No rare earth element experiments exist. [src: metal_fitness_atlas] Six metals have cross-species coverage (≥3 organisms): cobalt in 27 organisms [src: metal_fitness_atlas], nickel 26 [src: metal_fitness_atlas], copper 23 [src: metal_fitness_atlas], aluminum 22 [src: metal_fitness_atlas], zinc 17 [src: metal_fitness_atlas] and iron 3. [src: metal_fitness_atlas] See [[concepts/metal-fitness-breadth-and-magnitude-across-metals-and-organisms]].

The metal_cross_resistance report classified 452 metal experiments across 37 organisms and 14 metals (Al, Cd, Co, Cr, Cu, Fe, Hg, Mn, Mo, Ni, Se, U, W, Zn). [src: metal_cross_resistance] It extracted metal fitness data for 119,561 genes for 28 organisms with ≥3 metals (30 extracted, 2 excluded for having <3 metals). [src: metal_cross_resistance] It analyzed 317 organism-pair observations across 85 unique metal pairs. [src: metal_cross_resistance]

## Ortholog-transferred, metal and osmotic data

Ortholog-transferred fitness data (12,241 entries, 2,005 genes, 13 conditions) show Complex I orthologs with worse fitness on aromatic than non-aromatic conditions (mean = -1.35 vs -0.77, Mann-Whitney p < 0.0001). [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] This mixes organisms with different respiratory chains, so a single-organism measurement would be more definitive. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] A BacDive validation found 12 direct Fitness Browser–BacDive organism matches and used the metal experiment metadata. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

The counter-ion analysis identified 71 NaCl (sodium chloride) experiments across 25 organisms [src: counter_ion_effects] and 4,648 NaCl-important genes, a mean of 186 per organism. [src: counter_ion_effects] It proposes comparing metal–choline chloride overlap with metal–NaCl overlap to separate chloride from osmotic confounding, since several organisms have choline chloride experiments. [src: counter_ion_effects] See [[concepts/shared-metal-and-osmotic-stress-fitness-determinants]].

## Pathway dependency

Across 1,695 pathway-organism pairs from 48 Fitness Browser organisms, 15.8% of genomically complete pathways were latent capabilities with no detectable fitness importance under tested conditions (mean |t-score| < 1.0, < 5% essential genes). [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] The metrics span proteobacteria, firmicutes and other phyla. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] The latent fraction ranged 0–31.6% with mean 15.0%, highest in Pseudomonas syringae strains and Klebsiella michiganensis. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] Fitness coverage of 48 organisms is a small fraction of the 293,000 genomes with pathway predictions, so results may not generalize to all taxonomic groups. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] See [[concepts/latent-metabolic-capability-versus-fitness-dependency]].

The pathway_capability_dependency report crossed GapMind pathway completeness with Fitness Browser RB-TnSeq fitness to classify 161 organism-pathway combinations across 7 model bacteria into four categories. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] Only 7 of the 48 organisms have matching GapMind genome data. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] Their near-complete core genomes compress the conservation validation signal. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

## Matching to other datasets

### FW300-N2E3 and Web of Microbes

The fw300_metabolic_consistency report provides data/fb_experiments.tsv with 120 rows of carbon/nitrogen source experiments. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] It matched 28/58 (48%) WoM (Web of Microbes) metabolites to Fitness Browser carbon/nitrogen source conditions. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] Manual curation identified 28 WoM–Fitness Browser and 8 WoM–BacDive matches. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] Of 31 mapped conditions, 7 lacked FW300-N2E3 experiments: 4-aminobutanoate, 5-oxo-proline, Betaine, Guanine, Nicotinamide, Sarcosine and trans-Aconitate. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] The downstream analysis uses only the 24 conditions with fitness data. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] Across 21 WoM metabolites with matching experiments, FW300-N2E3 had significant fitness effects (|fit| > 1, |t| > 4) for 601 unique genes [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] and 4,764 significant gene-condition hits. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] See [[entities/pseudomonas-fluorescens-fw300-n2e3]] and [[concepts/matching-exometabolite-profiles-to-gene-fitness-experiments]].

### ENIGMA growth curves

ENIGMA growth curves align with Fitness Browser data through 486 (strain x condition) anchor pairs, 7 strains x 72 conditions where growth curves, gene fitness and genome annotations coexist. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma] The report claims this is the first condition-matched integration of the two datasets, which earlier work used separately. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma] Media names RCH2_defined_noCarbon, LB, R2A and M9 match exactly [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma], and 42 molecules align by normalized compound name. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma] Of the 486 pairs, 275 (56.6%) show measurable growth, giving balanced binary labels. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma] The Fitness Browser side is 7 anchor strains and 27M fitness scores. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma] The 7 anchor strains serve as biological validation, not training data. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma] Condition alignment is string-based, and the report suggests ChEBI-ID canonicalization would likely expand the 42 matches to 60-80. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma]

### Oak Ridge field ecology

Of 26 unique genera represented in the Fitness Browser, 14 are detected in Oak Ridge groundwater communities by 16S amplicon sequencing. [src: lab_field_ecology] The data/fb_genus_mapping.tsv file holds these 14 genera with prevalence and abundance statistics. [src: lab_field_ecology] The report claims the first test of whether lab fitness predicts field ecology at sites where many Fitness Browser organisms were isolated. [src: lab_field_ecology]

## Limits and coverage

- The 28 organisms in one study are lab-adapted, phylogenetically biased (many Pseudomonas) and ecologically limited. [src: amr_cofitness_networks]
- Laboratory conditions are biased toward rich media and standard stresses, leaving many ecological niches unrepresented. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] Coverage of 43 bacteria, primarily Proteobacteria, limits generalizability. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- The RB-TnSeq experiments underlying the essential gene data were run in rich media. [src: essential_metabolome] In the essential_metabolome pilot, GapMind coverage restricts the analysis to 7 organisms rather than the intended 45 with essential gene data. [src: essential_metabolome] Coverage is unknown for the remaining 38. [src: essential_metabolome] That report took 859 universally essential families across 45 organisms from essential_families.tsv [src: essential_metabolome] and used an 8-row manual organism-to-genome mapping. [src: essential_metabolome]
- Nearly all experiments use shaken liquid culture, where that report considers flagella useless. [src: amr_cofitness_networks] Most use rich or defined media with amino acid supplements, where biosynthesis is redundant. [src: amr_cofitness_networks]
- ADP1 was checked in the organism table and not found. The connection scan gave 0 matches for 1 ADP1 entry. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] The ADP1 mutant growth data are therefore a unique resource in the lakehouse. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] The report proposes comparing them with related Fitness Browser organisms through a cluster ID mapping. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer]
- The ADP1 essentiality report describes Fitness Browser TnSeq (transposon sequencing) as spanning diverse conditions across many studies. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] It uses RB-TnSeq data aggregated across studies for binary essentiality and for essentiality fraction and fitness. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] In the original study the binary calls come from minimal media. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]
- In the validation set, all Gram-typed organisms are Gram-negative, which precludes within-set testing of H1a. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] All urease-typed organisms are urease-negative yet routinely tested against nickel. This is consistent with urease status not predicting metal tolerance at pangenome scale. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]
- One study selected fourteen organisms with rich carbon-source RB-TnSeq coverage. [src: annotation_gap_discovery]
