# Pseudomonadota

## What this entity is

**Canonical name:** Pseudomonadota. [^lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]

**Known alias:** Proteobacteria. [^lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]

**Stable external identifier:** No stable external identifier was reported in the source document. [^lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]

Pseudomonadota is a bacterial phylum analyzed in the [lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas__REPORT](../summaries/lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas__REPORT.md) using GTDB-r214 taxonomy and pangenome functional-marker data. [^lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]

## Findings across functional markers

Pseudomonadota contains 2,988 genomes with xoxF calls and 171 genomes with mxaF calls among 117,619 genomes. [^lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]

The xoxF rate in Pseudomonadota is 2.5%, and the xoxF:mxaF ratio is 17.5:1. [^lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]

These results support the atlas-wide finding that xoxF is more frequently detected than mxaF across the pangenome, while the source cautions that annotation-based detection does not establish enzyme activity or methylotrophic physiology. [^lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]

Within Pseudomonadota, Pseudomonadaceae contributes 566 xoxF genomes and 1 mxaF genome. [^lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]

Beijerinckiaceae contains 171 xoxF genomes among 508 genomes, corresponding to a 33.7% xoxF rate. [^lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]

Hyphomicrobiaceae contains 33 xoxF genomes among 56 genomes, corresponding to a 58.9% xoxF rate. [^lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]

The distribution of xoxF across Pseudomonadota contributes to the report's hypothesis that lanthanide-dependent methanol oxidation extends beyond classical methylotroph lineages, although this interpretation remains based on marker detection rather than direct physiological measurements. [^lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]

The PHB study **refines** this marker-based profile: Pseudomonadota had the highest reported PHB prevalence, with phaC in 60.9% of species (4,544/7,456), and accounted for 74.9% of all 6,067 phaC-carrying species. [^phb_granule_ecology] This distribution **supports** the study's finding that PHB pathways are strongly concentrated in particular clades, while the study's environmental-selection and horizontal-transfer interpretations remain broader hypotheses requiring phylogenetically explicit validation. [^phb_granule_ecology]
