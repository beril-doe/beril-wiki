# AlphaEarth

## What this is

**Canonical name:** AlphaEarth. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]

**Known aliases:** No aliases were reported in the source. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]

**Stable external identifier:** None was reported in the source. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]

AlphaEarth is an environmental-embedding dataset used in the [[summaries/amr_environmental_resistome__REPORT]] analysis to provide a continuous environmental representation for species in the KBase KE pangenome collection. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] The BERDL atlas **supports** this reusable environmental-layer interpretation by inventorying 83,287 AlphaEarth environment embeddings indexed to KBase genomes. [src: berdl_data_atlas] The [[summaries/env_embedding_explorer__REPORT]] characterizes these as 64-dimensional satellite-derived embeddings for 83,287 genomes, representing 28.4% of 293,059 genomes; 79,449 records have valid values across all dimensions and 3,838 contain at least one NaN. [src: env_embedding_explorer]

The SNIPE analysis **supports** AlphaEarth as an ecological-context layer beyond AMR and gene-content studies: among species with embeddings, SNIPE-bearing species numbered n=1,069 and non-SNIPE species n=13,977, with significant differences in 22 of 64 dimensions after Welch’s t-tests with Bonferroni correction. [src: snipe_defense_system] The largest effect was dimension A19, with Cohen’s d = 0.26 and p = 5.0e-14; effect sizes ranged from |d| = 0.11–0.26, indicating a modest environmental shift rather than strict habitat segregation. [src: snipe_defense_system] The dimensions derive from satellite remote-sensing imagery at sample-collection latitude/longitude coordinates and may capture vegetation indices, temperature, precipitation, and land cover, but no named biological interpretation was assigned to A19 or the other dimensions. [src: snipe_defense_system] This **refines** existing environmental-association claims because AlphaEarth coverage was 28.4% of genomes and biased toward environmental isolates with latitude/longitude metadata. [src: snipe_defense_system]

The [[summaries/pitfalls]] **refines** these coverage figures by reporting 83,227 of 293,059 genomes, or 28.4%, as covered in one quality check; a separate check found 3,838 of 83,287 genomes, or 4.6%, with at least one NaN among 64 dimensions, leaving 79,449 after complete-value filtering. [src: pitfalls] Coverage depends on valid latitude and longitude and is especially sparse for clinical isolates. [src: pitfalls]

The [[summaries/lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas__REPORT]] **refines** the coverage limitation by reporting AlphaEarth coverage for 1,457 of 3,690 xoxF-bearing genomes (39.5%), compared with a 28% pangenome baseline, leaving 60.5% without environmental coordinates. [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas] It proposes coverage-restricted PCA (principal component analysis) and UMAP (Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection), stratified by phylum, to test whether xoxF carriers form distinct environmental or biogeographic clusters. [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]

The prophage ecology analysis **supports** AlphaEarth as a continuous environmental-gradient layer beyond AMR and marker-gene applications: among 2,008 species with at least 5 embedded genomes, environmental niche breadth was partially correlated with prophage module count after controlling for genome size (partial Spearman rho=0.468, p=8.41e-110). [src: prophage_ecology] This result **refines** interpretation of AlphaEarth-derived ecological breadth by showing a strong module-level association in a prophage analysis, while the study was limited to 28% of genomes and a biased subsample toward clinically and environmentally well-sampled lineages. [src: prophage_ecology]

## Evidence across source documents

The analysis included 2,659 species with both antimicrobial-resistance (AMR) data and AlphaEarth environmental embeddings. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] A separate atlas analysis included 2,684 species with at least 3 genomes and AlphaEarth embeddings. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] These are reported as distinct analysis cohorts; the sources do not reconcile their different counts. [src: amr_environmental_resistome, amr_pangenome_atlas]

Of 64 embedding dimensions, 52 significantly correlated with AMR diversity after false-discovery-rate correction at FDR < 0.05. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] Embedding dimension A34 had the strongest reported correlation with AMR diversity, with Spearman rho = +0.24. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]

A Mantel test found that environmental distance, measured as cosine distance between AlphaEarth embeddings, predicted AMR-profile distance, measured as Bray–Curtis distance between resistance-mechanism fractions, with r = 0.098 and p = 0.001. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] Stratified Mantel correlations were strongest for clinical species (r = 0.177), moderate for soil species (r = 0.129), and weakest for aquatic species (r = 0.061). [src: amr_environmental_resistome]

The atlas **supports** the continuous environmental-layer interpretation: environmental diversity predicted AMR count (Spearman rho=0.466, p=1.6e-144) and was negatively correlated with AMR core fraction (rho=-0.173, p=1.8e-19) among the 2,684-species cohort. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] These associations **refine** the earlier embedding evidence but do not establish causation. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]

The ecotype analysis **refines** these AMR-specific associations by showing that, across 172 species, the median partial correlation was 0.0025 for environment versus 0.0143 for phylogeny, with no significant environmental effect in 156 species (90.7%). [src: ecotype_analysis] Phylogeny dominated gene-content similarity in 60.5% of species, whereas environment dominated in 39.5%; this result **supports** a limited, context-dependent environmental signal rather than a uniformly strong whole-genome effect. [src: ecotype_analysis] The analysis further suggests the hypothesis that AlphaEarth embeddings do not fully capture ecologically relevant environmental variation. [src: ecotype_analysis]

The environmental-versus-human-associated reanalysis **supports** that hypothesis and **refines** the clinical-bias interpretation: environmental species (n=37) had median partial correlation 0.051, compared with 0.084 for human-associated species (n=93) and 0.109 for Mixed/Other species (n=53); the one-sided Mann-Whitney U test for Environmental > Human-associated gave U=1536 and p=0.83. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] The continuous analysis likewise found no relationship between the fraction of environmental genomes and partial-correlation strength (rho=-0.085, p=0.25), while the human-associated fraction gave rho=0.030 and p=0.69. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] Thus, the reanalysis **contradicts** the hypothesis that environmental species have stronger environment–gene-content correlations, while confirming that clinical sampling bias alone does not explain the weak signal. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]

The new explorer **refines** this interpretation by showing that environmental samples have a stronger geographic embedding gradient than human-associated samples: environmental categories rise from mean cosine distance 0.27 within <100 km to 0.90 beyond 10,000 km (3.4x), whereas human-associated categories rise from 0.37 to 0.75 (2.0x). [src: env_embedding_explorer] Across 50,000 sampled genome pairs, mean cosine distance increased monotonically across the reported geographic bins—from 0.41 at <100 km to 0.82 at 10K–20K km—and plateaued at intercontinental distances, supporting spatially autocorrelated environmental context rather than random variation. [src: env_embedding_explorer] This **supports** AlphaEarth as a spatial environmental layer but **refines** the gene-content interpretation: the relationship to environmental context does not by itself establish a strong environment–gene-content relationship. [src: env_embedding_explorer] The pitfalls analysis **supports** stratification by environment category by reporting a pooled near-versus-far ratio of 2.0x, compared with 3.4x for environmental samples and 2.0x for human-associated samples. [src: pitfalls]

The Gene Function Ecological Agora **supports** AlphaEarth as an ecological-context layer beyond AMR analyses: k = 10 clustering of embeddings placed 35.6% of 90 Cyanobacteriia species in a marine+sponge-dominant cluster, 34.0% of 50 Mycobacteriaceae species in a gut+sludge cluster and 20.0% in another gut cluster, while 729 Bacteroidota species were distributed across marine and gut-associated clusters. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] These results **refine** the earlier geographic-distance evidence by showing focal-clade environmental concentration, but they establish ecological consistency rather than causal effects of environment on gene innovation. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] The analysis is summarized in [[summaries/gene_function_ecological_agora__REPORT]].

The functional-dark-matter analysis **supports** AlphaEarth as an auxiliary environmental layer for testing unknown gene functions: among 151 accessory dark-gene clusters tested across 31 species, 10 of 137 clusters with environmental-category tests showed significant enrichment at FDR < 0.05, while 1 of 67 showed significant AlphaEarth embedding divergence. [src: functional_dark_matter] A pre-registered lab–field mapping found 29 of 47 testable clusters concordant (61.7%), but the one-sided binomial test gave p = 0.072; Fisher’s combined probability across 47 tests gave p = 0.031. [src: functional_dark_matter] NMDC validation confirmed four pre-registered abiotic predictions, including pH-associated carriers correlated with pH (ρ = +0.157, n = 4,366, FDR = 7.4e-25) and anaerobic carriers correlated negatively with dissolved oxygen (ρ = -0.298, n = 272, FDR = 1.5e-6). [src: functional_dark_matter] These results **refine** the earlier geographic-distance evidence by showing that AlphaEarth can contribute to gene-level environmental hypothesis testing, while leaving the strength of general lab–field concordance unresolved. [src: functional_dark_matter]

The PHB granule ecology analysis **supports** AlphaEarth as a niche-breadth layer while **refining** the interpretation of environmental associations. Among 2,008 species with sufficient embedding representation, PHB-positive species had median embedding variance of 0.3295 (n = 531) versus 0.2472 for PHB-negative species (n = 1,477), with Mann–Whitney U = 446,546 and p = 1.88 x 10^-6; the raw PHB–niche-breadth association was rho = 0.106 with p = 1.77 x 10^-06. [src: phb_granule_ecology] However, PHB-positive species had median genome size of 4.34 Mbp versus 2.44 Mbp for PHB-negative species, genome size correlated with niche breadth at rho = 0.302 with p = 1.5e-43, and the genome-size-controlled partial correlation was rho = -0.047 with p = 0.037, a 56.3% reduction and sign reversal. [src: phb_granule_ecology] This **contradicts** treating the raw PHB–embedding association as a PHB-specific effect and supports genome size as its primary correlate, while the analysis remains limited to 2,008 species, or 7.2% of total species diversity. [src: phb_granule_ecology]

The PHB study further found environmental enrichment across genome-size strata: high- versus low-variability environments showed 10.7% versus 2.5% phaC positivity in Q1, 18.5% versus 4.0% in Q2, 34.3% versus 10.9% in Q3, and 50.9% versus 37.4% in Q4, with enrichments of 4.4x, 4.6x, 3.1x, and 1.4x and p = 1.18 x 10^-11, p = 3.25 x 10^-47, p = 4.17 x 10^-70, and p = 3.21 x 10^-14, respectively. [src: phb_granule_ecology] These results **support** environmental context as relevant to PHB distribution but do not directly validate temporal variability, because AlphaEarth and NMDC measurements do not themselves measure feast/famine dynamics. [src: phb_granule_ecology]

The prophage study **supports** the geographic-gradient interpretation while adding a cross-modal ecological application: AlphaEarth niche breadth remained associated with prophage module count after genome-size control, and environmental effects on prophage composition were detected beyond genome size and family-level phylogeny. [src: prophage_ecology] In a complementary analysis of 1,773 species, PERMANOVA, a permutation-based multivariate analysis of variance, found environment significant at p=0.01, with F=30.04 versus F=6.17 for family-level phylogeny; the analysis therefore **refines** the earlier weak and context-dependent gene-content results by showing that environmental structure can be stronger for a specialized prophage-module phenotype than for whole gene-content similarity. [src: prophage_ecology] The result remains an association, and AlphaEarth coverage was only 28% of genomes. [src: prophage_ecology]

The new dataset characterization also **refines** the sampling-bias limitation. Among AlphaEarth genomes, 38% were human-associated: Human clinical included 16,390 genomes (20%), Human gut 13,466 (16%), and Human other 1,669 (2%); Soil included 6,073 (7%), Marine 5,850 (7%), and Freshwater 5,840 (7%). [src: env_embedding_explorer] Coordinate quality was heterogeneous: among 83,286 genomes with latitude and longitude, 50,109 (60.2%) were Good, 30,469 (36.6%) Suspicious cluster, and 2,708 (3.3%) Low precision. [src: env_embedding_explorer] The explorer reports that suspicious-cluster flags include legitimate field sites such as Rifle and Saanich Inlet, so the geographic signal should not be treated as free of metadata artifacts. [src: env_embedding_explorer]

The report treats the embedding analysis as supplementary confirmation of the discrete environment analysis, but notes that the embedding dimensions are opaque and that coverage was 28% of genomes. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] The atlas likewise reports limited AlphaEarth coverage and geographic-metadata bias, so the new associations should not be generalized beyond the sampled genomes. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] In the ecotype cohort, AlphaEarth embeddings covered 28.4% of genomes, which **refines** the earlier coverage estimate and reinforces the limitation on environmental inference. [src: ecotype_analysis] The reanalysis further found that environmental species had a 21% NaN partial-correlation rate versus 7% for human-associated species, so environmental species were disproportionately filtered rather than preferentially retained. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] The explorer independently reports NaN values in 3,838 of 83,287 records (4.6%), with their cause unknown. [src: env_embedding_explorer] The functional-dark-matter report similarly cautions that AlphaEarth embeddings cover only 28% of genomes (83K/293K), and that sparse environmental metadata limit interpretation. [src: functional_dark_matter] The lanthanide methylotrophy atlas **supports** this caution: 60.5% of its xoxF genomes lacked environmental coordinates, limiting geographic inference for that marker-defined group. [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas] The pitfalls analysis **refines** it further by noting that human-associated samples can dilute geographic patterns and that missing coordinates or metadata should not be interpreted as biological absence. [src: pitfalls]

The reanalysis used genome-level harmonized environment classifications and the same within-analysis methodology for comparison groups, but used all genomes with embeddings rather than diversity-maximizing downsampling; its median partial correlation across 183 species was 0.081 versus 0.003 in the original analysis, a reported 27x difference. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] The report states that absolute values are not comparable across methodologies, while the within-method environmental-versus-human-associated comparison remains valid. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]

The explorer further found uneven metadata coverage and harmonization: isolation_source covered 76,295 genomes (91.6%), host 53,091 (63.7%), env_broad_scale 34,800 (41.8%), env_local_scale 31,541 (37.9%), and env_medium 31,483 (37.8%). [src: env_embedding_explorer] Keyword matching mapped 5,774 unique isolation_source values to 12 broad categories, capturing 71% of genomes with a label; 17% remained Other and 12.5% were Unknown. [src: env_embedding_explorer] These limitations **refine** interpretation of both the earlier AMR and ecotype associations by showing that “environment” is partly inferred from incomplete, heterogeneous metadata.

The SNIPE environmental comparison likewise establishes statistical association rather than a specific causal habitat mechanism, and its AlphaEarth coverage was biased toward environmental isolates with geographic metadata. [src: snipe_defense_system] Its modest effect sizes **refine** stronger interpretations of environmental niche separation and make the result compatible with the existing evidence for spatial structure without establishing that a particular environmental variable causes SNIPE distribution. [src: snipe_defense_system]

These findings extend [[concepts/environmental-resistome]] by supplying a continuous environmental layer, contribute to [[concepts/multi-omics-integration]] by integrating environmental embeddings with AMR mechanism profiles, and inform [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] by testing environmental versus phylogenetic structure in gene content. [src: amr_environmental_resistome, amr_pangenome_atlas, ecotype_analysis] They also **support** [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]] by providing a systematic reanalysis of the null environment–gene-content relationship and its methodological boundary conditions. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] The new explorer **supports** [[concepts/environment-embedding-geography]] by establishing geographic distance-decay and environment-stratified embedding behavior, and **refines** [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]] by motivating an environmental-only reanalysis rather than treating a stronger relationship as established. [src: env_embedding_explorer] The Gene Function Ecological Agora **supports** [[concepts/environment-embedding-geography]] and **refines** [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]] through focal-clade environmental clustering, while integrating AlphaEarth with phylogenomic and functional evidence. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] The functional-dark-matter analysis **supports** [[concepts/environment-embedding-geography]] and [[concepts/cross-tenant-data-bridging]] by using AlphaEarth alongside Fitness Browser, pangenome, and NMDC evidence to prioritize environmentally testable dark genes. [src: functional_dark_matter] The lanthanide methylotrophy atlas **supports** [[concepts/environment-embedding-geography]] and **refines** [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] by applying the environmental layer to xoxF-bearing genomes while highlighting incomplete coordinate coverage and the need for phylum-stratified embedding analyses. [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas] The PHB study **supports** [[concepts/environment-embedding-geography]] by using embedding variance to quantify environmental breadth, while **refining** interpretation of that breadth by showing genome-size confounding and limited coverage. [src: phb_granule_ecology] The prophage ecology analysis **supports** [[concepts/environment-embedding-geography]] and [[concepts/cross-tenant-data-bridging]] by using AlphaEarth niche breadth to test prophage-module ecology while controlling for genome size and linking pangenome patterns to NMDC validation. [src: prophage_ecology] The SNIPE analysis **supports** [[concepts/environment-embedding-geography]] by testing environmental association for a defense-system gene family and **refines** interpretation through explicit coverage and causal-mechanism caveats. [src: snipe_defense_system] The pitfalls report **supports** [[concepts/provenance-aware-resource-discovery]] by documenting coverage, coordinate-quality, and missingness checks that should precede AlphaEarth joins and geographic interpretation.
