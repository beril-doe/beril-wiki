# Arsenic resistance ars genes

Arsenic resistance ars genes: arsD is among the dominant AMR (antimicrobial resistance) gene families, with 2,611 hits [src: amr_pangenome_atlas], and arsC is present in 89% of cultured genomes versus 14% of subsurface MAGs (metagenome assembled genomes). [src: oak_ridge_cultivation_gap] Aliases: arsenate reductase arsC, ars operon, arsC.

## Key facts

- Arsenic resistance genes arsD, arsN1, arsN2 and arsC add about 6,000 hits to the AMR annotations. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]
- Mer and ars heavy-metal genes are prominent in the atlas because the NCBI Reference Gene Catalog defines AMR broadly and includes stress response genes. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] See [[concepts/metal-resistance-genes-within-amr-catalogs]] and [[entities/mercury-resistance-mer-genes]].

## Annotation support

Spot checks of prevalent arsD and merP families showed the expected distributions, high in contaminated-environment genera and lower in strict aerobes. The report calls this informal support for the annotations. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] See [[concepts/validity-of-curated-metal-gene-annotation]].

## arsC in cultured genomes versus subsurface MAGs

arsC is far more common in cultured genomes than in subsurface MAGs: 89% versus 14%. [src: oak_ridge_cultivation_gap] All 4 metal-resistance marker KOs (KEGG orthologs) were enriched in cultured genomes, with log2 ratios of +1.5 to +5.9. [src: oak_ridge_cultivation_gap] In the ENIGMA comparison, metal resistance had a mean log2 ratio of +3.33 and 4/4 significant KOs. The report scores this as a partial match to its predictions, because genomic enrichment was seen.
