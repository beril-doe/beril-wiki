# Glyoxylate Shunt

## What this entity is

**Canonical name:** Glyoxylate shunt. [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes]

**Known aliases:** No aliases are reported in this document. [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes]

**Stable external identifier:** No stable external identifier is reported in this document. [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes]

The glyoxylate shunt is a metabolic pathway identified here through acetate-associated deletion phenotypes. [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes]

## Evidence across studies

Acetate condition-specific genes included fadB, malate synthase G, and citB, which the report associates with fatty acid β-oxidation and the glyoxylate shunt. [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes]

These findings place the glyoxylate shunt within the acetate-specific metabolic architecture detected in the *Acinetobacter baylyi* ADP1 deletion collection. [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes]

The acetate result contributes to the broader finding that 625 genes, or 31% of the complete 2,034-gene matrix, had a condition-specificity score of at least 1.0. [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes]

The Harvard Forest warming study **refines** this acetate-associated evidence by detecting increased RNA signals for the glyoxylate-cycle genes aceA/icl (K01637) and aceB/glcB (K01638) in heated mineral soil: log2 fold changes were +0.460 and +0.268, respectively, with p=0.037 for each. [src: harvard_forest_warming] These gene-level signals extend the pathway’s observed environmental context beyond ADP1 deletion phenotypes, but they were not established as FDR-significant across 14K KOs. [src: harvard_forest_warming]
