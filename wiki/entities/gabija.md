# Gabija

## What it is

**Canonical name:** Gabija. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

**Known aliases:** No aliases are reported in the source document. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

**Stable external identifier:** No stable external identifier is reported in the source document. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

Gabija is one of seven anti-phage defense-system families analyzed across the BERDL pangenome. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

## Key facts

Gabija was detected in 22.8% of species-level pangenomes in the analysis. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

Its prevalence reached 30–40% in Bacillota and remained below 10% in Actinomycetota. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

Gabija formed a strong positive co-occurrence association with [[entities/restriction-modification-type-ii|restriction-modification Type II]] systems: 2,429 observed co-occurrences versus a null mean of 1,555, with z = 46.1 and odds ratio (OR) = 24.0, after 1,000 phylum-stratified column-permutation null permutations and Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

The R-M Type II × Gabija pairing was presented as a novel pan-bacterial defense syndrome at this scale. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

The two systems in this pairing both act on double-stranded DNA: R-M Type II uses sequence-specific cleavage, whereas Gabija uses a nuclease/helicase pair triggered by nucleotide-pool depletion. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

Gabija markers were enriched in the accessory pangenome: 9.1% of Gabija-associated gene clusters were core and 68.8% were singleton, compared with a background of 46.8% core and 37.9% singleton gene clusters. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

The singleton fraction for Gabija was 1.8× above the 37.9% background baseline, consistent with possible horizontal transfer of defense loci through mobile genetic elements. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

The report recommends PADLOC MacSyFinder-style multi-PFam and gene-order rules for refining Gabija detection. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]
