# Xanthomonas vasicola

## Identity

**Canonical name:** Xanthomonas vasicola. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

**Known aliases:** No aliases were reported in the source document. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

**Stable external identifier:** No stable external identifier was supplied in the source document. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

## Key Findings

Xanthomonas vasicola was one of 65 plant-associated species assessed for within-species host or subclade structure, but only 18 of the 65 species had any phylogenetic-tree distance data. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

Among the 17 species that met the final testability criteria, Xanthomonas vasicola passed the Bonferroni-corrected Fisher test with p = 1.6×10⁻⁴. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The species also satisfied Cochran’s rule for chi-square validity, indicating that its host-by-subclade contingency analysis met the report’s stated validity criterion. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

Xanthomonas vasicola showed strong segregation toward Zea mays: 47 of 52 genomes occurred in one subclade, with p = 1.4×10⁻¹¹ before correction and p = 1.7×10⁻¹⁰ after correction. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

This result supports [ecotype-environment-gene-content](../concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content.md), because it links host association with within-species genomic subclade structure, while the limited phylogenetic-tree coverage means the result should not be generalized to all candidate species. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The broader analysis recovered 1,306 of 1,306 genomes and 599 plant-associated genomes after correcting a genome-identifier mismatch between phylogenetic tables and environmental metadata. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The source interprets the overall evidence as weak support for within-species plant-association segregation in 5 of 17 testable species, with the strongest evidence concentrated in Xanthomonas and Pseudomonas lineages. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

## Limitations and Next Steps

The Xanthomonas vasicola result is limited by incomplete phylogenetic-tree coverage: only 18 of 65 candidate species were represented by any tree-distance data, so the prevalence of comparable segregation across the full candidate set cannot be estimated. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The report recommends accessory-genome profiling, gene-content-tree analysis, expanded BERDL phylogenetic-tree coverage, and crop-specific validation to test whether the Zea mays-associated subclade carries genomic adaptations to that host. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
