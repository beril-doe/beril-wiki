# pqqC

## What this entity is

**Canonical name:** pqqC. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]

**Known aliases:** No aliases were reported in this document. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]

**Stable external identifier:** No stable external identifier was reported in this document. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]

pqqC is a plant-growth-promoting gene marker analyzed across the BERDL pangenome. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]

## Key facts

pqqC showed the strongest positive co-occurrence among the analyzed focal-gene pairs with [[entities/acds]], with OR = 7.24, n = 286 co-occurring species, and q = 1.2e-83 after Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]

pqqC also co-occurred significantly with [[entities/hcnc]] (OR = 1.91) and [[entities/ipdc]] (OR = 1.55), forming a putative rhizosphere-effectiveness module. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]

In the environmental comparison, pqqC occurred in 43.8% of 1,039 soil/rhizosphere species and 21.2% of 10,233 species from other environments, corresponding to OR = 2.90 and q = 2.8e-53. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]

These findings support the [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]] interpretation that pqqC is associated with soil/rhizosphere gene content. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]

pqqC had a core fraction of 81.5%, an auxiliary fraction of 7.8%, and a singleton fraction of 10.7%, with q = 0.0 for its higher core fraction relative to the genome-wide baseline. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]

The genome-wide baseline was 46.8% core, whereas the mean accessory fraction across all analyzed plant-growth-promoting genes was 29.7% compared with 53.2% genome-wide. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]

The pqqC distribution therefore supports the [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] and [[concepts/gene-function-acquisition-depth]] findings that the analyzed plant-growth-promoting genes were predominantly core rather than accessory. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]

The report identified a non-diazotrophic pqqC + [[entities/acds]] module as a stable, specialized rhizosphere niche marker, but treated this interpretation as an ecological inference from co-occurrence and environmental enrichment rather than evidence of physical genomic linkage. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]

Whether pqqC and acdS occupy the same genomic island, operon, or separate loci remains unresolved. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]
