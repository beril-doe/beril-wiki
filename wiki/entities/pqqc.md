# pqqC

## What this entity is

**Canonical name:** pqqC. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

**Known aliases:** No aliases were reported in this document. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

**Stable external identifier:** No stable external identifier was reported in this document. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

pqqC is a plant-growth-promoting gene marker analyzed across the BERDL pangenome. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

## Key facts

pqqC showed the strongest positive co-occurrence among the analyzed focal-gene pairs with [acds](acds.md), with OR = 7.24, n = 286 co-occurring species, and q = 1.2e-83 after Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

pqqC also co-occurred significantly with [hcnc](hcnc.md) (OR = 1.91) and [ipdc](ipdc.md) (OR = 1.55), forming a putative rhizosphere-effectiveness module. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

In the environmental comparison, pqqC occurred in 43.8% of 1,039 soil/rhizosphere species and 21.2% of 10,233 species from other environments, corresponding to OR = 2.90 and q = 2.8e-53. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

These findings support the [ecotype-environment-gene-content](../concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content.md) interpretation that pqqC is associated with soil/rhizosphere gene content. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

pqqC had a core fraction of 81.5%, an auxiliary fraction of 7.8%, and a singleton fraction of 10.7%, with q = 0.0 for its higher core fraction relative to the genome-wide baseline. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

The genome-wide baseline was 46.8% core, whereas the mean accessory fraction across all analyzed plant-growth-promoting genes was 29.7% compared with 53.2% genome-wide. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

The pqqC distribution therefore supports the [pangenome-integration](../concepts/pangenome-integration.md) and [gene-function-acquisition-depth](../concepts/gene-function-acquisition-depth.md) findings that the analyzed plant-growth-promoting genes were predominantly core rather than accessory. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

The report identified a non-diazotrophic pqqC + [acds](acds.md) module as a stable, specialized rhizosphere niche marker, but treated this interpretation as an ecological inference from co-occurrence and environmental enrichment rather than evidence of physical genomic linkage. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]

Whether pqqC and acdS occupy the same genomic island, operon, or separate loci remains unresolved. [^pgp_pangenome_ecology]
