# Protocatechuate 3,4-dioxygenase

Protocatechuate 3,4-dioxygenase (pcaGH) is a non-heme Fe²⁺-dependent ring-cleavage enzyme in ADP1. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Its iron requirement is the report's explanation for a group of iron acquisition genes in the quinate-specific network. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

## Key facts

- pcaGH is a non-heme Fe²⁺-dependent ring-cleavage enzyme. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]
- The report calls the non-heme Fe²⁺ requirement a defining feature of intradiol dioxygenases. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]
- The ADP1 quinate-specific network includes 7 iron acquisition genes that supply Fe²⁺ for ring cleavage by this enzyme. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]
- These 7 genes cover siderophore biosynthesis, ExbD/TolR transport, a ferrichrome receptor and a TonB-dependent receptor. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

## Role in support-gene networks

The central digest places the iron acquisition genes (7 genes, Fe²⁺ for the ring-cleavage dioxygenase) among three biochemically rational support subsystems of ADP1 aromatic catabolism. The other two are Complex I (21 genes, NADH reoxidation, where NADH is reduced nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) and PQQ biosynthesis (2 genes, cofactor for quinate dehydrogenase, where PQQ is pyrroloquinoline quinone).
