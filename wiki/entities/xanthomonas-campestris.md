# Xanthomonas campestris

## What this entity is

**Canonical name:** Xanthomonas campestris. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

**Known aliases:** None reported in this document. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

**Stable external identifier:** None reported in this document. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

Xanthomonas campestris is a plant-associated bacterial species analyzed for host-linked within-species genomic structure in [[summaries/plant_microbiome_ecotypes__REPORT]]. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

## Key facts from the document

Xanthomonas campestris was one of 17 plant-associated species with sufficient phylogenetic-tree coverage and sampling to undergo within-species host-association testing. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

It passed both within-species and across-species Bonferroni corrections in the host-by-subclade analysis. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The species showed segregation toward Brassica hosts, with 46 of 47 genomes in one subclade. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The association had a raw p-value of 2.7×10⁻¹² and a corrected p-value of 3.3×10⁻¹¹. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

This result provides strong, but species-specific, evidence that host association can coincide with within-species subclade structure, contributing to the [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]] synthesis. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The document reports that only 5 of 17 testable species passed Bonferroni-corrected Fisher tests, so the Xanthomonas campestris result is part of a minority pattern rather than evidence that host-linked subclade segregation is universal across plant-associated species. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The broader analysis identified 1,136 plant-associated species and 7,995 plant-associated genomes, but phylogenetic-tree coverage was available for only 18 of 65 candidate species, limiting estimates of how common this pattern is. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The report attributes recovery of the subclade signal to correcting a genome-identifier mismatch between bare NCBI accessions in phylogenetic tables and GTDB-prefixed identifiers in environmental metadata; the correction recovered 1,306 of 1,306 genomes and 599 plant-associated genomes. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The Xanthomonas campestris finding supports the report’s conclusion that accessory-genome profiling, gene-content trees, and expanded phylogenetic-tree coverage are needed to determine which genomic features underlie host-specialized lineages. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
