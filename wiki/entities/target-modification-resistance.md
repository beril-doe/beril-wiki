# Target modification resistance

Target modification is an antimicrobial resistance (AMR) mechanism class that includes ribosomal protection proteins. In the amr_environmental_resistome report it is strongly enriched in gut and clinical species (Kruskal-Wallis, η² = 0.100), and the evidence comes from direct statistical comparison across environments. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]

## Key facts

- The report counts 11,067 target-modification gene clusters in the assembled resistome. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]
- It classifies tetracycline-resistance genes tet(M,O,Q,W), which are ribosomal protection proteins, as target modification. It classifies tet(A-E,K,L) as efflux. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]
- In the bacterial AMR mechanism census of amr_pangenome_atlas, 6,955 hits (8.4%) are target modification, and 19.6% of these are core. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]

## Distribution across environments

Target modification peaks in gut and clinical environments at 28-44% of AMR. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] It accounts for 44% of human gut AMR, 28% of clinical AMR and 6% of aquatic AMR. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] The fractions are: clinical 27.5%, human gut 43.6%, host-associated 22.8%, soil 10.1%, aquatic 6.2%, and other environmental 25.1%. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]

For bacterial species, the difference by environment is significant (KW H=1394.9, p=1.7×10⁻²⁹⁹, η²=0.100).
