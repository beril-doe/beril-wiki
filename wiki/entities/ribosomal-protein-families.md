# ribosomal protein families

Ribosomal protein families (also called ribosomal proteins) are the ribosomal protein gene families rpsC, rplW, rplK, rplB, rplA, rplF, rps11, rpsJ, rpsI and rpsM, which were found in the absolute essential core. [src: discoveries, essential_genome] In a cross-organism essentiality analysis across 48 bacteria, these ten sit among 15 families that are essential in every organism. [src: discoveries]

## Key facts

- The ten ribosomal protein families in the core are rpsC, rplW, rplK, rplB, rplA, rplF, rps11, rpsJ, rpsI and rpsM. [src: discoveries, essential_genome]
- The analysis covered 48 diverse bacteria, 221,005 genes and 17,222 ortholog families. [src: discoveries]
- Fifteen families were essential in every one of the 48 bacteria. [src: discoveries]
- The other five are the chaperonin groEL, CTP synthase pyrG, translation elongation factor G fusA, valyl-tRNA synthetase valS and geranyltranstransferase SelGGPS. [src: discoveries, essential_genome]

## Relation to universal conservation

The essential_genome report compares its 15 pan-bacterial essential families with Koonin (2003), who estimated about 60 universally conserved proteins across all life, mostly translation-related. [src: essential_genome] The report calls its set consistent with that estimate, because ribosomal proteins and translation machinery dominate it. [src: essential_genome] It also notes that the set includes cell wall biosynthesis and nucleotide metabolism genes, which may be bacteria-specific essentials. [src: essential_genome]
