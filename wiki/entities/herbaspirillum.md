# Herbaspirillum

## Identity

**Canonical name:** *Herbaspirillum*. [^lab_field_ecology]

**Known aliases:** No aliases were specified in the source. [^lab_field_ecology]

**Stable external identifier:** No stable external identifier was specified in the source. [^lab_field_ecology]

*Herbaspirillum* is a bacterial genus represented in the [kescience-fitnessbrowser](kescience-fitnessbrowser.md) and detected in the [enigma-coral](enigma-coral.md) groundwater communities studied at the [oak-ridge-field-research-center](oak-ridge-field-research-center.md). [^lab_field_ecology]

## Evidence from Lab–Field Ecology

*Herbaspirillum* was one of 26 unique genera represented in the [kescience-fitnessbrowser](kescience-fitnessbrowser.md), and it was detected in the Oak Ridge groundwater community dataset collected across 108 sites. [^lab_field_ecology]

The communities were profiled using [16s-amplicon-sequencing](16s-amplicon-sequencing.md), a marker-gene method for characterizing microbial composition. [^lab_field_ecology]

*Herbaspirillum* abundance increased with uranium concentration, with Spearman rho=+0.336, p=3.8e-4, and Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate q=0.001. [^lab_field_ecology]

This association passed the report's FDR threshold of q<0.05 and was one of five significant genus-level associations among 11 tested genera. [^lab_field_ecology]

The positive uranium association supports the hypothesis that *Herbaspirillum* is favored, directly or indirectly, along the uranium contamination gradient, but the report cautions that field ecology also varies with redox conditions, carbon and energy sources, competition, and other geochemical factors. [^lab_field_ecology]

The study did not establish that laboratory metal tolerance alone caused the observed *Herbaspirillum* pattern, because the aggregate laboratory metal-tolerance score showed only a suggestive, non-significant relationship with field abundance ratios across 12 genera. [^lab_field_ecology]

## Interpretation and Follow-up

The genus-level result may conceal species- or strain-level differences because 16S data did not match Fitness Browser organisms at species or strain resolution. [^lab_field_ecology]

The report proposes species- or strain-level matching against ENIGMA CORAL metagenomic genome or assembly tables, multivariate CCA or RDA analysis controlling for pH, redox, and carbon sources, and temporal analysis across sampling dates as follow-up analyses for the laboratory–field relationship. [^lab_field_ecology]
