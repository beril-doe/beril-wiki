# Thousands of multi-member protein families have no literature

In PaperBLAST, 5,218 multi-member protein families representing 14,534 sequences have no literature whatsoever and are called dark protein families. [src: paperblast_explorer]

## Key figures

- **5,218 families**: multi-member protein families with no literature [src: paperblast_explorer]
- **14,534 sequences**: sequences in those families [src: paperblast_explorer]

## Evidence

> 5,218 multi-member families — representing 14,534 sequences — have no literature whatsoever. These are the "dark" protein families. [src: paperblast_explorer]

From the report [[summaries/paperblast_explorer__REPORT]].

## Caveat

The report has no negative controls and cannot easily distinguish genuinely unstudied genes from genes whose literature was missed by text mining. [src: paperblast_explorer]

> - **No negative controls**: We cannot easily distinguish genes that are genuinely unstudied from genes whose literature was missed by text mining. [src: paperblast_explorer]

## Other caveats in this report

- In PaperBLAST, 14,798 organisms received an Unknown domain classification, indicating many organisms are not easily classified using name heuristics alone. [src: paperblast_explorer]
- PaperBLAST includes approximately 19% of the full SwissProt database (~570K reviewed entries as of 2024), specifically the subset matched to PMC open-access literature. [src: paperblast_explorer]
- PaperBLAST text-mined paper mentions do not imply functional characterization, and the report suggests that the 65.6% with 1 paper likely include many incidental mentions. [src: paperblast_explorer]
- PaperBLAST mines full text only from PubMed Central, missing paywalled papers and introducing systematic bias against fields or journals with lower open-access rates. [src: paperblast_explorer]
- The report's heuristic organism-to-domain mapping classified 35% of organisms as Unknown, and a formal taxonomy lookup would improve accuracy. [src: paperblast_explorer]
