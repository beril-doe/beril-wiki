# Singleton genes are not systematically detrimental

Novel/singleton genes are not systematically detrimental in the Fitness Browser analysis; their mean fitness is near zero, similar to auxiliary genes. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

## Evidence

> - Novel/singleton genes are NOT systematically detrimental -- their mean fitness is near zero, similar to auxiliary genes [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

From the report [[summaries/fitness_effects_conservation__REPORT]].

## Caveat

Singleton/novel genes may lack fitness data due to poor transposon coverage rather than true neutrality. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

> - Singleton/novel genes may lack fitness data due to poor transposon coverage rather than true neutrality [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

## Other caveats in this report

- The laboratory conditions in the Fitness Browser analysis are biased toward rich media and standard stresses, leaving many ecological niches unrepresented. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- The report states that its statistically robust 16pp gradient nevertheless means fitness importance only weakly predicts conservation. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- The Fitness Browser fitness measurements concern single-gene knockouts and do not capture epistatic interactions. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- Fitness Browser coverage of 43 bacteria, primarily Proteobacteria, limits the generalizability of the report's analysis. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
