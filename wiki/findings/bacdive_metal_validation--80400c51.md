# No contamination signal in Bacillota or Bacteroidota

The phylum-stratified analysis shows the signal is absent in Bacillota and Bacteroidota. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

## Evidence

> The phylum-stratified analysis shows the signal is real within Pseudomonadota and Actinomycetota but absent in Bacillota and Bacteroidota. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

From the report [[summaries/bacdive_metal_validation__REPORT]].

## Caveat

The signal is not significant within Bacillota (p=0.285) or Bacteroidota (p=0.456), though sample sizes for contamination isolates in these phyla are small. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

> The signal is not significant within Bacillota (p=0.285) or Bacteroidota (p=0.456), though sample sizes for contamination isolates in these phyla are small. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

## Other caveats in this report

- Positive BacDive metal utilizers had lower metal tolerance scores, but the report cautions against overinterpreting this surprising, nonsignificant direction because the sample was tiny. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- The observed BacDive heavy metal effect d=1.00 barely exceeded the detection threshold, providing just enough power to detect the effect. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- The report states that a larger heavy metal contamination dataset is needed to precisely estimate the effect size. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- Although RESEARCH_PLAN.md referenced n=31 BacDive heavy metal isolates, only 10 matched pangenome species with metal tolerance scores after the species-name bridge. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- The report proposes biological differences in metal exposure as one explanation for the phylum-specific BacDive results: Pseudomonadota and Actinomycetota include major soil bacteria exposed to metals, whereas Bacillota and Bacteroidota are primarily gut-associated. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
