# Pathway completeness is near-universal rather than strictly universal in this sample

The report concludes the data show near-universal rather than strictly universal metabolic pathway completeness, and suggests the remaining diversity likely reflects ecological adaptation and nutrient availability. [src: essential_metabolome]

## Evidence

> **Conclusion**: The data reveal **near-universal** rather than **strictly universal** metabolic pathway completeness. Even among organisms with experimental essential gene data, some metabolic diversity exists, likely reflecting ecological adaptation and nutrient availability. [src: essential_metabolome]

From the report [[summaries/essential_metabolome__REPORT]].

## Caveat

GapMind completeness calls are computational rather than experimental, use the complete and likely_complete categories, and may miss partial or non-canonical pathways. [src: essential_metabolome]

> - GapMind predictions are **computational**, not experimental - "Complete" and "likely_complete" categories used; may miss partial pathways - Non-canonical or poorly characterized pathways not detected [src: essential_metabolome]

## Other caveats in this report

- The ecological rationale for DvH serine auxotrophy is speculative: external serine availability and dependency were not tested. [src: essential_metabolome]
- Shared central catabolic capacity is an interpretation of the predictions, not direct metabolic measurement. [src: essential_metabolome]
- The report attributes E. coli absence to GTDB pangenome construction excluding species with too many genomes. [src: essential_metabolome]
- GapMind coverage constrained this pilot to 7 organisms instead of the intended 45 Fitness Browser organisms with essential-gene data. [src: essential_metabolome]
- Ancestral amino-acid prototrophy is a hypothesis inferred from only 6 of 7 organisms (85.7%), not an established pan-bacterial finding. [src: essential_metabolome]
