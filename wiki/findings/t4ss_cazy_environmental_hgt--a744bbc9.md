# T4SS machinery is common in high-quality environmental MAGs

T4SS/conjugative machinery was identified in 6,652 of 30,497 high-quality environmental MAGs (21.8%) using the multi-marker definition (VirB4/6/8/9/10/11, VirD4, TraI/D, TrwB, TraG). [src: t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt]

## Key figures

- **21.8%**: share of high-quality environmental MAGs carrying T4SS/conjugative machinery (denominator 30,497 high-quality environmental MAGs; scope multi-marker definition) [src: t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt]
- **6,652 MAGs**: MAGs with T4SS/conjugative machinery (denominator 30,497 high-quality environmental MAGs) [src: t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt]

## Evidence

> **21.8% of 30,497 high-quality environmental MAGs** carry T4SS/conjugative machinery (6,652 MAGs; multi-marker definition: VirB4/6/8/9/10/11, VirD4, TraI/D, TrwB, TraG). [src: t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt]

## Other caveats in this report

- T4SS-mediated dissemination of carbohydrate-active enzymes is presented as a hypothesis, not a demonstrated mechanism; the associations are observational and require experimental validation. [src: t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt]
- The report links CAZy–T4SS synteny to a metal-resistance niche-breadth hypothesis; describing these genomes as transfer hubs is interpretive. [src: t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt]
- Pending checks are a synteny-threshold permutation test requiring Spark unfiltered data, Node_4915 BLAST validation against NCBI nr, a housekeeping-gene null baseline, and biome-enrichment factorisation using the stated θ formula. [src: t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt]
