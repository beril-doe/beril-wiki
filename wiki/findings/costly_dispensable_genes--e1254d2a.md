# Costly non-conserved genes are mostly mobile-element debris from horizontal transfer

The report concludes that costly+dispensable genes are almost exclusively mobile element debris rather than degrading metabolic pathways, which suggests HGT-mediated genome expansion is the primary source of costly non-conserved genes in bacteria. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

## Evidence

> The finding that costly+dispensable genes are almost exclusively mobile element debris -- rather than degrading metabolic pathways -- narrows the biological interpretation and suggests that HGT-mediated genome expansion is the primary source of costly non-conserved genes in bacteria. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

From the report [[summaries/costly_dispensable_genes__REPORT]].

## Caveat

The genes are predominantly selfish elements rather than metabolic genes, and may represent an earlier stage where the gene hasn't yet been lost despite its cost. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

> Our costly+dispensable genes are candidates for this process, though they are predominantly selfish elements rather than metabolic genes -- they may represent an earlier stage where the gene hasn't yet been lost despite its cost. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

## Other caveats in this report

- The report cautions that Virulence enrichment in costly+dispensable genes is based on small counts of 21 vs 4 genes. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]
- The report cautions that the singleton contrast is partly structural because core genes cannot be singletons by definition. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]
- In the costly+dispensable gene analysis, burden is defined as max_fit > 1 in any experiment, so a single experiment can classify a gene as burdensome and make the classification sensitive to fitness-data noise. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]
- SEED/KEGG annotations cover only 56-79% of genes in the costly+dispensable gene analysis, and the unannotated fraction may have different functional profiles. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]
- The report uses a binary pangenome core/auxiliary classification and notes that the fraction of genomes carrying a gene would provide more resolution. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]
