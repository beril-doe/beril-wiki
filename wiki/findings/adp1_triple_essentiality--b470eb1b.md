# FBA class does not predict growth defects among dispensable genes

Among 478 triple-covered, TnSeq-dispensable Acinetobacter baylyi ADP1 genes, FBA class is not significantly associated with growth defects (chi-squared = 0.93, p = 0.63, 2 df). [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]

## Key figures

- **p = 0.63**: chi-squared p-value (scope 478 genes) [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]

## Evidence

> Among 478 triple-covered genes (all TnSeq-dispensable), FBA class was **not significantly associated** with growth defect status (chi-squared = 0.93, p = 0.63, 2 df). [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]

From the report [[summaries/adp1_triple_essentiality__REPORT]].

## Caveat

All 478 triple-covered genes (having TnSeq + FBA + growth data) are TnSeq-dispensable on minimal media. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]

> All 478 triple-covered genes (having TnSeq + FBA + growth data) are **TnSeq-dispensable** on minimal media. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]

## Other caveats in this report

- The report explains that TnSeq-essential Acinetobacter baylyi ADP1 genes lack viable deletion mutants and therefore cannot have growth rate measurements. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]
- The positive glucarate flux–growth correlation in Acinetobacter baylyi ADP1 is opposite to prediction and suggests condition-specific model inaccuracies. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]
- The Acinetobacter baylyi ADP1 Q25 growth-defect threshold flags ~25% of genes per condition, so aggregating any defect across 8 conditions creates a high background rate by design, with expected 1 − 0.75⁸ = 90% under independence versus observed 72%. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]
- The original Acinetobacter baylyi ADP1 study had no direct lethality measurements and used TnSeq as a proxy. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]
- The report emphasizes that RB-TnSeq and complete gene deletion capture different biology in Acinetobacter baylyi ADP1 because transposon insertion is not gene deletion. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]
