# Lab-antibiotic and heavy-metal genes are least conserved in DvH

In DvH, lab-antibiotic and heavy-metal resistance genes are the least conserved (73.4% and 71.2%), below the 76.3% baseline. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]

## Key figures

- **73.4%**: core fraction of lab-antibiotic genes [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]
- **71.2%**: core fraction of heavy-metal genes [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]
- **76.3%**: baseline core fraction [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]

## Evidence

> The most striking finding is that **lab-antibiotic** and **heavy-metal resistance** genes are the least conserved (73.4% and 71.2%), well below the 76.3% baseline. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]

From the report [[summaries/field_vs_lab_fitness__REPORT]].

## Caveat

The lab-antibiotic dip at -2 is driven by a small sample (n=109 at -2 vs n=45 at -3). [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]

- The DvH fitness-conservation analysis excludes 678 essential genes (80.1% core) because no transposon mutants were recovered and they lack fitness data. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]
