# Bacterial literature favors pathogens and model strains over environmental organisms

Among 15,312 bacterial organisms, the top 100 capture 44.3% of bacterial literature, with M. tuberculosis H37Rv (9,079 papers), E. coli K-12 (8,860) and P. aeruginosa PAO1 (5,928) leading, and environmental and non-pathogenic organisms described as dramatically underrepresented. [src: paperblast_explorer]

## Key figures

- **44.3%**: share of bacterial literature captured by the top 100 bacterial organisms (denominator bacterial literature; scope 15,312 bacterial organisms) [src: paperblast_explorer]
- **9,079 papers**: papers for M. tuberculosis H37Rv (scope PaperBLAST) [src: paperblast_explorer]
- **8,860 papers**: papers for E. coli K-12 (scope PaperBLAST) [src: paperblast_explorer]
- **5,928 papers**: papers for P. aeruginosa PAO1 (scope PaperBLAST) [src: paperblast_explorer]

## Evidence

> Among 15,312 bacterial organisms, the top 100 capture **44.3%** of bacterial literature. The top 3 bacteria are all well-known pathogens or model organisms: *M. tuberculosis* H37Rv (9,079 papers), *E. coli* K-12 (8,860), and *P. aeruginosa* PAO1 (5,928). Environmental and non-pathogenic organisms are dramatically underrepresented. [src: paperblast_explorer]

## Caveat

The report's bacterial analysis counts 15,312 bacterial organisms but does not explain how this population relates to the separate Bacteria classification in its domain table. [src: paperblast_explorer]

## Other caveats in this report

- The approximately 40% estimate concerns protein residues lacking structural or functional information; the report's 55% concerns 50%-identity families with 0 or 1 papers. Their denominators and definitions differ. [src: paperblast_explorer]
- A text-mined paper mention does not establish functional characterization; some single-paper links may be incidental. [src: paperblast_explorer]
- Mining only PubMed Central full text misses paywalled papers and can bias measured coverage by open-access rate. [src: paperblast_explorer]
- The 50% sequence-identity definition of a protein family is conventional, not a universal biological boundary. [src: paperblast_explorer]
- Only 19% of SwissProt is represented in PaperBLAST; missing matching PMC full text is offered as a likely explanation, not demonstrated as the cause. [src: paperblast_explorer]
