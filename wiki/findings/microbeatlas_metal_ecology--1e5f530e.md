# Metal-type association loses significance under strict prevalence filtering

When niche breadth was recomputed under strict prevalence filtering, the metal-type association was nonsignificant (p = 0.092 versus original 1.5×10⁻⁴). [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

## Key figures

- **0.092**: p-value of the metal-type association under strict prevalence filtering [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]
- **1.5×10⁻⁴**: original p-value (scope before strict filtering) [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

## Caveat

The report cautions that the 5% threshold is quite conservative, excluding 89% of OTUs, many of which it considers genuinely distributed across environments but uncommon within any one. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

> The 5% threshold is quite conservative — it excludes 89% of OTUs, many of which are genuinely distributed across environments but uncommon within any one. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

## Other caveats in this report

- The signal ordering is consistent with, but does not establish, vertical inheritance of core resistance and horizontal transfer or local exposure shaping accessory genes and metal-type expansion. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]
- The PGLS subset comprises 606 bacterial genera with ≥ 3 OTUs, GTDB r214 tree membership, and metal AMR data from kbase_ke_pangenome; predictors were z-scored. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]
- The core-fraction association with number of environments (β = +0.142, p = 0.038) is nominal, not Bonferroni significant. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]
- PGLS jointly estimated λ by maximum likelihood; data were sorted into tree-tip order before fitting. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]
- The species-with-AMR count covariate is borderline (β = +0.0093, p = 0.068). [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]
