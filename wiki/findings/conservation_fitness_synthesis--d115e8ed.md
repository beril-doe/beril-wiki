# The core-burden excess is driven by specific functional categories

The burden paradox is function-specific: motility and chemotaxis genes (+7.8pp core-burden excess), RNA metabolism (+12.9pp), and protein metabolism (+6.2pp) drive the effect. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

## Key figures

- **+12.9pp percentage points**: core-burden excess for RNA metabolism genes (scope RNA metabolism) [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]
- **+7.8pp percentage points**: core-burden excess for motility and chemotaxis genes (scope motility and chemotaxis) [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]
- **+6.2pp percentage points**: core-burden excess for protein metabolism genes (scope protein metabolism) [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

## Caveat

The flagellar and ribosomal explanations for these categories are interpretations offered by the report, not direct measurements in this synthesis. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

> This makes biological sense: flagella are energetically expensive but essential for chemotaxis in natural environments; ribosomal components are costly but required for rapid growth responses. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

## Other caveats in this report

- The report cautions that laboratory conditions poorly represent natural environments. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]
- The proposed explanation is that a gene costly in rich media may be needed in soil, biofilm, or host tissue; this is a possibility, not a direct field measurement. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]
- The report attributes the null breadth-conservation trend to an already-high baseline core fraction. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]
- The report offers greater detectability of effects in core genes as an explanation for the condition-specific pattern. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]
- The identities of the 5,526 costly, dispensable genes remain unresolved; mobile elements and recently acquired genes are posed as possibilities. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]
