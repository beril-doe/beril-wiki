# Nine lab modules in DvH show strikingly low conservation

A revised DvH module classification using mean rather than median core fraction reveals 9 lab modules with mean core fraction 0.516, suggesting accessory-genome modules do exist and tend to respond to lab-type conditions. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]

## Key figures

- **9**: lab modules [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]
- **0.516**: mean core fraction of lab modules [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]

## Evidence

> However, the revised module classification (using mean rather than median core fraction) reveals 9 "lab" modules with strikingly low conservation (mean core fraction 0.516), suggesting that accessory-genome modules do exist and tend to respond to lab-type conditions. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]

## Caveat

Condition classification relies on manual mapping of condition_1 labels; edge cases (e.g., zinc sulfate as metal vs sulfate source) required subjective judgment. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]

> Condition classification relies on manual mapping of `condition_1` labels; edge cases (e.g., zinc sulfate as metal vs sulfate source) required subjective judgment [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]

## Other caveats in this report

- The report considers ENIGMA CORAL field geochemistry and community-composition data potentially useful for future analyses but not for gene-level fitness analysis. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]
- The DvH fitness-conservation analysis excludes 678 essential genes (80.1% core) because no transposon mutants were recovered and they lack fitness data. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]
- The report attributes the DvH lab-antibiotic conservation dip at -2 to a small sample (n=109 at -2 vs n=45 at -3). [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]
