# No significant environment-effect difference between free-living and host-associated bacteria was detected

The ecotype correlation analysis found no significant difference in environment effects between free-living environmental bacteria and host-associated bacteria (p=0.66). [src: ecotype_analysis]

## Key figures

- **p=0.66**: test of difference in environment effects between free-living and host-associated bacteria [src: ecotype_analysis]

## Evidence

> Tested whether "environmental" bacteria (free-living, where lat/lon is meaningful) show stronger environment effects than host-associated bacteria. **No significant difference found** (p=0.66). [src: ecotype_analysis]

From the report [[summaries/ecotype_analysis__REPORT]].

## Caveat

Host-associated organisms have less meaningful lat/lon data, as the reported coordinates reflect collection site rather than the organism's actual microenvironment. [src: ecotype_analysis]

> Host-associated organisms have less meaningful lat/lon data, as the reported coordinates reflect collection site rather than the organism's actual microenvironment. [src: ecotype_analysis]

## Other caveats in this report

- The report suggests that AlphaEarth embeddings may not fully capture ecologically relevant environmental variation. [src: ecotype_analysis]
- AlphaEarth embeddings have only 28.4% genome coverage in the report, limiting the environmental signal available for analysis. [src: ecotype_analysis]
- Missing or imprecise geographic coordinates in NCBI metadata reduce the quality of environmental associations in the ecotype analysis. [src: ecotype_analysis]
- The report cautions that partial correlations assume linear relationships between distance matrices and may miss nonlinear ecological effects. [src: ecotype_analysis]
