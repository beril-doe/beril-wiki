# Carbon-utilization pathways dominate the soil–freshwater separation axis, with amino acids secondary

PC1 is loaded almost entirely on carbon utilization pathways (glucuronate, fumarate, succinate, cellobiose, galactose) with near-uniform positive loadings, which the report takes to indicate that Soil communities have broadly higher carbon substrate completeness than Freshwater communities, while amino acid pathways load more strongly on PC2, separating within the Soil cluster. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

## Evidence

> PC1 is loaded almost entirely on carbon utilization pathways (glucuronate, fumarate, succinate, cellobiose, galactose) with near-uniform positive loadings — indicating that Soil communities have broadly higher carbon substrate completeness than Freshwater communities. Amino acid pathways load more strongly on PC2, separating within the Soil cluster. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

From the report [[summaries/nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology__REPORT]].

## Caveat

The ecosystem separation is consistent with, but does not demonstrate, the hypothesis that habitat physicochemistry selects different community metabolic configurations; carbon as primary axis and amino acids as secondary is a suggestion. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

> The strong ecosystem separation in PC space is consistent with the metabolic niche hypothesis: habitat physicochemistry (aquatic vs. terrestrial) selects for fundamentally different community metabolic configurations, not just different taxa. The dominance of **carbon utilization pathways** on PC1 (not amino acid pathways) suggests that carbon substrate availability is the primary axis differentiating ecosystem metabolic type at the community level, with amino acid biosynthesis playing a secondary (PC2-level) role. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

## Other caveats in this report

- The 220-sample cohort comprised 126 soil, 33 freshwater, and 61 ecosystem-unrecorded samples. All 33 freshwater samples lacked paired metabolomics, excluding them from H1 but not H2. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]
- H1 was overwhelmingly single-study: 125/131 samples (95%) came from nmdc:sty-11-r2h77870. All leucine samples came from that study, and the arginine association persisted after excluding the six samples from nmdc:sty-11-547rwq94. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]
- The listed positive, soil-directed PC1 loadings are glucuronate, fumarate, succinate, lysine, and cellobiose. The source calls all five carbon-utilization pathways, although lysine is also identified elsewhere in the report as an amino-acid pathway. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]
- The report interprets the leucine and arginine associations in light of stated synthesis costs of 37 and 26 ATP equivalents, respectively. This is a mechanistic interpretation, not direct evidence of gene loss; the predicted benefit of more methionine data remains untested. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]
- Correcting three misassigned isoleucine compounds changed the leucine result from r −0.326 to −0.390 and q 0.045 to 0.022. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]
