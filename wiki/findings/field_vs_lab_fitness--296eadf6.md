# Heavy-metal fitness genes are not significantly less core than baseline

Heavy-metal-important genes (n=198) were 71.2% core, with OR 0.77 and FDR q 0.14, so their deficit relative to baseline is not significant. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]

## Key figures

- **71.2%**: core fraction of heavy-metal-important genes (denominator 198 genes; scope DvH non-essential genes) [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]
- **0.77**: odds ratio versus baseline (scope heavy-metal-important genes) [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]
- **0.14**: FDR q (scope heavy-metal-important genes) [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]

## Evidence

> | Heavy-metals | 198 | 71.2% | 0.77 | 0.14 | [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]

## Caveat

The Nitratidesulfovibrio vulgaris pangenome has relatively few genomes, giving a coarse core/auxiliary classification with a high baseline core fraction (76.3%) that compresses effect sizes. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]

> - The *Nitratidesulfovibrio vulgaris* pangenome has relatively few genomes, creating a coarse core/auxiliary classification with a high baseline core fraction (76.3%), which compresses effect sizes [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]

## Other caveats in this report

- ENIGMA CORAL has 4,346 field samples across 596 Oak Ridge locations and 213,044 ASVs, but these do not provide DvH gene-level fitness. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]
- The 678 essential genes (80.1% core) were excluded because no transposon mutants and hence no fitness data were available. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]
- The lab-nutrient enrichment and nonsignificant field-versus-lab-specific comparison limit the ecological-specificity hypothesis. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]
- Accessory resistance and mobile-element involvement are interpretations, not directly demonstrated mechanisms. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]
- The proposed mechanistic explanation for the metal-class contrast is tentative. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]
