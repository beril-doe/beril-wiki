# PA14 assay strain is unrepresentative of cystic fibrosis isolates

T3SS typing of 6,760 PA genomes reveals CF isolates are 94% ExoS+ while PA14 is ExoU+ — the reference strain used in the inhibition assays represents <5% of CF PA. [src: cf_formulation_design]

## Key figures

- **94%**: CF isolates that are ExoS+ (scope CF PA genomes) [src: cf_formulation_design]
- **6,760 genomes**: PA genomes typed for T3SS [src: cf_formulation_design]

## Evidence

> T3SS typing of 6,760 PA genomes reveals CF isolates are 94% ExoS+ while PA14 is ExoU+ — the reference strain used in our inhibition assays represents <5% of CF PA. [src: cf_formulation_design]

From the report [[summaries/cf_formulation_design__REPORT]].

## Caveat

The inhibition measurements themselves have not been validated against ExoS+/PAO1-type strains. [src: cf_formulation_design]

> While our pangenome analysis confirms AA catabolism is identical across ExoU+ and ExoS+ strains, the inhibition measurements themselves have not been validated against ExoS+/PAO1-type strains. [src: cf_formulation_design]

## Other caveats in this report

- In the integrated PROTECT analysis of Pseudomonas aeruginosa PA14 inhibition, metabolic overlap explains only 27% of variance. [src: cf_formulation_design]
- The report states that repeated antibiotic courses used to treat cystic fibrosis airway infection select for resistance and disrupt the commensal microbiome. [src: cf_formulation_design]
- The report uses GTDB species taxonomy, which may differ from NCBI names. [src: cf_formulation_design]
- The PROTECT isolate collection reflects the typical cystic fibrosis airway microbiome plus deliberate oversampling of pathogens. [src: cf_formulation_design]
- Pseudomonas aeruginosa PA14's amino acid specialization is not universal among cystic fibrosis pathogens, suggesting protective formulations may need to be pathogen-specific. [src: cf_formulation_design]
