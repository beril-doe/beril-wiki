# Herbaspirillum is enriched at high-uranium sites

Herbaspirillum abundance correlated positively with uranium (Spearman rho +0.336; p=3.8e-4; FDR q=0.001), so it is more abundant at high-U sites. [src: lab_field_ecology]

## Key figures

- **+0.336**: Spearman rho between Herbaspirillum abundance and uranium (scope Oak Ridge sites) [src: lab_field_ecology]
- **0.001**: BH-FDR q-value [src: lab_field_ecology]

## Evidence

> | *Herbaspirillum* | +0.336 | 3.8e-4 | **0.001** | More abundant at high-U sites | [src: lab_field_ecology]

From the report [[summaries/lab_field_ecology__REPORT]].

## Caveat

Confounding variables (pH, dissolved oxygen, carbon sources) are not controlled for in the uranium associations. [src: lab_field_ecology]

> - Confounding variables (pH, dissolved oxygen, carbon sources) are not controlled for [src: lab_field_ecology]

## Other caveats in this report

- Shewanella, Dechlorosoma, and Marinobacter were excluded from correlation analysis because they occurred at <10 sites. [src: lab_field_ecology]
- The nonsignificant tolerance–field-abundance trend was in the predicted direction, but the small number of genera limited statistical power. [src: lab_field_ecology]
- The report cautions that the community shift is not simply enrichment of metal-tolerant organisms; other ecological changes may matter. [src: lab_field_ecology]
- Differences in pH, redox potential, carbon sources, sulfate, nitrate, and other conditions may decouple lab metal tolerance from field abundance. [src: lab_field_ecology]
- Community interactions may decouple single-organism lab fitness from field abundance. [src: lab_field_ecology]
