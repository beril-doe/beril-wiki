# Condition-specific fitness effects do not imply accessory status

Genes with strong condition-specific fitness effects are more likely to be core, not more accessory, so condition-specific does not mean niche-specific. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

## Evidence

> - **Condition-specific doesn't mean niche-specific**: Genes with strong condition-specific fitness effects are MORE likely core, not more accessory. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

From the report [[summaries/conservation_fitness_synthesis__REPORT]].

## Caveat

The report offers greater detectability of effects in core genes as an explanation for the pattern: core genes simply have more detectable effects. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

> Core genes simply have more detectable effects. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

## Other caveats in this report

- The report cautions that laboratory conditions poorly represent natural environments. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]
- The proposed explanation is that a gene costly in rich media may be needed in soil, biofilm, or host tissue; this is a possibility, not a direct field measurement. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]
- The flagellar and ribosomal explanations are interpretations offered by the report, rather than direct measurements in this synthesis. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]
- The report attributes the null breadth-conservation trend to an already-high baseline core fraction. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]
- The identities of the 5,526 costly, dispensable genes remain unresolved; mobile elements and recently acquired genes are posed as possibilities. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]
