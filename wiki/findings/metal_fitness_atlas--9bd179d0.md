# Essential-metal tolerance genes are more core-enriched than toxic-metal genes

Essential metal tolerance genes (Fe, Mo, W, Se, Mn) show a mean core fraction delta of +0.148, nearly double the toxic metal delta of +0.081 (Mann-Whitney U=39, p=0.015, one-sided). [src: metal_fitness_atlas]

## Key figures

- **+0.148**: mean core fraction delta for essential metals [src: metal_fitness_atlas]
- **+0.081**: mean core fraction delta for toxic metals [src: metal_fitness_atlas]
- **p=0.015**: one-sided Mann-Whitney p-value [src: metal_fitness_atlas]

## Evidence

> Essential metal tolerance genes (Fe, Mo, W, Se, Mn) show a mean core fraction delta of **+0.148**, nearly double the toxic metal delta of **+0.081**. This difference is statistically significant (Mann-Whitney U=39, p=0.015, one-sided). [src: metal_fitness_atlas]

From the report [[summaries/metal_fitness_atlas__REPORT]].

## Caveat

Metal coverage is uneven: cobalt and nickel were tested in 27 organisms, but uranium, chromium, mercury, cadmium, selenium and manganese in only 1-2 organisms, so rare-metal patterns reflect DvH and psRCH2 biology. [src: metal_fitness_atlas]

> - **Uneven metal coverage**: Cobalt and nickel tested in 27 organisms; uranium, chromium, mercury, cadmium, selenium, manganese in only 1-2 organisms. Cross-species patterns for rare metals reflect DvH and psRCH2 biology. [src: metal_fitness_atlas]

## Other caveats in this report

- The atlas conservation analysis covers 22 of 31 metal-tested organisms (71%). [src: metal_fitness_atlas]
- The 9 organisms excluded from atlas conservation analysis—Putida, Keio, SynE, Miya, Kang, BFirm, Cola, Ponti, and Dino—lack Fitness Browser–pangenome links. [src: metal_fitness_atlas]
- The report considers systematic bias from excluded organisms unlikely because they are taxonomically diverse. [src: metal_fitness_atlas]
- The atlas's functional predictions for unknown genes are based solely on cross-species fitness data. [src: metal_fitness_atlas]
- Without normalization, species with large open pangenomes such as K. pneumoniae and P. aeruginosa dominate atlas metal scores, reflecting genome size rather than metal biology. [src: metal_fitness_atlas]
