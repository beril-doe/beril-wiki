# Novel candidate families are less often metal-specific than annotated ones

Across all 149 novel metal candidate families, 45.6% have a dominant specificity of metal-specific, compared to 58.2% for annotated families (Fisher exact OR=0.60, p=0.003). [src: metal_specificity]

## Key figures

- **45.6%**: novel metal candidate families dominantly metal-specific (denominator 149 novel families) [src: metal_specificity]
- **58.2%**: annotated families dominantly metal-specific [src: metal_specificity]
- **0.60**: Fisher exact odds ratio [src: metal_specificity]
- **0.003**: Fisher exact p-value [src: metal_specificity]

## Evidence

> Across all 149 novel metal candidate families, 45.6% have a dominant specificity of "metal-specific" — compared to 58.2% for annotated families (Fisher exact OR=0.60, p=0.003). [src: metal_specificity]

From the report [[summaries/metal_specificity__REPORT]].

## Caveat

The report attributes the lower fraction partly to sampling: many novel candidates came from deeply profiled DvH, Btheta and psRCH2, where more experiments give more opportunities to detect pleiotropy. [src: metal_specificity]

> This reflects the composition of the novel set: many novel candidates were identified in deeply-profiled organisms (DvH, Btheta, psRCH2) where the high experiment count provides more opportunities to detect pleiotropic effects, pushing genes toward "general sick." [src: metal_specificity]

## Other caveats in this report

- The metal-specific fraction was ~41% at a 2% sick-rate cutoff and ~67% at 10%. [src: metal_specificity]
- Seven of 31 metal-tested organisms were excluded because locusId formats did not match fitness-matrix indices. [src: metal_specificity]
- Included organisms represented 7,609 of 12,838 metal-important gene records (59.3%). [src: metal_specificity]
- The report expects no systematic bias from the taxonomically diverse exclusions, but does not establish that empirically. [src: metal_specificity]
- The conservation analysis covered 22 organisms with pangenome links and distinguished pooled from organism-mean core fractions. [src: metal_specificity]
