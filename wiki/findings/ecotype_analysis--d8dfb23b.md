# Phylogenetic history is inferred to predict gene content better than environment

The report concludes that for most bacterial species, phylogenetic history is a stronger predictor of gene content than environmental similarity. [src: ecotype_analysis]

## Evidence

> For most bacterial species, phylogenetic history is a stronger predictor of gene content than environmental similarity. [src: ecotype_analysis]

From the report [[summaries/ecotype_analysis__REPORT]].

## Caveat

The report suggests, rather than directly establishes, that vertical inheritance dominates the gene content signal. [src: ecotype_analysis]

> 1. Vertical inheritance dominates the gene content signal [src: ecotype_analysis]

## Other caveats in this report

- The report proposes that environmental adaptation may affect specific gene subsets rather than whole-genome gene content. [src: ecotype_analysis]
- AlphaEarth embeddings may miss ecologically relevant environmental variation. [src: ecotype_analysis]
- The report cites Garud et al. (2019) and Shapiro et al. (2012) as literature consistent with its phylogeny-dominant interpretation; this is contextual support, not an additional result of this analysis. [src: ecotype_analysis]
- The report relates its weak genome-wide environmental effects to Polz et al. (2013)'s locus-specific horizontal-gene-transfer argument; the proposed gene-subset interpretation remains a hypothesis. [src: ecotype_analysis]
- AlphaEarth embeddings had only 28.4% genome coverage, limiting the environmental signal available for analysis. [src: ecotype_analysis]
