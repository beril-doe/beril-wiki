# Trade-off genes are enriched among core genes

Trade-off genes are 1.29x more likely to be core (OR=1.29, p=1.2e-44). [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]

## Key figures

- **1.29**: odds ratio for trade-off genes being core [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]
- **1.2e-44**: p-value for the enrichment [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]

## Evidence

> These are 1.29x more likely to be core (OR=1.29, p=1.2e-44). [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]

From the report [[summaries/core_gene_tradeoffs__REPORT]].

## Caveat

Participation in more pathways with condition-dependent costs and benefits is proposed as the explanation, an interpretation not separately quantified here. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]

> Core genes have more trade-offs because they participate in more pathways with condition-dependent costs and benefits. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]

## Other caveats in this report

- The core-gene burden pattern is not universal across functional categories. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]
- The report interprets 28,017 costly conserved genes as possibly maintained by selection for functions needed in natural environments absent from laboratory experiments; natural-environment essentiality was not directly measured here. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]
- The report uses motility genes as a case study and proposes natural-environment chemotaxis as the reason costly flagellar machinery remains conserved; that ecological explanation is interpretive. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]
- The report cautions that laboratory conditions are an incomplete proxy for natural environments. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]
- The report interprets 28,017 costly-but-conserved genes as evidence consistent with purifying selection despite metabolic cost; selection was inferred rather than directly measured. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]
