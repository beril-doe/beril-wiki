# Accessory genes are not systematically burdensome, contrary to streamlining

Contrary to the "streamlining" hypothesis, accessory genes are not systematically burdensome; they are less costly than core genes, not more. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

## Evidence

> - **Accessory genes are NOT systematically burdensome**: Contrary to the "streamlining" hypothesis, accessory genes are LESS costly than core genes, not more. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

From the report [[summaries/conservation_fitness_synthesis__REPORT]].

## Caveat

The report cautions that lab conditions are an impoverished proxy for nature, so burden measured in the lab may not reflect cost in natural environments. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

> **Lab conditions are an impoverished proxy for nature.** [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

## Other caveats in this report

- The proposed explanation is that a gene costly in rich media may be needed in soil, biofilm, or host tissue; this is a possibility, not a direct field measurement. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]
- The flagellar and ribosomal explanations are interpretations offered by the report, rather than direct measurements in this synthesis. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]
- The report attributes the null breadth-conservation trend to an already-high baseline core fraction. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]
- The report offers greater detectability of effects in core genes as an explanation for the condition-specific pattern. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]
- The identities of the 5,526 costly, dispensable genes remain unresolved; mobile elements and recently acquired genes are posed as possibilities. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]
