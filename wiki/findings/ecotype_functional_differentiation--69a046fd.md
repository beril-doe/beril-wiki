# Unknown-function and replication/repair categories show the largest ecotype differences

The largest reported mean effects were for S (unknown function), 0.039 and significant in 11/12 species, and L (replication, recombination, repair), 0.034 and significant in 9/12 species. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]

## Key figures

- **0.039**: mean effect size, COG S (unknown function) (scope significant in 11/12 species) [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- **0.034**: mean effect size, COG L (replication, recombination, repair) (scope significant in 9/12 species) [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]

## Evidence

> - **S** (Unknown function): mean effect = 0.039, significant in 11/12 species - **L** (Replication, recombination, repair): mean effect = 0.034, significant in 9/12 species [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]

From the report [[summaries/ecotype_functional_differentiation__REPORT]].

## Caveat

The largest mean effect sizes (S: 0.039, L: 0.034) represent ~3-4 percentage point differences in COG category proportions between ecotypes, so the biological magnitude is modest despite statistical significance. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]

> - **Effect sizes are small**: The largest mean effect sizes (S: 0.039, L: 0.034) represent ~3-4 percentage point differences in COG category proportions between ecotypes. While statistically significant due to large sample sizes, the biological magnitude is modest. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]

## Other caveats in this report

- The report assigns 1,820 genomes across 12 species to ecotypes and invokes a prior 172-species analysis. It says these species span 6 phyla but names only four; that discrepancy is unresolved. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- The prespecified adaptive-versus-housekeeping hypothesis received only partial support; adaptive COGs were V, P, G, E, Q, M and K, while housekeeping COGs were J, F, H and C. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- Housekeeping categories also differentiated frequently, so the significance-rate contrast was modest (1.16x); adaptive effect sizes were reported as 2.1x larger (one-sided Mann–Whitney U, p = 2.53 x 10^-6). [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- The report connects ecotype differentiation in category L to mobile-element genes and to a separate project's reported novel-versus-core enrichment of L (+10.88%) and V (+2.83%); this is a cross-project interpretation, not direct evidence of mechanism here. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- The large S-category effect points to an annotation gap: some ecotype-differentiating genes have unknown function. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]
