# Breadth of metal resistance, not gene depth, tracks ecological versatility

The report interprets the PGLS results as associating ecological versatility with breadth of metal resistance rather than depth, meaning many genes for few metals, because total AMR gene cluster count and core AMR fraction do not predict niche breadth. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

## Evidence

> Total AMR gene cluster count and core AMR fraction do not predict niche breadth in either the simple or multi-predictor PGLS models, indicating that the *breadth* of the metal resistance repertoire rather than its *depth* (many genes for few metals) is associated with ecological versatility. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

From the report [[summaries/microbeatlas_metal_ecology__REPORT]].

## Caveat

The cross-sectional design, using observational global survey data, means the direction of the association cannot be established. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

> The cross-sectional design of this study — comparing genera at a single snapshot in evolutionary time using observational global survey data — means the direction of this association cannot be established. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

## Other caveats in this report

- The report says its five robustness analyses only partially address its main caveats. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]
- The archaeal PGLS had 11% power at α = 0.05, so the report cautions that its non-significant result is not evidence against an association in archaea. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]
- The report attributes the strict prevalence result's loss of significance primarily to a 37% sample-size drop (606 → 379), with β changing from +0.0215 to +0.0166, a 23% reduction. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]
- The report states that the conservative 5% prevalence threshold excludes 89% of OTUs, many genuinely distributed across environments but uncommon within any one environment. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]
- The report proposes a 1–2% prevalence threshold as a reasonable middle ground for future niche-breadth analyses. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]
