# Core genes interpreted as an ancient conserved metabolic engine

The report interprets the core-enriched categories as an ancient, conserved metabolic engine involving translation, energy, and biosynthesis. [src: cog_analysis]

## Evidence

> - Core genes = ancient, conserved "metabolic engine" (translation, energy, biosynthesis) [src: cog_analysis]

From the report [[summaries/cog_analysis__REPORT]].

## Caveat

The 32-species sample may be insufficient to reveal phylum-specific patterns, qualifying the report's assertion of universality across bacterial phyla. [src: cog_analysis]

> - Analysis uses 32 species — a larger sample might reveal phylum-specific patterns [src: cog_analysis]

## Other caveats in this report

- The report proposes mobile defense islands as a possible interpretation of the LV signal, rather than demonstrating such modules directly. [src: cog_analysis]
- The report interprets novel genes as recent acquisitions potentially involved in ecological adaptation; acquisition timing and adaptive effects are not directly established by the category enrichments. [src: cog_analysis]
- The report asserts that horizontal gene transfer is the primary innovation mechanism, but the quoted category analysis alone does not directly test transfer against vertical inheritance. [src: cog_analysis]
- The +10.88% COG L enrichment suggests the hypothesis that mobile elements account for most genomic novelty; the enrichment is not a measured proportion of all genomic novelty. [src: cog_analysis]
- The report interprets the sampled cross-phyla pattern as universal and suggestive of deep evolutionary constraint. This extrapolation is in tension with its stated limitation that a larger sample might reveal phylum-specific patterns. [src: cog_analysis]
