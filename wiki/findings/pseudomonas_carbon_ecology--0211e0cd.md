# Carbon profiles alone cannot discriminate fine-grained environments among free-living species

Carbon profiles carry real ecological signal but are insufficient alone to discriminate fine-grained environment types among free-living species. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

## Evidence

> Carbon profiles carry real ecological signal but are insufficient alone to discriminate fine-grained environment types among free-living species. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

From the report [[summaries/pseudomonas_carbon_ecology__REPORT]].

## Caveat

The report hypothesizes that GapMind's 62 carbon pathways may be too coarse to capture niche-specific differences operating at the strain level within environmental species. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

> The modest predictive accuracy for fine-grained environment type (0.41) within free-living species is consistent with Guo et al. (2026), who found that hydrocarbon degradation genes are concentrated in the accessory genome and show strain-level rather than species-level variation. GapMind's 62 carbon pathways (Price et al. 2022) may be too coarse to capture the niche-specific metabolic differences that operate at the strain level within environmental species. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

## Other caveats in this report

- The principal pathway-profile axis separates subgenera, while lifestyle categories overlap within Pseudomonas_E; ecological interpretation is therefore limited by phylogenetic separation. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- Only 64.2% of genomes (8,171/12,732) had classifiable isolation sources; categories were assigned by keywords. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- Small classes, overlapping environments and coarse pathway resolution may explain the classifier's moderate accuracy. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- The report contrasts within-infection metabolic loss with a hypothesis of ancestral lineage-level streamlining; the timing remains an interpretation. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- Release from selection for plant-associated metabolism during an animal-host transition is an interpretation of the pathway-loss pattern, not a directly tested transition. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
