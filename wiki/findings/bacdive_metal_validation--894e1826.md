# Host-associated bacteria scored higher, reversing the predicted direction

Contrary to H1d, which predicted lower scores for host-associated than environmental bacteria, the data show the opposite: host-associated bacteria score slightly but significantly higher (d=+0.14, p<0.0001). [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

## Key figures

- **+0.14**: Cohen's d, host-associated bacteria (scope BacDive isolates) [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

## Evidence

> The data show the opposite: host-associated bacteria score slightly but significantly higher (d=+0.14, p<0.0001). [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

From the report [[summaries/bacdive_metal_validation__REPORT]].

## Caveat

The report proposes genome-size and metabolic-complexity confounding (Pseudomonas, Klebsiella, Acinetobacter pathogens with large genomes) as the explanation, but the comparison does not establish it. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

> This likely reflects genome size confounding: host-associated bacteria in BacDive are dominated by Pseudomonadota pathogens (e.g., *Pseudomonas*, *Klebsiella*, *Acinetobacter*) with large genomes and correspondingly more KEGG-annotated gene clusters, inflating the normalized metal score. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

## Other caveats in this report

- Positive metal utilizers had lower predicted scores, but the direction was nonsignificant and should not be over-interpreted. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- With n=10 heavy-metal isolates versus ~5,000 baseline isolates, the estimated minimum detectable effect at 80% power was approximately d=0.93. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- The observed d=1.00 was only slightly above the reported detection threshold. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- The plan referenced n=31 heavy-metal isolates, but only 10 matched scored pangenome species after bridging. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- The report attributes an 88% core estimate for metal-tolerance genes to the separate metal_specificity project; this is secondhand evidence here. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
