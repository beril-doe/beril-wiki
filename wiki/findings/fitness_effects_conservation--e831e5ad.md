# Essential genes are more often core than always-neutral genes

Across ~194,000 genes in 43 diverse bacteria, the report describes gene fitness importance and pangenome conservation as positively correlated, with essential genes 82% core and always-neutral genes 66% core, a 16-percentage-point gradient. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

## Key figures

- **82%**: core-gene frequency among essential genes (scope 43 bacteria) [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- **66%**: core-gene frequency among always-neutral genes (scope 43 bacteria) [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- **16-percentage-point percentage points**: gradient between essential and always-neutral genes (scope ~194,000 genes across 43 diverse bacteria) [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- **~194,000 genes**: genes spanned by the gradient (scope 43 diverse bacteria) [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

## Evidence

> The results paint a consistent picture: **gene fitness importance and pangenome conservation are positively correlated across the full spectrum**. Essential genes are 82% core; always-neutral genes are 66% core. This 16-percentage-point gradient spans ~194,000 genes across 43 diverse bacteria. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

From the report [[summaries/fitness_effects_conservation__REPORT]].

## Caveat

Despite the statistically robust 16pp gradient, conservation is only weakly predicted by fitness importance. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

> - The 16pp gradient, while statistically robust, means conservation is only weakly predicted by fitness importance [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

## Other caveats in this report

- Condition-dependent trade-offs are proposed as a possible explanation for beneficial effects of some core-gene deletions, not established as their cause. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- Participation in well-characterized pathways is offered only as a possible explanation for more measurable condition-specific effects among core genes. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- Greater detectability of conditional effects is proposed as a possible, rather than demonstrated, explanation for the core-gene enrichment. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- The report interprets larger effects in both directions among core genes as suggesting greater functional activity under laboratory conditions; the proposed pathway explanation is inferential. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- The assayed laboratory conditions favor rich media and standard stresses and do not represent many ecological niches. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
