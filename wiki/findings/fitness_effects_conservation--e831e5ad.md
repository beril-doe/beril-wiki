# Fitness importance and pangenome conservation correlate positively across the whole spectrum

The report finds that gene fitness importance and pangenome conservation are positively correlated across ~194,000 genes from 43 diverse bacteria, with essential genes 82% core and always-neutral genes 66% core, a 16-percentage-point gradient. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

## Key figures

- **82%**: share of essential genes that are core [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- **66%**: share of always-neutral genes that are core [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- **~194,000 genes**: genes spanned (scope 43 diverse bacteria) [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

## Evidence

> The results paint a consistent picture: **gene fitness importance and pangenome conservation are positively correlated across the full spectrum**. Essential genes are 82% core; always-neutral genes are 66% core. This 16-percentage-point gradient spans ~194,000 genes across 43 diverse bacteria. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

From the report [[summaries/fitness_effects_conservation__REPORT]].

## Caveat

The 16pp gradient, while statistically robust, means conservation is only weakly predicted by fitness importance. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

> - The 16pp gradient, while statistically robust, means conservation is only weakly predicted by fitness importance [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

## Other caveats in this report

- The laboratory conditions in the Fitness Browser analysis are biased toward rich media and standard stresses, leaving many ecological niches unrepresented. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- The Fitness Browser fitness measurements concern single-gene knockouts and do not capture epistatic interactions. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- Fitness Browser coverage of 43 bacteria, primarily Proteobacteria, limits the generalizability of the report's analysis. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- Singleton or novel genes may lack fitness data because of poor transposon coverage rather than true neutrality. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
