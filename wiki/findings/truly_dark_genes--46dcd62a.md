# Methanococcus strains account for over half of truly dark genes

Methanococcus strains (S2 and JJ) account for 55% of all truly dark genes, which the report attributes to underrepresentation of archaea in annotation databases. [src: truly_dark_genes]

## Key figures

- **55%**: share of truly dark genes from Methanococcus S2 and JJ [src: truly_dark_genes]

## Evidence

> Truly dark genes are concentrated in specific organisms: Methanococcus strains (S2 and JJ) account for 55% of all truly dark genes, reflecting the underrepresentation of archaea in annotation databases. [src: truly_dark_genes]

From the report [[summaries/truly_dark_genes__REPORT]].

## Caveat

Some bakta hypothetical-protein calls may be false negatives for genes with known functions that failed to match the PSC database version tested (v6.0). [src: truly_dark_genes]

> **Bakta false negatives**: Some "hypothetical protein" calls may be bakta false negatives — genes with known functions that didn't match bakta's PSC database at the version tested (v6.0). [src: truly_dark_genes]

## Other caveats in this report

- An additional 17,479 Fitness Browser dark genes lack pangenome links and could not be assessed. [src: truly_dark_genes]
- The report estimates ~2,841 additional truly dark genes among 17,479 unlinked dark genes at the 16.3% truly-dark rate, including 2,208 with strong fitness phenotypes. [src: truly_dark_genes]
- The ranked list covers ~69% of the estimated total truly dark gene population. [src: truly_dark_genes]
- The report could not assess 17,479 dark genes (31%) with bakta because they lack pangenome links; an estimated ~2,841 may be truly dark, representing a 31% coverage gap in the prioritized list. [src: truly_dark_genes]
- BBH orthologs cover only 32 of 48 Fitness Browser organisms, leaving truly dark genes from the remaining 16 organisms without concordance and ortholog-breadth data. [src: truly_dark_genes]
