# Genomic economy may explain apparent serine auxotrophy in Desulfovibrio vulgaris

The report proposes that apparent serine auxotrophy in Desulfovibrio vulgaris may represent genomic economy, losing energetically costly genes when their product is freely available. [src: essential_metabolome]

## Evidence

> This may represent **genomic economy** - losing genes that are energetically costly when the product is freely available [src: essential_metabolome]

From the report [[summaries/essential_metabolome__REPORT]].

## Caveat

The pathway may be present but use divergent enzymes that fall below GapMind's homology threshold. [src: essential_metabolome]

> The pathway may be present but use **divergent enzymes** that fall below GapMind's homology threshold [src: essential_metabolome]

## Other caveats in this report

- Escherichia coli genomes are completely absent from GapMind in the KBase pangenome collection. [src: essential_metabolome]
- The report's GapMind coverage limitation restricts the analysis to a pilot study with 7 organisms rather than the intended 45 Fitness Browser organisms with essential gene data. [src: essential_metabolome]
- Desulfovibrio vulgaris may possess a non-canonical serine biosynthesis pathway absent from GapMind's reference database. [src: essential_metabolome]
- Serine biosynthesis genes in Desulfovibrio vulgaris may be present but unannotated in the genome assembly. [src: essential_metabolome]
- The analyzed sample has limited phylogenetic diversity, consisting mostly of Proteobacteria plus 1 Deltaproteobacterium. [src: essential_metabolome]
