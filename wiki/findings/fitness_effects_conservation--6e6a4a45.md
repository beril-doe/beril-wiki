# Core genes are more often beneficial to delete than auxiliary genes

Counter to the expectation that accessory genes might be costly to carry, core genes are more likely to show positive fitness effects when deleted (24.4% ever beneficial vs 19.9% for auxiliary, OR=0.77 for auxiliary vs core). [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

## Key figures

- **24.4%**: core genes ever beneficial [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- **19.9%**: auxiliary genes ever beneficial [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- **OR=0.77**: odds ratio for auxiliary vs core [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

## Evidence

> Counter to the expectation that accessory genes might be costly to carry, **core genes are MORE likely to show positive fitness effects** when deleted (24.4% ever beneficial vs 19.9% for auxiliary, OR=0.77 for auxiliary vs core). [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

## Caveat

The Fitness Browser covers 43 bacteria, primarily Proteobacteria, limiting generalizability. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

> - The Fitness Browser covers 43 bacteria -- primarily Proteobacteria -- limiting generalizability [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

## Other caveats in this report

- The laboratory conditions in the Fitness Browser analysis are biased toward rich media and standard stresses, leaving many ecological niches unrepresented. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- The report states that its statistically robust 16pp gradient nevertheless means fitness importance only weakly predicts conservation. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- The Fitness Browser fitness measurements concern single-gene knockouts and do not capture epistatic interactions. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- Singleton or novel genes may lack fitness data because of poor transposon coverage rather than true neutrality. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
