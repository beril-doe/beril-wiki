# Core genes are more often beneficial to delete than auxiliary genes

Counter to the expectation that accessory genes might be costly to carry, deletion is ever beneficial for 24.4% of core genes versus 19.9% of auxiliary genes (OR=0.77 for auxiliary vs core). [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

## Key figures

- **24.4%**: core genes ever beneficial when deleted (scope core genes) [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- **19.9%**: auxiliary genes ever beneficial when deleted (scope auxiliary genes) [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- **0.77**: odds ratio for auxiliary vs core [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

## Evidence

> Counter to the expectation that accessory genes might be costly to carry, **core genes are MORE likely to show positive fitness effects** when deleted (24.4% ever beneficial vs 19.9% for auxiliary, OR=0.77 for auxiliary vs core). [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

From the report [[summaries/fitness_effects_conservation__REPORT]].

## Caveat

Condition-dependent trade-offs are proposed as a possible explanation for beneficial effects of some core-gene deletions, not established as their cause. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

> This may reflect that core genes are more likely to participate in trade-off situations -- they help in some conditions but cost in others. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

## Other caveats in this report

- Participation in well-characterized pathways is offered only as a possible explanation for more measurable condition-specific effects among core genes. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- Greater detectability of conditional effects is proposed as a possible, rather than demonstrated, explanation for the core-gene enrichment. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- The report interprets larger effects in both directions among core genes as suggesting greater functional activity under laboratory conditions; the proposed pathway explanation is inferential. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- The assayed laboratory conditions favor rich media and standard stresses and do not represent many ecological niches. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- Despite the statistically robust 16pp gradient, fitness importance weakly predicts conservation. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
