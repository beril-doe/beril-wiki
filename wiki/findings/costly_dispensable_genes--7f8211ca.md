# Costly dispensable genes are not inert: some have condition-specific phenotypes

14.1% of costly+dispensable genes have condition-specific phenotypes (vs 16.7% for costly+conserved and 2.7% for neutral+dispensable), which the report suggests means these genes can affect fitness under specific conditions and may slow their loss from the genome. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

## Key figures

- **14.1%**: costly+dispensable genes with condition-specific phenotypes (denominator costly+dispensable genes) [src: costly_dispensable_genes]
- **16.7%**: costly+conserved genes with condition-specific phenotypes (denominator costly+conserved genes) [src: costly_dispensable_genes]
- **2.7%**: neutral+dispensable genes with condition-specific phenotypes (denominator neutral+dispensable genes) [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

## Evidence

> Despite being burdensome and non-conserved, 14.1% of costly+dispensable genes have condition-specific phenotypes (vs 16.7% for costly+conserved). This is much higher than neutral+dispensable genes (2.7%), suggesting these genes are not inert -- they can affect fitness under specific conditions, which may slow their loss from the genome.
