# Human-associated environments enriched for complete prophages and anti-defense genes beyond phylogeny

Human-associated and clinical environments show significantly higher prevalence of structurally complete prophages (tail, head morphogenesis modules) and anti-defense genes than freshwater, marine, and animal-associated environments, after controlling for host family composition and genome size. [src: prophage_ecology]

## Evidence

> Human-associated and clinical environments show significantly higher prevalence of structurally complete prophages (tail, head morphogenesis modules) and anti-defense genes compared to freshwater, marine, and animal-associated environments. This holds after controlling for host family composition and genome size. [src: prophage_ecology]

From the report [[summaries/prophage_ecology__REPORT]].

## Caveat

NCBI isolation_source metadata is sparse and inconsistently labeled, and the classification into 10 categories collapses substantial within-category variation. [src: prophage_ecology]

> NCBI isolation_source metadata is sparse and inconsistently labeled. Our classification into 10 categories collapses substantial within-category variation. [src: prophage_ecology]

## Other caveats in this report

- The report suggests packaging, lysis, and lysogenic regulation modules likely include many domesticated prophage remnants, defective prophages under purifying selection. [src: prophage_ecology]
- The report considers prophage module definitions validated for core backbone modules A, D, and F but less validated for accessory functions E and G. [src: prophage_ecology]
- The report attributes its higher prophage prevalence likely to identifying individual prophage-associated gene clusters, including domesticated remnants, rather than intact prophages. [src: prophage_ecology]
- Annotation-based identification likely inflates prophage prevalence by including domesticated remnants and bacterial homologs of phage genes, and its false positive rate is uncharacterized. [src: prophage_ecology]
- Residual genome-size confounding cannot be excluded despite control through stratification and partial correlations, since larger genomes have more genes of all types. [src: prophage_ecology]
