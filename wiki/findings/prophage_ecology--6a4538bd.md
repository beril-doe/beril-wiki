# Genome size dominates prophage content, ahead of environment and phylogeny

Genome size is the dominant predictor of prophage composition (rho=0.717 with prophage cluster count), followed by environment, then phylogeny. [src: prophage_ecology]

## Key figures

- **0.717 rho**: Spearman rho of genome size with prophage cluster count [src: prophage_ecology]

## Evidence

> Genome size is the dominant predictor (rho=0.717 with prophage cluster count), followed by environment, then phylogeny. [src: prophage_ecology]

From the report [[summaries/prophage_ecology__REPORT]].

## Caveat

Residual genome-size confounding cannot be excluded despite stratification and partial correlations, since larger genomes have more genes of all types. [src: prophage_ecology]

> Genome size is the dominant predictor of prophage burden (rho=0.717), and while we control for it via stratification and partial correlations, residual confounding cannot be excluded. Larger genomes have more genes of all types, not just prophage genes. [src: prophage_ecology]

## Other caveats in this report

- The report suggests packaging, lysis, and lysogenic regulation modules likely include many domesticated prophage remnants, defective prophages under purifying selection. [src: prophage_ecology]
- The report considers prophage module definitions validated for core backbone modules A, D, and F but less validated for accessory functions E and G. [src: prophage_ecology]
- The report attributes its higher prophage prevalence likely to identifying individual prophage-associated gene clusters, including domesticated remnants, rather than intact prophages. [src: prophage_ecology]
- Annotation-based identification likely inflates prophage prevalence by including domesticated remnants and bacterial homologs of phage genes, and its false positive rate is uncharacterized. [src: prophage_ecology]
- Sparse and inconsistently labeled NCBI isolation_source metadata and classification into 10 categories collapse substantial within-category environmental variation. [src: prophage_ecology]
