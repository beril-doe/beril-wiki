# Metal–COG relationships differ among soil, marine and wastewater biomes

Biome-stratified PGLS shows soil, marine, and wastewater have distinct metal-COG relationships, indicating environment-specific functional responses rather than a universal resistance programme. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]

## Evidence

> - **Biome-stratified PGLS**: soil, marine, and wastewater show distinct metal-COG relationships, indicating environment-specific functional responses rather than a universal resistance programme. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]

From the report [[summaries/soil_metal_functional_genomics__REPORT]].

## Caveat

All associations are observational, and co-variation of Cr, Cu, Pb and Zn in industrial soils confounds whether COG-metal associations are metal-specific or a generic multi-metal stress response, which remains unresolved. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]

> However, all associations are observational. Co-contamination by multiple metals is a significant confound: Cr, Cu, Pb, Zn co-vary in many industrial soils. Whether individual COG-metal associations are metal-specific or reflect a generic stress response to multi-metal contamination is unresolved (NB05 partial correlation models). [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]

## Other caveats in this report

- The report is preliminary: Spearman correlation, db-RDA and PGLS analyses were complete, while effect-size and spatial validation remained pending. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]
- Because db-RDA conditioned on project accession, R² = 0.799 concerns residual rather than necessarily total COG variance. The unconditional metals-only R² was not reported and could be substantially lower; the report calls for both values alongside the conditional figure. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]
- The report flags a high 60% discovery rate from 2,355 significant associations among 3,915 tests (9 metals × 435 COGs) in 51,748 samples. It argues that metal co-contamination and dependent tests may make its reported Benjamini–Hochberg FDR anti-conservative, and calls for a partial-correlation model. This is the report's methodological assessment, not a validated recalculation of FDR. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]
- A 10 km sample-to-genome matching threshold may not establish site co-location; the report requires sensitivity analyses at 5 km and 20 km before attributing COG–copper associations to measured sites. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]
- A pending effect-size audit would examine the ρ distribution across 2,355 associations and flag ρ < 0.05. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]
