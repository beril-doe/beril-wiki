# Uncharacterized gene families show conserved metal phenotypes across organisms

149 novel metal biology candidates were identified as gene families with conserved metal fitness phenotypes across ≥2 organisms but lacking full functional annotation: 89 truly unknown, 43 with DUF/UPF domains, and 17 with partial functional hints, with predictions based solely on cross-species fitness data. [src: metal_fitness_atlas]

## Key figures

- **149 families**: candidate gene families with conserved metal phenotypes but incomplete annotation (scope conserved across ≥2 organisms) [src: metal_fitness_atlas]
- **89 families**: truly unknown families [src: metal_fitness_atlas]
- **43 families**: families with DUF/UPF domains [src: metal_fitness_atlas]
- **17 families**: families with partial functional hints [src: metal_fitness_atlas]

## Evidence

> 149 novel metal biology candidates were identified: gene families with conserved metal fitness phenotypes across ≥2 organisms but lacking full functional annotation. These break down into three categories: 89 truly unknown (no annotation), 43 with DUF/UPF domains (known structural domain, unknown metal role), and 17 with partial functional hints (e.g., "transporter", "hydrolase") but uncharacterized metal function. These represent function predictions for genes of unknown function based solely on cross-species fitness data. [src: metal_fitness_atlas]

From the report [[summaries/metal_fitness_atlas__REPORT]].

## Caveat

Direct validation of the 149 novel candidates is prospective and conditional on proposed experiments, not an observed result. [src: metal_fitness_atlas]

> If these experiments were performed, the atlas would expand from 14 to 16+ metals (adding REEs), from 24 to 27+ organisms with fitness data, and from 1-2 organisms for critical metals to 5+. The cross-species statistical power for manganese, chromium, uranium, and tungsten would increase from suggestive to definitive. The 149 novel gene candidates could be directly validated in organisms where they are predicted to be important. [src: metal_fitness_atlas]

## Other caveats in this report

- Excluding 4 duplicate P. fluorescens FW300 strains reduces the analysis from 22 to 18 organisms while retaining core enrichment (OR=2.065, p=5.9e-141 versus OR=2.083, p=4.3e-162). [src: metal_fitness_atlas]
- Conservation analysis covers 22 of 31 metal-tested organisms (71%); 9 lack Fitness Browser–pangenome links. [src: metal_fitness_atlas]
- Without genome-size normalization, K. pneumoniae and P. aeruginosa dominate scores because of large open pangenomes rather than metal biology. [src: metal_fitness_atlas]
- The 31 organisms with metal data include only 24 with fitness matrices; the experiment-coverage scope is broader than the gene-fitness dataset. [src: metal_fitness_atlas]
- The prior DvH condition-specific heavy-metal analysis found 71.2% core; this atlas includes all genes with metal fitness defects, a broader definition that explains the differing conservation result. [src: metal_fitness_atlas]
