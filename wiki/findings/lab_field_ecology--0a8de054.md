# Aggregate lab metal tolerance does not significantly predict field uranium response

The correlation between lab-derived metal tolerance score and the high-uranium/low-uranium field abundance ratio is suggestive but not significant (Spearman rho=0.503, p=0.095, n=12 genera). [src: lab_field_ecology]

## Key figures

- **0.503**: Spearman rho between lab metal tolerance score and high-U/low-U field abundance ratio (scope 12 genera) [src: lab_field_ecology]
- **0.095**: p-value [src: lab_field_ecology]
- **12 genera**: genera in the analysis [src: lab_field_ecology]

## Evidence

> The correlation between lab-derived metal tolerance score (negative mean fitness under stress, higher = more tolerant) and the high-uranium/low-uranium field abundance ratio is suggestive but not significant (Spearman rho=0.503, p=0.095, n=12 genera). [src: lab_field_ecology]

From the report [[summaries/lab_field_ecology__REPORT]].

## Caveat

The trend is in the predicted direction, but statistical power is limited by the small number of genera. [src: lab_field_ecology]

> The trend is in the predicted direction -- genera with higher lab tolerance tend to have higher abundance at contaminated sites -- but statistical power is limited by the small number of genera. [src: lab_field_ecology]

## Other caveats in this report

- Shewanella, Dechlorosoma, and Marinobacter were excluded from correlation analysis because they occurred at <10 sites. [src: lab_field_ecology]
- The report cautions that the community shift is not simply enrichment of metal-tolerant organisms; other ecological changes may matter. [src: lab_field_ecology]
- Differences in pH, redox potential, carbon sources, sulfate, nitrate, and other conditions may decouple lab metal tolerance from field abundance. [src: lab_field_ecology]
- Community interactions may decouple single-organism lab fitness from field abundance. [src: lab_field_ecology]
- Genus-level 16S resolution cannot directly match Fitness Browser strains. [src: lab_field_ecology]
