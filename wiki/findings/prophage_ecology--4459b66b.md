# Module-level environment effects without lineage-level effects suggest modular exchange

Modules show environment effects beyond phylogeny but TerL lineages do not, which suggests modular exchange rather than whole-phage adaptation to environments. [src: prophage_ecology]

## Evidence

> **Module-level and lineage-level ecology are decoupled** — modules show environment effects beyond phylogeny, but lineages do not, suggesting modular exchange rather than whole-phage adaptation to environments [src: prophage_ecology]

From the report [[summaries/prophage_ecology__REPORT]].

## Caveat

Annotation-based identification likely inflates prophage prevalence by including domesticated remnants and bacterial homologs of phage genes, and the false positive rate is uncharacterized. [src: prophage_ecology]

> This likely inflates prophage prevalence by including domesticated remnants and bacterial homologs of phage genes (e.g., bacterial integrases). The false positive rate is uncharacterized. [src: prophage_ecology]

## Other caveats in this report

- The report suggests packaging, lysis, and lysogenic regulation modules likely include many domesticated prophage remnants, defective prophages under purifying selection. [src: prophage_ecology]
- The report considers prophage module definitions validated for core backbone modules A, D, and F but less validated for accessory functions E and G. [src: prophage_ecology]
- The report attributes its higher prophage prevalence likely to identifying individual prophage-associated gene clusters, including domesticated remnants, rather than intact prophages. [src: prophage_ecology]
- Residual genome-size confounding cannot be excluded despite control through stratification and partial correlations, since larger genomes have more genes of all types. [src: prophage_ecology]
- Sparse and inconsistently labeled NCBI isolation_source metadata and classification into 10 categories collapse substantial within-category environmental variation. [src: prophage_ecology]
