# Amino acid prototrophy may be the ancestral state in free-living bacteria

The report interprets complete biosynthesis pathways for all 18 amino acids in 6 of 7 organisms (85.7%) as suggesting that amino acid prototrophy is the ancestral state for free-living bacteria. [src: essential_metabolome]

## Key figures

- **6 of 7 organisms**: organisms with all 18 amino acid pathways complete (denominator 7 organisms; scope GapMind sample) [src: essential_metabolome]
- **85.7%**: share of organisms with complete pathways (denominator 7 organisms; scope GapMind sample) [src: essential_metabolome]

## Evidence

> The finding that **6 of 7 organisms** (85.7%) possess complete biosynthesis pathways for all 18 amino acids suggests that **amino acid prototrophy is the ancestral state** for free-living bacteria. [src: essential_metabolome]

From the report [[summaries/essential_metabolome__REPORT]].

## Caveat

The sample has limited phylogenetic diversity (mostly Proteobacteria + 1 Deltaproteobacterium). [src: essential_metabolome]

> Limited phylogenetic diversity (mostly Proteobacteria + 1 Deltaproteobacterium) [src: essential_metabolome]

## Other caveats in this report

- Escherichia coli genomes are completely absent from GapMind in the KBase pangenome collection. [src: essential_metabolome]
- The report's GapMind coverage limitation restricts the analysis to a pilot study with 7 organisms rather than the intended 45 Fitness Browser organisms with essential gene data. [src: essential_metabolome]
- Desulfovibrio vulgaris may possess a non-canonical serine biosynthesis pathway absent from GapMind's reference database. [src: essential_metabolome]
- The serine biosynthesis pathway in Desulfovibrio vulgaris may use divergent enzymes below GapMind's homology threshold. [src: essential_metabolome]
- Serine biosynthesis genes in Desulfovibrio vulgaris may be present but unannotated in the genome assembly. [src: essential_metabolome]
