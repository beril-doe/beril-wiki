# Carbon-cycling genes enriched among warming-increased DNA functions in organic horizon only

Carbon-cycling KOs were enriched among heated-up DNA-organic KOs (5 / 57 versus 428 / 12,806; Fisher OR=2.78, one-sided p=0.042), whereas DNA-mineral had 0 / 57 versus 2 / 12,806 and RNA pools had 0 / 57 hits. [src: harvard_forest_warming]

## Key figures

- **2.78 odds ratio**: Fisher odds ratio for carbon-cycling KO enrichment among heated-up KOs (denominator 5 / 57 versus 428 / 12,806; scope DNA × organic) [src: harvard_forest_warming]
- **0.042**: one-sided Fisher p-value (scope DNA × organic) [src: harvard_forest_warming]

## Caveat

All organic-horizon DNA samples were lab-incubated and direct organic DNA was not produced, so incubation cannot be cleanly factored out of the DNA-organic analysis. [src: harvard_forest_warming]

> - **Organic-horizon DNA samples are all lab-incubated**; direct organic DNA was not produced by the NMDC pipeline. This makes it impossible to factor incubation cleanly out of the DNA pool's organic-horizon analysis. [src: harvard_forest_warming]

## Other caveats in this report

- The analyses used nmdc_metadata and nmdc_results, excluding nmdc_arkin. [src: harvard_forest_warming]
- The paired n=25 analysis appeared to favor DNA (pseudo-F 3.12, R²=11.9%, p=0.020) over RNA (pseudo-F 0.76, R²=3.2%, p=0.60), but organic samples were all incubated and mineral samples all direct, confounding horizon with incubation; H1 was not supported. [src: harvard_forest_warming]
- The report proposes, rather than demonstrates, long-term compositional turnover as a possible reason DNA and RNA warming sensitivities converge after roughly 25 years of heating. [src: harvard_forest_warming]
- The single collection date, 2017-05-24, may leave RNA subject to diurnal and microspatial variation, weakening its signal of chronic +5°C warming. [src: harvard_forest_warming]
- The metatranscriptome recovered 14,302 distinct KOs versus 12,863 in the metagenome; the report proposes that this richer repertoire could dilute treatment signals under relative-abundance analysis. [src: harvard_forest_warming]
