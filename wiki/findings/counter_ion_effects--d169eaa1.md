# Core-genome enrichment in the Metal Fitness Atlas survives shared-stress removal

After removing the ~40% shared-stress genes and restricting to metal-specific genes, the core genome enrichment is fully preserved for 12 of 14 metals. [src: counter_ion_effects]

## Key figures

- **12 of 14 metals**: metals with core enrichment fully preserved [src: counter_ion_effects]
- **~40%**: shared-stress genes removed [src: counter_ion_effects]

## Evidence

> After removing the ~40% shared-stress genes and restricting to metal-specific genes, the core genome enrichment is fully preserved for 12 of 14 metals. [src: counter_ion_effects]

From the report [[summaries/counter_ion_effects__REPORT]].

## Caveat

Cadmium and Iron have very low gene counts (n=92 and n=9 respectively, each from a single organism) and their corrected deltas are statistically unreliable. [src: counter_ion_effects]

> Cadmium and Iron have very low gene counts (n=92 and n=9 respectively, each from a single organism) and should be interpreted with caution — their corrected deltas are statistically unreliable. [src: counter_ion_effects]

## Other caveats in this report

- Synechococcus elongatus (SynE) was a shared-stress outlier at 88.6% (565/638 genes), driven by 12 NaCl dose-response experiments spanning 0.5–250 mM, compared with 1–6 NaCl experiments for other organisms. [src: counter_ion_effects]
- The n_sick >= 1 threshold was easier to satisfy for SynE because it had 12 experiments. [src: counter_ion_effects]
- The cadmium enrichment analysis had very low statistical power because it included only 92 original genes from 1 organism. [src: counter_ion_effects]
- The psRCH2 copper salt comparison was severely confounded by aerobic versus anaerobic growth, with hundreds of genes differing independently of copper. [src: counter_ion_effects]
- Manganese conservation analysis is low-powered (n=30, 1 org), although the report considers its 100% core result robust by ceiling effect. [src: counter_ion_effects]
