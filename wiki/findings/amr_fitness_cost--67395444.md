# Resistance mechanism predicts conservation status though not fitness cost

While mechanism does not predict fitness cost, the report states that it strongly predicts conservation status (χ² = 69.3, p = 1.4×10⁻¹³). [src: amr_fitness_cost]

## Key figures

- **69.3**: χ² for mechanism versus conservation status [src: amr_fitness_cost]
- **1.4×10⁻¹³**: p-value of the χ² test [src: amr_fitness_cost]

## Evidence

> While mechanism doesn't predict fitness cost, it strongly predicts conservation status (χ² = 69.3, p = 1.4×10⁻¹³). [src: amr_fitness_cost]

From the report [[summaries/amr_fitness_cost__REPORT]].

## Caveat

Most FB species have few genomes in GTDB (median 9, range 2–399), making the ≥95% prevalence threshold for core designation imprecise, especially with 2–9 genomes. [src: amr_fitness_cost]

> An important caveat: most FB species have few genomes in GTDB (median 9, range 2–399), which makes the ≥95% prevalence threshold for "core" designation imprecise. With only 2–9 genomes, a gene present in all 9 is labeled "core" but could be accessory at larger sampling depth. [src: amr_fitness_cost]

## Other caveats in this report

- The report argues that low AMR putative essentiality reduces concern about strong right-censoring. [src: amr_fitness_cost]
- The report proposes antibiotic mismatch, rather than nonfunction, as an explanation for the 43% of AMR genes without a flip. [src: amr_fitness_cost]
- Small per-class samples and organism heterogeneity are proposed, not established, explanations for the class-matched null result. [src: amr_fitness_cost]
- Compensatory evolution as the explanation for uniform mechanism costs is a hypothesis, not a direct measurement. [src: amr_fitness_cost]
- An irreducible gene-maintenance cost floor is speculative. [src: amr_fitness_cost]
