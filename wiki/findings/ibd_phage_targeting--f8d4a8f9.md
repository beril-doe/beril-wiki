# Paired data argue against lactate cross-feeding with A. caccae

Lactate correlations had opposite signs for A. caccae (+0.18) versus F. plautii (−0.23) and E. bolteae (−0.20), which the report calls the strongest evidence against the cross-feeding hypothesis. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

## Key figures

- **+0.18**: lactate × A. caccae correlation [src: ibd_phage_targeting]
- **−0.23**: lactate × F. plautii correlation [src: ibd_phage_targeting]
- **−0.20**: lactate × E. bolteae correlation [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

## Evidence

> 2. **Lactate × A. caccae = +0.18\*** **vs lactate × F. plautii = −0.23\*** and × E. bolteae = −0.20\* — **opposite signs**. If lactate were a cross-feeding intermediate (pathobiont produces, *A. caccae* consumes), we would expect same-sign positive (both increase together) or asymmetric same-sign (production-consumption coupling). The **opposite-sign pattern is the strongest evidence against the cross-feeding hypothesis** — *A. caccae* and the metabolic-coupling-candidate pathobionts occupy **different metabolic niches** at the cohort level.
