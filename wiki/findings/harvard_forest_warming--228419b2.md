# Heated mineral soil has fewer detectable metabolites

Heated mineral samples at Harvard Forest have fewer detectable metabolites: 155 vs 167 ChEBI per sample, MW p=0.012. [src: harvard_forest_warming]

## Key figures

- **155 ChEBI per sample**: mean ChEBI count, heated mineral [src: harvard_forest_warming]
- **167 ChEBI per sample**: mean ChEBI count, control mineral [src: harvard_forest_warming]
- **0.012**: Mann-Whitney p [src: harvard_forest_warming]

## Evidence

> Heated mineral samples also have **fewer detectable metabolites** (155 vs 167 ChEBI per sample, MW p=0.012). [src: harvard_forest_warming]

From the report [[summaries/harvard_forest_warming__REPORT]].

## Caveat

The report has no quantitative metabolomics, NOM, or proteomics because it excludes nmdc_arkin tables, though equivalent layers exist in nmdc_arkin.{nom_gold, metabolomics_gold, proteomics_gold}. [src: harvard_forest_warming]

> - **No quantitative metabolomics, NOM, or proteomics** because the project excludes `nmdc_arkin` tables. Equivalent quantitative layers exist in `nmdc_arkin.{nom_gold, metabolomics_gold, proteomics_gold}` and could be added if scope expands. [src: harvard_forest_warming]

## Other caveats in this report

- The Harvard Forest paired DNA/RNA subset confounds horizon with incubation because every organic sample is incubated and every mineral sample is direct. [src: harvard_forest_warming]
- The report proposes that long-term compositional turnover could explain the comparable DNA and RNA warming responses at Harvard Forest, with DNA genome content reflecting warming-selected lineages. [src: harvard_forest_warming]
- All Harvard Forest samples were collected on a single date (2017-05-24), and the report proposes diurnal and microspatial transcript noise as a possible explanation for H1's failure. [src: harvard_forest_warming]
- The report argues that the Harvard Forest DNA pool integrates over years and is a cleaner readout of the chronic +5°C signal in this design. [src: harvard_forest_warming]
- The report proposes that the richer rare-organism functional repertoire in Harvard Forest RNA increases per-KO variance under relative-abundance analysis and reduces power to detect treatment shifts. [src: harvard_forest_warming]
