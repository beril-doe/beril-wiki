# Integration genes do not co-occur with the prophage backbone

## Other caveats in this report

- The report suggests packaging, lysis, and lysogenic regulation modules likely include many domesticated prophage remnants, defective prophages under purifying selection. [src: prophage_ecology]
- The report considers prophage module definitions validated for core backbone modules A, D, and F but less validated for accessory functions E and G. [src: prophage_ecology]
- The report attributes its higher prophage prevalence likely to identifying individual prophage-associated gene clusters, including domesticated remnants, rather than intact prophages. [src: prophage_ecology]
- Annotation-based identification likely inflates prophage prevalence by including domesticated remnants and bacterial homologs of phage genes, and its false positive rate is uncharacterized. [src: prophage_ecology]
- Residual genome-size confounding cannot be excluded despite control through stratification and partial correlations, since larger genomes have more genes of all types. [src: prophage_ecology]
