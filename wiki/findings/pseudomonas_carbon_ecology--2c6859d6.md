# Carbon pathway profiles associate with isolation environment among free-living and plant-associated species

Among 54 free-living and plant-associated species (>=5 genomes, >=60% majority environment agreement), carbon pathway profiles are significantly associated with isolation environment in a PERMANOVA-like permutation test (999 permutations) with p = 0.006, with between-group mean distance (2.054) exceeding within-group mean distance (1.890). [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

## Caveat

Carbon profiles carry real ecological signal but are insufficient alone to discriminate fine-grained environment types among free-living species. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

## Other caveats in this report

- The report describes Pseudomonas carbon pathway profiles as not strongly predictive of isolation environment despite carrying ecological signal. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- Classifiable isolation source metadata was available for 64.2% of the Pseudomonas genomes (8,171/12,732). [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- The report attributes moderate ecology-classification accuracy in Pseudomonas to small class sizes, overlapping environment types such as soil and rhizosphere, and the coarse resolution of 62 GapMind pathways. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- GapMind's 62 carbon pathways (Price et al. 2022) may be too coarse to capture strain-level niche-specific metabolic differences within environmental Pseudomonas species. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- Pseudomonas aeruginosa comprises 53% of all genomes (6,760/12,732) due to clinical importance, whereas many environmental species have <10 sequenced genomes, affecting power of species-level comparisons. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
