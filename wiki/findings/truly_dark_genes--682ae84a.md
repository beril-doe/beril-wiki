# Truly dark genes are nearly invisible to cross-organism analysis

Only 3 of 65 dark-gene ortholog groups with cross-organism concordance data contain truly dark genes. [src: truly_dark_genes]

## Key figures

- **3**: ortholog groups containing truly dark genes (denominator 65 dark-gene ortholog groups with concordance data) [src: truly_dark_genes]

## Evidence

> Only 3 of 65 dark-gene ortholog groups with cross-organism concordance data contain truly dark genes — they are nearly invisible to cross-organism analysis. [src: truly_dark_genes]

From the report [[summaries/truly_dark_genes__REPORT]].

## Caveat

BBH orthologs cover only 32 of 48 FB organisms, so truly dark genes from the remaining 16 organisms lack concordance and ortholog breadth data. [src: truly_dark_genes]

> **Ortholog coverage**: BBH orthologs cover only 32 of 48 FB organisms. Truly dark genes from the remaining 16 organisms lack concordance and ortholog breadth data. [src: truly_dark_genes]

## Other caveats in this report

- An additional 17,479 Fitness Browser dark genes lack pangenome links and could not be assessed. [src: truly_dark_genes]
- The report estimates ~2,841 additional truly dark genes among 17,479 unlinked dark genes at the 16.3% truly-dark rate, including 2,208 with strong fitness phenotypes. [src: truly_dark_genes]
- The ranked list covers ~69% of the estimated total truly dark gene population. [src: truly_dark_genes]
- The report could not assess 17,479 dark genes (31%) with bakta because they lack pangenome links; an estimated ~2,841 may be truly dark, representing a 31% coverage gap in the prioritized list. [src: truly_dark_genes]
- Some bakta hypothetical-protein calls may be false negatives for genes with known functions that failed to match the PSC database version tested (v6.0). [src: truly_dark_genes]
