# Chloride-free zinc sulfate shows more NaCl overlap than chloride metals

Zinc sulfate (0 mM Cl⁻) shows 44.6% NaCl overlap, higher than most chloride-delivered metals including cobalt (41.3%, up to 500 mM Cl⁻), copper (41.0%), and nickel (39.3%). [src: counter_ion_effects]

## Key figures

- **44.6%**: zinc sulfate NaCl overlap [src: counter_ion_effects]
- **41.3%**: cobalt NaCl overlap [src: counter_ion_effects]
- **41.0%**: copper NaCl overlap [src: counter_ion_effects]
- **39.3%**: nickel NaCl overlap [src: counter_ion_effects]

## Caveat

Manganese, cadmium, selenium, mercury, iron, molybdenum, and tungsten were tested in only 1 organism (DvH), so their overlap statistics have no cross-organism replication. [src: counter_ion_effects]

> - **Single-organism metals**: Manganese, cadmium, selenium, mercury, iron, molybdenum, and tungsten are tested in only 1 organism (DvH). Their overlap statistics have no cross-organism replication. [src: counter_ion_effects]

## Other caveats in this report

- Synechococcus elongatus (SynE) was a shared-stress outlier at 88.6% (565/638 genes), driven by 12 NaCl dose-response experiments spanning 0.5–250 mM, compared with 1–6 NaCl experiments for other organisms. [src: counter_ion_effects]
- The n_sick >= 1 threshold was easier to satisfy for SynE because it had 12 experiments. [src: counter_ion_effects]
- The cadmium enrichment analysis had very low statistical power because it included only 92 original genes from 1 organism. [src: counter_ion_effects]
- The psRCH2 copper salt comparison was severely confounded by aerobic versus anaerobic growth, with hundreds of genes differing independently of copper. [src: counter_ion_effects]
- The report warns that corrected enrichment deltas for cadmium (n=92) and iron (n=9), each from a single organism, are statistically unreliable because of very low gene counts. [src: counter_ion_effects]
