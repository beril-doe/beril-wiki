# No greater warming sensitivity of the transcript pool than the DNA pool

After removing the horizon-by-incubation confound, DNA and RNA treatment R² were comparable at 10-13%, so the premise that the transcript pool is more sensitive is not supported in this dataset. [src: harvard_forest_warming]

## Key figures

- **10-13%**: treatment R² in DNA and RNA pools in direct-sample sensitivity analysis (scope direct samples only) [src: harvard_forest_warming]

## Evidence

> DNA and RNA pools show **comparable** treatment R² (10-13%). H1's premise that the transcript pool is more sensitive is not supported in this dataset. [src: harvard_forest_warming]

From the report [[summaries/harvard_forest_warming__REPORT]].

## Caveat

The paired n=25 analysis appeared to favor DNA (pseudo-F 3.12, R²=11.9%, p=0.020) over RNA (pseudo-F 0.76, R²=3.2%, p=0.60), but organic samples were all incubated and mineral all direct, confounding horizon with incubation. [src: harvard_forest_warming]

> The originally proposed H1 (RNA composition shifts more than DNA under warming) is **not supported** once a horizon × incubation confound is removed. In the paired n=25 subset (samples with both DNA and RNA), PERMANOVA pseudo-F for treatment was 3.12 in DNA (R²=11.9%, p=0.020) vs 0.76 in RNA (R²=3.2%, p=0.60), seemingly contradicting H1 in the opposite direction. However, the paired subset confounds horizon with incubation (every organic sample is incubated, every mineral sample is direct). [src: harvard_forest_warming]

## Other caveats in this report

- The analyses used nmdc_metadata and nmdc_results, excluding nmdc_arkin. [src: harvard_forest_warming]
- The report proposes, rather than demonstrates, long-term compositional turnover as a possible reason DNA and RNA warming sensitivities converge after roughly 25 years of heating. [src: harvard_forest_warming]
- The single collection date, 2017-05-24, may leave RNA subject to diurnal and microspatial variation, weakening its signal of chronic +5°C warming. [src: harvard_forest_warming]
- The metatranscriptome recovered 14,302 distinct KOs versus 12,863 in the metagenome; the report proposes that this richer repertoire could dilute treatment signals under relative-abundance analysis. [src: harvard_forest_warming]
- Substrate depletion and global transcript down-regulation are offered as hypothetical explanations, not measured mechanisms, for the absence of a dominant RNA treatment axis. [src: harvard_forest_warming]
