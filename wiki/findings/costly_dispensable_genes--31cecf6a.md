# Costly dispensable genes are strongly enriched for mobile-element keywords

Costly+dispensable genes are 7.45x more likely to contain mobile element keywords (transposase, integrase, phage, IS element, recombinase, prophage) in their descriptions (OR=7.45, p=4.6e-71). [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

## Key figures

- **7.45 odds ratio**: odds ratio for mobile element keywords in descriptions of costly+dispensable genes (scope costly+dispensable genes) [src: costly_dispensable_genes]
- **4.6e-71**: p-value for the enrichment [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

## Evidence

> They are **7.45x more likely** to contain mobile element keywords in their descriptions (transposase, integrase, phage, IS element, recombinase, prophage; OR=7.45, p=4.6e-71). [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

## Caveat

The interpretation of these genes as HGT-associated genomic debris (insertion sequences, prophage remnants, transposases, defense systems) is interpretation rather than direct provenance measurement. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

> The costly+dispensable genes represent the **genomic debris of horizontal gene transfer** -- insertion sequences, prophage remnants, transposases, and defense systems that impose metabolic cost on their host and are not conserved across the species pangenome. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

## Other caveats in this report

- The singleton contrast is partly structural because core genes cannot be singletons by definition. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]
- The report interprets costly+conserved genes as costly in lab assays but retained by environmental selection. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

- The report hypothesizes ongoing loss of costly+dispensable genes unless some environments provide compensating selective advantage. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]
- The authors propose HGT-mediated genome expansion rather than metabolic-pathway degradation as the main explanation, but this is interpretive. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]
