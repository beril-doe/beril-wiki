# Heavy-metal contamination isolates score higher on predicted metal tolerance than baseline

Organisms isolated from heavy metal contamination sites have metal tolerance scores a full standard deviation above the environmental baseline (Cohen's d = +1.00, Mann-Whitney p=0.006, n=10). [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

## Key figures

- **+1.00**: Cohen's d of heavy-metal contamination isolates versus environmental baseline (scope BacDive isolates linked to GTDB species metal-tolerance scores) [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- **0.006**: Mann-Whitney p-value for heavy-metal group versus baseline [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- **10 isolates**: number of heavy-metal contamination isolates [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

## Caveat

The observed d=1.00 barely exceeds the reported detection threshold, meaning the analysis had just enough power to detect the effect. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

> The observed d=1.00 barely exceeds this threshold, meaning we had just enough power to detect the effect. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

## Other caveats in this report

- Positive metal utilizers had lower predicted scores, but the direction was nonsignificant and should not be over-interpreted. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- With n=10 heavy-metal isolates versus ~5,000 baseline isolates, the estimated minimum detectable effect at 80% power was approximately d=0.93. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- The plan referenced n=31 heavy-metal isolates, but only 10 matched scored pangenome species after bridging. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- The report attributes an 88% core estimate for metal-tolerance genes to the separate metal_specificity project; this is secondhand evidence here. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- The report proposes that within-species core-gene enrichment can coexist with between-species differences in total metal-tolerance gene content; this is an interpretation, not a separate test in this report. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
