# Does Ca. Methanoperedens in sediment indicate nitrate-driven methane oxidation at the plume edge?

Is the sediment-restricted methane-oxidizing archaeon Ca. Methanoperedens active in the Oak Ridge subsurface where methane and nitrate overlap?

## Hypothesis

Ca. Methanoperedens abundance in sediment cores rises with measured methane and nitrate and carries genes for reverse methanogenesis and nitrate reduction.

## Why it matters

Anaerobic methane oxidation coupled to nitrate would be a methane sink and a nitrate removal route in a nitrate-contaminated aquifer that is currently unconsidered.

## How to test it

Correlate Ca. Methanoperedens abundance in sediment cores with gas and anion measurements in the same wells and zones, then examine sediment metagenomes for mcr-type and nitrate reductase genes, and incubate cores with labelled methane and nitrate.

## What would support or refute it

Support: abundance co-varies with methane and nitrate, the genes are present, and labelled methane is oxidized only with nitrate. Refute: no covariation and no activity, suggesting the taxon is a relic or uses another acceptor.

## Data

Approach: analysis then experiment. Readiness: unknown. Gas and anion staging tables exist, but their columns, wells and overlap with sediment cores were not shown.
