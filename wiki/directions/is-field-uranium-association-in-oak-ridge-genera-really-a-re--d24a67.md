# Is field uranium association in Oak Ridge genera really a response to acidity or nitrate?

Do Herbaspirillum enrichment and Caulobacter depletion at high-uranium sites come from uranium itself or from the low pH and nitrate that travel with it?

## Hypothesis

In defined media, uranium alone does not change the growth of Caulobacter or Herbaspirillum, whereas lowering pH and adding nitrate reproduces the field pattern (Herbaspirillum favoured, Caulobacter disfavoured).

## Why it matters

A lab consortium kept Caulobacter growing and dominant with uranium, but in the field it was less abundant at high-uranium sites. Deciding which variable filters taxa tells us whether uranium tolerance scores can predict field communities.

## How to test it

Fit field abundances against uranium, pH and nitrate across the Oak Ridge sites, then grow Caulobacter and Herbaspirillum isolates in synthetic groundwater crossing uranium, pH and nitrate. Compare with existing Caulobacter fitness experiments for metal and acid conditions.

## What would support or refute it

Support: pH or nitrate, not uranium, controls growth and matches the field direction. Refute: uranium alone reproduces the field abundance pattern in the isolates.

## Data

Approach: analysis then experiment. Readiness: needs new measurements. Isolate growth under crossed conditions is not in the catalog; only the Caulobacter fitness tables and field community tables were shown.
