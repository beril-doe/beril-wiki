# Does commensal inhibition of P. aeruginosa work through shared-resource depletion or through interference?

Given that PA14 outgrows the average commensal on every one of the 22 tested substrates, is the observed inhibition produced by joint depletion of PA14's preferred substrates by several commensals, or by non-nutritional interference?

## Hypothesis

Inhibition is from depletion: M. luteus (which grows on 9 of PA14's 11 preferred substrates) and its partners remove shared substrates. Replenishing those substrates will relieve inhibition, and the combination will follow or exceed the additive prediction.

## Why it matters

The substrate screen found no selective prebiotic, so exclusion must come from community-level effects. If depletion explains it, nutrient-niche design of formulations is sound. If it does not, interference mechanisms must be identified and engineered.

## How to test it

Co-culture PA14 with M. luteus, N. mucosa and S. salivarius, singly and combined, in defined medium with PA14's 11 preferred substrates at limiting versus excess levels. Use spent-medium assays with and without add-back of those substrates. Compare PA14 growth with the additive prediction (mean of member inhibitions). Repeat with ExoS+ P. aeruginosa strains, because all prior assays used PA14.

## What would support or refute it

Support: inhibition falls when the substrates are in excess or added back to spent medium, and combinations track the additive prediction. Refute: inhibition persists at excess substrate and after add-back, which implies interference; synergy or antagonism would show the additive assumption fails.

## Data

Approach: experiment. Readiness: needs new measurements. The existing PROTECT-derived growth data give the predictions, but the substrate-limitation, add-back and combination tests need new co-culture experiments.
