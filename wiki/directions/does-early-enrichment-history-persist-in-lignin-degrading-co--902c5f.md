# Does early enrichment history persist in lignin-degrading communities because of priority effects?

Why does earlier enrichment history explain more of the later bacterial community variance than the current carbon source, and does the memory fade with further passaging?

## Hypothesis

Early colonizers, such as copiotrophs favored by labile carbon, hold their positions through priority effects. History will therefore keep outweighing the current carbon source, and the memory index will decay only slowly over further passages.

## Why it matters

If history persists, enrichment outcomes depend on inoculum past and not only on the substrate. That would change how lignin-degrading consortia are designed and compared.

## How to test it

Extend the lignin enrichment with added replicates and a Round 3 passage, including a reciprocal switch of carbon sources between lignin-only and lignin plus labile carbon histories. Inoculate copiotroph-enriched communities into lignin-only medium. Compare history and current-carbon effects on bacterial 16S, and add more replicates for fungal ITS. Add a no-lignin passage control.

## What would support or refute it

Support: history still explains more variance than current carbon after the switch, and copiotroph-seeded communities keep their profile. Refute: current carbon takes over, or memory vanishes. Fungal ITS memory was not detectable at the earlier replicate level, so extra replicates also show whether that was low power.

## Data

Approach: experiment. Readiness: needs new measurements. The test needs new enrichment rounds, switches and added replicates that are not in the findings.
