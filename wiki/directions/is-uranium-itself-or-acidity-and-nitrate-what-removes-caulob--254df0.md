# Is uranium itself, or acidity and nitrate, what removes Caulobacter from contaminated wells?

Does uranium directly lower the abundance of Caulobacter and raise Herbaspirillum, or do these field patterns come from co-varying pH, nitrate and carbon?

## Hypothesis

In factorial microcosms, uranium alone at neutral pH does not lower Caulobacter, while low pH or high nitrate does; Herbaspirillum responds to the chemistry rather than to uranium.

## Why it matters

It would settle whether field correlations with uranium can be read as tolerance or sensitivity, which decides if community composition can be used to monitor uranium. The only lab precedent shows Caulobacter growing in a uranium-containing consortium, not its response relative to an uranium-free control.

## How to test it

Build microcosms from Oak Ridge groundwater inocula crossing uranium, pH and nitrate levels, including uranium-free controls, then follow Caulobacter and Herbaspirillum by 16S profiling. Compare with the field abundances in ENIGMA CORAL, fitting uranium with pH and nitrate as joint predictors. Check whether Fitness Browser Caulobacter metal-stress data agree with the direct uranium response.

## What would support or refute it

Support: uranium alone has little effect on Caulobacter in microcosms while pH or nitrate does, and the field uranium signal shrinks with these covariates. Refutation: Caulobacter declines under uranium at neutral pH and low nitrate.

## Data

Approach: analysis then experiment. Readiness: needs new measurements. Field abundance and uranium data exist, but the uncontrolled covariates require new manipulative microcosms.
