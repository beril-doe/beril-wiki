# Does warming raise methane oxidation by activating existing methanotrophs rather than growing them?

In long-term heated forest soil, methane-oxidation and glyoxylate-cycle transcripts rose under warming in suggestive signals, Actinobacteria rose and Acidobacteria fell, yet the RNA pool was no more warming-sensitive than DNA. Is warming activating a subset of existing organisms?

## Hypothesis

Warming increases methane oxidation by raising pmoA activity per cell in existing methanotroph populations, with little change in their abundance.

## Why it matters

It would show that warming responses can occur without community turnover, which affects how soil methane sinks are forecast.

## How to test it

Measure methane uptake and pmoA DNA and RNA from heated and control Harvard Forest soils, both organic and mineral horizons, at several seasons. Include labelled-methane incubations to identify active methanotrophs. Relate transcript-to-DNA ratios to uptake rates. Check in NMDC Multi-omics warming datasets whether pmoA carriers are recovered as genomes.

## What would support or refute it

Support: higher methane uptake and pmoA transcript-to-DNA ratios in heated soil with similar pmoA DNA. Refutation: uptake follows pmoA DNA abundance, or heated soils show no uptake difference.

## Data

Approach: analysis then experiment. Readiness: needs new measurements. Existing data come from one timepoint and the methane-oxidation signals were only suggestive once the many genes tested were taken into account, so new sampling is required.
