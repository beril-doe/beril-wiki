# Do groundwater iron oxidisers persist in the water phase while sediment anaerobes stay attached?

Gallionella and Sideroxydans are more common in groundwater, while Anaeromyxobacter and Methanoperedens are more common in sediment. Do these genera differ because of a real lifestyle contrast in how they respond to oxygen and ferrous iron, rather than because the two materials were sampled about 18 months apart?

## Hypothesis

In sealed microcosms of sediment and groundwater from the same well, iron oxidisers grow in the water phase when ferrous iron and low oxygen are supplied. Anaeromyxobacter and Methanoperedens remain on the solids and do not move into the water.

## Why it matters

A real lifestyle split would mean groundwater sampling systematically misses attached iron reducers and methane-oxidising archaea, so inferences about site metabolism from water alone would be incomplete.

## How to test it

Collect a sediment core and groundwater from the same well on the same day. Set up microcosms with sediment plus groundwater, manipulating oxygen and ferrous iron. Sequence the water and solid fractions over time and track Gallionella, Sideroxydans, Anaeromyxobacter, Arcobacter, Methanoperedens and Rhodanobacter. Existing community and sample records help pick the well.

## What would support or refute it

Support: iron oxidisers rise in the water fraction under ferrous iron and low oxygen, while the others stay in the solid fraction. Refutation: all genera partition the same way regardless of treatment, or the contrast disappears, which would suggest the original difference came from sampling time.

## Data

Approach: experiment. Readiness: needs new measurements. The catalog shows sample and abundance tables for choosing a well, but the catalog did not show date-matched sediment and groundwater pairs, so microcosms need new sampling.
