# Do siderophores let Crohn's pathobionts out-compete commensals when iron is restricted?

The core pathobiont gene clusters are strongly enriched for iron-siderophore and genotoxin themes. Does siderophore production give these species a growth advantage in an inflamed, iron-limited gut?

## Hypothesis

Deleting the siderophore cluster in a pathobiont reduces its expansion in an iron-limited community. Adding a siderophore-independent iron source restores that expansion.

## Why it matters

It would turn an enrichment seen against a rare-background comparison into a mechanism. It would also say whether iron restriction could make phage targeting of these species more or less effective.

## How to test it

In E. coli, a known siderophore producer among the targets, build siderophore-synthesis deletion mutants. Add each mutant or its parent to a defined commensal community in anaerobic chemostats. Vary iron by chelator or by supplementation with hemin or ferric salt. Track strain abundance by amplicon or barcode counts. Run the same iron-limited chemostat with the E. coli parent in the presence and absence of phage.

## What would support or refute it

Support: the mutant loses ground only at low iron, and rescue with a non-siderophore iron source restores it. Refutation: mutant and parent expand equally across iron levels.

## Data

Approach: experiment. Readiness: needs new measurements. Needs mutant construction and community cultures; the enrichment is a computational observation against a catalog background.
