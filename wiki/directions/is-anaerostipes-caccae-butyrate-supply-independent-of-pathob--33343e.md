# Is Anaerostipes caccae butyrate supply independent of pathobiont-derived lactate in Crohn's disease?

Does A. caccae depend on lactate from pathobionts in the gut, or does it draw lactate from other partners, so that cocktail-driven pathobiont depletion would leave butyrate supply intact?

## Hypothesis

A. caccae grows on lactate from Bacteroides or Bifidobacterium partners rather than from F. plautii or E. bolteae, so removing these pathobionts will not reduce its growth or butyrate output.

## Why it matters

The paired-data null argues against a pathobiont-produces/A. caccae-consumes model. If co-culture confirms it, pathobiont depletion would not erode butyrate supply, which lowers the ecological risk of a cocktail.

## How to test it

Co-culture A. caccae in defined medium with F. plautii, E. bolteae or Bacteroides, tracking lactate, growth and butyrate. Then repeat in a defined community with and without each partner. Compare against the cohort lactate correlations (A. caccae +0.18; F. plautii −0.23; E. bolteae −0.20).

## What would support or refute it

Support: A. caccae growth and butyrate depend on Bacteroides lactate and are unchanged when the pathobionts are removed. Refutation: A. caccae grows on pathobiont lactate and loses butyrate output when they are removed. The cohort null is a single-cohort data-scope limit (PARTIAL status), not a refutation, so co-culture can overturn it either way.

## Data

Approach: experiment. Readiness: needs new measurements. The cohort correlation exists, but co-culture data are not in the lakehouse.
