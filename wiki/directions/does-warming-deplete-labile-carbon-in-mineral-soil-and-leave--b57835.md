# Does warming deplete labile carbon in mineral soil and leave fewer detectable metabolites?

Do long-term heated mineral soils have fewer detectable metabolites because the easily used compounds have been consumed, and does adding such substrates restore the microbial response?

## Hypothesis

The loss of detectable metabolites in heated mineral soil is concentrated in labile compound classes such as sugars and amino acids, and added labile substrate gives a larger respiration increase in heated than in control soil.

## Why it matters

It would support substrate depletion, not microbial acclimation, as the reason warming effects fade, which affects predictions of long-term soil carbon loss.

## How to test it

Classify the detected metabolites of heated and control mineral samples by compound class in NMDC Multi-omics and compare class-level losses, since no single metabolite changed on its own. Then add labile substrates to heated and control mineral soil from the same plots and measure respiration.

## What would support or refute it

Support: losses concentrate in labile classes and heated soil responds more to added substrate. Refutation: losses are spread evenly across classes and substrate addition gives equal responses.

## Data

Approach: analysis then experiment. Readiness: needs new measurements. Metabolite lists are in the lakehouse, but the substrate-addition test requires new incubations of heated and control soil.
