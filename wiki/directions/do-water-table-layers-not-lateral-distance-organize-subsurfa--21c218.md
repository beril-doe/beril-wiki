# Do water-table layers, not lateral distance, organize subsurface communities at the Oak Ridge site?

Is the strong community change with depth within one well caused by stacked hydrogeological layers with different electron-acceptor chemistry, rather than by a contaminant plume moving sideways?

## Hypothesis

Community differences between depths of the same well are explained by layer-specific chemistry (oxygen, nitrate, iron, sulfate), and the weak sideways structure disappears once layer is accounted for. If the plume instead moves laterally, distance along its path will explain community change within a layer.

## Why it matters

It would show that sampling at the wrong depth, not the wrong well, hides contamination effects at subsurface sites, and it would tell sampling designs where to spend effort. The reported share of sediment variance attributed to zone is uncertain, because the number of samples behind it is stated inconsistently (37 samples versus zone counts of 33, 25, 54 and 45), so the size of the zone effect needs to be re-established within this test.

## How to test it

Using ENIGMA CORAL, compare community dissimilarity against depth difference while holding horizontal distance fixed, and the reverse, within each hydrogeological zone, using a sample list whose counts per zone are fixed first. Then load the registered but not yet loaded geochemistry samples (nitrate, pH, metals, isotopes) and test whether they predict the vertical turnover. Follow with a new depth-resolved geochemical profile in one well to see whether the chemistry steps match the community steps.

## What would support or refute it

Support: depth and zone explain community change after horizontal distance is removed, and chemistry steps line up with community steps. Refutation: horizontal position along a northeast-to-southwest gradient explains as much as depth within a layer, or geochemistry does not track the community.

## Data

Approach: analysis then experiment. Readiness: needs new measurements. Community data exist, but the geochemistry samples are registered and not yet loaded, and the depth-resolved well profile is a new measurement.
