# Does long-term warming raise methane-oxidation activity in forest soil without adding methanotrophs?

In long-term warmed Harvard Forest soil, do the higher transcripts of the methane-oxidation genes pmoA and pmoB reflect higher activity of existing methanotrophs rather than more of them?

## Hypothesis

If the weak methane-oxidation transcript signal is real, transcripts are higher in heated plots while pmoA and pmoB DNA abundance and methanotroph taxa are unchanged, so warming raises activity per cell instead of enriching methanotrophs.

## Why it matters

Whether warmed forest soils take up more or less methane depends on this. If warming mainly changes expression, DNA-based community surveys would miss a change in a greenhouse-gas sink. The same experiment tests whether a warming-selected group of fast-responding taxa exists.

## How to test it

The existing data cannot settle this: the transcript signals were weak and did not hold up once thousands of gene functions were tested together (the pmoB mineral-soil signal was borderline even before that), only one sampling date exists, and horizon is confounded with incubation. Analysis: in the Harvard Forest warming data, compare pmoA/pmoB RNA-to-DNA ratios between control and heated plots, and check the NMDC Multi-omics warming or thaw studies for the same signal. Experiment: sample control and heated plots in each horizon across seasons with replicate cores, all handled the same way, measure methane oxidation rates, and quantify pmoA DNA and RNA by targeted PCR.

## What would support or refute it

Support: transcript ratios and measured methane oxidation rise in heated plots with flat DNA, in both horizons and across seasons. Refute: the transcript signal vanishes with replication, or DNA abundance rises as much as RNA, which would mean enrichment instead of activation.

## Data

Approach: analysis then experiment. Readiness: needs new measurements. Only a single sampling date exists, the signals are weak and confounded by incubation, so seasonal replication and process measurements are required.
