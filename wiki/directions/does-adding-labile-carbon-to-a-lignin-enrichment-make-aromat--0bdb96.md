# Does adding labile carbon to a lignin enrichment make aromatic degraders depend on cross-feeding?

When labile carbon is added to a lignin enrichment, do Acinetobacter and Aeromonas grow on the labile carbon alone while Pseudomonas and other specialists decline, and does this alter community dependence?

## Hypothesis

Acinetobacter and Aeromonas isolates from the labile-carbon enrichment grow on the labile carbon but not on lignin-derived aromatics alone, whereas the lignin-only Pseudomonas isolates grow on aromatics alone.

## Why it matters

It would show whether the copiotroph shift is a change in who uses the lignin carbon or only in who uses the added sugar. That affects how enrichments are used to find lignin degraders.

## How to test it

Isolate Acinetobacter, Aeromonas and Pseudomonas from the lignin-only and lignin plus labile-carbon enrichments. Test growth on lignin-derived aromatics alone, labile carbon alone, and both. Run pairwise co-cultures to look for cross-feeding. Use at least 6 replicate enrichments, since the original had 3 per group.

## What would support or refute it

Support: Acinetobacter and Aeromonas fail on aromatics alone but grow in co-culture with Pseudomonas. Refute: they grow on aromatics alone, which would make them direct degraders.

## Data

Approach: experiment. Readiness: needs new measurements. Isolates and growth assays are not in the catalog, and the original enrichments had only 3 replicates per group.
