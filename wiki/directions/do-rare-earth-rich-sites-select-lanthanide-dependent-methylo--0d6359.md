# Do rare-earth-rich sites select lanthanide-dependent methylotrophs only when acidity does not dominate?

Acid-mine-drainage genomes from a rare-earth mining site carried little xoxF and no lanmodulin. Does acid and metal stress override lanthanide availability, so that xoxF enrichment appears only at rare-earth-rich sites with milder chemistry?

## Hypothesis

Lanthanide-dependent methylotrophs are enriched at lanthanide-rich sites of near-neutral pH but not in acidic, metal-laden drainage, where stress genes dominate.

## Why it matters

It would show that lanthanide supply alone does not recruit lanthanide-dependent organisms and that pH or co-occurring metals gate it. This sets where to look for them in rare-earth mining and recovery settings.

## How to test it

Search NMDC Multi-omics and NMDC BioSamples for metagenomes from rare-earth-rich soils, weathered rock, mine tailings and acid drainage, plus matched non-rare-earth controls, and compare xoxF and lanmodulin carriage and stress-gene profiles by pH class. Then run methanol enrichments of neutral and acidic rare-earth-rich material with and without added lanthanum, and measure xoxF expression and lanthanide uptake.

## What would support or refute it

Support: xoxF enrichment at neutral rare-earth-rich sites but not acidic ones, and lanthanum-stimulated enrichments only at neutral pH. Refutation: acidic sites are equally enriched, or neutral rare-earth sites show no excess over matched controls.

## Data

Approach: analysis then experiment. Readiness: needs new measurements. The earlier rare-earth set was small and from a single project, so more metagenomes or new enrichments are needed.
