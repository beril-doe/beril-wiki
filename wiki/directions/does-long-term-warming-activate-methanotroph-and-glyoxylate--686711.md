# Does long-term warming activate methanotroph and glyoxylate-cycle transcription in forest soil?

Are the nominal RNA increases in pmoA, pmoB, aceA and aceB under long-term warming real, and do they replicate in new, independent measurements?

## Hypothesis

Long-term warming raises transcripts of methane oxidation and glyoxylate-cycle genes in a taxon subset, so an independent set of warmed and control soils will show increases of the same sign for these genes, passing correction within that new test.

## Why it matters

It would link the organic-horizon phylum shift (Actinobacteria up, Acidobacteria down) to a functional mechanism and bear on warming effects on the methane sink.

## How to test it

Treat the original signals (pmoA K10944, pmoB K10945, mineral aceA K01637 and aceB K01638, all nominal and not surviving FDR across 14K KOs) as hypotheses from the discovery set, not a prespecified confirmatory panel. Collect new RNA and DNA from direct organic and mineral samples of heated and control Harvard Forest plots at several dates, including organic-horizon DNA that was absent here, and test these genes with a denominator fixed in advance. Resolve which of the 298 MAGs carry them, and confirm pmoA transcripts by qPCR.

## What would support or refute it

Support: same-sign increases in independent samples that pass correction, localized to specific MAGs. Refute: no increase in new samples. RNA and DNA layers must be judged separately: incubation confounds the DNA-organic result, while the RNA organic samples were direct.

## Data

Approach: experiment. Readiness: needs new measurements. Independent validation requires new transcript, DNA and qPCR measurements; the original report used nmdc_metadata and nmdc_results, so access through a listed collection is not established.
