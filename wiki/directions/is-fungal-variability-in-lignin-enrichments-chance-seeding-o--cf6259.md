# Is fungal variability in lignin enrichments chance seeding or priority effects?

Bacterial communities responded to lignin enrichment reproducibly while fungal communities varied hugely between replicates. Does fungal chance arise from the few propagules that seed each flask, or from early colonisers shaping what follows?

## Hypothesis

Fungal replicate variability falls as the amount of inoculum rises. If it persists at high inoculum, priority effects, not sampling of rare propagules, produce it.

## Why it matters

It would tell whether fungal lignin-degrading consortia can be assembled reproducibly by raising inoculum size, or whether they are inherently history-dependent.

## How to test it

Repeat the lignin enrichment from the same soil with several inoculum sizes and at least five replicates per size, sequencing fungal markers at matched depth. Compare replicate spread across inoculum sizes. In a second arm, start flasks with an identical early-colonising fungus added first, and compare with flasks without it.

## What would support or refute it

Support for sampling: spread shrinks with larger inoculum. Support for priority effects: spread persists and the early colonist changes later community makeup. Refutation of both: neither inoculum size nor early colonist changes the spread.

## Data

Approach: experiment. Readiness: needs new measurements. The existing enrichment had too few replicates and low fungal read depth, so the question needs new cultures.
