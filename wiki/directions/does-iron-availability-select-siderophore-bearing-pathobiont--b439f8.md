# Does iron availability select siderophore-bearing pathobionts in Crohn's disease?

Does host or dietary iron availability shape which pathobionts expand in Crohn's disease, through selection for siderophore-bearing strains?

## Hypothesis

Increased luminal iron favours siderophore-bearing E. coli strains and their expansion, so iron restriction would reduce their advantage, with the effect confined to hosts with an inflammatory phenotype.

## Why it matters

If it holds, iron status would become a patient-stratification variable for pathobiont-directed phage therapy. It would also suggest iron restriction as a co-intervention.

## How to test it

In colitis-susceptible and colitis-resistant mouse models colonised with marker-positive and marker-negative E. coli, vary dietary iron and measure strain competition and inflammation. Separately, check whether any available cohort documents oral iron supplementation; if it does, test whether supplemented patients carry more siderophore-bearing strains. The Tier-A iron_siderophore enrichment (OR 44.4) is only the motivating observation, since it was measured against the full catalog and is partly inflated by background rarity.

## What would support or refute it

Support: marker-positive strains outcompete marker-negative strains under higher iron, and the advantage shrinks under restriction, mainly in inflamed hosts. Refutation: competition is unaffected by iron, or does not differ by host phenotype.

## Data

Approach: experiment. Readiness: needs new measurements. The mouse competition experiments need new measurements; whether any cohort metadata records oral iron supplementation is not established by the supplied evidence.
