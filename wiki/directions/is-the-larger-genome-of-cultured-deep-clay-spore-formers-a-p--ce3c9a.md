# Is the larger genome of cultured deep-clay spore-formers a property of the habitat or of cultivation?

Cultured deep-clay Bacillota_B genomes are larger than soil relatives, against the expectation that deep habitats favor small genomes, and their sulfate-reduction enrichment looks like a porewater-culture bias. Are uncultured rock-attached relatives also larger?

## Hypothesis

Uncultured rock-attached Bacillota_B from clay and similar deep rock have genomes no larger than soil relatives, so the enlargement in cultures reflects which lineages grow in the lab.

## Why it matters

It would decide whether deep subsurface lineages expand their genomes, as the cultures imply, or follow the streamlining seen in groundwater lineages. It would also show how far culture collections can support ecological claims.

## How to test it

Compare cultured deep-clay Bacillota_B genomes in the Pangenome Collection with published metagenome-assembled genomes of Bacillota_B from rock-attached clay and other deep rock, which must be brought in, and with soil relatives within the same genera (Desulfosporosinus and the clay-isolated genera). Compare genome size and gene content per genome, restricting to genomes of comparable completeness. The ten cultured anchors cluster in a few genera, so compare within genus.

## What would support or refute it

Support: uncultured deep-rock genomes are similar in size to soil relatives or smaller, and the cultured genomes stand out. Refute: uncultured deep-rock genomes are as large as the cultured ones, so expansion is a real feature of the habitat.

## Data

Approach: lakehouse analysis. Readiness: unknown. Cultured anchors are in the lakehouse; whether rock-attached deep-clay assemblies are available there or must be brought in is not established.
