# Is the groundwater-versus-sediment split in Oak Ridge taxa real habitat preference?

Do Rhodanobacter, Gallionella and Sideroxydans really prefer flowing groundwater, while Anaeromyxobacter, Arcobacter and Ca. Methanoperedens prefer attached sediment, or does the 18-month gap between sampling create the pattern?

## Hypothesis

When groundwater and sediment are sampled together from the same wells, the same taxa remain partitioned, and the partition tracks metabolic lifestyle: iron oxidizers in the water, methane-oxidizing and iron- or sulfur-reducing taxa on solids.

## Why it matters

A real partition means groundwater monitoring cannot see the sediment-attached communities that may store and process contaminants. A time artifact would mean the two sample types can be interchanged.

## How to test it

Collect paired sediment cores and groundwater at the same wells in a single campaign, with 16S profiling and shotgun metagenomes. Compare the abundance ratios of the named genera between materials and check whether genomes of water-enriched versus sediment-enriched taxa carry the inferred iron-oxidation, denitrification, methane-oxidation and sulfate-reduction genes. Use the existing ENIGMA CORAL profiles as the baseline.

## What would support or refute it

Support: the named genera keep their direction of enrichment in simultaneous samples and gene content matches the lifestyle. Refutation: the ratios collapse toward one, showing the earlier contrast came from timing.

## Data

Approach: experiment. Readiness: needs new measurements. Existing sediment and groundwater samples were taken 18 months apart, so a simultaneous resampling and shotgun sequencing is required.
