# Does the butyrate producer Anaerostipes caccae live on lactate made by Crohn's pathobionts?

Is lactate from Crohn's-associated gut bacteria such as Flavonifractor plautii and Eubacterium bolteae a real food source for Anaerostipes caccae? If so, removing those bacteria would cost the gut some butyrate.

## Hypothesis

Anaerostipes caccae gains no growth or butyrate from lactate made by these pathobionts. Adding them to a defined community will not raise its lactate use or butyrate output, and removing them will not lower butyrate.

## Why it matters

A phage cocktail aimed at these pathobionts is judged partly by whether it would starve a beneficial butyrate producer. A cross-feeding link would be a cost of targeting them; its absence would remove that worry.

## How to test it

Grow Anaerostipes caccae in defined medium with lactate as the main carbon source, alone and together with Flavonifractor plautii, with Eubacterium bolteae, and with a known lactate-producing partner as a positive control. Measure lactate, butyrate and growth over time. Repeat in a defined multi-species community with and without each pathobiont. Compare with the cohort pattern in which lactate tracked Anaerostipes caccae in the opposite direction from the two pathobionts.

## What would support or refute it

Support: butyrate and Anaerostipes caccae growth do not depend on the pathobionts, while the positive-control partner does feed it. Refutation: the pathobionts raise its lactate use or butyrate output, or their removal lowers butyrate.

## Data

Approach: experiment. Readiness: needs new measurements. It needs anaerobic co-culture and metabolite assays; the cohort correlation is the only existing evidence, it comes from one cohort, and its authors call the status a data-scope limit rather than a refutation.
