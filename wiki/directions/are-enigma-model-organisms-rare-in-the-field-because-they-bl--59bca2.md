# Are ENIGMA model organisms rare in the field because they bloom only after carbon addition?

Do Desulfovibrio and Pseudomonas, which showed no field link to uranium, rise from rarity only when carbon is added to Oak Ridge groundwater or sediment?

## Hypothesis

Desulfovibrio and Pseudomonas stay at very low abundance in unamended Oak Ridge material, and increase substantially after carbon addition once electron acceptors are depleted.

## Why it matters

The primary model organism was detected at only 34% of sites and at very low abundance, so its 16S-based uranium analysis is unreliable. If it is a conditional bloomer, lab fitness data apply to amended or reduced conditions rather than to the ambient community.

## How to test it

Set up microcosms of Oak Ridge groundwater and sediment with and without added carbon, sampling over time. Track both genera by 16S amplicon sequencing, with a Desulfovibrio-specific quantitative PCR to rule out 16S detection bias, and record nitrate and sulfate depletion. Before starting, check whether the existing community tables record any carbon-amendment status; the catalog did not show this.

## What would support or refute it

Support: a clear rise after carbon addition that is absent in unamended controls, tracking electron-acceptor depletion. Refute: abundance stays near its low baseline in amended microcosms, suggesting these organisms are not ecologically relevant in situ or are poorly detected.

## Data

Approach: experiment. Readiness: needs new measurements. The catalog did not show amendment-status data for these wells, so the carbon-addition comparison would need to be generated with new microcosms.
