# Is scarce xoxF in rare-earth mine drainage an acid-stress or rare-earth effect?

Only 4/37 REE-acid-mine-drainage MAGs carried xoxF and 0/37 carried bakta-validated lanmodulin or xoxJ, with a functional signature reading as acid-mine-drainage stress. Does rare-earth enrichment itself change lanthanide-machinery carriage beyond what acid and metal stress explain?

## Hypothesis

Acid and metal stress, not rare-earth content, explains the low xoxF carriage: non-REE acid-mine-drainage communities from independent sites will show similarly low carriage, and REE-impacted communities from independent sites will not differ from them.

## Why it matters

It would show whether rare-earth-rich habitats are poor in known lanthanide biology or distinct from other acid-drainage environments, informing where to look for lanthanide-utilizing organisms.

## How to test it

The 37-MAG REE-AMD anchor is a single bioproject and descriptive only, so it serves as a hypothesis source, not as the test. Assemble MAGs from several independent REE-impacted sites and several independent non-REE acid-mine-drainage sites in public archives and NMDC samples. Compare xoxF, lanmodulin, PQQ and stress-gene carriage with a model that treats bioproject as a random effect and phylum as a stratum, so site and study effects are separated from REE effect.

## What would support or refute it

Support: carriage in independent REE sites matches non-REE AMD sites and both are low. Refutation: independent REE sites differ in xoxF carriage from non-REE AMD sites after accounting for bioproject. Absence of enough independent REE sites means the question stays unresolved.

## Data

Approach: lakehouse analysis. Readiness: needs compute. Only the single-bioproject REE-AMD anchor (n=37) is in hand; independent REE and non-REE metagenome sets must still be assembled and annotated.
