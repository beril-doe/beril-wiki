# Does direct antagonism, rather than niche coverage, drive commensal suppression of Pseudomonas aeruginosa?

In defined commensal consortia for cystic fibrosis airways, does direct killing or inhibition of the pathogen explain suppression better than overlap in nutrient use?

## Hypothesis

A two-member consortium chosen for strong direct antagonism suppresses P. aeruginosa as well as a three-member consortium chosen for full niche coverage, even though its predicted coverage is much lower.

## Why it matters

The design disagreement over two versus three organisms decides which consortia go forward to animal testing. If antagonism dominates, engraftable two-member designs are justified. If coverage dominates, persistence of poorly engrafting species becomes the key problem.

## How to test it

Grow the k=2 and k=3 consortia from the formulation design (including M. luteus ASMA-2965, N. mucosa ASMA-3643, S. salivarius ASMA-737) against PA14 in defined media and in cell-free supernatant, separating contact-independent antagonism from nutrient competition. Include a strain typed ExoS+ alongside PA14. Measure mixtures directly rather than assuming additive inhibition, then compare in a mouse colonization model.

## What would support or refute it

Support: the k=2 mixture matches k=3 suppression and supernatant alone reproduces much of it. Refute: k=3 clearly outperforms k=2 and supernatant has little effect, pointing to nutrient competition.

## Data

Approach: experiment. Readiness: needs new measurements. Requires new mixture assays and mouse work; the existing scores assume additive inhibition and cannot separate the mechanisms.
