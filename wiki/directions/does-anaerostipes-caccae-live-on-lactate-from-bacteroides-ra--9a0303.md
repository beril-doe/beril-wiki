# Does Anaerostipes caccae live on lactate from Bacteroides rather than from Crohn's pathobionts?

In Crohn's disease, where does the lactate that Anaerostipes caccae converts to butyrate come from? Cohort data did not support supply from the pathobionts Flavonifractor plautii or Enterocloster bolteae.

## Hypothesis

A. caccae grows on lactate only when a Bacteroides or Bifidobacterium partner supplies it. Pathobiont partners such as F. plautii and E. bolteae do not raise its butyrate output in co-culture.

## Why it matters

If true, depleting these pathobionts with phage would not cut butyrate supply through lost cross-feeding. If false, cocktail design needs a butyrate-protection step.

## How to test it

Grow A. caccae in lactate-limited defined medium alone and with each of F. plautii, E. bolteae, a Bacteroides species and a Bifidobacterium species. Measure lactate, butyrate and A. caccae growth over time. Add transwell and conditioned-medium controls to separate contact from diffusible metabolites. Then repeat in a defined mixed community with each partner removed in turn.

## What would support or refute it

Support: butyrate and A. caccae growth rise with the Bacteroides or Bifidobacterium partner but not with the pathobionts. Refutation: pathobiont partners raise growth and butyrate as much as the commensal partners do.

## Data

Approach: experiment. Readiness: needs new measurements. Needs anaerobic co-culture work; the cohort data gave only opposite-sign lactate correlations, and that result was flagged as a limit of single-cohort data scope rather than a refutation.
