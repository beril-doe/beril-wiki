# Do commensal mixtures suppress Pseudomonas aeruginosa beyond what their members do alone?

Since no single substrate favors commensals over PA14, does a mixture of commensals suppress P. aeruginosa through combined depletion of the nutrients it prefers, including in the more common ExoS-type strains?

## Hypothesis

A three-member mixture of Micrococcus luteus, Neisseria mucosa and Streptococcus salivarius suppresses P. aeruginosa more than the average of its members, and the extra suppression depends on how many of the pathogen's preferred substrates the members jointly use.

## Why it matters

The additive scoring behind formulation design is unvalidated, and the substrate-overlap link to inhibition is modest. A measured non-additive effect would change how mixtures are designed, and ExoS-type testing would address the PA14 limitation.

## How to test it

In synthetic cystic fibrosis sputum medium, co-culture PA14 and an ExoS-type strain (PAO1-like) with each commensal alone, each pair, and the triple. Measure pathogen growth and substrate depletion, and compare observed suppression with the average of members. Vary substrates to test whether suppression follows combined substrate use.

## What would support or refute it

Support: mixtures beat the member average and the gain scales with combined use of preferred substrates, in both strain types. Refute: mixtures match or fall below the average, or ExoS-type strains escape.

## Data

Approach: experiment. Readiness: needs new measurements. Only five unique commensal pairs have been measured, and no ExoS-type strain or mixture co-culture data are described.
