# Why is the model sulfate reducer Desulfovibrio nearly absent from Oak Ridge groundwater?

Is Desulfovibrio rare because it is genuinely absent from the aquifer, or because it lives on sediment or in anoxic pockets that groundwater 16S misses, with other sulfate reducers doing its job?

## Hypothesis

Desulfovibrio and other sulfate reducers are enriched in sediment and in isolated anaerobic pockets, so targeted assays show higher abundance there than in groundwater, and the lab results with the model organism describe a minor field member.

## Why it matters

It would show whether laboratory work on the favored uranium-reducing model organism transfers to the field, or whether other lineages should be the targets of lab study.

## How to test it

Compare Desulfovibrio and other sulfate-reducer genera between sediment and groundwater profiles in ENIGMA CORAL, noting that the two were sampled at different times. Then run a new sulfate-reducer gene assay (dsrAB) with deeper sequencing on sediment and groundwater from the anaerobic wells, and check whether sulfate-reducer abundance rises in zones where Anaeromyxobacter and Arcobacter dominate.

## What would support or refute it

Support: sulfate reducers are much more abundant in sediment or anaerobic zones than in groundwater. Refutation: they stay rare and undetectable in all material types and depths.

## Data

Approach: analysis then experiment. Readiness: needs new measurements. Community profiles for both materials are in the lakehouse, but the decisive dsrAB assay and deeper sequencing are new measurements.
