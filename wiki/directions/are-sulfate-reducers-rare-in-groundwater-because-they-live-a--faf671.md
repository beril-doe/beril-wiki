# Are sulfate reducers rare in groundwater because they live attached to deeper sediment?

Desulfovibrio, the main laboratory model organism for this site, is detected at very low abundance in field surveys. Is it concentrated in sediment or other attached niches that groundwater sampling underrepresents?

## Hypothesis

Sulfate-reducer marker genes are far more abundant in DNA from deeper saturated-zone sediment than in groundwater from the same wells.

## Why it matters

If true, the gap between laboratory model organisms and field data reflects where the field is sampled, not the irrelevance of those organisms. Laboratory tolerance scores for sulfate reducers could then be tested against sediment communities.

## How to test it

Extract DNA from sediment cores and groundwater from the same wells at matched depths. Quantify the sulfate-reduction marker genes dsrAB and Desulfovibrio 16S by quantitative PCR in both. The catalog did not show a mapping from community sequence variants to genus names, so detection is done directly.

## What would support or refute it

Support: marker abundance is much higher in sediment than groundwater, especially in deeper zones. Refutation: markers are equally scarce in both materials, pointing to genuine rarity at the site.

## Data

Approach: experiment. Readiness: needs new measurements. Sediment and groundwater DNA with gene-targeted quantification must be generated; the catalog did not show taxonomy assignments for existing community records.
