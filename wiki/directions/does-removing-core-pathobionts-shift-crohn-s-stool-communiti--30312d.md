# Does removing core pathobionts shift Crohn's stool communities toward a non-IBD metabolite profile?

If the six core pathobionts are selectively removed from Crohn's stool communities grown ex vivo, does the community's metabolite profile shift toward that of non-IBD communities?

## Hypothesis

The joint species-metabolite axis separating Crohn's from non-IBD is driven by the pathobionts themselves. Selective removal moves a Crohn's community along that axis toward non-IBD, while removing a matched number of other species does not.

## Why it matters

The axis is a correlation. A shift on removal would show it is a causal handle, supporting pathobiont targeting; no shift would mean the pathobionts are markers of a disease state and not its drivers.

## How to test it

Culture stool from Crohn's and non-IBD donors anaerobically. In Crohn's cultures, remove the pathobionts with targeted phages or selective inhibitors, and in control cultures remove random abundance-matched non-pathobionts. Profile species by sequencing and metabolites by untargeted mass spectrometry at several time points, then project each culture onto the canonical axis built from the human cohort.

## What would support or refute it

Support: targeted removal shifts cultures toward the non-IBD end of the axis more than control removal. Refutation: both removals shift cultures equally or not at all, or the axis position is unchanged while the pathobionts fall.

## Data

Approach: experiment. Readiness: needs new measurements. The catalog shows cohort data only; ex vivo removal experiments with paired metabolomics have not been run.
