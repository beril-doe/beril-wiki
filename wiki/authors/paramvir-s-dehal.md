## Contributions

### Acinetobacter baylyi ADP1

The [[summaries/acinetobacter_adp1_explorer__REPORT]] project found that the ADP1 database holds 15 tables with 461,522 rows and 135 MB for ADP1 and 13 related genomes. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] Its genome_features table has 5,852 genes and 51 annotation columns across six modalities. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] TnSeq essentiality covers 58% of genes, and mutant growth fitness on 8 carbon sources covers 39%. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer]

The [[summaries/adp1_deletion_phenotypes__REPORT]] project found that the 8 carbon sources split into demanding, moderate and robust tiers. [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes] Mean growth ratio spans a 3.3-fold range, from 0.41 for urea to 1.36 for quinate. [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes] In the [[summaries/adp1_triple_essentiality__REPORT]] project, inverted fitness gave ROC AUC 0.700 in rich media across 1,933 genes. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] Log2 proteomics gave ROC AUC 0.743 in minimal media across 2,288 genes, the best overall continuous predictor. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]

The [[summaries/respiratory_chain_wiring__REPORT]] project found that the ADP1 branched respiratory chain has 62 genes across 8 subsystems. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] The cross-species test of NDH-2 compensation was a null result. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] The [[summaries/aromatic_catabolism_network__REPORT]] project assigned 44 of 51 genes (86%) to four subsystems plus regulators by co-fitness analysis. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

### Antimicrobial resistance across pangenomes

The [[summaries/amr_pangenome_atlas__REPORT]] project found that 30.3% of AMR genes are core, against a 46.8% baseline (OR=0.49). [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] The [[summaries/amr_environmental_resistome__REPORT]] project found that clinical species have a median of 5 AMR gene clusters, against 2 for soil, aquatic and host-associated species. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] The [[summaries/amr_strain_variation__REPORT]] project found that across 1,305 species and 180,025 genomes, 51.3% of AMR gene occurrences are rare, 41.3% variable and 7.5% fixed. [src: amr_strain_variation]

The [[summaries/amr_fitness_cost__REPORT]] project found a pooled fitness shift of +0.086 for AMR knockouts across 25 organisms, with all 25 positive. [src: amr_fitness_cost] The [[summaries/amr_cofitness_networks__REPORT]] project found that 769 of 801 AMR genes (96%) have at least one extra-operon cofitness partner at |r| > 0.3. [src: amr_cofitness_networks] That report warns the enrichment may reflect shared dispensability rather than co-regulation. [src: amr_cofitness_networks]

### Conservation and fitness

The [[summaries/essential_genome__REPORT]] project found that only 859 of 17,222 ortholog families (5.0%) are universally essential across 48 bacteria. [src: essential_genome] The [[summaries/fitness_effects_conservation__REPORT]] project found a 16-percentage-point gradient between essential and always-neutral genes across about 194,000 genes from 43 bacteria. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] The [[summaries/conservation_vs_fitness__REPORT]] project found essential genes more often core (86.1% vs 81.2%), significant in 18 of 33 organisms. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] The [[summaries/module_conservation__REPORT]] project found module genes 86.0% core versus 81.5% of all genes. [src: module_conservation]

The [[summaries/costly_dispensable_genes__REPORT]] project found that costly and dispensable genes are 7.45x more likely than costly and conserved genes to contain mobile-element keywords. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] The [[summaries/field_vs_lab_fitness__REPORT]] project, in one organism, found that the field-specific versus lab-specific contrast was not significant. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]

### Metals, environment and data resources

The [[summaries/metal_fitness_atlas__REPORT]] project built 383,349 gene × metal fitness records across 24 organisms and 14 metals. [src: metal_fitness_atlas] The [[summaries/metal_specificity__REPORT]] project found that 4,177 of 7,609 metal-important gene records (54.9%) are metal-specific. [src: metal_specificity] The [[summaries/counter_ion_effects__REPORT]] project found that 39.8% of metal-important gene records overlapped with NaCl-important genes. [src: counter_ion_effects] The [[summaries/ecotype_analysis__REPORT]] project found phylogeny dominated in 60.5% of species and environment in 39.5%. [src: ecotype_analysis] The [[summaries/ecotype_env_reanalysis__REPORT]] project found no stronger environmental correlation in environmental species than human-associated ones (p=0.83). [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] The [[summaries/paperblast_explorer__REPORT]] project found that Homo sapiens accounts for 46.7% of all gene-paper records. [src: paperblast_explorer]
