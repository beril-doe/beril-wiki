# Paramvir S. Dehal

ORCID: [0000-0001-5810-2497](https://orcid.org/0000-0001-5810-2497)

## Contributions

The [[summaries/acinetobacter_adp1_explorer__REPORT]] project analyzed a SQLite database of 15 tables, 461,522 rows, and 135 MB covering *Acinetobacter baylyi* ADP1 and 13 related genomes, and found that its `genome_features` table holds 5,852 genes with 51 annotation columns across six modalities — TnSeq essentiality at 58% coverage, FBA (flux balance analysis) metabolic flux at 15%, mutant growth fitness on 8 carbon sources at 39%, proteomics across 7 strains at 41%, pangenome classification at 54%, and COG/KO/Pfam/UniRef functional annotations at 34–55% — with no single gene covered by all six modalities [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer]. That project also documented 7 engineered ADP1 strains, wild-type plus 6 aromatic-amino-acid-pathway derivatives involving ΔaroF and ΔaroG or dgoA variants, all with proteomics data [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer]. [[summaries/adp1_triple_essentiality__REPORT]] tested whether FBA distinguishes growth-defective genes among 478 TnSeq-dispensable genes and found no association (chi-squared = 0.93, p = 0.63, 2 df), with defect rates of 73.1% (57/78) for FBA-essential, 73.5% (150/204) for FBA-variable, and 69.4% (136/196) for FBA-blocked genes, and a non-significant Kruskal-Wallis test on mean growth rates (H = 1.67, p = 0.43); the null persisted across Q10–Q35 thresholds, with chi-squared p-values of 0.985, 0.887, 0.405, 0.629, 0.313, 0.058, and 0.048 at Q10 through Q40 [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]. [[summaries/adp1_deletion_phenotypes__REPORT]] measured 2,034 genes across 8 carbon sources and found a three-tier essentiality landscape spanning a 3.3-fold range of mean growth ratios, from 0.41 on urea, where 97.9% of genes showed severe defects at ratio < 0.5, to 1.36 on quinate, where only 1.6% were defective [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes]. [[summaries/aromatic_catabolism_network__REPORT]] defined a 51-gene quinate-catabolism support network around the β-ketoadipate pathway and assigned 44/51 genes (86%) by co-fitness to four subsystems — 8 aromatic-pathway, 21 Complex I, 7 iron-acquisition, and 2 PQQ-biosynthesis genes — leaving 6 regulators and 7 unassigned [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]. [[summaries/respiratory_chain_wiring__REPORT]] mapped 62 respiratory-chain genes across 8 subsystems and found that quinate requires only Complex I, acetate requires Complex I, cytochrome bo3, ACIAD3522 and additional components, glucose requires no specific respiratory component, and lactate specifically requires cytochrome bo3 [src: respiratory_chain_wiring].

On conservation and fitness, [[summaries/conservation_vs_fitness__REPORT]] mapped 44 of 48 Fitness Browser organisms to pangenome species clades, producing 177,863 gene-to-cluster links at 100.0% median protein identity and 94.2% median gene coverage, of which 145,821 (82.0%) were core and 32,042 (18.0%) auxiliary, and identified 27,693 putative essential genes among 148,826 protein-coding genes across 33 organisms (18.6%, range 12.9–28.9%); four organisms — Cola, Kang, Magneto, and SB2B — were unmatched for lack of GTDB genomes, and Dyella79 was excluded for a locus-tag mismatch [src: conservation_vs_fitness]. [[summaries/fitness_effects_conservation__REPORT]] compared RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing) fitness with conservation across ~194,000 genes from 43 bacteria and found a conservation gradient — 82% core for essential genes (n=27,693) down to 66% core for always-neutral genes (n=94,889) — with fitness breadth only weakly associated with core status (Spearman rho=0.086) [src: fitness_effects_conservation]. [[summaries/conservation_fitness_synthesis__REPORT]] reported the same modest gradient over 194,216 protein-coding genes from 43 bacteria and a core-genome burden paradox in which 24.4% of core genes show positive fitness when deleted versus 19.9% of accessory genes, with core genes 1.78x more likely to have strong condition-specific phenotypes and 1.29x more likely to be trade-offs [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]. [[summaries/core_gene_tradeoffs__REPORT]] found this burden paradox to be function-specific, with core-gene burden differences of +6.2, +7.8, and +12.9 percentage points in Protein Metabolism, Motility, and RNA Metabolism but a reversal of −14.1 percentage points for Cell Wall genes, and identified 25,271 true trade-off genes (17.8%) enriched 1.29-fold in the core (p=1.2e-44) [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]. [[summaries/costly_dispensable_genes__REPORT]] characterized 5,526 costly-and-dispensable genes among 142,190 genes from 43 bacteria and found them 7.45x more likely than costly+conserved genes to carry mobile-element keywords (OR=7.45, p=4.6e-71), 50.8% SEED-annotated versus 74.9%, and 44.5% orphan versus 13.1%, with median ortholog breadth 15 versus 31 organisms (Mann–Whitney p=4.0e-99, r=0.233) [src: costly_dispensable_genes]. [[summaries/cog_analysis__REPORT]] examined COG (Clusters of Orthologous Groups) categories across 32 species, 9 phyla, and 357,623 genes and found novel/singleton genes enriched in COG L (mobile elements) by +10.88% with 100% consistency and core genes depleted in COG J (translation) by −4.65% with 97% consistency [src: cog_analysis]. [[summaries/essential_genome__REPORT]] analyzed 221,005 genes from 48 bacteria, found 15 gene families essential in all 48 organisms and 41,059 essential genes (18.6%), and classified 17,222 ortholog families as 859 (5.0%) universally essential, 4,799 (27.9%) variably essential, and 11,564 (67.1%) never essential [src: essential_genome]. [[summaries/fitness_modules__REPORT]] applied ICA (independent component analysis) to RB-TnSeq data from 32 organisms, recovering 1,116 stable modules of which 94.2% showed elevated within-module cofitness, and reported that a |weight| >= 0.3 threshold raised enrichment from 59% to 94%, while adding PFam domains together with lowering the enrichment-overlap threshold from 3 to 2 raised module annotation from 8% to 80%; that project stated that ortholog transfer remains the stronger gene-level function-prediction method [src: fitness_modules]. [[summaries/module_conservation__REPORT]] found module genes 86.0% core versus 81.5% for all genes (OR=1.46, p=1.6e-87), with 577 of 974 modules (59%) core, but no relationship between module-family breadth and conservation (rho=-0.01, p=0.914) [src: module_conservation]. [[summaries/cofitness_coinheritance__REPORT]] tested 2,253,491 cofit pairs against 22,534,910 prevalence-matched random pairs in 9 organisms and found only a weak pairwise co-occurrence signal — mean delta phi +0.011 across organisms, aggregate delta=+0.003 (Mann-Whitney p=1.66e-29) — with the Wilcoxon signed-rank test across organisms non-significant (W=9, p=0.13) [src: cofitness_coinheritance].

Across antimicrobial resistance, [[summaries/amr_pangenome_atlas__REPORT]] annotated 27,690 pangenome species and found 83,008 AMRFinderPlus hits over 82,908 clusters, 1,939 gene families, and 14,723 species, with 53.2% of species carrying at least one hit; AMR genes were 30.3% core versus a 46.8% baseline (OR=0.49, p≈0), beta-lactamases were 54.9% core while regulatory genes were 6.5% core, and blaTEM, tet(C), and ant(2'')-Ia were 0% core [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]. [[summaries/amr_fitness_cost__REPORT]] linked 1,352 AMR genes across 43 organisms to fitness data and found under non-antibiotic conditions a pooled AMR-versus-background shift of +0.086 [95% CI +0.074, +0.098], z = 14.3, p ~ 0, positive in 25/25 organisms with median Cohen's d 0.18 and I² = 54.3% (Q = 52.54, p = 0.0007); only two organisms, acidovorax_3H11 and Koxy, were significant at q<0.05 after false-discovery-rate (FDR) correction, which controls the expected share of false positives among significant results, and the report stated the effect is relative, not beneficial absolute growth [src: amr_fitness_cost]. [[summaries/amr_environmental_resistome__REPORT]] compared 14,723 species across 293K genomes and found clinical-source species carried a median of 5 AMR gene clusters versus 2 for soil, aquatic, and host-associated species (Kruskal–Wallis H = 781.9, p = 9.4×10⁻¹⁶⁷, η² = 0.056), with 13 of 15 pairwise comparisons significant after FDR correction, while noting the study is observational and limited by sampling, classification, and annotation [src: amr_environmental_resistome]. [[summaries/amr_strain_variation__REPORT]] analyzed 1,305 species and 180,025 genomes and found 51.3% of 37,444 AMR gene-species records rare (<=5% of strains), 41.3% variable, and 7.5% fixed, a median pairwise Jaccard distance — a distance based on shared versus differing gene repertoires — of 0.435, indicating that strains within the same species shared less than 60% of their AMR repertoire, and 1,517 resistance islands [src: amr_strain_variation]. [[summaries/amr_cofitness_networks__REPORT]] examined 28 organisms and found 192 of 801 AMR genes with fitness data (24%) assigned to ICA modules, AMR-containing modules larger at a median of 46 versus 27 genes (p = 1.7×10⁻⁸), 769/801 (96%) AMR genes with an extra-operon cofitness partner at |r| > 0.3, and no module-size difference between efflux and enzymatic mechanisms (both median 48, p = 0.91), while cautioning that enrichment may reflect shared laboratory dispensability rather than co-regulation [src: amr_cofitness_networks].

On metals and environment, [[summaries/metal_fitness_atlas__REPORT]] assembled 559 experiments across 31 organisms and 16 metals with 383,349 gene × metal fitness records and found metal-important genes 87.4% core versus 76.9% baseline (OR=2.08, p=4.3e-162) across 22 organisms and 14 metals, reversing its initial accessory-enrichment hypothesis and contrasting with a prior *Desulfovibrio vulgaris* Hildenborough result of 71.2% core [src: metal_fitness_atlas]. [[summaries/metal_cross_resistance__REPORT]] analyzed 452 metal experiments across 37 organisms and 14 metals and found 311/317 (98.1%) organism-metal-pair fitness correlations positive and 99.1% significant, with strongest consensus for Fe-Zn (mean r = 0.61, n = 6) and Co-Ni (r = 0.56, n = 28), and aluminium the most independent metal at mean r = 0.34 [src: metal_cross_resistance]. [[summaries/metal_specificity__REPORT]] classified 7,609 metal-important gene records from 24 organisms and found 4,177 (54.9%) metal-specific, 2,888 (38.0%) general sick, and 544 (7.2%) metal+stress, with the metal-specific fraction moving to ~41% at a 2% sick threshold and ~67% at 10%; seven of 31 metal-tested organisms could not be processed owing to locusId format mismatches, excluding 5,229 records [src: metal_specificity]. [[summaries/counter_ion_effects__REPORT]] tested chloride confounding across 71 NaCl experiments and 10,821 metal-important gene records and found 4,304 (39.8%) also NaCl-important — 36.7% after excluding a *Synechococcus elongatus* outlier contributing 565/638 shared genes — but no evidence that chloride dose is the primary driver, with Metal Fitness Atlas core enrichment robust to removing shared-stress genes [src: counter_ion_effects]. [[summaries/bacdive_metal_validation__REPORT]] matched 42,227 of 97,334 BacDive strains (43.4%) to 6,426 GTDB species and found heavy-metal-contamination isolates one standard deviation above the environmental baseline (median 0.240 versus 0.187, Cohen's d = +1.00, p=0.006), noting that n=10 makes the estimate imprecise [src: bacdive_metal_validation]. [[summaries/bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance__REPORT]] tested 10 phenotype features over 3,994 species with at least 5 features and found 7 significant after FDR correction, the largest being Gram stain (d = -0.610, q = 4.0e-60), but reported the association is almost entirely between-lineage and confounded with phylogeny [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]. [[summaries/lab_field_ecology__REPORT]] detected 14 of 26 Fitness Browser genera in Oak Ridge groundwater across 108 sites and found five genera significantly associated with uranium after BH-FDR correction, including *Herbaspirillum* increasing (rho=+0.336, q=0.001) and *Caulobacter* decreasing, with aggregate tolerance only suggestive [src: lab_field_ecology]. [[summaries/field_vs_lab_fitness__REPORT]] classified 757 *Desulfovibrio vulgaris* Hildenborough experiments into six categories and concluded that condition type matters less than fitness importance for predicting conservation, and separately found that ENIGMA CORAL contains 47 tables but no DvH fitness data [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]. [[summaries/enigma_contamination_functional_potential__REPORT]] built a workflow over 108 ENIGMA samples and found no robust monotonic genus-level association between contamination and broad functional scores, with exploratory defense associations sensitive to coverage, covariates, taxonomic resolution, and multiple-testing correction [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]. [[summaries/ecotype_analysis__REPORT]] tested 172 species and found median partial correlations of 0.0025 for environment versus 0.0143 for phylogeny, with no significant environment effect in 156 species (90.7%) [src: ecotype_analysis]; [[summaries/ecotype_env_reanalysis__REPORT]] found environmental species (n=37, median 0.051) did not exceed human-associated species (n=93, median 0.084), with U=1536, p=0.83 [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]; and [[summaries/pangenome_openness__REPORT]] found openness unrelated to environment effect (rho = -0.05, p = 0.54) or phylogeny effect (rho = 0.03, p = 0.73) [src: pangenome_openness]. [[summaries/env_embedding_explorer__REPORT]] characterized 64-dimensional AlphaEarth embeddings for 83,287 genomes, covering 28.4% of 293,059 genomes, and found a 3.4x geographic gradient for environmental samples (0.27 at <100 km to 0.90 at >10,000 km) versus a flatter 2.0x gradient for human-associated samples [src: env_embedding_explorer].

Several projects characterized literature and metabolic resources on the KBase Data Lakehouse. [[summaries/paperblast_explorer__REPORT]] inventoried the `kescience_paperblast` collection of 12.4 million rows across 14 tables and found *Homo sapiens* accounts for 46.7% of gene-paper records, the top five organisms for 72.8%, and the top 1,000 of 20,723 organisms for 94.2%, with 551K of 841K genes (65.6%) linked to exactly one paper [src: paperblast_explorer]. [[summaries/webofmicrobes_explorer__REPORT]] characterized a 2018 Web of Microbes snapshot and found four action codes over 10,744 observations — 742 `D`, 1,023 `N` for the control, and 1,338 `I`, 1,155 `E`, 7,509 `N` for organisms — with `E` and `I` mutually exclusive and no organism consumption action present, although Kosina et al. (2018) describes "decrease" as valid [src: webofmicrobes_explorer]. [[summaries/fw300_metabolic_consistency__REPORT]] cross-referenced *Pseudomonas* FW300-N2E3 across four databases and found 58 produced or increased WoM metabolites (27 emerged, 31 increased), of which 21 were testable: 17/21 (81%) fully concordant, 4/21 (19%) partially concordant, none fully discordant, mean concordance 0.94, with Fitness Browser concordant 21/21 and GapMind 13/13 while BacDive utilization matched only 3/7 [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]. [[summaries/essential_metabolome__REPORT]] used GapMind across 7 organisms and found 17 of 18 amino-acid biosynthesis pathways present in all 7, with serine biosynthesis present in 6 of 7 (85.7%) and absent in DvH, and noted the analysis is a pilot because only 7 of 45 organisms mapped successfully [src: essential_metabolome]. [[summaries/pathway_capability_dependency__REPORT]] classified 161 organism-pathway pairs across 7 bacteria and 23 GapMind pathways as 57 (35.4%) Active Dependency, 66 (41.0%) Latent Capability, 24 (14.9%) Incomplete but Important, and 14 (8.7%) Missing, using a composite score weighting essentiality 40%, fitness breadth 30%, and fitness magnitude 30% [src: pathway_capability_dependency].
