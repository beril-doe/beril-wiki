## Contributions

[[summaries/harvard_forest_warming__REPORT]] analyzed a 25-year +5°C soil-warming experiment at Harvard Forest Barre Woods (NMDC `nmdc:sty-11-8ws97026`; 42 biosamples) [src: harvard_forest_warming]. It found a real but modest community-level response that reproduced the published Actinobacteria-up/Acidobacteria-down signal [src: harvard_forest_warming]. In organic soil, Actinobacteria rose from 0.249 to 0.315 (log2 FC +0.341, q=0.049) and Acidobacteria fell from 0.035 to 0.024 (log2 FC −0.549, q=0.049) [src: harvard_forest_warming]. Mineral soil showed no phylum-level changes after false-discovery-rate (FDR) correction [src: harvard_forest_warming]. PERMANOVA (permutational multivariate analysis of variance) on Bray–Curtis genus distances attributed 7.6% of variance to treatment (p=0.069), 30.6% to horizon (p=0.0002), and 41% to the treatment × horizon factor (p=0.0002) [src: harvard_forest_warming]. The paired n=25 subset confounded horizon with incubation, because every organic sample was incubated and every mineral sample was direct [src: harvard_forest_warming]. Once that confound was removed, direct-sample analyses gave comparable DNA and RNA treatment R² (10-13%), so the hypothesis that RNA composition shifts more than DNA under warming was not supported [src: harvard_forest_warming]. The report notes that this is in tension with the larger transcript-level effect reported by Roy Chowdhury et al. (2021) at the same site [src: harvard_forest_warming]. It also notes that differences in sampling timepoint, replication and analytical pipeline complicate the comparison [src: harvard_forest_warming].

The same project tested a curated 62-KO (KEGG Orthology gene family) carbon-cycling list for enrichment among heated-up KOs [src: harvard_forest_warming]. The list was enriched among heated-up DNA KOs in organic soil: 5 of 57 hits versus 428 of 12,806, Fisher odds ratio 2.78, one-sided p=0.042 [src: harvard_forest_warming]. There was no enrichment in the other horizon × pool combinations, and no individual RNA KO survived FDR (q<0.10) [src: harvard_forest_warming]. In the RNA pool, pmoA (K10944) and pmoB (K10945) were directionally positive in both horizons; for example, pmoA had log2 FC +0.730 in organic soil (p=0.009) and +0.743 in mineral soil (p=0.029) [src: harvard_forest_warming]. Glyoxylate-cycle genes aceA/icl and aceB/glcB increased in heated mineral RNA (p=0.037 each) [src: harvard_forest_warming]. All of these signals were nominal and none survived FDR across 14K KOs [src: harvard_forest_warming]. Heated mineral samples had fewer detectable metabolites (155 ± 4 versus 167 ± 9 ChEBI identifications; Mann–Whitney p=0.012), while organic samples showed the same direction without significance (p=0.209) [src: harvard_forest_warming]. No individual metabolite passed Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate (BH-FDR) correction [src: harvard_forest_warming]. The report proposes compositional dilution, substrate depletion, and compositional turnover over 25 years as explanations for the RNA-pool results [src: harvard_forest_warming]. It presents all three as author-proposed explanations rather than tested results [src: harvard_forest_warming].

[[summaries/snipe_defense_system__REPORT]] surveyed the SNIPE phage-defense system across the KBase Data Lakehouse pangenome [src: snipe_defense_system]. It found DUF4041/PF13250 in 4,572 gene clusters across 1,696 species and 33 bacterial and archaeal phyla, which exceeds the >500 homologues reported in the original paper [src: snipe_defense_system]. Of these clusters, 13.3% (604) were core, 30.7% were accessory, and 56.1% (2,566) were singleton [src: snipe_defense_system]. The report reads this as consistent with mobile defense-island carriage, but mobility is inferred from pangenome category composition rather than measured directly [src: snipe_defense_system]. The project identified PF13455 (Mug113) as the SNIPE nuclease family, distinct from canonical GIY-YIG PF01541 [src: snipe_defense_system]. It found zero gene clusters containing both DUF4041/PF13250 and PF01541 [src: snipe_defense_system]. The report records two conflicting species counts for IPR025280: 2,466 in its domain table and 1,373 in its Methods [src: snipe_defense_system]. The source gives no way to reconcile them [src: snipe_defense_system]. Among species with AlphaEarth embeddings (n=1,069 SNIPE-bearing versus n=13,977 non-SNIPE), 22 of 64 dimensions differed significantly under Welch's t-tests with Bonferroni correction [src: snipe_defense_system]. The largest effect was dimension A19, with Cohen's d = 0.26 and p = 5.0e-14, and effect sizes of |d| = 0.11–0.26 indicated a modest environmental shift rather than strict habitat segregation [src: snipe_defense_system]. AlphaEarth coverage was 28.4% of genomes and was biased toward environmental isolates with latitude/longitude metadata [src: snipe_defense_system].

Using RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing) data from the Fitness Browser, the SNIPE project found the strongest *E. coli* K-12 ManXYZ fitness defects on D-glucosamine and D-mannose [src: snipe_defense_system]. On D-mannose, *manX*, *manY* and *manZ* averaged -2.75, -3.00 and -2.74 [src: snipe_defense_system]. The report reads these results as contradicting the UniProt "fructose-specific" annotation for ManX [src: snipe_defense_system]. It frames SNIPE as giving phage resistance without the metabolic cost of losing *man* transporter genes [src: snipe_defense_system]. It offers no direct comparative fitness measurement, so this remains a mechanistic proposal [src: snipe_defense_system]. The project also identified one full two-domain SNIPE protein in *Methanococcus maripaludis* JJ (MMJJ_RS01635) [src: snipe_defense_system]. Its minimum fitness was -1.16 on formate/acetate, and it was dispensable under most conditions [src: snipe_defense_system].
