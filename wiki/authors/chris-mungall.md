# Chris Mungall

ORCID: [0000-0002-6601-2165](https://orcid.org/0000-0002-6601-2165)

## Contributions

The [[summaries/harvard_forest_warming__REPORT]] project analyzed a 25-year +5°C soil-warming experiment at Harvard Forest Barre Woods (NMDC `nmdc:sty-11-8ws97026`, 42 biosamples) and found a real but modest community response reproducing the published Actinobacteria-up/Acidobacteria-down signal, with a structured functional-gene response of approximately 12% R² in DNA and 10–11% in RNA [src: harvard_forest_warming]. In 14 control and 14 heated direct samples, that project found kraken2 read-based Actinobacteria relative abundance rising from 0.249 to 0.315 in organic soil (log2 FC +0.341, q=0.049) and Acidobacteria falling from 0.035 to 0.024 (log2 FC −0.549, q=0.049), while mineral soil showed no phylum-level changes after FDR (false discovery rate) correction [src: harvard_forest_warming]. PERMANOVA on Bray–Curtis genus distances attributed 7.6% of variance to treatment (p=0.069), 30.6% to horizon (p=0.0002), and 41% to the treatment × horizon four-cell factor (p=0.0002) [src: harvard_forest_warming]. The project reported that in the paired n=25 subset treatment pseudo-F was 3.12 in DNA (R²=11.9%, p=0.020) versus 0.76 in RNA (R²=3.2%, p=0.60), but that this subset confounded horizon with incubation because every organic sample was incubated and every mineral sample was direct; once that confound was removed, DNA and RNA responses were comparable and the proposed hypothesis that RNA shifts more than DNA was not supported [src: harvard_forest_warming]. That project qualified this null result, treating comparable long-term DNA and RNA response magnitude as compatible with, but not excluding, an earlier transient in which RNA could lead DNA, and noting that the single-timepoint RNA pool may include diurnal and microspatial variation from moisture pulses, root-exudate availability, and temperature conditions unrelated to chronic warming [src: harvard_forest_warming].

At the gene level, the same project found a curated 62-KO carbon-cycling list — KOs being KEGG Orthology functional gene families — enriched among heated-up DNA KOs in organic soil (5 of 57 carbon-cycling hits versus 428 of 12,806 other hits; Fisher odds ratio 2.78, one-sided p=0.042), with no such enrichment in DNA × mineral (0 of 57 versus 2 of 12,806; odds ratio 0.0, p=1.0) or in either RNA stratum (0 of 57 versus 0 of 14,245; NaN, p=1.0) [src: harvard_forest_warming]. It reported directionally positive RNA signals for particulate methane monooxygenase — pmoA (K10944) log2 FC +0.730 in organic (p=0.009) and +0.743 in mineral (p=0.029); pmoB (K10945) +0.669 organic (p=0.012) and +0.880 mineral (p=0.054) — and noted these nominal signals did not survive FDR across 14K KOs [src: harvard_forest_warming]. Glyoxylate-cycle genes aceA/icl (K01637) and aceB/glcB (K01638) increased in heated mineral RNA (log2 FC +0.460 and +0.268, p=0.037 each), chitinase chiA (K01183) increased in heated organic RNA (log2 FC +0.236, p=0.074), and lpdA (K00382) decreased (log2 FC −0.161, p=0.021) [src: harvard_forest_warming]. Warming responses were mostly horizon-specific: organic-versus-mineral KO log2 FC correlations were Pearson r=0.075 (p=1.78e-17) and Spearman ρ=0.216 (p=2e-134) for DNA and Pearson r=0.034 (p=6e-5) and Spearman ρ=0.120 (p=4e-47) for RNA, with approximately 39% of DNA KOs organic-only, mineral-only, or sign-flipping, though the curated carbon-cycling list was not differentially enriched in horizon-specific classes (odds ratio <1, p>0.87 everywhere) [src: harvard_forest_warming]. Heated mineral samples had fewer detectable metabolites (155 ± 4 ChEBI identifications per sample versus 167 ± 9 in controls, Mann–Whitney p=0.012), while organic samples trended the same way without significance (160 ± 13 versus 173 ± 6, p=0.209), and no individual metabolite passed BH-FDR [src: harvard_forest_warming]. The project recorded that `abiotic_features` was all zeros because of an NMDC parsing artifact, leaving the +5°C treatment label as the only environmental contrast, that a single 2017-05-24 sampling date precluded seasonal effects, and that metatranscriptome KO counts came from contig annotations representing transcript-pool composition rather than TPM-quantified expression [src: harvard_forest_warming].

The [[summaries/snipe_defense_system__REPORT]] project evaluated the SNIPE defense system across the KBase Data Lakehouse pangenome and corrected its domain architecture, identifying PF13250 as the Pfam assignment for DUF4041 (InterPro IPR025280) and PF13455/Mug113 — a member of GIY-YIG clan CL0418 but distinct from canonical GIY-YIG PF01541 — as the SNIPE nuclease family, with *E. coli* SNIPE protein A0A0A1A5Z2 at 558 aa carrying PF13250 at 232–333 and PF13455 at 443–520 [src: snipe_defense_system]. That project found zero surveyed gene clusters containing both DUF4041/PF13250 and PF01541, and of 4,572 DUF4041-containing clusters only 54 carried the description "Meiotically up-regulated gene 113," leading it to recommend searching PF13250, DUF4041, or T5orf172 together with PF13455 rather than using PF01541 as the nuclease marker [src: snipe_defense_system]. It found DUF4041/PF13250 in 4,572 gene clusters across 1,696 species and 33 bacterial and archaeal phyla — substantially exceeding the >500 homologues reported in the original paper — with the largest species counts in Pseudomonadota (556), Actinomycetota (334), Bacillota_A (275), Bacillota (206), Bacteroidota (114), Nitrospirota (45), Cyanobacteriota (28), and Planctomycetota (22) [src: snipe_defense_system]. Of those clusters, 13.3% were core, 30.7% accessory, and 56.1% singleton (86.7% combined accessory-plus-singleton), which the project read as strong support for frequent gain and loss consistent with mobile defense-island carriage, a finding it slots into [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] [src: snipe_defense_system]. It estimated 3,675/4,572 clusters (80.4%) as a high-confidence T5orf172-or-DUF4041 SNIPE-family set, with the remaining ~20% (897 clusters) carrying primary annotations such as histidine kinase (319) and seryl-tRNA aminoacylation (238) that may reflect secondary-domain detection or annotation noise [src: snipe_defense_system].

The same project used RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing) data from the Deutschbauer/Price Fitness Browser — 48 organisms, 228K genes, 27M fitness scores, 7,552 experiments — covering 168 *E. coli* K-12 experiments per ManXYZ gene, and reported worst/average fitness and counts of strongly deleterious conditions of −3.93/−0.249 with 6 below −1 and 4 below −2 for *manX*, −3.82/−0.171 with 10 below −1 and 4 below −2 for *manY*, and −4.14/−0.082 with 7 below −1 and 4 below −2 for *manZ* [src: snipe_defense_system]. It found the strongest defects on D-glucosamine (*manX* −3.79, *manY* −2.79, *manZ* −3.63) and D-mannose (−2.75, −3.00, −2.74), with cofitness correlations of 0.851 (*manX*↔*manZ*), 0.705 (*manX*↔*manY*), and 0.725 (*manY*↔*manZ*) supporting single-operon operation, and found these measurements contradict the UniProt "fructose-specific" ManX annotation, since on D-fructose the three genes scored −0.22, +0.15, and −0.07 while *fruA* scored −1.44 — evidence it slots into [[concepts/condition-specific-fitness]] [src: snipe_defense_system]. Comparing 1,069 SNIPE-bearing species with 13,977 non-SNIPE species having AlphaEarth embeddings, it found Welch's t-tests with Bonferroni correction significant in 22 of 64 dimensions at p < 0.05, the largest effect at dimension A19 (Cohen's d = 0.26, p = 5.0e-14), with effect sizes of |d| = 0.11–0.26 indicating a modest but consistent environmental shift rather than strict habitat segregation; the project assigned no named biological interpretation to A19 and noted AlphaEarth coverage was 28.4% of genomes and biased toward environmental isolates with latitude/longitude metadata [src: snipe_defense_system]. It found ManXYZ present only in *E. coli* K-12 among Fitness Browser organisms and one full two-domain SNIPE protein in *Methanococcus maripaludis* JJ (MMJJ_RS01635, carrying PF13250, PF13455, and PF10544, 129 experiments, minimum fitness −1.16 on formate/acetate, dispensable under most conditions), while PF13455 occurred in 7 genes across 6 organisms with only *M. maripaludis* showing the complete architecture [src: snipe_defense_system]. From the PhageFoundry database — 188 *E. coli* strains, 96 phages, 17,672 possible binary infection outcomes (3,929 positive, 13,743 negative), and a model using 1,582 gene-cluster presence/absence features achieving AUC = 0.883 and accuracy = 84.3% — it reported that Lambdavirus phage 411_P1 infected 1 of 188 strains (0.5%) versus 43.4% for Myoviridae, 13.0% for Podoviridae, and 9.7% for Siphoviridae, and read this as consistent with widespread ManYZ variation or loss [src: snipe_defense_system]. It detected DUF4041 in *Klebsiella* (1 DUF4041, 1 GIY-YIG, 98 nuclease, 42 restriction, 2 abortive infection) but not in *Acinetobacter*, *P. aeruginosa*, or *P. viridiflava*, with the three *Klebsiella* DUF4041 proteins (JJW41_17025, KFB13_RS08150, KFB31_RS01490) each 558 aa [src: snipe_defense_system]. The project noted that eggNOG Pfam annotations may miss divergent homologues, that no *Klebsiella* SNIPE or ManYZ fitness data were available, that a planned comparison of COG (Clusters of Orthologous Groups) category V defense-gene density could not be completed via the REST API because it required Spark Connect for a 93M × 132M row join, that NMDC metagenome ecosystem analysis matched only 2 samples, and that the environmental result establishes statistical association rather than a causal habitat mechanism while the archaeal example does not establish the Enterobacterales phage-lambda mechanism in archaea [src: snipe_defense_system].
