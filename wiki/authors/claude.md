## Contributions

The [[summaries/prophage_amr_comobilization__REPORT]] project (Prophage-AMR Co-mobilization Atlas) analyzed associations between antimicrobial-resistance (AMR) genes and prophage markers across the GTDB pangenome, cataloguing 83,008 AMR gene clusters and 3,465,244 broad prophage marker clusters (strict prophage-marker count 1,261,929), and found that prophage markers were 83.8% accessory and 53.8% singleton while AMR genes were 69.7% accessory and 36.1% singleton; of 27,702 species, 14,669 (52.9%) carried both AMR and prophage markers [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. Sampling 36,041 AMR gene instances from 1,953 genomes across 100 species, the project found 20,073 (55.7%) on contigs carrying strict prophage markers, 12,026 (33.4%) within 50 genes of a prophage marker, 7,137 (19.8%) within 20 genes, 3,731 (10.4%) within 10 genes and 1,991 (5.5%) within 5 genes, with a median distance of 34 genes among co-localized AMR genes [src: prophage_amr_comobilization].

On the gene-level proximity question, the project reported that AMR genes within 10 genes of a prophage marker were 67.6% accessory versus 65.5% for distal AMR genes, a Fisher's exact odds ratio of 1.10 (one-sided p=0.005; bootstrap 95% CI [1.024, 1.185]) corresponding to a modest 2.1 percentage point difference [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. It further found the effect to be threshold-dependent — odds ratios of 0.78 at 3 genes, 0.92 at 5, 1.10 at 10, 1.19 at 15 and 1.28 at 50 genes — and heterogeneous across species, with only 33 of 74 testable species above an odds ratio of 1 and a median species-level odds ratio of 0.85 [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. At the species level, the project found prophage marker density positively associated with AMR repertoire breadth across 4,770 species (Spearman rho=0.572, p<10^-300), a log-log slope of 0.823 (SE=0.018, p<10^-300, R²=0.30) which the report states means a 10-fold increase in prophage density predicts a ~6.6-fold increase in AMR breadth, and a partial Spearman rho of 0.464 (p=1.0×10^-253) after controlling for genome count; the association held in Pseudomonadota (rho=0.54), Bacillota_A (rho=0.55), Bacillota (rho=0.40), Bacteroidota (rho=0.59) and Actinomycetota (rho=0.29) [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. The report states that this correlation does not establish phage-mediated AMR transfer [src: prophage_amr_comobilization].

The same project attempted but could not complete a fitness-cost comparison, because the fitness browser contains RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing) data for only 48 model organisms with poor overlap with the GTDB pangenome species analyzed, leaving open whether prophage-proximal AMR genes carry distinct fitness costs [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. It recorded methodological caveats: distances were computed from ordinal gene positions parsed from gene_id formats rather than base-pair coordinates; prophage markers were identified by keyword and Pfam matching in bakta_annotations rather than by dedicated predictors such as PHASTER or geNomad, which may admit false positives including phage-defense systems and miss divergent prophages; the co-localization analysis sampled 20 genomes per species rather than all 293K genomes; and core/accessory labels depend on species-level pangenome calling with motupan [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. Its findings feed [[concepts/environmental-resistome]], [[concepts/phage-defense-syndromes-and-arms-race]] and [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] [src: prophage_amr_comobilization].
