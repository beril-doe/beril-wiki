## Contributions

[[summaries/prophage_amr_comobilization__REPORT]] built a pangenome-wide inventory of antimicrobial-resistance (AMR) genes and prophage markers across GTDB and found 83,008 AMR gene clusters and 3,465,244 broad prophage marker clusters, with a strict prophage-marker count of 1,261,929 [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. The project found prophage markers were 83.8% accessory and 53.8% singleton while AMR genes were 69.7% accessory and 36.1% singleton, and that 14,669 of 27,702 species (52.9%) carried both marker classes [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. Sampling 20 genomes per species across 100 species, it examined 36,041 AMR gene instances from 1,953 genomes and found 20,073 (55.7%) on contigs carrying strict prophage markers, 12,026 (33.4%) within 50 genes of a prophage marker, 7,137 (19.8%) within 20 genes, 3,731 (10.4%) within 10 genes, and 1,991 (5.5%) within 5 genes, with a median distance of 34 genes among co-localized AMR genes [src: prophage_amr_comobilization].

Testing whether prophage proximity tracks accessory status, the project found AMR genes within 10 genes of a prophage marker were 67.6% accessory versus 65.5% for distal AMR genes, a Fisher's exact odds ratio of 1.10 (one-sided p=0.005, bootstrap 95% CI [1.024, 1.185]) that it reported as statistically significant but modest — a 2.1 percentage point difference [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. It found the effect was threshold-dependent, with odds ratios of 0.78 at 3 genes, 0.92 at 5 genes, 1.10 at 10 genes, 1.19 at 15 genes, and 1.28 at 50 genes, and that only 33 of 74 testable species had species-level odds ratios above 1, with a median species-level odds ratio of 0.85 [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. The report offered the reversal at very close range as possibly reflecting core phage components rather than recently acquired cargo, and the broader-threshold association as possibly capturing genomic islands, stating explicitly that these are interpretations and not direct tests of mobilization mechanisms [src: prophage_amr_comobilization].

At the species level the project found prophage marker density positively associated with AMR repertoire breadth across 4,770 species (Spearman rho=0.572, p<10^-300), with a log-log regression slope of 0.823 (SE=0.018), p<10^-300 and R²=0.30, reporting that a 10-fold increase in prophage density predicts a ~6.6-fold increase in AMR breadth; after controlling for genome count the partial Spearman correlation remained rho=0.464 (p=1.0×10^-253) [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. It found the association significant in all five reported major phyla — Pseudomonadota (rho=0.54), Bacillota_A (rho=0.55), Bacillota (rho=0.40), Bacteroidota (rho=0.59) and Actinomycetota (rho=0.29) — and interpreted this cross-phylogenetic consistency as evidence against a purely phylogenetic explanation, while stating that the correlation does not establish phage-mediated AMR transfer and is consistent with either direct transduction or independent acquisition by species with high recombination potential [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. These results feed [[concepts/environmental-resistome]] and [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] [src: prophage_amr_comobilization].

The project reported a null result on fitness cost: the proposed comparison could not be tested because the KBase Data Lakehouse fitness browser contains RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing) data for only 48 model organisms, with poor overlap with the GTDB pangenome species analyzed, leaving open whether prophage-proximal AMR genes carry distinct fitness costs [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. The report recorded further limits: distances came from ordinal gene positions parsed from gene_id formats rather than base-pair coordinates, so reported gene distances may differ from true genomic distances; prophage markers were called by keyword and Pfam matching in bakta_annotations rather than dedicated predictors such as PHASTER or geNomad, which may admit false positives including phage-defense systems and miss divergent prophages — a distinction it carries into [[concepts/phage-defense-syndromes-and-arms-race]]; and core/accessory labels depend on species-level pangenome calling with motupan, so the same gene can have different conservation status in different species [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. It proposed applying geNomad or PHASTER to the KBase Data Lakehouse genomes, using scaffold sequences for base-pair-resolution distances, revisiting fitness costs as fitness-browser coverage expands, separating phage from plasmid and ICE mobilization, and analyzing all available genomes from Klebsiella pneumoniae, Acinetobacter baumannii, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, and Escherichia coli [src: prophage_amr_comobilization].
