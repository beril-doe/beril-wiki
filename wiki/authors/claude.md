## Contributions

[[summaries/prophage_amr_comobilization__REPORT]] tested associations between antimicrobial-resistance (AMR) genes and prophage markers across the GTDB pangenome [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. It identified 83,008 AMR gene clusters and 3,465,244 broad prophage marker clusters, of which 1,261,929 were strict prophage markers, and found that 14,669 of 27,702 species (52.9%) carried both [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. In the top-100 AMR-burdened species (1,953 genomes, 20 sampled per species), the project analyzed 36,041 AMR gene instances [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. It found that 20,073 (55.7%) lay on contigs carrying strict prophage markers, 3,731 (10.4%) lay within 10 genes of a marker, and the median distance among co-localized AMR genes was 34 genes [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. The project computed distances from ordinal gene positions rather than base-pair coordinates, and it identified prophage markers by keyword and Pfam matching rather than with dedicated predictors such as PHASTER or geNomad [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. The project noted that this approach may include phage-defense false positives and may miss divergent prophages [src: prophage_amr_comobilization].

The project found that AMR genes within 10 genes of a prophage marker were 67.6% accessory, compared with 65.5% for distal genes (Fisher's exact odds ratio 1.10, one-sided p=0.005, bootstrap 95% confidence interval [1.024, 1.185]) [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. It described this effect as modest, a 2.1 percentage point difference [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. The project noted that its core and accessory labels depend on species-level pangenome calling with motupan, so the same gene can have different conservation status in different species [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. The project found that the effect depended on the distance threshold, with an odds ratio of 0.78 at 3 genes and 1.28 at 50 genes [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. Only 33 of 74 testable species had odds ratios above 1, and the median species-level odds ratio was 0.85 [src: prophage_amr_comobilization].

At species level, the project found that prophage marker density was positively associated with AMR repertoire breadth across 4,770 species (Spearman rho=0.572, p<10^-300) [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. A log-log regression gave a slope of 0.823 (SE=0.018) and R²=0.30, and the partial correlation controlling for genome count was rho=0.464 (p=1.0×10^-253) [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. The project found the association significant in all five reported major phyla, with rho ranging from 0.29 in Actinomycetota to 0.59 in Bacteroidota [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. The report stated that the correlation does not establish phage-mediated AMR transfer, because species with open pangenomes may independently accumulate both prophages and AMR genes [src: prophage_amr_comobilization].

The project could not run its planned fitness-cost comparison (H3) [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. The project reported that the KBase Data Lakehouse fitness browser holds RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing) data for only 48 model organisms, and that these overlap poorly with the GTDB pangenome species analyzed [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. Whether prophage-proximal AMR genes carry distinct fitness costs therefore remained untested [src: prophage_amr_comobilization].
