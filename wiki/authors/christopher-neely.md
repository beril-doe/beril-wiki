# Christopher Neely

ORCID: [0000-0002-2620-8948](https://orcid.org/0000-0002-2620-8948)

## Contributions

[[summaries/metabolic_capability_dependency__REPORT]] compared GapMind genomic pathway completeness with Fitness Browser gene-fitness data, using SEED subsystem annotations as a pathway-membership proxy, across 1,695 pathway-organism pairs from 48 organisms [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. It found that 267 (15.8%) were latent capabilities (complete but fitness-neutral under tested conditions), 547 (32.3%) were intermediate and 881 (51.9%) were active dependencies, with pathway category predicting dependency class (χ²=163.6, df=4, p=2.5×10⁻³⁴) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. In that project, 217 of 892 (24.3%) carbon source utilization pathways were latent against 48 of 735 (6.5%) amino acid biosynthesis pathways, which the report expresses as carbon pathways being 3.7× more likely to be latent; the report's prediction that carbon-pathway genes are preferentially lost in nutrient-rich environments was not tested [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. A sensitivity analysis across 16 threshold combinations gave latent fractions from 4.7% to 21.1% (SD = 5.9 percentage points) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. The project reported several caveats: SEED-proxy mapping can introduce false-positive genes that dilute fitness signal, and laboratory-biased fitness conditions may inflate the latent fraction [src: metabolic_capability_dependency].

The same project found that pathway-level conservation did not distinguish latent from active pathways (mean 0.869 vs 0.829; Mann–Whitney U p = 0.94, rank-biserial r = 0.052), so H2a was not supported [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. At the clade level, the latent capability rate correlated positively with pangenome openness (Spearman ρ = 0.69, p = 0.0004, n = 22 clades), supporting H2b, and the report reads the combined results as mixed support for the Black Queen framework [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. The project also clustered within-species pathway profiles and found that all 10 target species had silhouette scores above 0.2, ranging from 0.349 for *PALSA-747* sp. to 0.894 for *Salmonella enterica* [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Clusters were associated with isolation environment in *Salmonella enterica* (χ²=1570.2, df=25, p<0.0001) and *Phenylobacterium* sp. (χ²=12.2, df=1, p=0.0005) but not in the marine organisms tested [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. The report states that these associations are observational and may be confounded by phylogenetic structure, because no phylogenetic correction was performed [src: metabolic_capability_dependency].

[[summaries/nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology__REPORT]] combined NMDC community taxonomy and liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry (LC-MS) metabolomics with GapMind pathway completeness for GTDB pangenome species from the KBase Data Lakehouse [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]. It computed community-weighted completeness for 220 samples × 80 pathways, and all samples passed the 30% bridge-coverage quality-control threshold, with mean coverage of 94.6% [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]. Across 13 testable amino-acid pathways, the project found that 11 of 13 (85%) correlated negatively with ambient metabolite intensity, the direction the Black Queen Hypothesis (BQH) predicts (sign test p = 0.011) [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]. Leucine (r = −0.390, q = 0.022, n = 62) and arginine (r = −0.297, q = 0.049, n = 80) were significant after Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction (FDR, the expected share of false positives among significant calls) [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]. Methionine showed the largest effect (r = −0.496, n = 18, q = 0.117) but was underpowered, and tyrosine was an anti-BQH outlier (r = +0.419, ns), for which the report offers phenylalanine hydroxylation as an untested explanation [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]. Glutamine (n = 4) and proline (n = 9) were skipped for insufficient metabolomics coverage [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology].

That project's robustness checks found that leucine stayed significant in a soil-only analysis (r = −0.390, q = 0.022, n = 62) [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]. Arginine stayed directionally consistent there but lost FDR significance (r = −0.264, q = 0.117, n = 78) [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]. It noted that 125/131 H1 samples (95%) came from a single NMDC study [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]. It also reported that correcting an isoleucine/leucine compound-mapping collision strengthened the leucine correlation from r = −0.326 to r = −0.390 [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]. The report frames the energetic-cost explanation for leucine and arginine as a mechanistic interpretation rather than direct evidence of gene loss [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology].
