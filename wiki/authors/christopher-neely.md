## Contributions

### Latent metabolic capabilities and pangenome structure

In [[summaries/metabolic_capability_dependency__REPORT]], 15.8% of 1,695 genomically complete pathway-organism pairs across 48 Fitness Browser organisms were latent capabilities, meaning they showed no detectable fitness importance under the conditions tested. Of the 1,695 pairs, 881 (51.9%) were active, 547 (32.3%) intermediate and 267 (15.8%) latent [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Carbon source utilization pathways were 3.7× more likely to be latent than amino acid biosynthesis pathways (24.3% vs 6.5%) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Latent fractions depend on classification thresholds. Across 16 threshold combinations they ranged from 4.7% to 21.1% [src: metabolic_capability_dependency].

The project ran two Black Queen tests (see [[entities/black-queen-hypothesis]]). The pathway-level test did not support the hypothesis. Conservation did not differ between latent and active pathways (Mann-Whitney U p = 0.94, rank-biserial r = 0.052) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. The clade-level test did support it. Latent fraction correlated positively with [[entities/pangenome-openness]] (Spearman ρ = 0.69, p = 0.0004, n = 22 clades) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency].

### Within-species metabolic ecotypes

All 10 target species in the same project showed metabolic clustering with silhouette scores above 0.2. Scores ranged from 0.35 (PALSA-747 sp.) to 0.89 (*Salmonella enterica*) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Clusters correlated with isolation environment in *Salmonella enterica* (χ²=1570.2, df=25, p<0.0001) and in *Phenylobacterium* sp. (χ²=12.2, df=1, p=0.0005) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Environment-cluster associations were non-significant in the marine organisms Stutzerimonas, Alteromonas, Pelagibacter and Prochlorococcus [src: metabolic_capability_dependency].

### Community metabolic ecology in NMDC soils

[[summaries/nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology__REPORT]] found a weak but consistent Black Queen signal. Of 13 testable amino acid pathways, 11 (85%) had negative correlations between community pathway completeness and ambient amino acid metabolite intensity (sign test p = 0.011) [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]. Only leucine (r = −0.390, q = 0.022, n = 62) and arginine (r = −0.297, q = 0.049, n = 80) were significant after Benjamini-Hochberg correction [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]. Tyrosine was the outlier, with a positive correlation (r = +0.419) [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]. Restricting to Soil samples left leucine unchanged. Arginine lost FDR significance (q = 0.117) [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]. In the H1 dataset, 125 of 131 samples came from one study, so it is effectively single-study [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology].

Pathway completeness also separated ecosystems. Soil and Freshwater communities occupied nearly non-overlapping regions of PCA space, with PC1 explaining 49.4% of variance [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]. PC1 loaded almost entirely on carbon utilization pathways [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]. 17 of 18 amino acid pathways differed across ecosystems (q < 0.05). Tyrosine was the exception (q = 0.71) [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology].
