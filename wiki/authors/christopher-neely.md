# Christopher Neely

ORCID: [0000-0002-2620-8948](https://orcid.org/0000-0002-2620-8948)

## Contributions

[[summaries/metabolic_capability_dependency__REPORT]] compared genomic metabolic capability with experimentally measured metabolic dependency across 1,695 complete pathway-organism pairs from 48 Fitness Browser organisms, combining GapMind pathway-completeness predictions with gene-fitness data and SEED subsystem annotations as a pathway-membership proxy [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. It classified 267 pairs (15.8%) as latent capabilities, 547 (32.3%) as intermediate, and 881 (51.9%) as active dependencies, and found that pathway category strongly predicted dependency class (χ²=163.6, df=4, p=2.5×10⁻³⁴) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Carbon source utilization pathways were latent in 217 of 892 cases (24.3%) versus 48 of 735 amino acid biosynthesis pathways (6.5%), a 3.7× difference using the reported fractions [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. A 16-combination threshold sensitivity analysis (active thresholds 1.5–2.5, latent thresholds 0.75–1.25) produced latent fractions from 4.7% to 21.1% (SD = 5.9 percentage points), and the project noted that fitness experiments are biased toward laboratory conditions, so pathways important in untested environments may appear latent — findings that slot into [[concepts/condition-specific-fitness]] [src: metabolic_capability_dependency].

The same project tested pathway conservation and pangenome dynamics against those dependency classes. Pathway-level conservation did not separate latent from active pathways (mean 0.869 for 248 latent versus 0.829 for 755 active capabilities, both with median 1.000; Mann–Whitney U p = 0.94, rank-biserial r = 0.052), so H2a was not supported [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. At the clade level, after aggregating 41 organisms into 22 unique species clades, latent capability rate correlated positively with pangenome openness (Spearman ρ = 0.69, p = 0.0004, n = 22 clades), linking fitness-neutral pathways to [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Within-species analysis of 10 target species found metabolic clustering with silhouette scores above 0.2 in all of them, from 0.349 for *PALSA-747* sp. to 0.894 for *Salmonella enterica*, with clusters significantly associated with isolation environment in *Salmonella enterica* (χ²=1570.2, df=25, p<0.0001) and *Phenylobacterium* sp. (χ²=12.2, df=1, p=0.0005) but not in the marine taxa *Stutzerimonas*, *Alteromonas*, *Pelagibacter*, and *Prochlorococcus*; the report flagged these associations as observational and uncorrected for phylogenetic structure [src: metabolic_capability_dependency].

[[summaries/nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology__REPORT]] integrated NMDC community taxonomy and metabolomics with GTDB pangenome and GapMind records across 220 samples, 27,690 GTDB species, and 80 pathways (18 amino-acid, 62 carbon-utilization) to test community-scale Black Queen Hypothesis dynamics [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]. Of 13 testable amino-acid biosynthesis pathways, 11 (85%) showed negative Spearman correlations between community pathway completeness and ambient metabolite intensity, a direction significant by binomial sign test (p = 0.011); leucine (r = −0.390, q = 0.022, n = 62) and arginine (r = −0.297, q = 0.049, n = 80) were significant after Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction, while methionine had the largest effect but was underpowered (r = −0.496, n = 18, q = 0.117) and tyrosine ran in the anti-BQH direction (r = +0.419, ns) [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]. The project also reported ecosystem differentiation of metabolic potential: PCA of the 220 × 80 completeness matrix explained 49.4% of variance in PC1 and 16.6% in PC2, Soil and Freshwater separated at Mann-Whitney U = 3,674, p < 0.0001 (median PC1 +3.86 versus −6.28), and 17 of 18 amino-acid pathways differed significantly by ecosystem type (q < 0.05), feeding [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]] [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]. It documented that all 33 Freshwater samples lacked paired metabolomics, making H1 effectively soil-only; that 125 of 131 H1 samples (95%) came from a single NMDC study; that pH, temperature, and total organic carbon were all NaN in the 174-sample matrix, precluding partial correlations; and that an isoleucine/leucine compound-mapping collision was corrected, strengthening leucine from r = −0.326, q = 0.045 to r = −0.390, q = 0.022 [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology].
