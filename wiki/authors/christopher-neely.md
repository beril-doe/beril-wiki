# Christopher Neely

ORCID: [0000-0002-2620-8948](https://orcid.org/0000-0002-2620-8948)

## Contributions

The [[summaries/metabolic_capability_dependency__REPORT]] project compared genomic metabolic capability with experimentally measured metabolic dependency across 1,695 pathway-organism pairs from 48 organisms, using GapMind pathway completeness predictions, Fitness Browser gene-fitness data, and SEED subsystem annotations as a pathway-membership proxy [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. It classified a pathway as an active dependency when mean absolute fitness t-score exceeded 2.0 or more than 20% of its genes were essential, and as a latent capability when mean absolute t-score was below 1.0 and fewer than 5% of genes were essential, finding 267 (15.8%) latent, 547 (32.3%) intermediate, and 881 (51.9%) active, with pathway category strongly predicting dependency class (χ²=163.6, df=4, p=2.5×10⁻³⁴) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. The project found carbon source utilization pathways most likely to be latent (217 of 892, 24.3%) versus amino acid biosynthesis pathways (48 of 735, 6.5%), a 3.7× difference using those reported fractions, and reported per-organism latent fractions from 0–31.6% with a mean of 15.0%, highest in *Pseudomonas syringae* strains and *Klebsiella michiganensis* [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. A sensitivity analysis over 16 threshold combinations (active 1.5–2.5, latent 0.75–1.25) produced latent fractions from 4.7% to 21.1% with SD = 5.9 percentage points, and the project stated that fitness experiments are biased toward laboratory conditions, so pathways important in untested environments may appear latent [src: metabolic_capability_dependency].

The same project tested whether latent capabilities track pathway conservation and pangenome openness [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Testing the hypothesis that pathway-level conservation distinguishes latent capabilities from active dependencies, it found no distinction: mean conservation was 0.829 (median 1.000) among 755 active dependencies, 0.907 (median 1.000) among 508 intermediate pathways, and 0.869 (median 1.000) among 248 latent capabilities, with Mann–Whitney U p = 0.94 for active > latent and rank-biserial r = 0.052, a null result that left that hypothesis unsupported [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Testing instead whether clade-level latent capability rate tracks pangenome openness, and after aggregating 41 organisms to 22 unique species clades, it found a positive correlation (Spearman ρ = 0.69, p = 0.0004, n = 22 clades), supporting that clade-level version of the hypothesis and feeding [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Within a 7,334-species pangenome dataset, the project reported *Klebsiella pneumoniae* as most open (99.05% open; 0.95% core genes) and *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* as most closed (97.4% open; 2.6% core genes) among well-sampled pathogens with more than 2,500 genomes [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. It noted that an attempted organism-to-clade linkage using NCBI taxonomy IDs returned zero matches because the relevant column contained boolean strings rather than numeric taxids, that only 48 organisms were fitness-tested among 293,000 genomes with pathway predictions, and that SEED-proxy mapping can introduce false positives; two GapMind pathways (deoxyribonate, myoinositol) were excluded for lacking matching SEED roles, and alanine was excluded for every organism for falling below the minimum coverage threshold [src: metabolic_capability_dependency].

The project also tested within-species metabolic ecotypes and found all 10 target species (each with at least 50 genomes and at least 15 variable pathways) clustered with silhouette scores greater than 0.2, ranging from 0.349 for *PALSA-747* sp. (51 genomes, k=2) to 0.894 for *Salmonella enterica* (11,396 genomes, k=6) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. It found metabolic clusters significantly associated with isolation environment in *Salmonella enterica* (χ²=1570.2, df=25, p<0.0001) and *Phenylobacterium* sp. (χ²=12.2, df=1, p=0.0005), but not in the marine organisms *Stutzerimonas*, *Alteromonas*, *Pelagibacter*, and *Prochlorococcus*, and reported the strongest pathway heterogeneity in valine/leucine biosynthesis (0.60), tryptophan biosynthesis (0.51), and lysine/threonine biosynthesis (0.49) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. The project stated this ecotype analysis is observational, that explicit phylogenetic correction was not performed so environment-cluster associations may be confounded by phylogenetic structure, and that the marine non-associations may reflect coarse NCBI isolation-source metadata or pathway annotations lacking resolution for relevant gene-content differences [src: metabolic_capability_dependency].

The [[summaries/nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology__REPORT]] project integrated NMDC community taxonomy and metabolomics with GTDB pangenome and GapMind records across 220 samples, 27,690 GTDB species, and 80 pathways (18 amino-acid and 62 carbon-utilization), with mean taxonomy-bridge coverage of 94.6%, a workflow that feeds [[concepts/multi-omics-integration]] [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]. Testing 13 amino-acid biosynthesis pathways for community-scale Black Queen Hypothesis (BQH) dynamics — a negative association between community pathway completeness and ambient amino-acid metabolite intensity — it found 11 of 13 (85%) showed negative Spearman correlations, significantly non-random by binomial sign test (p = 0.011); leucine (r = −0.390, q = 0.022, n = 62) and arginine (r = −0.297, q = 0.049, n = 80) were the two pathways significant after Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction, while methionine had the largest effect but was underpowered (r = −0.496, n = 18, q = 0.117), tyrosine ran in the opposite, anti-BQH direction (r = +0.419, ns), and isoleucine showed no signal (r = −0.057, n = 18, q = 0.823) [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]. In a soil-only re-analysis using 125 Soil samples after excluding 6 Unknown-ecosystem samples, the project found leucine still significant (r = −0.390, q = 0.022, n = 62) while arginine remained directionally consistent but lost FDR significance (r = −0.264, q = 0.117, n = 78), with the sign test still 11/13 negative (p = 0.011) [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]. The project reported the leucine correlation strengthening from r = −0.326 to r = −0.390 (q from 0.045 to 0.022) after correcting an isoleucine/leucine compound-mapping collision, noted that 125 of 131 samples (95%) in the pathway–metabolomics test came from a single NMDC study, and characterised the leucine and arginine results as weak but consistent support for community-scale Black Queen dynamics [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]. Separately, PCA of the 220-sample × 80-pathway matrix explained 49.4% of variance in PC1 and 16.6% in PC2 (PC1–5: 83.0%), with Soil versus Freshwater separation at Mann-Whitney U = 3,674, p < 0.0001 and median PC1 of +3.86 for Soil and −6.28 for Freshwater, and per-pathway tests found significant ecosystem-type differences for 17 of 18 amino-acid pathways, tyrosine being the only exception (q = 0.71), results the project slots into [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]] [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]. The project recorded that all 33 Freshwater samples lacked paired metabolomics, so the pathway–metabolomics test was effectively soil-only and whether BQH dynamics operate at the same scale in freshwater communities remains untested; that pH, temperature, and total organic carbon were all NaN in the 174-sample analysis matrix, so partial correlations controlling for environmental gradients could not be performed; and that GapMind measures genomic potential rather than expression [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology].
