# Justin Reese

ORCID: [0000-0002-2170-2250](https://orcid.org/0000-0002-2170-2250)

## Contributions

The [[summaries/phage_defense_arsenal__REPORT]] project analyzed seven anti-phage defense-system families across the KBase Data Lakehouse pangenome, detecting markers in 27,626 of 27,690 species-level pangenomes (99.8%) and producing 930,573 marker hits across 825,476 unique gene clusters, with a ≥5-genome quality-filtered analysis set of 7,323 species [src: phage_defense_arsenal]. It found species-level prevalence ranging from 7.2% for CBASS to 96.1% for CRISPR-Cas, with BREX at 80.1%, restriction-modification (R-M) Type I at 76.8%, DISARM at 58.4% under the strict call, Retron at 54.7%, R-M Type II at 38.9%, and Gabija at 22.8% [src: phage_defense_arsenal]. It reported that defense-system count correlated with prophage-cluster burden at Spearman ρ = 0.609 (p ≈ 0, n = 7,323), that residualizing on log10 median genome size and phylum left a partial ρ of 0.301 (p = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁵³), and that a negative-binomial generalized linear model gave positive contributions from prophage-cluster count (β = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴, p < 0.001) and log10 genome size (β = 0.755, p < 0.001), so each 10-fold genome-size increase was associated with e^0.755 ≈ 2.1× more defense systems [src: phage_defense_arsenal]. All nine major phyla showed positive significant partial correlations, from Campylobacterota (n = 102, ρ = 0.530, p = 9.8×10⁻⁹) down to Actinomycetota (n = 803, ρ = 0.185, p = 1.3×10⁻⁷) [src: phage_defense_arsenal]. Using 1,000 phylum-stratified column-permutation nulls with Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction, the project found 27 of 28 defense-system pairs significantly positively co-occurring at q < 0.05, the strongest being R-M Type II × Gabija (2,429 observed versus a null mean of 1,555, z = 46.1, odds ratio 24.0), which it presents as a novel pan-bacterial syndrome at this scale; CRISPR-Cas × CBASS was the only non-significant pair (z = 0.21, p_emp = 0.98) [src: phage_defense_arsenal]. Six of seven systems were significantly enriched in the auxiliary and singleton pangenome against a background of 46.8% core and 37.9% singleton gene clusters (χ² p ≈ 0 for all systems), with singleton fractions 1.5–1.8× above baseline [src: phage_defense_arsenal]. The project stated several caveats: CRISPR-Cas prevalence is inflated by permissive EggNOG description matching, since EggNOG description-based detection gave 96% prevalence versus approximately 55% using the Cas1 Pfam marker PF01867 on the same pangenome, a gap of roughly 40 percentage points, so 96% is treated as an upper bound [src: phage_defense_arsenal]. The DISARM accessory-enrichment result is treated as unreliable because the DrmB SNF2 helicase anchor PF00176 also identifies widespread housekeeping helicases, Retron detection relies on the broad RVT_1 Pfam PF00078 and the stringent call dropped only 11 species (15,109 to 15,098), the ≥5-genome filter retained 26% of pangenome species and biases toward well-sampled organisms, the analysis controls for phylum categorically rather than with a phylogenetically corrected regression, and the negative-binomial GLM fixes dispersion at alpha = 1.0 rather than estimating it [src: phage_defense_arsenal]. On data engineering, the project used the 833-million-row `interproscan_domains` table rather than the 18.8-million-row `bakta_pfam_domains` table, which contained 0 Cas1 PF01867 hits versus ~25K in InterProScan, and noted that Spark Connect `.write.parquet()` writes to cluster storage rather than the client notebook filesystem [src: phage_defense_arsenal].

The [[summaries/prophage_amr_comobilization__REPORT]] project analyzed associations between antimicrobial-resistance (AMR) genes and prophage markers across the GTDB pangenome, identifying 83,008 AMR gene clusters and 3,465,244 broad prophage marker clusters (strict count 1,261,929), and found that 14,669 of 27,702 species (52.9%) carried both [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. Among 36,041 AMR gene instances from 1,953 genomes sampled across 100 species, it found 20,073 (55.7%) on contigs carrying strict prophage markers, 12,026 (33.4%) within 50 genes of a prophage marker, 7,137 (19.8%) within 20 genes, 3,731 (10.4%) within 10 genes, and 1,991 (5.5%) within 5 genes, with a median distance of 34 genes among co-localized AMR genes [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. It found AMR genes within 10 genes of a prophage marker were 67.6% accessory versus 65.5% for distal genes (Fisher's exact odds ratio 1.10, one-sided p=0.005, bootstrap 95% CI [1.024, 1.185]), a modest 2.1 percentage-point difference, and that the effect was threshold-dependent (odds ratio 0.78 at 3 genes, 0.92 at 5, 1.10 at 10, 1.19 at 15, 1.28 at 50), with only 33 of 74 testable species exceeding an odds ratio of 1 and a median species-level odds ratio of 0.85 [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. Across 4,770 species it found prophage marker density positively associated with AMR repertoire breadth (Spearman rho=0.572, p<10^-300), with a log-log slope of 0.823 (SE=0.018), p<10^-300, R²=0.30 — the report stating a 10-fold increase in prophage density predicts a ~6.6-fold increase in AMR breadth — and a partial Spearman of rho=0.464 (p=1.0×10^-253) after controlling for genome count, significant in Pseudomonadota (rho=0.54), Bacillota_A (rho=0.55), Bacillota (rho=0.40), Bacteroidota (rho=0.59), and Actinomycetota (rho=0.29) [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. The project reported a null result on its third hypothesis: the proposed fitness-cost comparison could not be tested because the KBase Data Lakehouse fitness browser holds RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing) data for only 48 model organisms with poor overlap with the GTDB species analyzed, leaving fitness costs of prophage-proximal AMR genes an open question [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. It stated that the density–breadth association is correlational and does not prove phage-mediated AMR transfer, that distances came from ordinal gene positions rather than base-pair coordinates, and that prophage markers were identified by keyword and Pfam matching in bakta_annotations rather than by dedicated predictors such as PHASTER or geNomad, which may admit false positives including phage-defense systems [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. Both projects feed [[concepts/environmental-resistome]], and the AMR atlas also slots into [[concepts/phage-defense-syndromes-and-arms-race]] [src: phage_defense_arsenal, prophage_amr_comobilization].

The [[summaries/ecotype_functional_differentiation__REPORT]] project tested whether within-species gene-content ecotypes differ functionally, clustering KBase Data Lakehouse pangenome genomes with PCA followed by KMeans and comparing their COG (Clusters of Orthologous Groups) profiles; it identified valid ecotypes in 12 of 15 sampled species (80%), assigning 1,820 genomes across 12 species spanning 6 phyla, averaging 3.7 ecotypes per species (range 2–6) with a mean silhouette score of 0.215 and median 0.174 [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. It found the clearest separation in *Erwinia amylovora* (silhouette = 0.468, 2 ecotypes) and *Bacteroides xylanisolvens* (0.366, 6 ecotypes) and the weakest in *Staphylococcus simulans* (0.118) and *Streptococcus pseudopneumoniae* (0.131) [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. Across 257 chi-square or Fisher's exact tests covering 12 species and 23 COG categories, 170 (66.1%) were significant after BH-FDR correction at q < 0.05, with every species showing at least one significantly differentiated category; the most frequently differentiated were E (amino acid metabolism), S (unknown function), and V (defense) at 11/12 species each (91.7%) and G (carbohydrate metabolism) at 10/12 (83.3%), while A (RNA processing) was significant in 1/9, B (chromatin) in 1/8, and Z (cytoskeleton) in 2/6 [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. The project reported partial support for its hypothesis that adaptive categories differentiate more than housekeeping categories: adaptive categories had a 79.8% significance rate (67/84) versus 68.8% (33/48) for housekeeping, a 1.16x ratio, but a mean effect size of 0.0136 versus 0.0064, a 2.13x ratio, with a one-sided Mann–Whitney U p = 2.53 x 10^-6 [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. It found the largest mean effect sizes in S (unknown function, 0.0392, significant in 11/12 species) and L (replication, recombination and repair, 0.0337, 9/12) [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. The project stated that effect sizes were small — approximately 3–4 percentage-point differences in COG-category proportions, with significance possibly reflecting large sample sizes — that ecotype boundaries overlap, that approximately 38% of gene clusters had COG annotations leaving 62% unannotated, that KMeans was used because HDBSCAN was unavailable on the cluster, and that without within-species phylogenetic controls such as core-genome trees the analysis cannot distinguish ecological adaptation from phylogenetic or demographic substructure [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. Of 27,702 species in the pangenome database, 457 had at least 50 genomes and 456 remained eligible after COG-coverage filtering, from which a stratified sample of 15 species was drawn; two species were lost to transient Spark S3 read errors during query times varying from 94s to 1642s, and *Limisoma* sp. had insufficient structure for valid clustering [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. This project slots into [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]] and, with the AMR atlas, into [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation, prophage_amr_comobilization].
