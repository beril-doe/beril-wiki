# Justin Reese

ORCID: [0000-0002-2170-2250](https://orcid.org/0000-0002-2170-2250)

## Contributions

The [[summaries/phage_defense_arsenal__REPORT]] project surveyed seven anti-phage defense-system families across the KBase Data Lakehouse pangenome and detected markers in 27,626 of 27,690 species-level pangenomes (99.8%), producing 930,573 marker hits across 825,476 unique gene clusters, with a ≥5-genome quality-filtered analysis set of 7,323 species [src: phage_defense_arsenal]. It reported species-level prevalence ranging from 7.2% for CBASS to 96.1% for CRISPR-Cas, with BREX at 80.1%, restriction-modification (R-M) Type I at 76.8%, DISARM at 58.4% under the strict call, Retron at 54.7%, R-M Type II at 38.9%, and Gabija at 22.8% [src: phage_defense_arsenal]. The project found that defense-system count correlated with prophage-cluster burden at Spearman ρ = 0.609 (p ≈ 0, n = 7,323), that the partial correlation remained ρ = 0.301 (p = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁵³) after residualizing on log10 median genome size and phylum, and that all nine major phyla showed positive significant partial correlations, from ρ = 0.530 in Campylobacterota to ρ = 0.185 in Actinomycetota [src: phage_defense_arsenal]. Testing co-occurrence with 1,000 phylum-stratified permutations and Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction, it found 27 of 28 defense-system pairs significantly positively co-occurring at q < 0.05 — the strongest being R-M Type II × Gabija (2,429 observed versus a null mean of 1,555, z = 46.1, odds ratio 24.0), presented as a novel pan-bacterial syndrome at this scale — with CRISPR-Cas × CBASS the only non-significant pair (z = 0.21, p_emp = 0.98) [src: phage_defense_arsenal]. It also reported that six of seven systems were enriched in the auxiliary and singleton pangenome against a background of 46.8% core and 37.9% singleton clusters, while flagging DISARM as unreliable because its DrmB SNF2 helicase Pfam anchor (PF00176) is a widespread housekeeping domain, and that EggNOG description-based CRISPR detection gave 96% prevalence versus approximately 55% using the Cas1 Pfam marker PF01867 on the same pangenome — a gap of roughly 40 percentage points that the report treats as making 96% an upper bound [src: phage_defense_arsenal]. These results feed [[concepts/phage-defense-syndromes-and-arms-race]] [src: phage_defense_arsenal, prophage_amr_comobilization].

The [[summaries/prophage_amr_comobilization__REPORT]] project analyzed antimicrobial-resistance (AMR) genes against prophage markers across the GTDB pangenome, inventorying 83,008 AMR gene clusters and 3,465,244 broad prophage marker clusters (1,261,929 under the strict call), and found that 14,669 of 27,702 species (52.9%) carried both [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. In a gene-neighborhood analysis of 36,041 AMR gene instances from 1,953 genomes across 100 species, it found 20,073 (55.7%) on contigs carrying strict prophage markers and 12,026 (33.4%) within 50 genes of a marker, with a median distance of 34 genes among co-localized AMR genes [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. AMR genes within 10 genes of a prophage marker were 67.6% accessory versus 65.5% for distal genes (Fisher's exact odds ratio 1.10, one-sided p = 0.005, bootstrap 95% CI [1.024, 1.185]), an effect the report calls modest and threshold-dependent, with odds ratios of 0.78 at 3 genes rising to 1.28 at 50 genes and only 33 of 74 testable species exceeding 1 (median species-level odds ratio 0.85) [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. At the species level across 4,770 species, prophage marker density was associated with AMR repertoire breadth at Spearman rho = 0.572 (p < 10⁻³⁰⁰), with a log-log slope of 0.823 (SE = 0.018) and R² = 0.30, a partial rho of 0.464 (p = 1.0 × 10⁻²⁵³) after controlling for genome count, and significance in all five reported major phyla; the report states the correlation does not establish phage-mediated AMR transfer [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. A planned fitness-cost comparison could not be run because the fitness browser holds RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing) data for only 48 model organisms with poor overlap with the GTDB species analyzed [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. The project slots into [[concepts/environmental-resistome]] and [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] [src: prophage_amr_comobilization].

The [[summaries/ecotype_functional_differentiation__REPORT]] project clustered bacterial genomes from the KBase Data Lakehouse pangenome database using PCA followed by KMeans (k = 2–6, best silhouette selected) and compared ecotype COG (Clusters of Orthologous Groups) profiles [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. From 27,702 species it identified 457 with at least 50 genomes and 456 eligible after COG-coverage filtering, drew a stratified random sample of 15 species, and recovered valid gene-content ecotypes in 12 of them (80%), assigning 1,820 genomes across 12 species spanning 6 phyla, averaging 3.7 ecotypes per species (range 2–6, mean silhouette 0.215, median 0.174), with the clearest separation in *Erwinia amylovora* (silhouette 0.468) and *Bacteroides xylanisolvens* (0.366) [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. Across 257 chi-square or Fisher's exact tests spanning 12 species and 23 COG categories, 170 (66.1%) were significant after BH-FDR correction at q < 0.05, and every species had at least one significantly differentiated category [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. Categories E (amino acid metabolism), S (unknown function), and V (defense) were each significant in 11/12 species and G (carbohydrate metabolism) in 10/12, while adaptive categories showed a 79.8% (67/84) significance rate versus 68.8% (33/48) for housekeeping categories and a mean effect size of 0.0136 versus 0.0064 (one-sided Mann–Whitney U p = 2.53 × 10⁻⁶); S had the largest mean effect size at 0.0392 and L (replication, recombination, and repair) the second largest at 0.0337 [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. The report notes that effect sizes correspond to roughly 3–4 percentage-point differences in COG proportions, that approximately 38% of gene clusters carried COG annotations, that KMeans was used because HDBSCAN was unavailable on the cluster, and that without within-species phylogenetic controls the analysis cannot separate ecological adaptation from phylogenetic or demographic substructure [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. Its findings feed [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]] [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation].
