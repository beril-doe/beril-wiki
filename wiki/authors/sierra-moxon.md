# Sierra Moxon

ORCID: [0000-0002-8719-7760](https://orcid.org/0000-0002-8719-7760)

## Contributions

[[summaries/metabolic_capability_dependency__REPORT]] compared genomic metabolic capability with experimentally measured metabolic dependency across 1,695 pathway-organism pairs from 48 Fitness Browser organisms, combining GapMind pathway-completeness predictions, gene-fitness data, and SEED subsystem annotations as a pathway-membership proxy [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Classifying pathways as active dependencies (mean absolute fitness t-score > 2.0 or > 20% essential genes), latent capabilities (mean absolute t-score < 1.0 and < 5% essential genes), or intermediate, the project found 267 (15.8%) latent, 547 (32.3%) intermediate, and 881 (51.9%) active pairs, with pathway category strongly predicting dependency class (χ²=163.6, df=4, p=2.5×10⁻³⁴) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Carbon source utilization pathways were latent in 217 of 892 cases (24.3%) versus 48 of 735 (6.5%) for amino acid biosynthesis — a 3.7× difference — while other pathways were latent in only 2 of 68 cases (2.9%) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Organism-level latent fractions ranged from 0–31.6% (mean 15.0%), and a sensitivity analysis over 16 threshold combinations (active 1.5–2.5, latent 0.75–1.25) produced latent fractions from 4.7% to 21.1% (SD = 5.9 percentage points), with the qualitative conclusion holding across that range [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. The report notes that fitness experiments are biased toward laboratory conditions, so pathways important in untested environments may appear latent, and that only 48 organisms were fitness-tested among 293,000 genomes with pathway predictions [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. These results feed [[concepts/condition-specific-fitness]] and [[concepts/gene-essentiality]] [src: metabolic_capability_dependency].

The same project tested whether latent capabilities track pathway conservation and pangenome openness. At the pathway level it found no distinguishing signal: mean conservation was 0.829 (median 1.000) among 755 active dependencies, 0.907 (median 1.000) among 508 intermediate pathways, and 0.869 (median 1.000) among 248 latent capabilities, with Mann–Whitney U p = 0.94 for active > latent and rank-biserial r = 0.052, a null result for the pathway-level hypothesis [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. At the clade level, after aggregating 41 organisms to 22 unique species clades, latent capability rate correlated positively with pangenome openness (Spearman ρ = 0.69, p = 0.0004, n = 22 clades) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Across the 7,334-species pangenome dataset, among pathogens with more than 2,500 genomes *Klebsiella pneumoniae* was reported as most open (99.05% open; 0.95% core genes) and *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* as most closed (97.4% open; 2.6% core genes) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. The report cautions that species-level averaging may inflate apparent conservation and that an attempted organism-to-clade linkage via NCBI taxonomy IDs returned zero matches because the relevant column held boolean strings rather than numeric taxids, so downstream analyses used organism-level fitness aggregates without explicit clade linkage [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. This work slots into [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] [src: metabolic_capability_dependency].

The project also examined within-species metabolic structure. All 10 target species (each with ≥ 50 genomes and ≥ 15 variable pathways) showed metabolic clustering with silhouette scores above 0.2, ranging from 0.349 for *PALSA-747* sp. (51 genomes, k=2) to 0.894 for *Salmonella enterica* (11,396 genomes, k=6), with intermediate values including *Stutzerimonas stutzeri* (149 genomes, k=2, 0.780), *Alteromonas macleodii* (56 genomes, k=2, 0.738), *Acetatifactor intestinalis* (59 genomes, k=6, 0.657), *Ruminococcus* E sp. (53 genomes, k=2, 0.609), *Phenylobacterium* sp. (51 genomes, k=2, 0.544), *Limivicinus* sp. (50 genomes, k=2, 0.528), *Pelagibacter* sp. (79 genomes, k=2, 0.430), and *Prochlorococcus* A sp. (74 genomes, k=2, 0.429) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Clusters were significantly associated with isolation environment in *Salmonella enterica* (χ²=1570.2, df=25, p<0.0001) and *Phenylobacterium* sp. (χ²=12.2, df=1, p=0.0005) but not in the marine organisms *Stutzerimonas*, *Alteromonas*, *Pelagibacter*, and *Prochlorococcus*, with the strongest pathway heterogeneity in valine/leucine biosynthesis (0.60), tryptophan biosynthesis (0.51), and lysine/threonine biosynthesis (0.49) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. The report states this ecotype analysis is observational, that no explicit phylogenetic correction was performed, and that marine non-associations may reflect coarse NCBI isolation-source metadata [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. On the annotation side, two GapMind pathways (deoxyribonate and myoinositol) were excluded for lacking matching SEED subsystem role descriptions, alanine was excluded from classification in every organism for having fewer than 3 SEED-annotated genes meeting the coverage threshold, and phenylalanine and tyrosine were recovered only through abbreviation-based matching on `phe` and `tyr` [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. These findings feed [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]] and [[concepts/metabolic-model-gapfilling]] [src: metabolic_capability_dependency].
