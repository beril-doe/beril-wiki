# Sierra Moxon

ORCID: [0000-0002-8719-7760](https://orcid.org/0000-0002-8719-7760)

## Contributions

[[summaries/metabolic_capability_dependency__REPORT]] compared genomic metabolic capability with measured metabolic dependency across 1,695 pathway-organism pairs from 48 organisms [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. It used GapMind pathway completeness predictions, Fitness Browser gene-fitness data, and SEED subsystem annotations as a pathway-membership proxy [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. The project found 267 (15.8%) latent pairs, meaning complete pathways with no detectable fitness importance under the tested conditions, alongside 547 (32.3%) intermediate and 881 (51.9%) active pairs [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Pathway category strongly predicted dependency class (χ²=163.6, df=4, p=2.5×10⁻³⁴) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Carbon source utilization pathways were 24.3% latent and amino acid biosynthesis pathways were 6.5% latent, making carbon pathways 3.7× more likely to be latent [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. The project framed a prediction that organisms in nutrient-rich environments should disproportionately lose carbon-pathway genes, but it did not test that prediction [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. The latent fraction ranged from 0–31.6% across organisms, with a mean of 15.0% [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. A sensitivity analysis over 16 threshold combinations gave latent fractions from 4.7% to 21.1% (SD = 5.9 percentage points), and the qualitative conclusion held across that range [src: metabolic_capability_dependency].

The same project tested whether latent capabilities track conservation and pangenome openness [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Pathway-level conservation did not distinguish latent from active pathways: mean conservation was 0.869 for latent pathways and 0.829 for active pathways (Mann–Whitney U p = 0.94 for active > latent, rank-biserial r = 0.052), so H2a was not supported [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. After aggregating 41 organisms to 22 species clades, the project found that latent capability rate correlated positively with pangenome openness (Spearman ρ = 0.69, p = 0.0004, n = 22 clades), supporting H2b [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. The report read these results as mixed support for the Black Queen framework, the idea that costly functions are lost when neighbours supply them, and as consistent with a signal operating at the level of genome dynamics and community context [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Across 7,334 species, it reported *Klebsiella pneumoniae* as the most open well-sampled pathogen (99.05% open) and *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* as the most closed (97.4% open) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency].

The project also clustered within-species pathway profiles in 10 target species using hierarchical clustering and principal component analysis [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. All 10 showed metabolic clustering with silhouette scores (a measure of how well-separated clusters are) greater than 0.2, ranging from 0.349 for *PALSA-747* sp. to 0.894 for *Salmonella enterica* (11,396 genomes, k=6, where k is the number of clusters) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Clusters were significantly associated with isolation environment in *Salmonella enterica* (χ²=1570.2, df=25, p<0.0001) and *Phenylobacterium* sp. (χ²=12.2, df=1, p=0.0005) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Associations were not significant in the marine organisms *Stutzerimonas*, *Alteromonas*, *Pelagibacter*, and *Prochlorococcus*, which the project suggested may reflect insufficient or coarse isolation-source metadata, depth- or nutrient-zone structure, or pathway annotations lacking resolution for relevant gene-content differences [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. The project stated that these associations are observational and may be confounded by phylogeny, because no explicit phylogenetic correction was performed [src: metabolic_capability_dependency].

The project reported several limitations [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. SEED-proxy mapping can introduce false-positive genes that dilute the fitness signal [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Laboratory-biased fitness conditions may inflate the latent fraction [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Only 48 fitness-tested organisms were available among 293,000 genomes, which limits how far the results generalize [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. An organism-to-clade linkage through `gtdb_metadata` taxids returned zero matches because the relevant column contained boolean strings rather than numeric taxids, so downstream analyses used organism-level fitness aggregates without an explicit clade-level linkage, which may reduce the precision of the H2a comparison [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. The deoxyribonate and myoinositol pathways were excluded from all analyses because they had no matching SEED role descriptions [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Alanine was excluded from classification for every organism because of insufficient SEED coverage [src: metabolic_capability_dependency].
