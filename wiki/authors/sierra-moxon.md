# Sierra Moxon

ORCID: [0000-0002-8719-7760](https://orcid.org/0000-0002-8719-7760)

## Contributions

The project [[summaries/metabolic_capability_dependency__REPORT]] compared genomic metabolic capability with experimentally measured metabolic dependency across 1,695 pathway-organism pairs from 48 organisms, combining GapMind pathway completeness predictions, Fitness Browser gene-fitness data, and SEED subsystem annotations as a pathway-membership proxy [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. It classified a pathway as an active dependency when mean absolute fitness t-score exceeded 2.0 or more than 20% of its genes were essential, as a latent capability when mean absolute t-score was below 1.0 and fewer than 5% of genes were essential, and as intermediate between these thresholds, finding 267 (15.8%) latent, 547 (32.3%) intermediate, and 881 (51.9%) active pairs, with pathway category strongly predicting dependency class (χ²=163.6, df=4, p=2.5×10⁻³⁴) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. It found carbon source utilization pathways most likely to be latent (217 of 892, 24.3%; 320 intermediate, 35.9%; 355 active, 39.8%), amino acid biosynthesis pathways predominantly active (48 of 735 latent, 6.5%; 220 intermediate, 29.9%; 467 active, 63.5%), and other pathways rarely latent (2 of 68, 2.9%; 7 intermediate, 10.3%; 59 active, 86.8%), a 3.7× enrichment of latency in carbon pathways over amino acid biosynthesis using the reported 24.3% and 6.5% fractions [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. It reported an organism-level latent fraction ranging from 0–31.6% with a mean of 15.0%, highest in *Pseudomonas syringae* strains and *Klebsiella michiganensis*, and ran a sensitivity analysis over 16 combinations of active thresholds from 1.5–2.5 and latent thresholds from 0.75–1.25 that produced latent fractions from 4.7% to 21.1% (SD = 5.9 percentage points), with the qualitative conclusion holding across the tested range [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. The report notes that fitness experiments are biased toward laboratory conditions, so pathways important in untested environments may appear latent, and that only 48 organisms were fitness-tested among 293,000 genomes with pathway predictions [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. These results feed [[concepts/condition-specific-fitness]] and [[concepts/gene-essentiality]] [src: metabolic_capability_dependency].

The same project tested whether latent capabilities track pathway conservation and pangenome openness, and found that pathway-level conservation did not distinguish the classes: mean conservation was 0.829 (median 1.000) among 755 active dependencies, 0.907 (median 1.000) among 508 intermediate pathways, and 0.869 (median 1.000) among 248 latent capabilities, so latent capabilities had slightly higher mean conservation than active dependencies (Mann–Whitney U p = 0.94 for active > latent, rank-biserial r = 0.052), a null result that did not support the hypothesis that pathway-level conservation distinguishes latent capabilities from active dependencies [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. At the clade level it did support the hypothesis that latent capability rate tracks pangenome openness: after aggregating 41 organisms into 22 unique species clades so that multiple Fitness Browser strains from one clade shared a single pangenome-openness value, latent capability rate correlated positively with pangenome openness (Spearman ρ = 0.69, p = 0.0004, n = 22 clades), which the report reads as a Black Queen framework signal operating at the level of genome dynamics rather than per-pathway conservation [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Its pangenome dataset contained 7,334 species, and among well-sampled pathogens with more than 2,500 genomes it reported *Klebsiella pneumoniae* as most open (99.05% open; 0.95% core genes) and *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* as most closed (97.4% open; 2.6% core genes) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. The report cautions that species-level averaging may inflate apparent conservation because many genomes in a clade were not fitness-tested, that pathway-level conservation cannot detect partial erosion of individual genes within otherwise complete pathways, and that the Black Queen signal may be hard to detect because gene loss can require longer evolutionary timescales than current sampling captures [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. This strand slots into [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] [src: metabolic_capability_dependency].

The project also tested for within-species metabolic ecotypes and found that all 10 target species, each with at least 50 genomes and at least 15 variable pathways, showed metabolic clustering with silhouette scores greater than 0.2, ranging from 0.35 for *PALSA-747* sp. to 0.89 for *Salmonella enterica* [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Its species-level results were *Salmonella enterica*, 11,396 genomes, k=6, silhouette 0.894; *Stutzerimonas stutzeri*, 149 genomes, k=2, 0.780; *Alteromonas macleodii*, 56 genomes, k=2, 0.738; *Acetatifactor intestinalis*, 59 genomes, k=6, 0.657; *Ruminococcus* E sp., 53 genomes, k=2, 0.609; *Phenylobacterium* sp., 51 genomes, k=2, 0.544; *Limivicinus* sp., 50 genomes, k=2, 0.528; *Pelagibacter* sp., 79 genomes, k=2, 0.430; *Prochlorococcus* A sp., 74 genomes, k=2, 0.429; and *PALSA-747* sp., 51 genomes, k=2, 0.349 [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. It found metabolic clusters significantly associated with isolation environment in *Salmonella enterica* (χ²=1570.2, df=25, p<0.0001) and *Phenylobacterium* sp. (χ²=12.2, df=1, p=0.0005) but not in the marine organisms *Stutzerimonas*, *Alteromonas*, *Pelagibacter*, and *Prochlorococcus*, and reported the strongest pathway heterogeneity in valine/leucine biosynthesis (0.60), tryptophan biosynthesis (0.51), and lysine/threonine biosynthesis (0.49) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. The report states that this ecotype analysis is observational and does not establish causality, that environment-cluster associations including those in *Salmonella* and *Phenylobacterium* may be confounded by phylogenetic structure because explicit phylogenetic correction was not performed, and that the marine non-associations may reflect coarse NCBI isolation-source metadata, depth- or nutrient-zone structure, or pathway-level annotations that miss ecologically relevant gene-content and expression differences [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. These results slot into [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]] [src: metabolic_capability_dependency].

On data handling, the project drew on `kbase_ke_pangenome.gapmind_pathways`, `pangenome`, and `gtdb_metadata` for pathway completeness, pangenome openness, and taxonomy, and on `kescience_fitnessbrowser.genefitness`, `gene`, and `seedannotation` for gene-level fitness scores, essentiality inference, and SEED-proxy pathway mapping, generating datasets including 23,424,480 per-genome pathway records, 27,690 species pathway summaries, 3,065 pathway-fitness aggregates, 1,695 classifications, 1,511 conservation records, 7,334 pangenome-openness records, 800 pathway-heterogeneity records, 12,018 metabolic ecotype assignments, 10 ecotype-cluster summaries, and 370 pathway-cluster signatures [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. It excluded two GapMind pathways, deoxyribonate and myoinositol, from all analyses because they had no matching SEED subsystem role descriptions, included full-name phenylalanine and tyrosine only via abbreviation-based matching on their three-letter equivalents `phe` and `tyr` after they lacked SEED coverage, and excluded alanine from classification for every organism because fewer than 3 SEED-annotated genes met the minimum coverage threshold [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. It reported that an attempted organism-to-clade linkage using NCBI taxonomy IDs from `kbase_ke_pangenome.gtdb_metadata` returned zero matches because the relevant column contained boolean strings rather than numeric taxids, so downstream analyses used organism-level fitness aggregates without an explicit clade-level linkage, which may reduce the precision of the comparison testing whether pathway-level conservation distinguishes latent capabilities from active dependencies [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. It further states that SEED-proxy mapping can introduce false positives because related subsystem annotations may not represent direct GapMind pathway membership, that direct GapMind per-step gene assignments would improve precision, and that the large intermediate zone (32.3%) makes the latent fraction moderately sensitive to threshold tightening as shown by the 4.7%–21.1% sensitivity range [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. These annotation choices feed [[concepts/metabolic-model-gapfilling]] and [[concepts/gene-function-acquisition-depth]] [src: metabolic_capability_dependency].
