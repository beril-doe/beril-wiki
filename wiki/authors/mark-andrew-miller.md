# Mark Andrew Miller

ORCID: [0000-0001-9076-6066](https://orcid.org/0000-0001-9076-6066)

## Contributions

The [[summaries/nmdc_context_audit__REPORT]] project audited KBase Data Lakehouse resources whose names contain `nmdc` and found that 20 NMDC-named database entries resolve to 7 real, maintained resources spread across three tenants and six provenance classes, so the `nmdc` label is not a reliable dataset boundary [src: nmdc_context_audit]. The audit found that two of the four databases in the `nmdc` tenant are external re-hosts — `nmdc.ncbi_biosamples` with 51,711,888 biosamples and 756,112,544 attribute rows, and `nmdc.ref_data` with 27,481 Pfam terms — while `nmdc_arkin`, `nmdc_mags`, and `nmdc_neon` sit in the `kbase` tenant instead [src: nmdc_context_audit]. It identified a ~3,000× scale trap between the genuine NMDC biosample universe of 16,640 samples in `nmdc.metadata.biosample_set` and the co-hosted NCBI mirror, and found that the freshest NMDC resource, `kbase.nmdc_mags` with 62,346 MAGs (metagenome-assembled genomes), sits in the tenant users are least likely to search [src: nmdc_context_audit]. The project found that `kbase.nmdc_neon` represents the National Ecological Observatory Network, an NSF program distinct from the DOE-BER National Microbiome Data Collaborative, so treating the namesake as NMDC would be an agency-attribution error [src: nmdc_context_audit]. It found that Iceberg snapshot ages span roughly four months, from `2026-03-09` for `nmdc.ncbi_biosamples` to `2026-07-02` for `kbase.nmdc_mags` and `kbase.nmdc_neon`, with no changelog exposing currency, and that catalog tables carry no comments, databases have empty properties, the canonical `docs/schemas/nmdc.md` link 404s, and `berdl_inventory.py` groups by catalog prefix and so never links `kbase.nmdc_*` back to NMDC [src: nmdc_context_audit]. The audit also found that `DESCRIBE DATABASE EXTENDED kbase.nmdc_*` raises `ForbiddenException` for a `kesciencero`/`microbialdiscoveryforge` principal even where `COUNT(*)` on the same tables succeeds, and that `get_databases()` returns both dotted and underscore aliases requiring de-duplication [src: nmdc_context_audit]. The project delivered a 15-file Open-Knowledge-Format directory and proposed, but did not apply, seven fixes; it notes its provenance classes are inferred from schema and prior usage rather than an ingestion manifest, and that it never directly observed a user choosing the wrong resource, so the label-overload-causes-bad-selection claim remains inferred [src: nmdc_context_audit].

The [[summaries/euk_in_prok_correlates__REPORT]] project measured eukaryotic read fractions across 2,759 NMDC ReadbasedAnalysis runs from 9 studies using native `nmdc.results` GOTTCHA2 classifications and found eukaryotic reads in 77% of runs, with a median eukaryotic fraction of 2.7%, a mean of 13.3%, 20% of runs above 20%, and 23% of runs with no detectable signal [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]. It found that among runs with detectable signal the plastid share had a median of 100%, indicating plant or algal chloroplast DNA rather than animal-host DNA, and that Kraken2 and Centrifuge returned approximately 0 domain-level Eukaryota because their NMDC reference databases were prokaryote-restricted, leaving GOTTCHA2 the only usable estimator [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]. The project found eukaryotic source tracked sample matrix — aquatic freshwater at 99.5% detection and plastid share 1.00, terrestrial soil at 55.7% and 0.43, plant-root at 100% and 0.03 — with a Kruskal–Wallis H=77.8, p=1.3×10⁻¹⁷, and all pairwise contrasts significant after BH-FDR (false discovery rate) [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]. Its central result was a batch-confounding warning: each biome was approximately 80–100% nested within a single study, and a gradient-boosted model gave R²=0.24 for `study_id` alone and R²=0.35 for environment under random cross-validation but R²=−0.30 for environment and R²=−0.39 for environment plus sequencing under GroupKFold out-of-study validation, with out-of-study detection AUC of 0.56 [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]. Within the dominant NEON soil study of 1,186 runs from one sampling program with a constant protocol and batch, the project found local vegetation predictive at H=119.1, p=7.6×10⁻²¹ (median eukaryotic fraction 23% in sedge/forb herbaceous soil, 14% emergent wetland, 13% dwarf scrub, 2% evergreen forest, and approximately 0 in deciduous forest, cropland and pasture) and geography across 47 sites at H=310.4, p=2.4×10⁻⁴⁶, with Arctic tundra highest (Utqiaġvik 30%, Caribou-Poker Creeks 22%, Toolik 17%) versus 2–8% in temperate forests, and a within-study five-fold R²=+0.17 ± 0.06; it stated that `env_local_scale` and geography may still track sub-batches such as sampling campaigns, that this within-study analysis is the best available control rather than a randomized design, and that the result was demonstrated for one soil study only and may not extend to aquatic or host-associated collections [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]. It reported a cross-collection sequencing-depth association of Spearman ρ=−0.29, p=5.2×10⁻⁷, n=292, but stated this was not batch-controlled and is suggestive only, since the NEON soil study had zero non-null depth values [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]. The project also reported that an earlier p≈10⁻⁵⁰ `ecosystem_type` result was a missingness artifact and was removed, that NMDC did not populate DNA-extraction kit, filtration, host-depletion, or library-prep fields, and that 1,067 of 2,759 runs were pooled from multiple biosamples and inherited metadata from one representative biosample, which it characterizes as a conservative bias [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]. This batch-control result feeds [[concepts/environment-embedding-geography]] [src: euk_in_prok_correlates].

The [[summaries/pseudomonas_carbon_ecology__REPORT]] project analyzed GapMind carbon-pathway predictions from the `kbase_ke_pangenome` collection across 12,732 genomes and 433 *Pseudomonas* species clades (GTDB r214) and found that 43 of 62 pathways differed significantly between 7 *Pseudomonas* s.s. and 189 *Pseudomonas_E* species with at least 5 genomes by Mann-Whitney U with Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction (q < 0.05) [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]. It found the largest losses in plant-derived sugars and sugar alcohols: xylose 0.0% versus 74.4%, ribose 27.9% versus 92.0%, arabinose 0.0% versus 62.6%, galacturonate 28.6% versus 88.4%, myo-inositol 0.0% versus 58.8%, mannitol 25.9% versus 77.5%, and sorbitol 25.9% versus 77.4%, while amino-acid and core organic-acid pathways stayed above 99% in both groups and 19 pathways showed no significant difference [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]. The project reported the null-direction exception that rhamnose and fucose were more complete in *P. aeruginosa* (66.8%) than in the *P. fluorescens* group (41.3% and 45.1%), but not significantly after FDR correction [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]. Among 54 free-living and plant-associated species meeting the ≥5-genome and ≥60% majority-environment thresholds, it found a significant environment association by a PERMANOVA-like 999-permutation test at p = 0.006 (between-group mean distance 2.054 versus within-group 1.890, ratio 1.087), with PCA capturing 74.9% of variance in 5 components (PC1 31.2%, PC2 17.9%) [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]. A Random Forest over four environment classes on 51 species (soil 13, freshwater 13, plant_surface 19, rhizosphere 6) achieved balanced accuracy 0.408 +/- 0.169 against a 0.250 chance baseline, with D-serine (0.132), arabinose (0.094), rhamnose (0.086), fucose (0.085) and xylose (0.070) most discriminating, which the project assessed as only partial support — carbon profiles alone are insufficient for fine-grained environment discrimination [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]. It found the primary PCA axis separates the subgenera rather than lifestyles, with lifestyle categories substantially overlapping within *Pseudomonas_E*, and reported pathway richness medians of 57 versus 55 pathways for free-living/plant-associated versus host-associated species [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]. The project recorded that *P. aeruginosa* comprised 53% of genomes (6,760/12,732) and that isolation-source metadata were available for 64.2% of genomes (8,171/12,732 with classifiable sources), while free-text keyword classification introduced approximately 6.7% "unknown" and approximately 29.1% "other" classifications [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]. It recorded that GapMind's 62 pathways omit aromatic degradation pathways such as toluene, naphthalene and benzoate degradation that are central to *P. putida* ecology, and that the analyses do not explicitly control for phylogenetic non-independence, proposing PGLS (phylogenetic generalized least squares, regression accounting for phylogenetic relatedness) or phylogenetic logistic regression using the GTDB species tree for that confounding [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]. Addressing the moderate classifier accuracy, it proposed genome-level prediction using the full 789K genome-pathway matrix, within-species analysis of *P. fluorescens* and *P. putida* metabolic ecotypes, and cross-reference with RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing) fitness data from the `kescience_fitnessbrowser` collection for experimental validation [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]. These results feed [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]] and [[concepts/metabolic-model-gapfilling]] [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology].
