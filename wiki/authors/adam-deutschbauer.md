# Adam Deutschbauer

ORCID: [0000-0003-2728-7622](https://orcid.org/0000-0003-2728-7622)

## Contributions

The [[summaries/metal_fitness_atlas__REPORT]] project (Pan-Bacterial Metal Fitness Atlas) assembled a cross-species atlas of bacterial fitness under metal stress from 559 metal-related experiments — 8.2% of 6,804 total Fitness Browser experiments — spanning 31 organisms and 16 metals, yielding 383,349 gene × metal fitness records across 24 organisms with fitness matrices [src: metal_fitness_atlas]. Using a broad metal-important definition of fit < -1 or n_sick ≥ 1, it identified 12,838 broad metal-important genes (3.3%), of which 5,667 were strict metal-important genes (1.5%) [src: metal_fitness_atlas]. Coverage was catalogued per metal and per organism: cobalt was tested in 27 organisms, nickel in 26, copper in 23, aluminum in 22, zinc in 17, and iron in 3, and DvH was the most metal-profiled organism with 149 experiments across 13 metals and 1,366 metal-important genes, or 49.8% of its genome [src: metal_fitness_atlas].

The same project tested whether metal fitness genes are accessory-genome resistance genes and found the opposite: across 22 organisms and 14 metals, genes with significant metal-associated fitness defects were 87.4% core versus 76.9% core for baseline genes (OR=2.08, p=4.3e-162), with 21 of 22 organisms showing metal-important genes more core than baseline and 14 significant at p<0.05 [src: metal_fitness_atlas]. It reported that essential-metal tolerance genes (Fe, Mo, W, Se, Mn) had a mean core-fraction delta of +0.148 against +0.081 for toxic metals (one-sided Mann-Whitney U=39, p=0.015), with manganese strongest (+0.198, all 30 important genes core) and cadmium (delta=-0.010, p=0.92) and uranium (delta=+0.035, p=0.34) non-significant [src: metal_fitness_atlas]. Excluding four duplicate *P. fluorescens* FW300 strains reduced the analysis from 22 to 18 organisms but left the enrichment robust (OR=2.065, p=5.9e-141) [src: metal_fitness_atlas]. The report contrasted its 87.4%-core genome-wide result with a prior condition-specific DvH heavy-metal result of 71.2% core, and proposed a two-tier model separating core general-stress functions from accessory specialized resistance functions such as efflux, sequestration, and enzymatic detoxification — material that feeds [[concepts/condition-specific-fitness]] and [[concepts/metal-cross-resistance]] [src: metal_fitness_atlas].

On conserved families, modules, and prediction, the project found 2,891 ortholog groups with metal phenotypes, 1,182 conserved across at least two organisms and 601 across at least three, with OG00128 the most broadly conserved (17 organisms, nine metals), and flagged 149 novel metal-biology candidates lacking full annotation (89 truly unknown, 43 with DUF/UPF domains, 17 with partial hints) [src: metal_fitness_atlas]. Module analysis on z-scored activity profiles found 600 metal-responsive module records with |z| > 2.0 among 19,453 module × metal-experiment records (3.1%), and the 183 responsive modules with conservation data had a mean core fraction of 0.826 (median 0.929); an earlier pass using raw activity scores (maximum 0.96) found zero responsive modules until z-normalization was applied [src: metal_fitness_atlas]. A metal functional signature of 1,286 KEGG KO terms scored 27,702 pangenome species, placing *Leptospirillum* at the 91st percentile, *Acidithiobacillus* at the 77th, *Marinobacter* at the 75th, and *Sulfobacillus* at the 71st, but bioleaching genera were not significantly enriched over background after genome-size normalization (Mann-Whitney p=0.17), and the simple gene-presence repertoire score failed to predict metal fitness [src: metal_fitness_atlas] — a negative result the report ties to [[concepts/gene-essentiality]] and to the need for regulatory or expression-based models [src: metal_fitness_atlas]. The report also recorded limits: uneven metal coverage (uranium, chromium, mercury, cadmium, selenium, and manganese tested in only one or two organisms), unnormalized metal doses (nickel spanning 0.01-2.0 mM across organisms), and exclusion of putatively essential genes (~14.3% of protein-coding genes, ~82% core), which makes the core enrichment a conservative estimate [src: metal_fitness_atlas].
