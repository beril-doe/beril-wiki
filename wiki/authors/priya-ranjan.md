# Priya Ranjan

ORCID: [0000-0002-0357-1939](https://orcid.org/0000-0002-0357-1939)

## Contributions

The project [[summaries/pgp_pangenome_ecology__REPORT]] analyzed plant-growth-promoting (PGP) gene distribution, co-occurrence, environmental enrichment, and core/accessory status across the KBase Data Lakehouse pangenome, covering 27,702 total species, of which 11,272 carried at least one of 13 PGP gene markers, alongside 32,736 PGP gene clusters, 27,690 species with GapMind pathway scores, and 291,279 genomes with isolation-source metadata [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]. The project tested 10 focal-gene pairs for co-occurrence and found 8 significantly associated after Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate (BH-FDR) correction — five positive and three negative — with the strongest positive association being pqqC × acdS (OR = 7.24, n = 286 co-occurring species, q = 1.2e-83), and pqqC also co-occurring with hcnC (OR = 1.91) and ipdC (OR = 1.55) in what the report calls a putative rhizosphere-effectiveness module [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]. The same analysis found nifH negatively associated with hcnC (OR = 0.23, q = 5.8e-29) and pqqC (OR = 0.57, q = 2.9e-19) and not significantly associated with ipdC (OR = 1.13, q = 0.54), supporting ecological separation between diazotrophs and pqqC/acdS-bearing rhizobacteria and a primarily non-diazotrophic classical suite of plant-growth-promoting bacteria (PGPB, bacteria that benefit plant growth) in this dataset [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]. It also found that only 157 species (1.4%) carried at least three focal traits, with pqqC + acdS the most common multi-trait genotype (n = 153), followed by nifH + pqqC (n = 225) despite the overall negative association between those two genes [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology].

Comparing 1,039 soil/rhizosphere species against 10,233 species from other environments, the project found acdS, pqqC, and hcnC significantly enriched in soil after BH-FDR correction: acdS at 15.8% versus 2.6% prevalence (OR = 7.02, q = 5.1e-62), pqqC at 43.8% versus 21.2% (OR = 2.90, q = 2.8e-53), and hcnC at 11.3% versus 6.4% (OR = 1.85, q = 6.1e-08) [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]. The same comparison found nifH depleted in soil-classified species (12.9% versus 19.7%, OR = 0.60, q = 5.5e-08) and ipdC not significantly enriched in the raw comparison (soil 1.5%, other 1.9%, OR = 0.79, q = 0.47) [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]. The acdS enrichment held under phylum-level fixed effects in logistic regression (OR = 6.98, p = 4.8e-61) and in a strict rhizosphere-only sensitivity analysis restricted to genomes with "rhizosphere" or "root nodule" in their isolation source (OR = 10.6, q = 7.6e-38), while Bacillota_A was an exception for nifH, showing soil enrichment (OR = ∞, q = 2.5e-4) [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]. The report cautions that environment classification was conservative and noisy — only 1,637 species (5.9% of species with an environment label) were classified as soil/rhizosphere dominant, though 93.5% of the 291,279 genomes with isolation-source metadata were classifiable — and that NCBI's bias toward clinical and host-associated sampling may mean the acdS and pqqC effects underestimate true rhizosphere enrichment [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]. These environment-stratified results feed [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]] and [[concepts/environmental-resistome]] [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology].

The project rejected the hypothesis that PGP genes are predominantly horizontally transferred accessory genes: all 13 PGP genes had significantly higher core fractions than the genome-wide baseline of 46.8% core (BH-FDR q < 0.05 for all genes), with pqqC 81.5% core / 7.8% auxiliary / 10.7% singleton (q = 0.0), pqqB 78.1% / 8.9% / 13.0% (q = 3.0e-247), hcnA 78.5% / 10.8% / 10.8% (q = 9.2e-37), ipdC 76.5% / 9.7% / 13.7% (q = 3.4e-19), acdS 70.4% / 13.4% / 16.2% (q = 7.9e-25), nifH 63.8% / 15.1% / 21.0% (q = 4.3e-71), and pqqD 55.5% / 17.0% / 27.5% (q = 2.2e-86) [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]. It found a mean accessory fraction across all PGP genes of 29.7% against 53.2% genome-wide, and a negative correlation between pangenome openness (singleton fraction) and PGP gene richness (Spearman ρ = −0.195, p = 2.0e-97, n = 11,272 species with ≥2 genomes), consistent with PGP-rich species having more closed pangenomes [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]. pqqD was flagged as the outlier and partial exception to the vertical-inheritance pattern, with the lowest core fraction (55.5%) and highest singleton fraction (27.5%) among highlighted genes, suggesting it can sometimes spread as a standalone gene while the functional pqqB–pqqC unit was predominantly core [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]. These pangenome-structure results feed [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] and [[concepts/gene-function-acquisition-depth]] [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology].

On pathway completeness, the project found that tryptophan biosynthesis completeness assessed with GapMind predicted ipdC presence — ipdC occurred in 2.5% of species with a complete pathway (GapMind score ≥ 0.9) versus 0.9% of species with an incomplete pathway (Fisher OR = 2.81, p = 6.3e-10) — with a tryptophan-only logistic model giving OR = 2.81, 95% CI 1.97–4.01, p = 1.4e-08, n = 11,272, and adding soil status giving OR = 2.87, p = 7.0e-09 [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]. It found the tyrosine pathway predicted ipdC presence at a similar effect size (OR = 3.62, p = 2.3e-11), so the result does not support a tryptophan-specific mechanism, which the report reads as consistent with TyrR regulation of ipdC in *Enterobacter cloacae* since TyrR responds to tryptophan, tyrosine, and phenylalanine [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]. The association reversed within soil/rhizosphere species (n = 1,039, OR = 0.30, p = 0.02) while remaining positive in non-soil species (n = 10,233, OR = 3.56, p = 7.7e-13); because ipdC occurred in only 214 of 11,272 species (1.9%), the report treats the soil reversal as hypothesis-generating rather than conclusive and suggests the hypothesis that soil PGPB obtain aromatic amino acids from plant exudates while retaining ipdC for indole-3-acetic-acid production from plant-supplied substrate [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]. A phylum-plus-soil logistic model failed outright due to quasi-complete separation caused by ipdC rarity [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]. Further stated caveats: PGP detection relied on Bakta annotations matching exact gene names such as nifH, acdS, and pqqC, so product-only annotations and variant names could be missed; gene clusters were not functionally validated, with truncations, frameshifts, and pseudogenization unfiltered; GapMind tryptophan and tyrosine scores may proxy for overall metabolic completeness and should be controlled for genome size, COG coverage (COG being clusters of orthologous groups, a functional annotation scheme), or total pathway count; and co-occurrence does not establish physical linkage, leaving unresolved whether pqqC and acdS occupy the same genomic island, operon, or separate loci [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]. The report also notes the nifH count was 2,756 rather than the approximately 1,913 estimated in its research plan, reflecting incremental growth in the GTDB r214 pangenome, and states the discrepancy did not affect the analyses [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology].
