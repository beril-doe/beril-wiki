# Gazi S. Mahmud

## Contributions

The project [[summaries/alphafold_msa_annotation__REPORT]] evaluated AlphaFold multiple-sequence-alignment (MSA) depth — the number of homologous sequences assembled for a protein — as a proxy for functional annotation richness across bacterial pangenome gene clusters, joining `kbase_ke_pangenome`, `bakta_annotations`, `interproscan_domains`, and `kescience_alphafold`. Of 132,531,501 total gene clusters, it found that 38,804,903 (29.3%) had a real UniProt accession and 38,051,842 (28.7%) bridged to AlphaFold MSA depths, while 70.7% lacked UniRef100 IDs or carried UniParc-only identifiers with no AlphaFold entry [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. The project reported that core gene clusters had a median MSA depth of 15,308 versus 5,299 for auxiliary+singleton and 5,527 for auxiliary non-singleton clusters (2.89× and 2.77× higher, respectively), with 10th-percentile depths of 334 for core genes against 25–32 for accessory genes [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. It also found hypothetical-protein rates of 3.8% in core clusters, 11.6% in auxiliary non-singleton clusters and 13.8% in auxiliary+singleton clusters, with chi-square tests overwhelmingly significant (χ² > 500,000; p ≈ 0) and odds ratios of 0.25 for core versus auxiliary+singleton and 0.31 for core versus auxiliary non-singleton [src: alphafold_msa_annotation].

The same project quantified the relationship between MSA depth and domain annotation, reporting a Spearman ρ = 0.7563 across 38,051,842 gene cluster–UniProt pairs, with mean domain hits rising from 0.59 at MSA depth < 10 to 10.83 at MSA depth ≥ 10,000 and mean distinct InterPro (IPR) families rising from 0.059 to 4.601 — an 18× span in mean domain hits [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. It found the monotone MSA-depth/domain-hit relationship held within core, auxiliary non-singleton, and auxiliary+singleton classes, with core genes slightly richer in domains per MSA bin at equivalent depth [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. Across all 132,531,501 clusters, the project found 111,035,431 (83.8%) carried at least one InterProScan domain annotation, with mean hits of 7.5 and mean distinct IPR families of 3.3 — coverage exceeding the 29.3% AlphaFold bridge coverage [src: alphafold_msa_annotation].

The project further identified 415,603 distinct core clusters with MSA depth < 10 spanning 14,768 species clades, with mean and median MSA depths of 4.57 and 4.0; of these, 286,439 (68.9%) were hypothetical, 137 (0.033%) were EC-annotated and 346 (0.083%) were KEGG-mapped, against a 3.8% hypothetical rate for all core genes [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. It reported that top-ranked such proteins at MSA depth = 1 came primarily from poorly characterised marine and soil bacteria including *Oceanicoccus*, *Dwaynesavagella*, and CAILRJ01, with non-hypothetical entries including an RNA polymerase ω-subunit family protein and an FXSXX-COOH domain protein [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. The report treated the prioritisation of these clusters for experimental structural characterisation as a hypothesis rather than a validated result, and resolved the apparent core/accessory contradiction by distinguishing an MSA-depth-driven annotation gap affecting all pangenome classes from a pangenome-class gap in which accessory and singleton genes show higher hypothetical rates [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. It noted caveats including analysis of only the representative sequence per cluster, phylogenetic imbalance among the 293K genomes with over-representation of *Pseudomonas* and *E. coli*, an unstratified Spearman ρ, and a static version-6 AlphaFold snapshot whose depths later UniProt deposits may change [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. These findings feed [[concepts/pangenome-integration]], [[concepts/multi-omics-integration]], and [[concepts/gene-essentiality]], the last via the proposed join of the 415,603 clusters to fitness scores [src: alphafold_msa_annotation].
