# Gazi S. Mahmud

ORCID: [0009-0006-4046-889X](https://orcid.org/0009-0006-4046-889X)

## Contributions

The [[summaries/alphafold_msa_annotation__REPORT]] project evaluated AlphaFold multiple-sequence-alignment (MSA) depth — the number of homologous sequences aligned for a structure prediction — as a proxy for functional annotation richness across bacterial pangenome gene clusters, joining `kbase_ke_pangenome`, `bakta_annotations`, `interproscan_domains`, and `kescience_alphafold` on the KBase Data Lakehouse [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. It found that of 132,531,501 total gene clusters, 38,804,903 (29.3%) had a real UniProt accession and 38,051,842 (28.7%) bridged successfully to AlphaFold MSA depths, while 70.7% lacked UniRef100 IDs or carried UniParc-only identifiers without an AlphaFold entry [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. The same project reported that 111,035,431 of the 132,531,501 clusters (83.8%) had at least one InterProScan domain annotation, with mean hits of 7.5 and mean distinct InterPro (IPR) families of 3.3, so domain-annotation coverage exceeded the 29.3% AlphaFold bridge coverage [src: alphafold_msa_annotation].

On pangenome structure, the project found that core gene clusters had a median MSA depth of 15,308 versus 5,299 for auxiliary+singleton clusters and 5,527 for auxiliary non-singleton clusters — 2.89× and 2.77× higher respectively — and that the 10th-percentile MSA depth was 334 for core genes against 25–32 for accessory genes [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. It measured, among bridged clusters, 25,571,299 core clusters with 415,733 (1.6%) below MSA depth 10 and 979,912 (3.8%) hypothetical, 5,384,900 auxiliary non-singleton clusters with 245,002 (4.6%) below depth 10 and 622,748 (11.6%) hypothetical, and 7,095,643 auxiliary+singleton clusters with 392,959 (5.5%) below depth 10 and 979,300 (13.8%) hypothetical [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. The project reported chi-square tests that were overwhelmingly significant (χ² > 500,000; p ≈ 0), with odds ratios of 0.25 for core versus auxiliary+singleton and 0.31 for core versus auxiliary non-singleton [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. Across the 38,051,842 gene cluster–UniProt pairs it computed a Spearman ρ = 0.7563 between MSA depth and domain-hit count, with mean domain hits rising from 0.59 at MSA depth < 10 to 10.83 at MSA depth ≥ 10,000 and mean distinct IPR families rising from 0.059 to 4.601, an 18× span in mean domain hits [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. It further found this monotone relationship held within core, auxiliary non-singleton, and auxiliary+singleton classes, with core genes showing slightly higher domain richness per MSA bin than accessory genes at equivalent depth [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. These results feed [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] [src: alphafold_msa_annotation].

The project also identified 415,603 distinct core clusters with MSA depth < 10 spanning 14,768 species clades, with mean and median MSA depths of 4.57 and 4.0, of which 286,439 (68.9%) were hypothetical, 137 (0.033%) were EC-annotated, and 346 (0.083%) were KEGG-mapped — a 68.9% hypothetical rate against 3.8% for all core genes [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. It found that top-ranked such proteins at MSA depth = 1 came primarily from poorly characterised marine and soil bacteria including *Oceanicoccus*, *Dwaynesavagella*, and CAILRJ01, and that non-hypothetical entries at MSA depth = 1 included an RNA polymerase ω-subunit family protein and an FXSXX-COOH domain protein, both described as conserved structural components without solved structures for those specific lineages [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. The project interpreted these clusters as conserved by pangenome classification yet structurally isolated from characterised sequence space and therefore candidates for experimental structural characterisation, stating explicitly that this is a prioritisation hypothesis rather than direct experimental validation [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. It proposed joining the 415,603 clusters to Fitness Browser measurements to identify conserved, structurally novel proteins with condition-specific or growth-essential phenotypes, linking the work to [[concepts/gene-essentiality]] and [[concepts/multi-omics-integration]] [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. The project resolved the apparent contradiction between lower overall hypothetical rates in core genes and the 415,603 low-depth core clusters by distinguishing two annotation-gap layers — an MSA-depth-driven gap affecting all pangenome classes, and a pangenome-class gap in which accessory and singleton genes show higher hypothetical rates, likely associated with horizontal transfer, rapid evolution, and taxonomically narrow distribution [src: alphafold_msa_annotation].

The project stated several limits on these results: the analysed subset is biased toward better-studied organisms, so the annotation gap among the unbridged 70.7% is likely larger, but that inference is not directly measured [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. It noted that MSA depth was looked up for each gene cluster's representative sequence, so within-cluster sequence diversity was ignored and the representative may have higher or lower MSA depth than typical cluster members [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. It reported that the 293K genomes were not phylogenetically balanced, with common taxa such as *Pseudomonas* and *E. coli* over-represented, influencing core-gene counts and MSA-depth distributions [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. It also flagged that Spearman ρ = 0.7563 was computed on the full 38,051,842-pair dataset without subgroup stratification, so its value may differ among core, auxiliary, and singleton clusters and among organisms with different annotation gaps, and that the analysis used a static version-6 AlphaFold snapshot whose MSA depths may change with later UniProt deposits [src: alphafold_msa_annotation].
