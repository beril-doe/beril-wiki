# Dileep Kishore

## Contributions

The [[summaries/pathway_capability_dependency__REPORT]] project crossed GapMind pathway-completeness predictions with RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing) fitness data from the Fitness Browser to classify 161 organism-pathway combinations across 7 model bacteria, separating genomic capability from experimentally observed dependency [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. Of those 161 pairs, spanning 7 Fitness Browser organisms and 23 GapMind pathways, it found 57 (35.4%) Active Dependency pairs — complete pathways containing fitness-important genes — 66 (41.0%) Latent Capability pairs, 24 (14.9%) Incomplete but Important pairs, and 14 (8.7%) Missing pairs [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. The project built a composite importance score weighting essentiality at 40%, fitness breadth at 30%, and fitness magnitude at 30%, and mapped Fitness Browser genes to GapMind pathways through Fitness Browser-native KEGG annotations via the besthitkegg-to-keggmember-to-EC-to-KEGG-map-to-GapMind route [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. Stratifying by condition type, it reported that all 66 Latent Capability organism-pathway pairs became fitness-important under at least one condition type, with nitrogen limitation, stress, and carbon limitation the most frequent triggers, a result it feeds to [[concepts/condition-specific-fitness]] [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. The same report cautions that this reclassification is partly an artifact of the median-based importance threshold, since applying a median threshold to condition-specific subsets can push pathways across it by construction, and states that independent calibration against known essentials is needed before treating every reclassification as biological confirmation [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. It measured mean core gene completeness of 0.986 for Active Dependencies versus 0.975 for Latent Capabilities, and attributed the small difference partly to the use of model organisms with near-complete, well-annotated core genomes [src: pathway_capability_dependency].

Extending the analysis to 2,810 GTDB (Genome Taxonomy Database) species with at least 10 genomes, the project found that variable pathway count had a raw Spearman correlation with pangenome openness of rho=0.327, p=7.2e-71, rising to a partial Spearman correlation of rho=0.530, p=2.83e-203 after controlling for genome count [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. It reported the pathway-openness signal as positive in 13 of 18 genera tested and significant at p<0.05 in 5 of 18 — Clostridium, Eubacterium, Mesorhizobium, Pseudomonas, and Streptomyces — with the strongest within-genus correlations of rho=0.534 for Clostridium and rho=0.506 for Eubacterium [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. This analysis covered 80 GapMind pathways (18 amino acid biosynthesis and 62 carbon-source utilization) using data from approximately 293,000 genomes, and the report presents pathway variability as a mechanistically interpretable correlate of pangenome openness, contrasting it with the null relationships between openness and broader environment or phylogeny effect sizes reported by the pangenome_openness project [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. Clustering binary completeness profiles across the 80 pathways with hierarchical clustering under Jaccard distance for 225 species having at least 50 genomes and at least 3 variable pathways, it found a median of 4 metabolic ecotypes per species and a maximum of 8, observed in *Alistipes onderdonkii* and *Barnesiella intestinihominis* [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. Ecotype count correlated with pangenome openness at raw Spearman rho=0.262, p=6.8e-05 and partial rho=0.322, p=8.0e-07 after controlling for genome count, which the report reads as within-species metabolic diversity being associated with genome fluidity rather than sampling depth alone [src: pathway_capability_dependency].

Comparing core-only with all-gene GapMind completeness, the project located the largest accessory-dependent gaps in amino acid biosynthesis: leucine at 0.614 all-gene, 0.468 core-only, gap 0.146; valine at 0.614, 0.468, and 0.146; arginine at 0.613, 0.472, and 0.141; lysine at 0.804, 0.664, and 0.140; and threonine at 0.803, 0.663, and 0.140 [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. It interpreted this distribution as consistent with Black Queen dynamics and as a potential basis for community-level sharing of leucine, valine, arginine, and lysine biosynthetic capacity, while stating explicitly that the analysis does not itself demonstrate metabolite exchange [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. The report connected these results to prior KBase Data Lakehouse findings that 87.4% of metal-fitness genes were core with OR=2.08 for enrichment, that 28,017 genes were costly in laboratory conditions but conserved in nature, and that field-important genes were 83.6% core against a 76.3% baseline, framing Active Dependencies and accessory-dependent pathways as pathway-level extensions of those patterns [src: pathway_capability_dependency].

The project recorded several limits on its own results. Fitness Browser coverage restricted the Tier 1 set to 7 of 48 organisms with matching GapMind genome data — *Desulfovibrio vulgaris* Hildenborough, *Shewanella oneidensis* MR-1, *Pseudomonas putida* KT2440, *Pseudomonas stutzeri* RCH2, *Caulobacter crescentus*, *Sinorhizobium meliloti*, and *Azospirillum brasilense* — whose near-complete core genomes compressed the conservation-validation signal [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. GapMind's 80 pathways did not assess cofactor biosynthesis, lipid metabolism, or secondary metabolism, and KEGG-based mapping can miss genes lacking or carrying incorrect KEGG annotations, potentially underestimating genes assigned to a pathway [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. Ecotype counts depended on a fixed 50% maximum-distance cut in the hierarchical clustering, so the report treats the median of 4 as an order-of-magnitude estimate, and it notes that species with more sequenced genomes are more likely to show pathway variation and more ecotypes, a bias partial correlations reduce but do not eliminate [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. Correlations were controlled for genome count and checked within taxonomic groups but full phylogenetic independent contrasts were not computed, and the report cites the related ecotype_analysis project's finding that phylogeny dominates gene content in 60.5% of species [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. A proposed correlation between metabolic ecotypes and AlphaEarth environmental niche breadth was not executed, since AlphaEarth embeddings covered 28% of genomes (83K/293K), which would limit the available sample size [src: pathway_capability_dependency].
