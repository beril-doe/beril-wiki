# Dileep Kishore

## Contributions

The [[summaries/pathway_capability_dependency__REPORT]] project crossed GapMind pathway-completeness predictions with RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing) fitness data from the Fitness Browser to classify 161 organism-pathway combinations across 7 model bacteria, separating genomic capability from experimentally observed dependency. It found that of the 161 organism-pathway pairs, spanning 7 Fitness Browser organisms and 23 GapMind pathways, 57 (35.4%) were Active Dependency, 66 (41.0%) were Latent Capability, 24 (14.9%) were Incomplete but Important, and 14 (8.7%) were Missing [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. The project's composite importance score weighted essentiality at 40%, fitness breadth at 30%, and fitness magnitude at 30%, and mapped Fitness Browser genes to GapMind pathways through Fitness Browser-native KEGG annotations [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. Condition-type stratification in the same project showed that all 66 Latent Capability pairs became fitness-important under at least one condition type, most often nitrogen limitation, stress, or carbon limitation, though the report cautioned that the median-based importance threshold is circular for condition-specific reclassification and that independent calibration against known essentials is needed [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. It also reported that Active Dependencies had mean core gene completeness of 0.986 versus 0.975 for Latent Capabilities, attributing the small difference partly to the use of model organisms with near-complete core genomes; only 7 of the 48 Fitness Browser organisms had matching GapMind genome data, limiting Tier 1 to *Desulfovibrio vulgaris* Hildenborough, *Shewanella oneidensis* MR-1, *Pseudomonas putida* KT2440, *Pseudomonas stutzeri* RCH2, *Caulobacter crescentus*, *Sinorhizobium meliloti*, and *Azospirillum brasilense* [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. These findings feed [[concepts/condition-specific-fitness]], [[concepts/gene-essentiality]], and [[concepts/metabolic-model-gapfilling]] [src: pathway_capability_dependency].

The same project extended the analysis to 2,810 GTDB species with at least 10 genomes and found that variable pathway count correlated with pangenome openness at raw Spearman rho=0.327, p=7.2e-71, and at partial Spearman rho=0.530, p=2.83e-203 after controlling for genome count, supporting the hypothesis that species with more pathway variation have more open and fluid pangenomes [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. The pathway-openness signal was positive in 13 of 18 genera tested and significant at p<0.05 in 5 of 18 — Clostridium, Eubacterium, Mesorhizobium, Pseudomonas, and Streptomyces — with the strongest within-genus correlations rho=0.534 for Clostridium and rho=0.506 for Eubacterium [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. Among 225 species with at least 50 genomes and at least 3 variable pathways, the project clustered binary completeness profiles across 80 pathways by hierarchical clustering with Jaccard distance and found a median of 4 metabolic ecotypes per species and a maximum of 8, observed in *Alistipes onderdonkii* and *Barnesiella intestinihominis*; ecotype count correlated with openness at raw rho=0.262, p=6.8e-05 and partial rho=0.322, p=8.0e-07 [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. The report noted that ecotype counts depend on a fixed 50% maximum-distance clustering cut, so the median of 4 should be treated as an order-of-magnitude estimate, and that full phylogenetic independent contrasts were not computed [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. This material slots into [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] and [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]] [src: pathway_capability_dependency].

On gene-content structure, the project compared core-only with all-gene GapMind completeness and found the largest accessory-dependent gaps in amino acid biosynthesis: leucine at 0.614 all-gene, 0.468 core-only, gap 0.146; valine at 0.614, 0.468, 0.146; arginine at 0.613, 0.472, 0.141; lysine at 0.804, 0.664, 0.140; and threonine at 0.803, 0.663, 0.140 [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. It interpreted this distribution as consistent with Black Queen dynamics and as a potential basis for community-level sharing of leucine, valine, arginine, and lysine biosynthetic capacity, while stating that the analysis does not itself demonstrate metabolite exchange [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. The analysis covered 80 GapMind pathways — 18 amino acid biosynthesis and 62 carbon-source utilization pathways — over approximately 293,000 genomes, and did not assess cofactor biosynthesis, lipid metabolism, or secondary metabolism [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. The report related its results to prior KBase Data Lakehouse findings that 87.4% of metal-fitness genes were core with OR=2.08 for enrichment, that 28,017 genes were costly in laboratory conditions but conserved in nature, and that field-important genes were 83.6% core against a 76.3% baseline [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. A proposed correlation between metabolic ecotypes and AlphaEarth environmental niche breadth was not executed, as AlphaEarth embeddings covered 28% of genomes (83K/293K) [src: pathway_capability_dependency]; the accessory-completeness evidence slots into [[concepts/gene-function-acquisition-depth]] [src: pathway_capability_dependency].
