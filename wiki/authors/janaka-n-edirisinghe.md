# Janaka N. Edirisinghe

ORCID: [0000-0003-2493-234X](https://orcid.org/0000-0003-2493-234X)

## Contributions

The [[summaries/annotation_gap_discovery__REPORT]] project integrated metabolic-model gapfilling, Fitness Browser phenotypes, pangenome annotations, GapMind pathway evidence, and BLAST homology to identify genes underlying gapfilled reactions across 14 organisms and 18 carbon sources, evaluating draft models with flux-balance analysis (FBA), a constraint-based method for predicting metabolic flux and growth [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. The project found that 96 of 201 gapfilled enzymatic reaction-organism pairs (47.8%) received candidate genes with confidence scoring, exceeding its pre-specified H1 threshold of 30% [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. It reported 44 high-confidence pairs (21.9%) supported by BLAST homology, fitness evidence, and pangenome conservation; 19 medium-confidence pairs (9.5%) supported by BLAST homology with partial additional evidence; and 33 low-confidence pairs (16.4%) supported by a single evidence stream, leaving 105 pairs (52.2%) unresolved [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. The project noted that the 21.9% high-confidence proportion fell within its expected 15-25% range, and it contributed this triangulation framework to [[concepts/metabolic-model-gapfilling]] and [[concepts/multi-omics-integration]] [src: annotation_gap_discovery].

The project ran leave-one-out cross-validation and found 96 resolved pairs (47.8%) for the full pipeline, 86 (42.8%) without NB03 EC matching, 80 (39.8%) without NB04 Bakta annotations, and 73 (36.3%) without NB06 BLAST [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. It found that individual streams resolved 51 pairs (25.4%) for NB03 alone, 22 (10.9%) for NB04 alone, and 70 (34.8%) for BLAST alone, so that BLAST homology was the strongest single stream while the full pipeline added 13 percentage points over BLAST alone [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. The project measured a 3.5-fold spread in resolution across organisms, from 20% in *Bacteroides thetaiotaomicron* to 71% in *Klebsiella michiganensis*, reporting *K. michiganensis* (Koxy) with 7 total gaps and 5 resolved (71.4%), *Marinobacter* (Marino) 12 and 8 (66.7%), *Azospirillum brasilense* (azobra) 21 and 13 (61.9%), *Herbaspirillum seropedicae* (HerbieS) 17 and 10 (58.8%), *E. coli* Keio (Keio) 7 and 4 (57.1%), and *B. thetaiotaomicron* (Btheta) 15 and 3 (20.0%) [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. It found that reaction rxn02185, 2-acetolactate pyruvate-lyase (EC 2.2.1.6), and reaction rxn03436, acetohydroxy acid isomeroreductase (EC 1.1.1.86), were each resolved with high confidence in 9 of 14 organisms, consecutive steps in branched-chain amino acid biosynthesis [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. The project also found that 50 of 201 gapfilled reactions (24.9%) lacked an EC number in ModelSEED and were designated "dark reactions," of which only 8 of 50 (16%) were resolved against 88 of 151 (58.3%) for reactions with known EC numbers [src: annotation_gap_discovery].

The project selected 14 Fitness Browser organisms with rich carbon-source random-barcode transposon sequencing (RB-TnSeq), a method that measures genome-wide mutant fitness using barcoded transposon libraries, and built draft models from ModelSEED/RAST annotations and COBRApy [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. Across 574 organism-carbon source combinations it measured 42.5% baseline FBA accuracy, with recall of 86.5% (244 of 282 growth-positive conditions correctly predicted), precision of 42.5% (244 of 574 growth predictions correct), and 330 false positives [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. It applied conditional gapfilling to 38 false-negative cases, adding 219 reactions — 201 enzymatic, 14 transport, and 12 exchange — for an average of 5.8 reactions per case [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. The project identified 154 BLAST hits using DIAMOND v2.1.16 blastp against a set of 328 reviewed bacterial Swiss-Prot exemplar sequences covering 75/84 unique ECs, with high-confidence thresholds of at least 30% identity, at least 70% coverage, and e-value at most 1e-10, and medium-confidence thresholds of at least 25% identity, at least 50% coverage, and e-value at most 1e-5 [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. It further constructed a 57-EC by 14-organism presence/absence matrix, calculated fitness-specificity z-scores, identified 11 strong co-occurrence cases, and found four carbon-source-specific fitness defects, feeding [[concepts/condition-specific-fitness]] and [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] [src: annotation_gap_discovery].

The project inserted 23 gene-protein-reaction (GPR) rules — formal links between genes and reactions — into SBML models for high- and medium-confidence NB03 candidates, and its gene-knockout simulations produced zero wildtype growth on minimal carbon-source media, an expected limitation because the models required the gapfilled reactions themselves to grow on those carbon sources; the project therefore reported this FBA knockout validation as inconclusive and circular in that setting [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. It reported only partial concordance across 104 GapMind-gapfill pathway pairings, with GapMind frequently flagging incomplete pathways (`not_present` or `steps_missing`) where ModelSEED required gapfilling, and stated that exact concordance was limited because GapMind reports pathway-level step counts rather than individual step identities in the available KBase Data Lakehouse data [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. The project separately cautioned that GapMind covers ~80 carbon and amino acid pathways rather than full metabolism, so many gapfilled reactions fall outside its coverage [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. The project recorded that its draft models rested on automated RAST annotations with systematic errors, that the 42.5% baseline accuracy was dominated by false positives from overly permissive models, and that default ModelSEED gapfilling minimizes added reactions without guaranteeing biological optimality since gapfilling is non-unique [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. It also cautioned that mapping Fitness Browser experiment names to ModelSEED exchange reactions required manual curation of 109 carbon sources to compound IDs, so mapping errors could generate spurious false negatives, that fitness significance depends on the chosen absolute-fitness threshold and experiment count, and that the dataset was phylogenetically biased with 12 of 14 organisms Proteobacteria, the sole Bacteroidetes organism having the lowest resolution rate [src: annotation_gap_discovery].
