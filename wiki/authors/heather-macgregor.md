# Heather MacGregor

## Contributions

The [[summaries/metal_resistance_global_biogeography__REPORT]] project extracted 260,652 environmental MAGs from MGnify, retained 30,497 after filtering host-associated biomes, and identified usable coordinates for 22,356 MAGs. [src: metal_resistance_global_biogeography] It found that 2.8% of coordinate-associated MAGs carried at least one metal-resistance type and identified 11 significant hotspots and 3 coldspots using 5° grid cells, Fisher’s exact tests, and Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction. [src: metal_resistance_global_biogeography] The project found soil metal resistance at 5.8% prevalence (OR=5.05, q=1.8e-82) and marine metal resistance at 1.2% prevalence (OR=0.20, q=9.2e-80), providing evidence relevant to [[concepts/environmental-resistome]]. [src: metal_resistance_global_biogeography] The [[summaries/microbeatlas_metal_ecology__REPORT]] project linked metal-resistance annotations across 6,789 GTDB species with ecological niche breadth from a 464,000-sample MicrobeAtlas and found, in 606 genera, that metal type diversity predicted broader niche breadth in PGLS (β=+0.021, SE=0.0056, p=1.5×10⁻⁴), whereas total AMR cluster count and core AMR fraction were not significant. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

The [[summaries/soil_metal_functional_genomics__REPORT]] project analyzed 51,748 soil samples across nine metals and identified 2,355 significant COG–metal associations at FDR<0.05. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics] It found that chromium and lead produced the strongest signals, while transporters and biosynthesis genes dominated the top associations, and its conditional db-RDA model reported R²=0.799 with p=0.005 using 999 permutations. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics] The project also found environment-specific metal–COG relationships across soil, marine, and wastewater environments, while identifying metal co-contamination, spatial structure, and incomplete effect-size validation as unresolved limitations relevant to [[concepts/environmental-resistome]]. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics] The [[summaries/soil_frontier_genomics__REPORT]] project evaluated the clay shield hypothesis using 5,441 soil samples and found negative out-of-sample R² for the Soil & Climate (−0.205 ± 0.197), Geochemical (−0.331 ± 0.071), and Industrial (−0.221 ± 0.042) model families. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] It defined a Genomic Discovery Index as OTU Richness / (Mean Genome Completeness + 1), reported GDI values of 902.36 for forest, 890.82 for cropland, 503.42 for grassland, and 525.13 for wetland, and found a +0.8 pH-unit difference between frontier and mapped areas. [src: soil_frontier_genomics]

The [[summaries/t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt__REPORT]] project analyzed T4SS and conjugative machinery in 30,497 high-quality environmental MAGs and found that 6,652 (21.8%) carried these features. [src: t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt] It identified 92 CAZy families with elevated co-occurrence within 10 kb of T4SS loci, including GT2 glycosyltransferases, and detected 77 GT2 HGT events, of which 32 were normalized high-confidence cross-phylum events. [src: t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt] The project found that T4SS-positive genomes had 10× higher mobile genetic element density than other genomes (p<0.001), while ICEfinder detected no plasmid-borne CAZy genes and identified 12 integrative mobilizable elements among the top 100 accumulators, contributing to [[concepts/pangenome-integration]]. [src: t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt] It also found that GT2-neighborhood MAGs had a mean of 0.045 metal-resistance types versus 0.004 in non-GT2 MAGs (Mann–Whitney p=8.6e-27). [src: t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt]
