# Heather MacGregor

## Contributions

[[summaries/metal_resistance_global_biogeography__REPORT]] mapped environmental bacterial metal resistance by extracting 260,652 metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs) from MGnify, retaining 30,497 after host-associated biomes were filtered out, and obtaining usable coordinates for 22,356 MAGs (73.3% coordinate coverage) [src: metal_resistance_global_biogeography]. That project found that 2.8% of the coordinate-bearing MAGs carried at least one metal-resistance type, and a 5° grid analysis of 289 cells with ≥5 MAGs yielded 11 hotspots (OR>2, q<0.05) and 3 coldspots (OR<0.5, q<0.05), the top hotspot being the Atacama/Andean region of Chile at lat=−25°, lon=−70° with 21.8% prevalence, OR=9.83, q=7.6e-12, n=101 MAGs [src: metal_resistance_global_biogeography]. Its biome-stratified Fisher tests reported soil enrichment (5.8% prevalence, OR=5.05, q=1.8e-82), a non-significant rhizosphere result (1.9%, OR=0.66, q=0.30), and marine depletion (1.2%, OR=0.20, q=9.2e-80), while flagging a 30.8% per-sample coordinate gap (16,964 of 24,511 ENA sample records had valid latitude/longitude) that 0 of 532 MAG grid cells lacked, making it a per-sample rather than a geographic gap [src: metal_resistance_global_biogeography]. Relatedly, [[summaries/microbeatlas_metal_ecology__REPORT]] joined AMRFinderPlus pangenome annotations across 6,789 GTDB species to niche breadth from a 464,000-sample 16S atlas and found, using phylogenetic generalized least squares (PGLS, regression accounting for shared ancestry) on 606 genera, that metal type diversity was the only metal AMR predictor surviving Bonferroni correction (β = +0.021, SE = 0.0056, p = 1.5×10⁻⁴), whereas total AMR cluster count (p = 0.789) and core AMR fraction (p = 0.288) were not significant [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]. The same project reported intermediate phylogenetic signal for metal AMR traits (Pagel's λ = 0.335 for metal type diversity) against λ = 0.939 for nitrification in bacteria, saw the coefficient persist under genome-size control (β = +0.0218, p = 3.6×10⁻⁴) but lose significance under a strict 5% within-environment prevalence filter (β = +0.0166, p = 0.092, n reduced from 606 to 379), and found a null groundwater-specific fold-enrichment test (Spearman ρ = +0.042, p = 0.242) alongside a positive groundwater prevalence association (ρ = +0.112, p = 0.0019) [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]. Both projects feed [[concepts/environmental-resistome]] [src: metal_resistance_global_biogeography, microbeatlas_metal_ecology].

In soil, [[summaries/soil_metal_functional_genomics__REPORT]] tested associations between nine metals — Cu, Co, Cr, Ni, Zn, Pb, As, Cd, Hg — and functional gene content across 51,748 soil samples, identifying 2,355 significant COG–metal associations (COG = Cluster of Orthologous Groups category) at false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05, with chromium and lead giving the strongest signals and transporters and biosynthesis genes dominating the top hits [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]. Its distance-based redundancy analysis (db-RDA) reported R² = 0.799 at p = 0.005 over 999 permutations after conditioning on batch and project effects, a value the report itself flags as conditional because project accession was removed before fitting, and its copper-specific analysis of 116 COGs used 7,566 samples with KBase genomes within 10 km, pending 5 km and 20 km sensitivity checks [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]. The report also cautioned that co-varying chromium, copper, lead, and zinc make the tests non-independent, so Benjamini–Hochberg correction may be anti-conservative and the 60% discovery rate (2,355 of 3,915 implied tests) may overstate true discoveries — a direct test bearing on [[concepts/metal-cross-resistance]] [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]. [[summaries/soil_frontier_genomics__REPORT]] evaluated the clay shield hypothesis on 5,441 soil samples and returned a null: all model families had negative out-of-sample R² (Soil & Climate −0.205 ± 0.197; Geochemical −0.331 ± 0.071; Industrial −0.221 ± 0.042), and low-clay versus high-clay cross-validated R² differed by only 0.024 (95% CI −0.423, 0.161) [src: soil_frontier_genomics]. That project also defined a Genomic Discovery Index (GDI = OTU Richness / (Mean Genome Completeness + 1)) at 1° bins, reporting forest at 902.36 and cropland at 890.82 versus grassland 503.42 and wetland 525.13, and a +0.8 pH unit gap between frontier areas with GDI > 1000 (mean pH = 6.74) and mapped areas (mean pH = 5.94), while noting the index is novel, conflates sampling gap with richness, and needs a reverse-causality check [src: soil_frontier_genomics].

[[summaries/t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt__REPORT]] analyzed type IV secretion systems (T4SS) and carbohydrate-active enzyme (CAZy) gene neighborhoods in high-quality environmental MAGs, finding that 6,652 of 30,497 MAGs (21.8%) carry T4SS or conjugative machinery under a multi-marker definition, and that 92 CAZy families show elevated co-occurrence with T4SS loci at ≤10 kb, with GT2 glycosyltransferases the top hit in 767 genomes at an average length of 5,041 bp [src: t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt]. The project detected 77 horizontal gene transfer (HGT) events in the GT2 gene tree, including 32 normalized high-confidence cross-phylum events, with Node_4915 spanning 8 phyla at Max_Divergence = 4.843 and 82.9% synteny, and reported a negative divergence–synteny correlation (Spearman ρ = −0.615, p<0.001) [src: t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt]. It found no CAZy genes on plasmids by ICEfinder and 10× higher mobile genetic element density in T4SS-positive genomes (p<0.001), consistent with chromosomal or integrative transfer, and reported that GT2-neighborhood MAGs (n=376) averaged 0.045 metal-resistance types versus 0.004 for non-GT2 MAGs (n=260,276; Mann–Whitney p=8.6e-27) [src: t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt]. The 21.8% T4SS prevalence was used as a comparison point in [[summaries/metal_resistance_global_biogeography__REPORT]], which noted its 2.8% global metal-resistance prevalence is much lower, supporting the reading that metal-resistance genes and T4SS machinery are distinct and not uniformly co-distributed features [src: metal_resistance_global_biogeography].
