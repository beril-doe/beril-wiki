# Aindrila Mukhopadhyay

ORCID: [0000-0002-6513-7425](https://orcid.org/0000-0002-6513-7425)

## Contributions

[[summaries/counter_ion_effects__REPORT]] tested whether counter ions, especially chloride delivered by metal salts, confound genome-wide metal-fitness measurements, drawing on 71 NaCl experiments, 10,821 metal-important gene records, 86 organism × metal pairs, 14 metals, and 19 organisms [src: counter_ion_effects]. The project found that 4,304 of 10,821 metal-important gene records (39.8%) were also NaCl-important, with overlap occurring for every metal and ranging from 9.2% for molybdenum to 57.6% for manganese [src: counter_ion_effects]. It reported that excluding the *Synechococcus elongatus* outlier, which contributed 565/638 shared genes (88.6%) from 12 NaCl dose-response experiments, lowered overlap from 39.8% to 36.7% (3,739/10,183), so the result was not driven by that outlier [src: counter_ion_effects]. The project found that shared-stress genes were important for a mean of 4.1 metals per gene versus 2.5 metals per metal-specific gene, and that in DvH the 495 unique metal-important genes comprised 73 shared-stress genes (14.7%) and 422 metal-specific genes (85.3%), with 90.5% of metal-specific genes carrying SEED annotations compared with 78.1% of shared-stress genes [src: counter_ion_effects].

The project found that chloride concentration did not explain the overlap: zinc sulfate, which delivers 0 mM chloride, showed 44.6% NaCl overlap, exceeding cobalt at 41.3% despite cobalt delivering up to 500 mM chloride, copper at 41.0%, and nickel at 39.3%, with chloride-delivered metals averaging 41.6% overlap against 37.8% for non-chloride metals [src: counter_ion_effects]. It computed DvH whole-genome Pearson correlations with NaCl across 13 metals and 6 NaCl experiments — zinc r=0.715, manganese r=0.545, copper r=0.532, cobalt r=0.498, mercury r=0.478, nickel r=0.446, aluminum r=0.420, molybdenum r=0.396, uranium r=0.350, selenium r=0.342, chromium r=0.318, tungsten r=0.298, and iron r=0.086 — and found the hierarchy did not follow chloride concentration, since zero-chloride zinc sulfate ranked first [src: counter_ion_effects]. The project interpreted the shared signal as biology involving cell-envelope integrity, DNA repair, and ion homeostasis rather than counter-ion contamination, and noted that its mechanistic readings of high versus low NaCl correlation are extrapolations from the fitness-profile hierarchy rather than direct biochemical tests [src: counter_ion_effects]. It also reported that psRCH2 was the only organism with copper tested as both CuCl₂ and CuSO₄, giving a cross-salt correlation of r=0.439 against within-replicate correlations of r=0.720 for CuCl₂ and r=0.859 for CuSO₄, and that CuSO₄ correlated more strongly with NaCl than CuCl₂ (r=0.450 versus r=0.212) despite supplying no chloride — a comparison the project states is severely confounded because CuCl₂ was tested anaerobically and CuSO₄ aerobically [src: counter_ion_effects].

On atlas robustness, the project found that restricting analysis to metal-specific genes preserved core-genome enrichment for 12 of 14 metals, with seven metals strengthening: molybdenum +0.132 to +0.145, tungsten +0.129 to +0.134, mercury +0.116 to +0.133, selenium +0.115 to +0.131, nickel +0.088 to +0.098, chromium +0.056 to +0.069, and uranium +0.031 to +0.040 [src: counter_ion_effects]. It found aluminum weakened from +0.099 to +0.068, zinc from +0.145 to +0.115, and copper from +0.090 to +0.084, while manganese remained +0.182 and cobalt remained +0.076; cadmium changed from -0.008 to -0.108 and iron from -0.040 to +0.182, with cadmium at n=92 genes and iron at n=9 genes each from one organism, so those corrected values have low statistical power, and manganese's n=30 genes from one organism made its 100% core fraction a ceiling result [src: counter_ion_effects]. The project concluded that the original Metal Fitness Atlas result — metal-important genes 87.4% core, OR=2.08 — was not attributable to shared NaCl-stress genes, and that atlas users need not filter out NaCl-responsive genes for that core-enrichment conclusion [src: counter_ion_effects]. Against its stated hypotheses, it reported H1a (>20% overlap) supported at 39.8%, H1b (dose-dependent chloride relationship) rejected, H1c (weakened atlas enrichment after correction) rejected, H1d (chloride-versus-oxyanion functional-profile differences) not tested, and the null hypothesis that counter ions are negligible only partially supported, in that counter ions appeared negligible while shared-stress biology was substantial [src: counter_ion_effects].

The project recorded that its 39.8% overlap depends on the NaCl-importance threshold of fit < -1 or n_sick ≥ 1, and that stricter or more permissive thresholds could reduce or increase it, with the *S. elongatus* estimate especially sensitive because that organism had 12 NaCl experiments spanning 0.5–250 mM while others had 1–6 [src: counter_ion_effects]. It noted that NaCl cannot isolate chloride because it delivers both Na⁺ and Cl⁻ and produces osmotic effects, proposing KCl or choline chloride controls instead [src: counter_ion_effects]. It reported that approximately 14.3% of protein-coding genes, classified as putative essential genes, lacked transposon insertions and were absent from both NaCl and metal-fitness data, that these genes are 82% core, and that their exclusion affects the original and corrected conservation analyses equally — a limit relevant to [[concepts/gene-essentiality]] [src: counter_ion_effects]. The project further noted that manganese, cadmium, selenium, mercury, iron, molybdenum, and tungsten were each tested in only 1 organism, leaving their overlap statistics without cross-organism replication, and that its shared-stress versus metal-specific classification included no formal functional-enrichment tests, making the SEED annotation comparison descriptive and the proposed mechanistic categories hypotheses [src: counter_ion_effects]. It proposed follow-on work including formal COG, KEGG, and PFAM enrichment tests, module-level analysis by independent component analysis (ICA), matched CuCl₂/CuSO₄, ZnCl₂/ZnSO₄, and CoCl₂/CoSO₄ RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing) experiments under identical conditions, and DvH NaCl dose-response experiments at 0.1, 1, 10, 100, and 500 mM to match effective chloride doses, work that bears on [[concepts/condition-specific-fitness]] [src: counter_ion_effects].
