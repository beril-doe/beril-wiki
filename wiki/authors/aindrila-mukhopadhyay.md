# Aindrila Mukhopadhyay

ORCID: [0000-0002-6513-7425](https://orcid.org/0000-0002-6513-7425)

## Contributions

The project [[summaries/counter_ion_effects__REPORT]] tested whether counter ions — chiefly chloride delivered by metal salts — confound genome-wide metal-fitness measurements, working across 71 NaCl experiments, 10,821 metal-important gene records, 86 organism × metal pairs, 14 metals, and 19 organisms [src: counter_ion_effects]. It found that 4,304 of the 10,821 metal-important gene records (39.8%) were also NaCl-important, leaving 6,517 metal-specific records (60.2%), with overlap present for every metal and ranging from 9.2% for molybdenum to 57.6% for manganese [src: counter_ion_effects]. Excluding the *Synechococcus elongatus* outlier, which contributed 565/638 shared genes (88.6%) from 12 NaCl dose-response experiments, the overlap fell only from 39.8% to 36.7% (3,739/10,183) [src: counter_ion_effects]. Shared-stress genes were important for a mean of 4.1 metals per gene versus 2.5 metals per metal-specific gene, and in DvH the 495 unique metal-important genes comprised 73 shared-stress genes (14.7%) and 422 metal-specific genes (85.3%), with SEED annotations on 90.5% of metal-specific versus 78.1% of shared-stress genes [src: counter_ion_effects].

The same project reported that chloride dose did not explain the overlap: zinc sulfate, which delivers 0 mM chloride, showed 44.6% NaCl overlap, exceeding cobalt at 41.3% despite cobalt delivering up to 500 mM chloride, copper at 41.0%, and nickel at 39.3%; chloride-delivered metals averaged 41.6% overlap versus 37.8% for non-chloride metals [src: counter_ion_effects]. In DvH (13 metals, 6 NaCl experiments) it computed whole-genome Pearson correlations with NaCl spanning zinc r=0.715 down to iron r=0.086, a hierarchy that did not track chloride concentration since zero-chloride zinc sulfate ranked first [src: counter_ion_effects]. Its mechanistic reading of that hierarchy — broad toxicity via cofactor displacement for the high-correlation metals versus pathway-specific effects for molybdenum, uranium, selenium, chromium, tungsten, and iron — was stated as extrapolation from fitness profiles rather than direct biochemical test [src: counter_ion_effects]. A within-metal salt comparison in psRCH2, the only organism with copper tested as both CuCl₂ and CuSO₄, gave a cross-salt correlation of r=0.439 against within-replicate correlations of r=0.720 (CuCl₂) and r=0.859 (CuSO₄), and CuSO₄ correlated more strongly with NaCl than CuCl₂ (r=0.450 versus r=0.212) despite supplying no chloride — a comparison the project flagged as severely confounded because CuCl₂ was tested anaerobically and CuSO₄ aerobically [src: counter_ion_effects].

The project also re-ran the Metal Fitness Atlas core-genome enrichment after removing shared-stress genes and found enrichment preserved for 12 of 14 metals, strengthened for seven (molybdenum +0.132→+0.145, tungsten +0.129→+0.134, mercury +0.116→+0.133, selenium +0.115→+0.131, nickel +0.088→+0.098, chromium +0.056→+0.069, uranium +0.031→+0.040), weakened for aluminum (+0.099→+0.068), zinc (+0.145→+0.115) and copper (+0.090→+0.084), unchanged for manganese (+0.182) and cobalt (+0.076), and shifted for cadmium (−0.008→−0.108, n=92) and iron (−0.040→+0.182, n=9), the latter two from single organisms with low statistical power [src: counter_ion_effects]. On that basis it concluded the atlas result that metal-important genes are 87.4% core with OR=2.08 was not attributable to shared NaCl-stress genes, and that users of the atlas need not filter NaCl-responsive genes for that conclusion [src: counter_ion_effects]. Of its stated hypotheses, H1a (>20% overlap) was supported, H1b (dose-dependent chloride relationship) and H1c (weakened corrected enrichment) were rejected, and H1d (chloride-versus-oxyanion functional-profile differences) was not tested [src: counter_ion_effects]. The report recorded that the 39.8% figure depends on the NaCl-importance threshold (fit < −1 or n_sick ≥ 1), that NaCl cannot isolate chloride because it also delivers Na⁺ and osmotic stress, that ~14.3% of protein-coding genes classed as putative essential (82% core) lacked transposon insertions and were absent from both datasets, and that seven metals were tested in only one organism each [src: counter_ion_effects]. It proposed formal COG, KEGG and PFAM enrichment tests, a choline chloride comparison, independent component analysis for module-level decomposition, matched CuCl₂/CuSO₄, ZnCl₂/ZnSO₄ and CoCl₂/CoSO₄ RB-TnSeq experiments under identical conditions, and DvH NaCl dose-response experiments at 0.1, 1, 10, 100, and 500 mM [src: counter_ion_effects]. These findings feed [[concepts/condition-specific-fitness]], [[concepts/gene-essentiality]], and [[concepts/cofitness-network-architecture]] [src: counter_ion_effects].
