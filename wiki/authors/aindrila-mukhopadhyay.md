# Aindrila Mukhopadhyay

ORCID: [0000-0002-6513-7425](https://orcid.org/0000-0002-6513-7425)

## Contributions

The project [[summaries/counter_ion_effects__REPORT]] measured how much metal-important genes overlap with genes important under sodium chloride (NaCl) stress. Across 19 organisms and 14 metals (86 organism × metal pairs), 4,304 of 10,821 metal-important gene records (39.8%) were also important under NaCl stress. [src: counter_ion_effects] The NaCl data covered 71 experiments across 25 organisms and 4,648 NaCl-important genes, but only 19 of the 25 organisms entered the overlap analysis. [src: counter_ion_effects] 6,517 of 10,821 records (60.2%) were metal-specific. [src: counter_ion_effects] *Synechococcus elongatus* was an outlier at 88.6% (565/638 genes), with 12 NaCl dose-response experiments against 1–6 for other organisms. Excluding it lowered the overall overlap from 39.8% to 36.7% (3,739/10,183). [src: counter_ion_effects]

The same project tested whether chloride delivered by metal salts explains the overlap. Metal–NaCl gene overlap was uncorrelated with effective chloride concentration across the 86 organism × metal pairs, with effective chloride computed for 559 metal experiments. [src: counter_ion_effects] Zinc sulfate, with 0 mM Cl⁻, showed 44.6% overlap, which was higher than cobalt (41.3%), copper (41.0%) and nickel (39.3%). [src: counter_ion_effects] Chloride metals averaged 41.6% and non-chloride metals 37.8%. [src: counter_ion_effects] Several per-metal means (manganese, cadmium, iron, selenium, mercury, molybdenum and tungsten) come from a single organism, DvH. [src: counter_ion_effects] The report treats the overlap as shared stress biology and not counter-ion contamination. [src: counter_ion_effects]

In DvH, whole-genome fitness correlations with NaCl ranged from zinc (r=0.715) down to iron (r=0.086). [src: counter_ion_effects] Of 495 unique metal-important DvH genes, 73 (14.7%) were shared-stress and 422 (85.3%) were metal-specific. [src: counter_ion_effects] psRCH2 was the only organism tested with copper as both CuCl₂ (anaerobic) and CuSO₄ (aerobic). The cross-salt correlation was r=0.439. The report states this comparison is severely confounded by aerobic versus anaerobic growth. [src: counter_ion_effects]

The project also re-checked the Metal Fitness Atlas core-genome enrichment after removing shared-stress genes. The original Atlas found metal-important genes 87.4% core (odds ratio 2.08). [src: counter_ion_effects] After the correction, core-genome enrichment was fully preserved for 12 of 14 metals. [src: counter_ion_effects] Enrichment increased for 7 of 14 metals: Mo, W, Hg, Se, Ni, Cr and U. [src: counter_ion_effects] The report's hypothesis that chloride and oxyanion functional profiles differ was dropped and not tested. [src: counter_ion_effects]
