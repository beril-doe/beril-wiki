# Aindrila Mukhopadhyay

ORCID: [0000-0002-6513-7425](https://orcid.org/0000-0002-6513-7425)

## Contributions

[[summaries/counter_ion_effects__REPORT]] tested whether counter ions, especially chloride delivered by metal salts, confound genome-wide metal-fitness measurements [src: counter_ion_effects]. It found that 4,304 of 10,821 metal-important gene records (39.8%) were also NaCl-important, across 19 organisms, 14 metals and 86 organism × metal pairs [src: counter_ion_effects]. The project identified 71 NaCl experiments across 25 organisms [src: counter_ion_effects]. The report's novel-contribution summary describes the 39.8% overlap as spanning 25 organisms while the main result specifies 19, and both denominators are recorded without reconciliation [src: counter_ion_effects]. Overlap occurred for every metal, with per-metal organism-level mean overlaps (distinct from the pooled gene-record percentage) ranging from 9.2% for molybdenum to 57.6% for manganese [src: counter_ion_effects]. *Synechococcus elongatus* was an outlier at 88.6% shared-stress (565/638), and the report notes that its 12 NaCl experiments made the gene-importance threshold much easier to meet than the 1–6 experiments of other organisms [src: counter_ion_effects]. Excluding it lowered the overlap only to 36.7% (3,739/10,183) [src: counter_ion_effects]. In *Desulfovibrio vulgaris* Hildenborough, 73 of 495 unique metal-important genes (14.7%) were shared-stress, and 90.5% of metal-specific genes had SEED annotations compared with 78.1% of shared-stress genes [src: counter_ion_effects]. The report reads this as suggesting that the metal-specific set includes well-characterized metal homeostasis functions, an interpretation it did not formally test [src: counter_ion_effects].

The project rejected the predicted dose-dependent relationship between Cl⁻ concentration and NaCl overlap [src: counter_ion_effects]. Zinc sulfate, which delivers 0 mM chloride, had 44.6% overlap, higher than cobalt (41.3%), copper (41.0%) and nickel (39.3%) [src: counter_ion_effects]. Chloride-delivered metals averaged 41.6% overlap against 37.8% for non-chloride metals [src: counter_ion_effects]. In *D. vulgaris* Hildenborough, whole-genome Pearson correlations with NaCl ranged from zinc r=0.715 down to iron r=0.086, so the ranking did not follow chloride concentration [src: counter_ion_effects]. The report proposes that the ranking follows toxicity mechanism and attributes the overlap to shared stress biology, but neither explanation was directly tested [src: counter_ion_effects]. The report notes that NaCl is not a pure chloride control, because it also supplies sodium and osmotic stress [src: counter_ion_effects]. It also extrapolates, without measuring, that counter-ion effects would be proportionally smaller at the 0.05–2 mM concentrations typical of random barcode transposon sequencing (RB-TnSeq, pooled mutant fitness assays) [src: counter_ion_effects].

The project re-tested the Metal Fitness Atlas, whose metal-important genes were originally 87.4% core with an odds ratio (OR) of 2.08 [src: counter_ion_effects]. After restricting the analysis to metal-specific genes, core-genome enrichment was preserved for 12 of 14 metals, and the prediction that enrichment would weaken was rejected [src: counter_ion_effects]. Enrichment was reported as a delta, the difference between a metal's core fraction and its baseline, and the narrative counts 7 metals as strengthened, for example molybdenum, whose delta rose from +0.132 to +0.145 [src: counter_ion_effects]. The narrative reports modest decreases for aluminum (+0.099 → +0.068) and zinc (+0.145 → +0.115) and states that only cadmium reverses (-0.008 → -0.108) [src: counter_ion_effects]. The report's own table conflicts with that wording, because cadmium's delta stays negative while iron's changes sign (-0.040 → +0.182) [src: counter_ion_effects]. Iron's increased delta is also not counted among the 7 strengthened metals [src: counter_ion_effects]. The report calls both results statistically unreliable, because cadmium rests on n=92 genes and iron on n=9 genes, each from one organism [src: counter_ion_effects].

In psRCH2, the only organism tested with both CuCl₂ and CuSO₄, the project found a cross-salt correlation of r=0.439 [src: counter_ion_effects]. Within-replicate correlations were r=0.720 for CuCl₂ and r=0.859 for CuSO₄ [src: counter_ion_effects]. This comparison is severely confounded, because CuCl₂ was tested anaerobically and CuSO₄ aerobically [src: counter_ion_effects]. CuSO₄ correlated more strongly with NaCl than CuCl₂ did (r=0.450 versus r=0.212), despite supplying no chloride [src: counter_ion_effects].
