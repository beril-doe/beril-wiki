# David Lyon

ORCID: [0000-0002-1927-3565](https://orcid.org/0000-0002-1927-3565)

## Contributions

[[summaries/lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas__REPORT]] built a 293,059-genome atlas of lanthanide-dependent methanol oxidation across the KBase Data Lakehouse pangenome [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. It found eggNOG xoxF (K00114) in 3,690 genomes and mxaF (K14028) in 195 genomes, a global xoxF:mxaF ratio of 18.92 : 1 (Clopper-Pearson 95% CI [13.07, 27.69]; one-sided binomial p = 7.6 × 10⁻²² against the pre-registered 10/11 threshold) [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. The project tested this with three phylogenetic validations, and all three had 95% CI lower bounds above the 10 : 1 threshold [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. Naive genome-level pooling over 3,885 MDH-informative genomes gave an xoxF fraction of 0.950 [95% CI 0.942, 0.956] [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. Equal weighting across 271 GTDB families gave 0.960 [95% CI 0.941, 0.976] [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. A Bayesian binomial GLMM (generalized linear mixed model) with phylum and family random intercepts gave 0.993 [95% CI 0.992, 0.994], corresponding to ~143 : 1 [122, 169] [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. After Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate (FDR) correction, giving adjusted p-values (p_BH), the project found high xoxF rates in Acidobacteriota (28.3%, 95 : 1, p_BH = 1.2 × 10⁻⁷⁹) and Gemmatimonadota (25.4%, 98 : 1, p_BH = 1.5 × 10⁻²⁷) [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. The report noted that annotation-based detection does not establish enzyme activity or methylotrophic physiology [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas].

The same project found Bakta-validated lanmodulin in 62 genomes, all within Beijerinckiaceae, Acetobacteraceae or Hyphomicrobiaceae (p = 9.8 × 10⁻⁷ against an 80% threshold) [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. It found xoxF co-occurring with lanmodulin in 49/62 = 79.0% of these genomes, just below the pre-registered 80% co-occurrence threshold, so that hypothesis (H3b) was not formally supported (p = 0.65) [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. It found soil/sediment enriched for xoxF relative to the generic_environmental reference (OR = 1.92, p_BH = 6.1 × 10⁻³⁹) and host-associated samples depleted (OR = 0.058) [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. The rare-earth-element (REE)-impacted signal (n = 37, OR = 3.51, p_BH = 0.082) was descriptively elevated but did not clear the FDR threshold [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. The project found only 4/37 REE-acid-mine-drainage MAGs (metagenome-assembled genomes) carrying any xoxF and 0/37 carrying Bakta-validated lanmodulin, while stress products such as RecN (33/37) were prevalent [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. For PQQ, the obligate XoxF cofactor, it found that 1,288 (59%) of 2,185 xoxF genomes lacking eggNOG pqq annotation had at least 1 Bakta PQQ product, indicating annotation gaps [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. It found 897 xoxF genomes lacking PQQ evidence from either source [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. It also found 505 eggNOG lanM calls that were false positives concentrated in unrelated gut Bacillota [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas].

[[summaries/clay_confined_subsurface__REPORT]] compared 9 deep-confined clay genomes, 30 shallow-clay genomes and a soil baseline of 150 genomes (137 after quality filtering) [src: clay_confined_subsurface]. It found dissimilatory sulfate-reduction markers in 5/9 deep genomes versus 0.018 expected under the Mitzscherling et al. (2023) rock-attached null (binomial p = 4.0×10⁻¹²) [src: clay_confined_subsurface]. The report read this as matching the Bagnoud porewater paradigm and as a diagnostic of cultivation bias [src: clay_confined_subsurface]. The project found the deep cohort enriched for Wood–Ljungdahl and group 1 [NiFe]-hydrogenase (both p_BH = 0.004 after Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction), but within Bacillota_B neither difference was significant (BH-adjusted p = 1.0) [src: clay_confined_subsurface]. Within Bacillota_B, it found sulfate reduction still associated with deep clay (5/5 versus 4/19, p_BH reported as 0.04 and 0.044) [src: clay_confined_subsurface]. The project withdrew its original iron-reduction narrative because K07811, K17324 and K17323 had been misidentified as iron-reduction markers [src: clay_confined_subsurface]. Its corrected multi-heme cytochrome detector gave rates of 55.6% (n = 9), 40.0% (n = 30) and 40.9% (n = 149) for deep, shallow and baseline cohorts, with all Fisher p ≥ 0.46 [src: clay_confined_subsurface]. The project found GapMind amino-acid pathway completeness not elevated in the deep cohort: after CheckM filtering, 6 deep genomes averaged 15.50/18 versus 17.14/18 for 137 baseline genomes (p = 0.009, d = −0.84) [src: clay_confined_subsurface]. The report noted an 18-pathway ceiling and stated that the result does not reject self-sufficiency as a deep-subsurface adaptation [src: clay_confined_subsurface].

[[summaries/bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory__REPORT]] compared 10 deep-clay Bacillota_B genomes with 62 soil-baseline Bacillota_B genomes [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]. Its Fisher tests across 14,109 eggNOG orthologous groups (OGs) found 547 enriched OGs at q<0.05 [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]. Manual inspection found COG1977 (molybdopterin cofactor metabolism) in 10/10 anchors versus 11/62 baselines [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]. It also found a DsrE/DsrF/DsrH-like OG in 7/10 anchors versus 0/62 baselines [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]. The report estimated the H1-relevant total at 80–100 OGs, which it stated is not a finalized count [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]. The project rejected the prediction that deep-clay genomes would be smaller, in the opposite direction [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]. Anchor genomes averaged 4,110,038 bp versus 3,046,124 bp (Cohen's d = +1.39, p = 0.025) [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]. CheckM completeness was 94.7% versus 94.3% (p = 0.93), so the report concluded a genome-quality artifact did not explain the difference [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]. The project's corrected multi-heme cytochrome comparison found no significant cohort differences (e.g. deep versus shallow OR 1.88, p = 0.46) [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]. It found that multi-heme cytochrome PFAM protein-family domains were sparse within Bacillota_B (~6 clusters), so CXXCH motif counting carried most of the corrected signal [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]. The report cautioned that marginal effects with anchor counts of 3–5 should be treated descriptively, and that spanning four orders only partly mitigates genus-level phylogenetic confounding [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory].
