# Markus de Raad

ORCID: [0000-0001-8263-9198](https://orcid.org/0000-0001-8263-9198)

## Contributions

The project [[summaries/lignin_community_enrichment__REPORT]] examined how lignin enrichment, with and without added labile carbon, reshapes bacterial and fungal communities. It also tested whether first-round history still shapes a second round. It found that one lignin round almost completely replaced the base bacterial community. Its bacterial evidence is strong, with large effect sizes and consistent replicates. Its fungal evidence is weaker because replicates disagree, and tests on single OTUs are limited by n=3 per group. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

In the bacterial community, the unenriched base was dominated by unclassified taxa (Incertae Sedis, 44.3%), with Pseudomonas below 0.1%. After one lignin round, Pseudomonas (39.3%) and Acinetobacter (25.2%) together made up more than 64% of 16S reads. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Shannon diversity fell from 6.46 (Base) to 3.16 (L), and observed OTUs fell from 1,594 to 163, a 90% reduction. PERMANOVA (permutational multivariate analysis of variance) attributed 97.9% of community variance to treatment (R²=0.979, p=0.001). [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Adding labile carbon (LC) raised Acinetobacter to 41.7% from 25.2% in L, lowered Pseudomonas to 23.0% from 39.3%, and raised Aeromonas to 20.2% from 0.1%. Shannon diversity was 2.41 in LC versus 3.16 in L, and Pielou's evenness was 0.49 versus 0.62. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] The report's explanation, that labile carbon lets copiotrophic taxa co-dominate, is an interpretation and not a tested result. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

In Round 2, bacterial communities grouped by Round 1 history. PERMANOVA on the 12 Round 2 samples assigned 58.9% of variance to Round 1 history (F=14.31, R²=0.589, p=0.002) and 32.7% to the current Round 2 carbon source (F=4.85, R²=0.327, p=0.018). The history/R2 distance ratio was 1.15–1.59. The report presents this as quantitative evidence for ecological memory, because communities with different histories did not converge under identical Round 2 conditions. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Several genera responded. Flavobacterium rose from 0.1% in base to 14.1% in L, with a CLR (centered log-ratio) effect of +11.2, and fell to 1.4% in LC. Comamonas was enriched 17–51x in Round 2 groups (3.6–5.1%). Rhodococcus stayed at 0.03–0.67% across conditions. Sphingomonas was higher in base (1.74%) than in enriched conditions. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

Fungal responses were more extreme but less reliable. The base community was Fusarium-dominated (26.5%), with 253 OTUs and Shannon=3.50. Under lignin alone Fusarium persisted (43.4%) and Fusicolla emerged (30.3%). With labile carbon added, Chrysosporium reached 61.4% and Aspergillus 27.2%. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] In Round 2, Malassezia dominated L-L (63.4%) and LC-L (50.0%), and Pleurotus reached 50.0% in LC-LC. ITS diversity fell from 253 OTUs in Base to 3–8 OTUs per group. Within-group Bray-Curtis distances reached 0.99–1.00 for several Round 2 groups, versus 0.09 for 16S, which limits statistical inference for ITS. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]
