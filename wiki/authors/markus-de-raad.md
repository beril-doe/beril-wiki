# Markus de Raad

ORCID: [0000-0001-8263-9198](https://orcid.org/0000-0001-8263-9198)

## Contributions

The [[summaries/lignin_community_enrichment__REPORT]] project used user-provided 16S V3–V4 and ITS2 amplicon data from 21 samples across 7 groups, with 3 replicates per group, to test how lignin enrichment, labile-carbon co-supplementation, and sequential passaging restructure bacterial and fungal communities, and found that lignin acts as a strong bacterial selective filter: the base community was dominated by unclassified taxa (Incertae Sedis, 44.3%) with Pseudomonas at <0.1%, while after one lignin-enrichment round Pseudomonas reached 39.3% and Acinetobacter 25.2%, together comprising >64% of 16S reads [src: lignin_community_enrichment]. The project measured a fall in Shannon diversity from 6.46 to 3.16 and in observed OTUs from 1,594 to 163, a 90% reduction, and a global PERMANOVA — a permutation-based test of community-composition differences — attributing 97.9% of community variance to treatment (R²=0.979, p=0.001), with the Base-versus-Round-1 model giving R²=0.992 and p=0.008 [src: lignin_community_enrichment]. The project found that adding labile carbon to lignin increased Acinetobacter from 25.2% to 41.7%, reduced Pseudomonas from 39.3% to 23.0%, and increased Aeromonas from 0.1% to 20.2%, with Shannon diversity declining from 3.16 to 2.41 and Pielou's evenness from 0.62 to 0.49, which the project read as a shift toward fast-growing copiotrophic taxa alongside lignin-associated organisms rather than a simple increase in lignin-degrader abundance [src: lignin_community_enrichment].

The same project found that Round-1 carbon history persists into Round 2: lignin-history groups L-L and L-LC were Pseudomonas-dominated at 52–53% with Acinetobacter below 1–20%, while lignin-plus-labile-history groups LC-L and LC-LC were Acinetobacter-dominant at 31–34% with Pseudomonas at 24–26% and Enterobacter at 2.7–18.1% [src: lignin_community_enrichment]. In the 12-sample Round-2 factorial PERMANOVA the project found Round-1 history explained 58.9% of variance (F=14.31, R²=0.589, p=0.002) versus 32.7% for current Round-2 carbon source (F=4.85, R²=0.327, p=0.018), with history/Round-2 distance ratios of 1.15 for Round-2 lignin and 1.59 for Round-2 lignin plus labile carbon [src: lignin_community_enrichment]. The project found that communities with different Round-1 histories did not converge under identical Round-2 conditions — mean Bray–Curtis distance was 0.507 for L-L versus LC-L and 0.486 for L-LC versus LC-LC, against 0.441 for L-L versus L-LC and 0.306 for LC-L versus LC-LC — and reported a memory index of ~0.50, meaning communities with different histories retained approximately half of the maximum possible divergence under the same current condition, with OTU retention of 81% from L to L-L and 91% from LC to LC-LC; this result is slotted into [[concepts/ecological-memory]] [src: lignin_community_enrichment].

At the genus level the project found Pseudomonas increased from <0.1% in the base community to 23–53% across enriched conditions; Flavobacterium increased from 0.1% to 14.1% under lignin-only enrichment with a CLR (centered log-ratio, a compositional effect size) effect of +11.2 in the Base-versus-L comparison but declined to 1.4% with labile-carbon co-supplementation; Comamonas increased from 0.1% to 3.6–5.1% in Round-2 groups, a 17–51× enrichment; Aminobacter was absent from base and Round-1 groups but reached 7.6% in L-L and 6.4% in LC-L; Rhodococcus ranged from 0.03–0.67% and was highest in L at 0.67%; and Sphingomonas was higher in the base community at 1.74% than in enriched conditions [src: lignin_community_enrichment]. The main Base-versus-Lignin CLR effects the project reported were +12.3 for Acinetobacter, +11.5 for Pseudomonas, and +11.2 for Flavobacterium, and in the L-versus-LC comparison Aeromonas was enriched in LC at +6.4 and Shewanella at +6.6, while Peredibacter (-7.3) and Comamonas (-6.8) were enriched in L; the project stated these effect sizes remain interpretable even where small-sample p-values cannot support individual-OTU significance, and this condition-dependent enrichment feeds [[concepts/condition-specific-fitness]] [src: lignin_community_enrichment].

The project's fungal results were strong but poorly reproducible: the base fungal community contained 253 OTUs at Shannon 3.50 with Fusarium at 26.5%, lignin-only enrichment yielded 8 OTUs at Shannon 1.37 with Fusarium at 43.4% and Fusicolla at 30.3%, lignin plus labile carbon yielded 6 OTUs at Shannon 0.62 with Chrysosporium at 61.4% and Aspergillus at 27.2%, and in Round 2 Malassezia reached 63.4% in L-L and 50.0% in LC-L while Pleurotus reached 50.0% in LC-LC [src: lignin_community_enrichment]. The project found fungal replicate consistency substantially worse than bacterial, with within-group Bray–Curtis distances of 0.99–1.00 for several Round-2 groups versus 0.09 for 16S; the ITS global PERMANOVA gave R²=0.582 and p=0.001 and the Round-2 history effect was not statistically detectable (R²=0.142, p=0.090), and a preregistered Procrustes analysis comparing 16S and ITS ordinations was not completed because extreme ITS variability would make the rotation uninformative — a null outcome the project carried into [[concepts/multi-omics-integration]] [src: lignin_community_enrichment]. On power, the project reported that with n=3 per group pairwise Mann–Whitney U tests have a minimum achievable p-value of 0.10 because only 10 permutations exist for 3-versus-3 comparisons and pairwise PERMANOVA has a permutation floor of approximately p~0.10, so no individual OTUs reached FDR (false-discovery-rate) significance, while global Kruskal–Wallis tests were significant across all 7 groups for all reported metrics (16S p=0.006–0.010, ITS p=0.015–0.041) [src: lignin_community_enrichment]. The project further noted that PERMDISP was significant for both markers (16S p=0.0004, ITS p=0.0001), so PERMANOVA results reflect both location and dispersion differences; that sample LL_1 was excluded after retaining 74 reads, reducing L-L to n=2; that 97% vsearch OTUs rather than ASVs may merge closely related organisms; that ITS taxonomy used NCBI ITS_RefSeq_Fungi with 19,375 reference sequences rather than UNITE, so environmental taxa may be missed; that no technical metadata on extraction or library-preparation batches were available; and that the functional interpretation of lignin degradation rests on taxonomic associations and literature context rather than directly measured gene-level pathway enrichment [src: lignin_community_enrichment]. The project proposed cross-referencing Pseudomonas, Acinetobacter, and Comamonas against pangenome gene annotations, a bridge slotted into [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] [src: lignin_community_enrichment].
