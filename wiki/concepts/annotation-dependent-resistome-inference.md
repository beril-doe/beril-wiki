# Resistance Ecology Is Shaped by Annotation Completeness

Ecological comparisons of antimicrobial resistance (AMR) are partly comparisons of what annotation pipelines can recognize, not only comparisons of the underlying biology. The report used AMRFinderPlus-focused annotation, which may underestimate novel environmental resistance and favor clinical enrichment because resistance genes from clinical contexts are better characterized. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] This creates a potential link between [[concepts/annotation-dependent-resistome-inference]], [[concepts/bioinformatic-representation-coverage-bias]], and [[concepts/structural-annotation-gap]]: an apparent environmental resistome pattern can be strengthened, weakened, or redirected when unrecognized genes and incomplete functional labels are included. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]

## Evidence from the environmental resistome analysis

The assembled dataset contained 82,908 AMR gene clusters across 14,723 species, while mechanism classification assigned 26,220 clusters to enzymatic inactivation, 22,223 to metal resistance, 11,067 to target modification, and 5,224 to efflux. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] However, 15,550 clusters, representing 18.7% of all AMR clusters, could not be assigned to a mechanism from gene name or product annotation and were excluded from mechanism fractions. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] Because the excluded clusters may not be distributed evenly across environments, the reported mechanism comparisons could be biased. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]

The reported environment-dependent mechanism composition is therefore best treated as a result about annotated and classifiable AMR, rather than a complete census of resistance mechanisms. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] Among classified fractions, metal resistance represented 45.0% of AMR in aquatic species and 44.0% in soil species, compared with 6.1% in human-gut species, whereas target modification represented 43.6% in human-gut species and 6.2% in aquatic species. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] These contrasts are compatible with different ecological exposures, but the report explicitly identifies annotation incompleteness as a possible source of distortion. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]

The environment effects were statistically strong but not necessarily large in explanatory terms: the effect sizes for the four mechanism classes were η² = 0.055 for efflux, η² = 0.019 for enzymatic inactivation, η² = 0.107 for metal resistance, and η² = 0.100 for target modification. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] The report describes these results as explaining 2–13% of variance in AMR composition, while noting that the 18.7% of clusters lacking mechanism assignments could bias comparisons if unclassified clusters were distributed non-randomly across environments. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]

Annotation completeness also affects the interpretation of core and accessory AMR. The study used a 95% prevalence threshold to define core AMR, but noted that this threshold is dependent on species genome count and therefore produces imprecise core/accessory labels for sparsely sampled species. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] Consequently, the finding that clinical species had 68% accessory AMR whereas soil species had 43% accessory AMR may reflect both biological differences and unequal sampling or recognition of resistance genes. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]

## What this changes in ecological interpretation

The clinical-versus-environmental contrast remains substantial in the annotated dataset: clinical-source species had a median of 5 AMR gene clusters, compared with 2 for soil, aquatic, and host-associated species, with Kruskal–Wallis H = 781.9, p = 9.4×10⁻¹⁶⁷, and η² = 0.056. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] Yet this result should not be read as a direct estimate of total resistance diversity because the analysis may be more sensitive to known clinical resistance genes than to novel environmental genes. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]

The same qualification applies to the species-level association between clinical representation and AMR: among 823 species meeting the within-species criteria, the fraction of genomes from clinical sources predicted total AMR cluster count with Spearman rho = 0.465 and p = 2.2×10⁻⁴⁵. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] Clinical-dominated species had a mean of 72.9 AMR clusters versus 16.4 in environmental-dominated species, but this was a species-level proxy rather than a true per-genome within-species comparison. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] Annotation bias could therefore contribute to the apparent clinical enrichment, alongside sampling, ecology, and genuine acquisition of resistance.

This issue connects the report to [[concepts/environmental-resistome]] and [[concepts/functional-dark-matter]]. The environmental resistome result supports the hypothesis that ecological niches differ in resistance composition, but it does not establish that the magnitude of those differences is independent of annotation coverage. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] It also motivates integration with [[concepts/pangenome-integration]], because pangenome-scale accessory genes are precisely the component most likely to include poorly characterized or lineage-specific resistance candidates. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]

## Tensions

The report finds environment-dependent AMR diversity and mechanism composition after phylogenetic controls, including significant within-phylum effects in 5 of 6 major phyla and significant within-family effects in 20 of 141 testable families. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] At the same time, AMRFinderPlus-focused annotation may underestimate novel environmental resistance and preferentially recognize clinically characterized resistance genes. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] These findings are not mutually exclusive: ecological structure may be real, while its observed magnitude and mechanism profile remain annotation-dependent. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]

The report also found that 52 of 64 AlphaEarth environmental-embedding dimensions correlated significantly with AMR diversity at FDR < 0.05, with the top dimension A34 having rho = +0.24, and that a Mantel test gave r = 0.098 and p = 0.001 for environmental distance versus AMR-profile distance. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] Because the embedding dimensions are opaque and the embedding analysis covered 28% of genomes, these continuous associations provide supplementary support but do not resolve whether unannotated resistance would show the same ecological structure. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]

## Open Directions

- Re-annotate the 15,550 mechanism-unassigned clusters with broader homology, profile-HMM, and structure-based searches, then test whether environment-dependent mechanism fractions and effect sizes change. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]
- Compare AMRFinderPlus calls with independent resistance-gene resources and manually reviewed environmental candidates to quantify false-negative rates by environment and mechanism. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]
- Recalculate core and accessory AMR after stratifying species by genome-count depth, then test whether the clinical 68% versus soil 43% accessory contrast persists under matched sampling. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]
- Use metagenome-assembled genomes and isolate genomes with comparable annotation quality to test whether clinical enrichment remains after reducing differences in sampling and annotation coverage. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]
- Perform environment-specific gene identification on annotated and previously unclassified clusters, asking whether novel environmental candidates explain part of the observed metal-resistance enrichment. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]
