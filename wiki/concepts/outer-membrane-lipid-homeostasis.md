# Outer-membrane lipid homeostasis after lipid A loss

Outer-membrane lipid homeostasis after lipid A loss describes coordinated lipid replacement, transport, envelope remodeling, and temporally distinct regulatory responses that maintain bacterial envelope integrity when lipid A is absent. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] In the Δ*fur* Δ*sspB* background of *Caulobacter crescentus*, viability of Δ*lpxc* was evaluated using transcriptomics, published ChvI regulons, single-replicate outer-membrane proteomics, RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing, a pooled transposon-mutant fitness assay) fitness data, and comparative annotation. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] The combined evidence supports a phased, component-specific rescue architecture rather than a single-gene phenotype or a uniform transcriptional response, while leaving causal order unresolved. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

## Prior work the project builds on

The project's foundational reference is Zik et al. 2022 (PMID 35649364), authored by this project's data provider K.R. Ryan, which establishes that Δ*lpxc* viability in *C. crescentus* requires both Δ*fur* and anionic sphingolipid (CPG), and that "Fur-regulated processes (not iron status per se)" underlie viability. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] The same study reports Caulobacter lipid A as conditionally dispensable in the absence of *fur* and in the presence of anionic sphingolipids, so it is the premise the present work tests mechanistically rather than re-establishes. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

Independent literature supports both the pathway's Caulobacter restriction and its enzymology: Olea-Ozuna et al. 2020/2024 (PMIDs 33063925, 39093898) are cited as independent confirmation that Caulobacter encodes a sphingolipid biosynthesis pathway absent in the other three Gram-negatives. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] The 2020 paper reports five structural genes required for ceramide synthesis in *Caulobacter* and for bacterial survival, and the 2024 paper reports that genes required for phosphosphingolipid formation in *C. crescentus* contribute to bacterial virulence. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] Downstream steps are characterized separately: Dhakephalkar et al. 2025 (PMID 39829823) identify CpgD as a phosphoglycerate cytidylyltransferase required for ceramide diphosphoglycerate synthesis, and Dhakephalkar et al. 2023 (PMID 37286040) characterize an evolutionarily distinct bacterial ceramide kinase from *C. crescentus*. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The shared-component transport model comes from Uchendu, Isom and Klein 2026 (bioRxiv 10.1101/2026.04.12.717747), which identifies the Caulobacter sphingolipid inner-membrane transporters CCNA_01213/01214/01226 (*lptG2*/*F2*/*C2*) and shows that they share the canonical LptB ATPase with LPS transport. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] That work is a preprint reporting that homologues of the inner-membrane LPS transport proteins are required for sphingolipid transport in *C. crescentus*; its preprint status is material, so predictions drawn from it are treated here as a model to be tested rather than as settled. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The ChvI regulon definitions used in this project come from two published studies: Stein, Fiebig and Crosson 2021 (PMID 34124942) report that the ChvG-ChvI and NtrY-NtrX two-component systems coordinately regulate growth of *C. crescentus*, and Quintero-Yanes, Mayard and Hallez 2022 (PMID 36480504) report that the two-component system ChvGI maintains cell envelope homeostasis in *C. crescentus*. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

Two published Fur studies anchor the iron axis. Leaden et al. 2018 (PMID 30210482) report that iron deficiency generates oxidative stress and activation of the SOS response in *C. crescentus*, and this project re-analyzed their supplementary Table 2 to obtain a clean Fur-only differentially expressed gene signature. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] da Silva Neto et al. 2009 (PMID 19520766) report that Fur controls iron homeostasis and oxidative stress defense in the oligotrophic alpha-proteobacterium *C. crescentus*, and their earlier Fur ChIP/microarray data cross-validate the concordant-strong gene set—HutA, the FrpB-like cluster, and bacterioferritin—as canonical Fur targets. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The Δ*sspB* side of the strain background has its own literature context: Bhat et al. 2013 (PMID 23647068) identify ClpP substrates in *C. crescentus* and report a role for regulated proteolysis in bacterial development, and Flynn et al. 2004 (*Genes Dev* 18(18):2292) report that the SspB adaptor delivers a regulator of the extracytoplasmic-stress response to the AAA+ protease ClpXP for degradation. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The comparator literature establishes that LPS loss is survivable by other means. Moffatt et al. 2010 (PMID 20855724) report that colistin resistance in *A. baumannii* is mediated by complete loss of lipopolysaccharide production, and Kang et al. 2021 (PMID 33402533) report that septal class A penicillin-binding protein activity and ld-transpeptidases mediate selection of colistin-resistant, LOS-deficient *A. baumannii*. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] The report reads that route—*A. baumannii* Δ*lpxc* via PBP1A loss plus LdtJ/LdtK—as mechanistically distinct from but biologically analogous to the Caulobacter engagement of SdpA/PleA plus Pal-Tol peptidoglycan remodeling: both organisms reorganize peptidoglycan when LPS or LOS is removed, but via different enzyme-family specifics. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

Steeghs et al. 2001 (PMID 11742971) examined the outer membrane composition of an LPS-deficient *N. meningitidis* mutant—the cited title alone does not specify that composition—and the report labels that route *N. meningitidis* Δ*lpxA* via capsule substitution, consistent with its own finding that *N. meningitidis* has 9 capsule-biosynthesis PaperBLAST hits versus 2-3 in the others. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] Gao et al. 2008 (PMID 18795947) identify two late acyltransferase genes responsible for lipid A biosynthesis in *M. catarrhalis*, which the report labels the *M. catarrhalis* late-acyltransferase truncation route, consistent with its finding that the *lpxX*/*lpxL* family is present in *A. baumannii* (8) and *N. meningitidis* (3) but absent in *C. crescentus* (0). [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The Pal interpretation rests on two external papers rather than on this project's own measurements. Tan WB and Chng SS 2025 (PMID 40055349, *Nature Communications* 16(1):2293, DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-57630-y) establish that the primary function of the Tol-Pal complex is retrograde phospholipid transport for outer-membrane lipid homeostasis, not LPS-Pal structural anchoring as an earlier draft of the report supposed, and the adversarial review identified this as a missing citation. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] Yeh et al. 2010 (PMID 20693330, *Journal of Bacteriology* 192(19):4847-4858) establish that the Caulobacter Tol-Pal complex is essential for outer-membrane integrity, for outer-membrane constriction at cell division, and for the positioning of a polar localization factor, with Caulobacter Tol-Pal essential unlike *E. coli* Tol-Pal; this is literature context for the Pal upregulation finding rather than a new project measurement, and it matters because the rescued strain must complete division with no LPS. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] That essentiality complicates depletion experiments in Caulobacter, but the report notes it remains testable in conditional-depletion strains. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

## Caulobacter rescue architecture and regulatory timing

Comparative evidence most directly supports a Caulobacter-specific lipid-substitution route: *spt* and *cerR* were Caulobacter-unique across *C. crescentus*, *A. baumannii*, *N. meningitidis*, and *M. catarrhalis*, with patterns 1000, while ChvG and ChvI also showed pattern 1000. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] This supports the interpretation that Caulobacter uses a sphingolipid-based substitute pathway and a Caulobacter-restricted ChvG–ChvI regulatory circuit, whereas the tested comparator species lack both features. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] The comparative PaperBLAST presence/absence scan—PaperBLAST being a homology search over proteins mentioned in published papers—reported the sphingolipid biosynthesis pathway as Caulobacter-unique because *spt* (serine palmitoyltransferase), *bcerS* (bacterial ceramide synthase), and sphingosine kinase are absent in the other three species. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] An independent NCBI annotation count over the same four species, in *C. crescentus*/*A. baumannii*/*N. meningitidis*/*M. catarrhalis* order, gave ChvG(5,0,0,0) and ChvI(6,0,0,0), so the restriction of this regulatory circuit does not rest on a single search method. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] Both methods therefore support the report's reading of ChvG-ChvI as alphaproteobacterial-restricted in this comparison. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The comparator genomes instead encode the alternative lipid A-loss routes published for them: *A. baumannii* has PBP1A and Ld-transpeptidases for the Kang 2021 peptidoglycan-remodeling route; *N. meningitidis* has a large capsule biosynthesis locus with 9 PaperBLAST hits for the Steeghs 2001 capsule-substitution route; and *A. baumannii* and *N. meningitidis* both carry late acyltransferases (*lpxX*/*lpxL*, 8 and 3 hits) for the Gao 2008 acylation-truncation route. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] This **refines** rather than weakens the Caulobacter-unique result: survival without lipid A appears reachable by species-specific routes, and because each comparator route here is inferred from annotation-dependent homology rather than measured, the absence of a route in a comparator genome is weaker evidence than its presence. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The [[entities/sphingolipid-biosynthesis]] pathway was constitutive rather than induced in the tested comparison: 0/6 biosynthesis genes were UP, *spt* was DOWN by -0.64 with FDR 0.002—FDR being the false-discovery-rate-adjusted p-value—and *sphk* was DOWN by -0.40 with FDR 0.02. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] This **refines** an acute-induction model: the measured pathway showed no biosynthesis-gene induction under the tested condition, shifting attention toward transport, retention, and envelope remodeling. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] The orientation heatmap of the sphingolipid locus shows the locus neither induced nor strongly suppressed across the strain series, and the biosynthesis-cluster heatmap is flat to slightly decreasing across the same series, consistent with rescue drawing on an existing lipid pool rather than on new synthesis. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] Because *spt* and *sphk* are mildly DOWN rather than UP, the cell does not respond to lipid A loss by making more CPG-biosynthesis transcript; the report concludes that rescue must be flux-driven or post-transcriptional, which rules out the "Δ*fur* derepresses sphingolipid biosynthesis" framing while leaving the flux and post-transcriptional alternatives themselves proposed rather than measured. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The orientation pass also set the testing agenda before formal hypothesis tests: it rejected the initial framing that Δ*fur* derepresses sphingolipid biosynthesis, found the canonical Lpt apparatus maintained or up in line with a shared-component model, and added peptidoglycan remodeling as a fourth hypothesis after SdpA appeared at +4.8 log2 in the outer-membrane proteome. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The phased regulatory evidence comes from comparing published ChvI-induced regulons with the 4584-vs-4580 transcript contrast in the Δ*fur* Δ*sspB* Δ*rsaA* and Δ*rsaA* backgrounds. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] The published ChvI-induced regulons from Stein 2021 and Quintero-Yanes 2022 contained 488 genes in the tested universe. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] These genes partitioned into 20 unique-to-early genes, 10 genes induced in both phases, and 49 late-consequence genes, producing a disjoint total of 79 ChvI-induced genes engaged in these contrasts; the three cohorts describe only that engaged subset and not the whole 488-gene published union, and the phase-structure threshold of at least 10 genes per cohort was passed. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] The partition is disjoint by construction, 20 + 10 + 49 = 79, with ChvI itself in the both-phase set at logFC +1.45 in the early contrast; an earlier label that treated the 20 unique-to-early and the 10 both-phase genes as a single 30-gene early cohort conflated them and is superseded. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The 20-gene unique-to-early cohort—genes induced in the 4584-vs-4580 contrast but not in 4599-vs-4584—included the lasso peptide cyclase CCNA_02794 at +8.6 logFC, the ApbE iron-sulfur cluster repair protein at +3.8 logFC, and *imuB* SOS DNA polymerase at +1.5 logFC. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] These observations support an early response involving specialized envelope-associated products, iron-sulfur cluster maintenance, and stress-associated functions. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] The 10-gene both-phases cohort, induced in both contrasts as a cooperative continuation, included ChvI itself, CCNA_00237, at +1.45 logFC in the 4584-vs-4580 contrast with continued induction in 4599-vs-4584, which the report reads as ChvG–ChvI autoregulation. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] SIMPL-family CCNA_02378 changed from +1.9 to +3.9 logFC across the phase comparisons, and amelogenin/CpxP-related CCNA_03997 was also present in this cohort. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The 49-gene late-consequence cohort—genes induced only after lipid A is lost, in the 4599-vs-4584 contrast—included the LolA-family outer-membrane lipoprotein carrier CCNA_03820 at +2.89 logFC, Pal-like CCNA_00784 at +2.08 logFC, a *zot*-like membrane perturber, *osrP*, and multiple TBDTs. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] TBDTs, or TonB-dependent transporters, are therefore prominent among the later transport-associated consequences reported in this response. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] This **supports** connecting regulatory timing to lipid transport and envelope organization, but the late cohort's envelope, transport, and regulator enrichment was 24.5%, below the relaxed 50% criterion, and the late-versus-early Fisher test gave p = 0.243. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] Thus, separable early, shared, and late cohorts are supported, whereas the stronger claim that the late cohort is statistically more enriched for envelope functions than the early cohort is not established. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The pattern is consistent with an early ChvI-associated response followed by a later program involving transport, outer-envelope organization, and regulatory consequences. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] Because the late-cohort enrichment and late-versus-early comparison did not meet their relaxed criteria, this interpretation is a supported phase model rather than proof of a fully resolved causal sequence. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] Caulobacter SigU, CCNA_02977, is uncharacterized in the published literature, and a PaperBLAST scout returned zero substantive snippets. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] SigU may contribute to the late program, but its role as the driver of that cohort remains only partially supported. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

## Component-specific transcript–protein responses

Transcript–protein discordance—differences in the direction or magnitude of RNA and protein changes—indicates regulation between transcription and protein accumulation rather than a simple transcript-level response. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] In this rescue context, the strongest evidence is concentrated in lipid-transport and outer-membrane components, although the single-replicate proteome limits mechanistic inference. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] The measurements are summarized in [[summaries/caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss__REPORT]] and connect to [[concepts/multi-omics-integration]]. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

At the transcript level, no canonical Lpt component was DOWN; the MsbA-like CCNA_00307 was UP by +0.89 with FDR 0.01, and LptC-related CCNA_03716 was UP by +0.56 with FDR 0.005. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] The canonical LPS-transport apparatus heatmap shows MsbA-like CCNA_00307 visibly up in strain 4599 relative to the other strains. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] At the protein level, detected canonical components moved oppositely: LptD changed by log2(4672/4659) = -0.47 and by log2(4672/4580) = -0.62, while LptE changed by -0.78 and -0.68, respectively. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] CCNA_00307 was detected only in the rescued strain, at abundance 300 in 4672 and NaN in the other strains, so its direction relative to the wild-type baseline was uncomputable. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] This **contradicts** a model in which transcript increases uniformly propagate into increased Lpt protein abundance and instead supports regulation through translation, protein stability, complex assembly, or turnover; the responsible process remains unidentified. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] An alternative the project explicitly leaves open is that the decline reflects reduced demand for LPS-specific transport, the canonical Lpt apparatus being substrate-limited in the absence of LPS, rather than contradicting Uchendu 2026's shared-component model; which of the two holds cannot be resolved by single-replicate proteomics. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] The outer-membrane proteome here is single-replicate and cannot resolve the discordance; replicated proteomics are required to determine whether the canonical Lpt apparatus is functionally maintained, replaced, or downregulated at the protein level in the Δ*lpxc* state. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The sphingolipid transporter CCNA_01226, *lptC2*, showed transcript -0.60 with FDR 0.034 but protein log2(4672/4659) = +1.08. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] Because its protein level was already DOWN by -0.42 log2 in 4659/4580, its net protein change versus wild-type baseline was +0.66 log2, reported as approximately 1.58×. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] CCNA_01217 protein changed by +0.77 in 4672 versus 4659, or +0.74 versus wild type. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] Both are single-replicate observations, **consistent with** post-transcriptional stabilization or retention of sphingolipid-transport machinery but not statistically establishing it; the report classes them as a suggestive pilot observation requiring replicated proteomics. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] The report states specifically that the *lptC2* protein result is single-replicate and requires the proteome replication scheduled for summer 2026. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] The transporter-level protein increases **refine** the pathway result from biosynthetic induction to possible maintenance or stabilization of transport capacity during lipid A loss. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

One pre-registered component claim failed outright: CtpA/CCNA_03113 induction was rejected at the project's own bar, with transcript logFC +0.58, pvalue = 0.048 and FDR = 0.109 in the 4599-vs-4584 contrast and no detection in the outer-membrane proteome. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] The fallback borderline category was also not met, because it required both 0.05 < pvalue < 0.15 and protein detection, whereas the observed pvalue = 0.048 falls below that lower bound and the protein was absent from the proteome. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] The cumulative 4599-vs-4580 contrast was significant (logFC +0.76, FDR 0.035) but conflates Δ*fur* + Δ*sspB* and Δ*lpxc* effects, so it stands only as a supplemental observation consistent with CtpA being Fur-regulated and is not evidence for lipid A-loss-driven CtpA induction. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] A per-strain expression figure shows a monotonic rise of 37→42→63 CPM (counts per million) despite the borderline single-contrast FDR, which is suggestive but does not reinstate the rejected claim. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] The claim at stake was CtpA's specific role as the LpxF-equivalent processing step predicted by Zik 2022; because the pre-registered 4599-vs-4584 contrast was the one designed to isolate the Δ*lpxc*-specific response, that LpxF-substitute hypothesis remains untested by this work, and earlier drafts that mis-labeled CtpA as BORDERLINE were corrected. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

## Tol-Pal, peptidoglycan, and envelope remodeling

Pal/CCNA_00784 was induced at both transcript and protein levels, with +2.08 transcript and +2.84 protein, and it was among the late-cohort ChvI genes. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] The report interprets [[entities/tol-pal-system]] upregulation primarily through retrograde phospholipid transport and outer-membrane lipid homeostasis; direct Pal–peptidoglycan contacts and a Caulobacter division-associated role remain context rather than direct measurements in this project. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] An earlier explanation within the same project—that Tol-Pal normally depends on LPS-mediated Mg²⁺-bridged outer-membrane–LPS–Pal stacking for outer-/inner-membrane cohesion, so that with no LPS the cell upregulates Pal to compensate by direct protein anchoring—was subsequently flagged as uncited and replaced by the tentative retrograde-phospholipid-transport reading; Pal induction itself is measured, but neither compensatory mechanism is demonstrated here. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] The retrograde phospholipid-transport reading depends on Tan & Chng 2025 rather than on this project's data alone, and the earlier LPS-Mg²⁺-bridged stacking explanation was withdrawn as uncited and inconsistent with current Tol-Pal biology. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] Pal-Tol induction is therefore **consistent with** increased lipid-remodeling demand and with the late regulatory program, while its causal role in rescue remains unresolved. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The peptidoglycan-remodeling gene set was pre-registered—53 loci locked before differential-expression analysis—and 28 genes met the H4 threshold of at least 3 loci: 25 were transcript-significant in 4599-vs-4584 at FDR<0.05 and 6 had outer-membrane-proteome |log2|>1, deduplicated to 28 unique loci. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] The response was predominantly downregulatory, with 20 DOWN loci and specific inductions comprising 5 UP transcript hits plus 3 UP protein hits. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] Induced activities included the soluble lytic murein transglycosylase SdpA/CCNA_01252 at +4.8 log2 protein, the peptidoglycan-associated outer-membrane lipoprotein Pal/CCNA_00784 at +2.08 transcript and +2.84 protein—also a top late-cohort ChvI gene—plus PleA, PbpX, and CCNA_01754. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] Basal division and elongation machinery was broadly reduced, including FtsI, PbpZ at -1.08 transcript, PbpC at -1.15 protein, a D,D-transpeptidase at -1.27 protein, *murD*, *mviN/murJ*, multiple LdpD/E/F M23-family endopeptidases, *amiC*, *ripA*, and membrane-bound transglycosylase A at -2.47 log2 protein. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

Two members of the pre-registered peptidoglycan set are likely regex false positives—CCNA_00565 γ-glutamyltranspeptidase, a glutathione enzyme matched on "transpeptidase", and CCNA_01833 glucosylceramidase, a ceramide enzyme matched because "ceramidase" contains "amidase"—and their inclusion does not change the H4 verdict, which sits well above the threshold of at least 3 loci; downstream interpretation should not rest on them. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] The heatmap of peptidoglycan-remodeling enzymes meeting the H4 threshold shows a coordinated pattern of increases and decreases across the strain series. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

Pal shows concordant RNA and protein induction, whereas LptD and LptE decline despite transcript-level increases elsewhere in the apparatus. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] This **supports** a component-specific rather than globally uniform post-transcriptional response. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] Together, the Pal-Tol and peptidoglycan findings **support** coordinated outer-membrane lipid transport and envelope-structure remodeling, and **refine** the phased model by linking late-cohort genes to measured remodeling responses, but they do not establish causal order. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

## Fur, ChvI, and fitness context

The phased ChvI response occurs within a broader Δ*fur* derepression signal: the 4584-vs-4580 contrast correlated with Leaden 2018's Δ*fur* signal across 93 differentially expressed genes, with Spearman ρ = 0.315, p = 2.08e-03, and 71% sign concordance. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] Of those 93 DEGs, 53 were buffered in the present data, with logFC approximately 0 despite Leaden values of -5 to -9; the buffered set was dominated by the cbb3 / cyd / *fix*-NOPQ micro-aerobic respiratory operon, including CCNA_01466-01476, *ccoNOPQ*, *cydCDA*, and *fixG/H/I*. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] Fur derepression is statistically supported, but the mechanistic importance of the Δ*sspB*-buffered respiratory program for lipid A-loss rescue remains a hypothesis: the transcript-level buffering of 53 of 93 Leaden Δ*fur* DEGs, dominated by the cbb3/*fix* operon, is real, while the criticality of those genes for rescue is not. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] The scatter of Leaden Δ*fur* logFC against the 4584-vs-4580 logFC, marking concordant-strong, buffered, and discordant genes, shows a pronounced asymmetry: the amplified Fur-derepression cohort exceeds Leaden's logFC values while the buffered cohort clusters near zero across Leaden's full -9 to -5 range, with the iron-derepressed TBDT HutA the extreme case at +10.5 here versus +2.2 in Leaden. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] A companion panel reproducing Leaden's internal comparison of the Δ*fur* response against the iron-limitation response shows that the Fur regulon and the iron-limitation response are correlated but not identical, which the report treats as Leaden's own validation of the signal. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The ChvI-induced regulons contained 488 genes in the tested universe and partitioned into 20 unique-to-early genes, 10 genes induced in both phases, and 49 late-consequence genes, for a disjoint total of 79 ChvI-induced genes. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] The late cohort included LolA-family CCNA_03820 at +2.89, Pal-like CCNA_00784 at +2.08, a *zot*-like membrane perturber, *osrP*, and multiple TBDTs. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] The phase structure is supported, but envelope/transport/regulator enrichment was 24.5%, below the relaxed 50% criterion, and the late-versus-early Fisher test was p = 0.243. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] Thus, ChvI-associated envelope remodeling is plausible, whereas SigU as the driver of the late cohort remains only partially supported. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The Caulobacter RB-TnSeq compendium covered 198 experiments, including 22 envelope-stress experiments but zero iron-limitation experiments. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] The genome-wide envelope-stress background contained 1311 phenotype-bearing genes among 3943 genes, or 33.25%. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] The 26-gene Δ*sspB*-buffered set contained 9 phenotype-bearing genes, or 34.6%, corresponding to 1.04× enrichment with p = 0.515, and was indistinguishable from background. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] This **weakens** the interpretation that the buffered respiratory program is required for envelope remodeling, while the 32-gene concordant-strong Fur signature showed 17 phenotype-bearing genes, or 53.1% against the 33.25% genome background, corresponding to 1.60× enrichment with hypergeometric p = 0.016, an enrichment the report calls marginal. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] Accordingly, the fitness data do not selectively support the cbb3/*fix* genes as more critical than randomly drawn Caulobacter genes under envelope stress, and "respiratory ATP required to perform envelope-remodeling work" remains a mechanistically plausible model that these data do not establish. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] The pre-registered threshold of at least 10% phenotype-bearing genes itself sat below the 33.25% background rate—a methodological miscalibration surfaced by the adversarial review and recorded as a learned pattern—so the SspB-buffered Path B phenotype-bearing rate is indistinguishable from genome background and the respiratory-ATP arm of the dual-release switch model is a working hypothesis, not an established finding. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

ChvT, CCNA_03108, had envelope-stress |*t*| = 43.7, while other Fur-derepressed TBDTs included CCNA_02910, CCNA_00210, CCNA_02048, and CCNA_00028 with |*t*| values of 9-28. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] ChvT, HutA, and the FrpB cluster encode outer-membrane transport machinery whose normal job is iron acquisition; their suggested availability for sphingolipid (CPG) trafficking through the shared Lpt apparatus is a proposal, not something demonstrated here. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] Because that pre-registered phenotype-bearing threshold was not calibrated against the genome-wide background rate, it was not informative. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] These results support using background-calibrated fitness tests to distinguish transcriptional participation from condition-specific necessity, consistent with [[concepts/gene-essentiality]]. ## Outer-membrane maintenance under other stresses

Outside the lipid A-loss setting, a separate project in this corpus reads the Mla system's pleiotropic fitness defects as consistent with its established function in maintaining outer membrane integrity under diverse stresses. [src: metal_specificity] That independent observation **supports** the broader framing used here—that outer-membrane lipid-maintenance machinery is engaged across unrelated stress conditions, not only when lipid A is absent—while the Caulobacter evidence addresses the narrower question of which components are engaged once lipid A itself is gone, and relates to [[concepts/shared-stress-versus-stressor-specific-fitness]]. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss, metal_specificity] Neither line of evidence tests the other, so the parallel is cross-project context rather than a demonstrated shared mechanism. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss, metal_specificity]

## Tensions

The central tension is between transcript-level activation of selected Lpt-related genes and protein-level decline of LptD and LptE. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] The data support an RNA-level regulatory response but do not establish increased abundance or function of the canonical Lpt apparatus. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] A related tension is that *lptC2* and CCNA_01217 protein increases, and Pal induction, are compatible with stabilization or increased remodeling capacity, whereas LptD and LptE decline; the single-replicate design cannot distinguish translation, stability, assembly, turnover, or downstream stress effects. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] Pal induction is likewise compatible with increased remodeling demand, but cannot be shown causal for rescue. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]

The phase model is supported by cohort structure, but the proposed functional distinction between early and late responses is weakened by the late-cohort enrichment of 24.5%, the late-versus-early Fisher test p = 0.243, and the unresolved role of SigU. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] The transcript-level respiratory signature is clear, but its fitness enrichment is only 1.04× with p = 0.515, so transcriptional prominence should not be treated as evidence of causal necessity. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss]
