# Taxonomic nomenclature and identifier reconciliation across databases

Taxonomic nomenclature and identifier reconciliation is the process of aligning names, accessions, taxonomy versions, and database-specific identifier scopes before comparing or joining microbial records. [src: pitfalls] The central risk is that a syntactically successful join can connect biologically different organisms or silently discard valid matches when databases use different naming conventions, taxonomic ranks, or identifier namespaces. [src: pitfalls] This concept extends [[concepts/provenance-aware-resource-discovery]] and [[concepts/taxonomic-resolution-dependent-functional-inference]] by treating identifier provenance and taxonomic scope as analytical evidence rather than mere preprocessing details. [src: pitfalls]

## Why reconciliation is necessary

Short strain names are not globally unique across resources. [src: pitfalls] In one documented example, ENIGMA MT20 referred to *Rhodanobacter glycinis*, whereas GTDB MT20 referred to *Streptococcus pneumoniae*. [src: pitfalls] The resulting erroneous match involved 8,434 genomes, including 1,751 clinical genomes. [src: pitfalls] Among 32 pangenome linkages made through `ncbi_strain_identifiers`, 12 were incorrect genus matches. [src: pitfalls] These results directly support using assembly accessions such as `GCF_*` instead of short strain names whenever possible. [src: pitfalls]

Species names also diverge through taxonomic reclassification and database-specific lineage formats. [src: pitfalls] MetaPhlAn3 cross-cohort analyses encountered short names, full lineage strings, GTDB-renamed *Bacteroides vulgatus* and *Phocaeicola vulgatus*, *Eubacterium rectale* and *Agathobacter rectalis*, *Ruminococcus gnavus* and *Mediterraneibacter gnavus*, plus multiple *Clostridium*, *Lactobacillus*, and related reclassifications. [src: pitfalls] These examples show why simple string normalization is insufficient for cross-cohort taxonomic integration. [src: pitfalls]

## Evidence for analytical consequences

In the CrohnsPhage mart, failure to reconcile taxonomic names produced a log₂FC approximately 28, equivalent to approximately 2.68 × 10⁸ fold, because one cohort’s abundance collapsed to a pseudocount. [src: pitfalls] This **supports** the interpretation that apparent biological extremes can be generated by unmatched taxon names rather than by measured abundance differences. [src: pitfalls] A targeted battery used a hand-curated 23-entry synonym map, but full-taxonomy aggregation requires an NCBI-taxid-backed, GTDB-version-aware reconciliation layer. [src: pitfalls]

Taxonomic rank and identifier scope must also be checked inside a single database family. [src: pitfalls] Pangenome taxonomy tables must be joined through `genome_id`, not `gtdb_taxonomy_id`, because the genome table stores a genus-level taxonomy string while `gtdb_taxonomy_r214v1` stores a species-level string. [src: pitfalls] `gapmind_pathways.clade_name` uses the full `gtdb_species_clade_id`, including the representative accession, rather than the shorter `GTDB_species` value. [src: pitfalls] These constraints **refine** [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] by making the join key and taxonomic level explicit. [src: pitfalls]

Pangenome gene-cluster identifiers are species-specific and cannot be compared directly across species. [src: pitfalls] Cross-species comparisons should instead use shared representations such as COG categories, KEGG orthologs, or Pfam domains. [src: pitfalls] `eggnog_mapper_annotations.query_name` joins to `gene_cluster.gene_cluster_id`, not `gene.gene_id`, and Bakta and InterProScan annotations likewise join at the gene-cluster level. [src: pitfalls] This **supports** [[concepts/complementary-annotation-pipelines]] and [[concepts/evidence-triangulation-for-functional-annotation]] by showing that both the biological representation and its join level constrain valid comparisons. [src: pitfalls]

## Reconciliation workflow

1. Discover the live schema, catalog address, taxonomy version, and identifier columns before reusing historical queries. [src: pitfalls]
2. Prefer stable accessions such as `GCF_*` over short strain labels, and retain the original identifier, source database, taxonomy version, and reconciliation rule for every match. [src: pitfalls]
3. Build a synonymy layer that records renamed taxa and lineage aliases rather than applying unconstrained string normalization. [src: pitfalls]
4. Validate candidate matches with genus consistency and, where available, NCBI taxids, assembly accessions, or other stable identifiers. [src: pitfalls]
5. Confirm the taxonomic rank represented by each column and use the database-specified join key, such as `genome_id` for the cited pangenome taxonomy tables. [src: pitfalls]
6. Keep species-specific gene-cluster identifiers within species and translate to shared orthology or domain representations before cross-species analysis. [src: pitfalls]
7. Audit unmatched, multiply matched, and biologically implausible records separately instead of treating them as biological absence. [src: pitfalls]

## Tensions

A short-name workflow is convenient for exploratory analysis, but the documented MT20 collision and the 12 incorrect genus matches among 32 pangenome linkages show that convenience can contradict identifier validity. [src: pitfalls] A hand-curated 23-entry synonym map can resolve a targeted battery, whereas full-taxonomy aggregation requires a broader NCBI-taxid-backed, GTDB-version-aware layer; the appropriate reconciliation scope therefore depends on the analysis scope. [src: pitfalls]

Database names and schemas can change over time, so archived reports and notebooks should not be treated as automatically valid executable instructions without live inspection. [src: pitfalls] This **supports** [[concepts/provenance-aware-resource-discovery]] and makes taxonomy-version recording part of reproducibility rather than an optional annotation. [src: pitfalls]

## Open Directions

- Combine `ncbi_strain_identifiers`, assembly accessions, NCBI taxids, and GTDB taxonomy versions, then measure how many historical pangenome links change genus or species assignment under a validated reconciliation graph. [src: pitfalls]
- Apply the synonym layer to all MetaPhlAn3 cohort taxonomies and compare abundance, prevalence, and differential-effect estimates before and after reconciliation to determine which findings depend on naming mismatches. [src: pitfalls]
- Test `genome_id`, `gtdb_taxonomy_id`, and accession-based joins against manually reviewed genus-consistent matches, using rank-specific precision and recall to identify safe join keys for each pangenome table. [src: pitfalls]
- Quantify cross-species agreement when gene-cluster identifiers are replaced by COG categories, KEGG orthologs, or Pfam domains, and determine which representation best preserves the biological question without species-specific identifier leakage. [src: pitfalls]
