# Metabolic and Sampling Determinants of Pangenome Openness

Pangenome openness describes the propensity of a species' gene repertoire to remain expandable as additional genomes are sampled. In the BERDL analyses, its apparent determinants differed depending on whether openness was tested against environmental or phylogenetic effects, variable metabolic pathways, or ecological sampling breadth. [src: discoveries]

## Core Evidence

One analysis found no significant relationship between pangenome openness and environment or phylogenetic effects: environmental association had rho=-0.05 and p=0.54, while the phylogenetic association had rho=0.03 and p=0.73. [src: discoveries] The [[summaries/pangenome_openness__REPORT]] evidence **supports** this null result, reporting the same correlations and interpreting them as evidence that open versus closed pangenome status did not predict whether environment or phylogeny dominated gene-content variation. [src: pangenome_openness] This result indicates that broad environmental labels and phylogenetic effects were not sufficient predictors of openness in that analysis. [src: discoveries] The report further suggests, as hypotheses rather than direct demonstrations, that pangenome structure may be independent of eco-phylogenetic dynamics, that horizontal gene transfer (HGT) may be opportunistic rather than tied to environmental similarity, and that core/accessory classification may miss genes relevant to functional adaptation. [src: pangenome_openness]

A separate analysis found that variable metabolic pathways strongly predicted openness, with rho=0.327 and p=7.2e-71. [src: discoveries] The association strengthened after controlling for genome count, reaching partial rho=0.530 and p=2.83e-203. [src: discoveries] This **supports** a metabolic-determinant model in which variation in pathway repertoires captures genome-content dynamics that are not explained by environment or phylogeny alone. [src: discoveries] The [[summaries/pathway_capability_dependency__REPORT]] evidence **supports** this association: across 2,810 GTDB species with at least 10 genomes, the analysis used 80 GapMind pathways and found the same raw and genome-count-adjusted correlations. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] The pathway-openness association was positive in 13 of 18 genera and significant in 5, with the strongest within-genus correlations being rho=0.534 for Clostridium and rho=0.506 for Eubacterium. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] This **refines** the metabolic-determinant model by showing that the signal is not confined to a single pooled species comparison, while the absence of full phylogenetic independent contrasts leaves residual phylogenetic confounding possible. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

The new [[summaries/metabolic_capability_dependency__REPORT]] evidence **supports and extends** this model from pathway variability to measured pathway realization: among 22 species clades, latent capability rate—the fraction of complete pathways with low observed fitness dependence—correlated positively with pangenome openness (Spearman rho=0.69, p=0.0004, n=22 clades). [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] However, pathway-level conservation did not distinguish latent capabilities from active dependencies, so the result **refines** rather than replaces the openness model: the signal is more consistent with clade-level genome dynamics and community context than with a simple conservation difference for individual pathways. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] The comparison is also based on 48 fitness-tested organisms and should not be treated as equivalent to the broader 2,810-species analysis. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency; pathway_capability_dependency]

The [[summaries/fitness_effects_conservation__REPORT]] evidence **refines** this model by showing that conservation is biologically informative but only weakly predictive of measured fitness: across approximately 194,000 genes from 43 bacteria, essential genes were 82% core whereas always-neutral genes were 66% core, a 16-percentage-point gradient. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] This supports a connection between gene-content status and functional activity, but does not establish conservation or fitness importance as a determinant of openness. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] It also **refines** the hypothesis that core/accessory classification can miss adaptation: condition-specific genes were 77.3% core versus 70.3% for genes without specific phenotypes, and 4,450 genes (2.7%) were neutral overall but critical in one condition; these ephemeral-niche genes were more common among core genes (3.0%) than auxiliary genes (1.7%) or singleton genes (1.6%). [src: fitness_effects_conservation] The fitness evidence is nevertheless limited by rich-media and standard-stress coverage, single-gene perturbations, primarily Proteobacteria representation, and possible poor transposon coverage for singleton genes. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

The same analysis further found that, among 225 species with at least 50 genomes and at least 3 variable pathways, binary pathway-completeness profiles formed a median of 4 metabolic ecotypes per species, with a maximum of 8. Ecotype count correlated with openness at raw Spearman rho=0.262, p=6.8e-05, and partial rho=0.322, p=8.0e-07 after controlling for genome count. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] This **supports** the interpretation that within-species metabolic diversity is associated with genome fluidity, while the fixed 50% clustering cut makes the ecotype count threshold-dependent. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] The new analysis **also supports** this interpretation at organism level: all 10 target species formed metabolic clusters, although the reported environment-cluster associations were significant only for *Salmonella enterica* and *Phenylobacterium* sp.; the observational design and lack of explicit phylogenetic correction limit causal interpretation. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]

Ecological breadth was also associated with pathway completeness across 1,872 species with sufficient AlphaEarth coverage. [src: discoveries] Niche breadth predicted pathway completeness at r=0.392 and p=7.1e-70, embedding variance predicted pathway completeness at r=0.412 and p=1.8e-77, and geographic range predicted pathway completeness at r=0.360 and p=1.8e-58. [src: discoveries] These results **support** a connection between ecological breadth, metabolic repertoire completeness, and pangenome structure, but they do not by themselves establish that ecological breadth causes openness. [src: discoveries] The new analysis **refines** this interpretation by showing that metabolic ecotype diversity itself tracks openness after genome-count adjustment, without demonstrating that environmental niche breadth is the mechanism. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

## Sampling and Representation Limits

Only 6.8% of species, or 1,872 of 27,690, had sufficient AlphaEarth coverage for the ecological-breadth analysis. [src: discoveries] Consequently, the observed relationships may describe the subset of species with adequate environmental representation rather than the full catalog of 27,690 species. [src: discoveries]

The AlphaEarth dataset also contained 3,838 of 83,287 genomes with NaN dimensions, and 36.6% of genomes were clustered at coordinates containing more than 50 genomes of more than 10 species. [src: discoveries] These coverage and coordinate-crowding patterns can make apparent environmental breadth partly reflect uneven sampling or representation in embedding space. [src: discoveries]

The contrast between the null environment and phylogeny result and the strong metabolic-pathway result shows why openness analyses require explicit separation of biological predictors from sampling proxies. [src: discoveries] Genome count was an important adjustment variable because controlling for it changed the pathway association from rho=0.327 to partial rho=0.530. [src: discoveries] The new analysis **reinforces** this caution by noting that its species sample was restricted to taxa with both pangenome statistics and ecotype-analysis results, that openness is only one summary of pangenome structure, and that partial correlations may not fully disentangle confounded variables; limited genome counts may also reduce power for some species. [src: pangenome_openness] Its broader analysis required at least 10 genomes per GTDB species, while the ecotype analysis required at least 50 genomes and at least 3 variable pathways, making the latter especially vulnerable to sampling-selection effects. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] The fitness-linked analysis is additionally limited to 48 organisms among 293,000 genomes with pathway predictions, so its clade-level association may not generalize across taxa. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] The fitness-conservation result adds a related representation concern: singleton and novel genes may appear neutral because of poor transposon coverage rather than true absence of effects. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

## Tensions

The two openness analyses provide conflicting evidence about environmental determinants: one reported no significant environment or phylogenetic relationship, whereas another found strong associations between variable metabolic pathways and openness and between ecological breadth and pathway completeness. [src: discoveries] This **tension** may reflect differences in predictor definition, genome-count control, species inclusion, or environmental coverage rather than a simple contradiction about whether environment matters. [src: discoveries] The new analysis **supports** the null side of this tension for its harmonized openness-versus-effect test, but does not test the pathway or AlphaEarth associations directly. [src: pangenome_openness]

The strongest current interpretation is therefore that metabolic pathway variability is a reproducible candidate determinant or proxy of openness, while direct environmental and phylogenetic effects remain specification-dependent. [src: discoveries] The AlphaEarth associations should be treated as coverage-limited and not as population-wide causal estimates because only 6.8% of species had sufficient coverage. [src: discoveries] The HGT and core/accessory explanations remain hypotheses requiring functional and transfer-specific tests, not established consequences of the null correlations. [src: pangenome_openness] The ecotype-openness association **supports** metabolic diversification as an additional correlate, but its dependence on clustering thresholds and genome-count eligibility prevents it from establishing causality. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] The positive latent-capability association **further supports** this correlate while leaving unresolved whether openness promotes fitness-neutral capabilities, whether shared ecological context produces both, or whether both are affected by sampling and community interactions. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] The fitness study **supports** a functional distinction between core and accessory genes but **refines** any interpretation that conservation alone explains pangenome openness: its conservation gradient was statistically robust yet weak, and core genes also showed condition-specific and opposing fitness effects. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

## Relation to Pangenome Integration

These findings **refine** [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] by showing that pangenome synthesis should connect gene-content openness to variable metabolic pathways, pathway completeness, ecological breadth, and geographic representation rather than treating openness as an environment-independent species trait. [src: discoveries] They also **connect** to [[concepts/environment-embedding-geography]], because environmental embeddings and geographic range were used as predictors of pathway completeness, and to [[concepts/pangenome-openness-determinants]] only as the present concept's scope rather than as a separate target. [src: discoveries] The fitness comparison **further supports** integrating conservation and perturbation-derived functional evidence, while keeping the weak conservation-fitness relationship distinct from direct tests of openness. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

## Open Directions

- Recalculate openness, variable-pathway content, and pathway completeness across the full 27,690-species catalog using coverage-aware regression and explicit genome-count adjustment; test whether the metabolic association remains when the 1,872-species AlphaEarth subset is expanded or weighted for sampling coverage. [src: discoveries]
- Repeat the environment-versus-phylogeny comparison with the same species set, predictor definitions, and covariate structure used in the variable-pathway analysis; test whether the null correlations rho=-0.05 and rho=0.03 persist under harmonized modeling. [src: discoveries]
- Model AlphaEarth missingness and coordinate crowding explicitly, including the 3,838 genomes with NaN dimensions and the 36.6% of genomes in densely shared coordinates; test whether pathway-completeness associations survive inverse-coverage weighting or leave-one-coordinate-out validation. [src: discoveries]
- Partition variable metabolic pathways into core metabolic, secondary metabolic, and transport categories using pathway-level annotations; test which categories account for the partial rho=0.530 association with openness. [src: discoveries]
- Compare species-level openness with independent sampling-depth measures and geographic-range estimates; test whether the correlations for niche breadth, embedding variance, and geographic range reflect biological ecological breadth or uneven genome and environment sampling. [src: discoveries]
- Recompute metabolic ecotypes across alternative clustering distances and genome-count cutoffs, then test whether the partial rho=0.322 association survives phylogenetically independent or coverage-weighted models. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]
- Test whether the latent-capability rate correlation rho=0.69 persists after direct GapMind-to-fitness gene mapping, expanded fitness-condition coverage, and phylogenetically independent models; distinguish genome-dynamic effects from pathway-specific conservation. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]
- Stratify openness by functional categories, including mobile and defense-associated genes, and compare auxiliary fraction, Heap's law alpha, and pangenome fluidity across lifestyle groups; test whether the null broad environment result masks function-specific or lifestyle-dependent associations. [src: pangenome_openness]
- Link openness and functional categories to fitness breadth and condition-specific annotations across the 43-bacterium dataset; test whether the weak conservation-fitness gradient and core enrichment of ephemeral-niche genes persist after controlling for genome count, transposon callability, and phylogeny. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
