# Metabolic and Sampling Determinants of Pangenome Openness

Pangenome openness describes the propensity of a species' gene repertoire to remain expandable as additional genomes are sampled. In the KBase Data Lakehouse analyses, its apparent determinants differed depending on whether openness was tested against environmental or phylogenetic effects, variable metabolic pathways, or ecological sampling breadth. [src: discoveries]

## Core Evidence

One analysis found no significant relationship between pangenome openness and environment or phylogenetic effects: environmental association had rho=-0.05 and p=0.54, while the phylogenetic association had rho=0.03 and p=0.73. [src: discoveries] The [[summaries/pangenome_openness__REPORT]] evidence **supports** this null result, reporting the same correlations and interpreting them as evidence that open versus closed pangenome status did not predict whether environment or phylogeny dominated gene-content variation. [src: pangenome_openness] This result indicates that broad environmental labels and phylogenetic effects were not sufficient predictors of openness in that analysis. [src: discoveries] The report further suggests, as hypotheses rather than direct demonstrations, that pangenome structure may be independent of eco-phylogenetic dynamics, that horizontal gene transfer (HGT) may be opportunistic rather than tied to environmental similarity, and that core/accessory classification may miss genes relevant to functional adaptation, so that the genes that vary may not be the ones responding to environment. [src: pangenome_openness] The report also states that an open pangenome need not imply environment-driven adaptation, and that the uncorrelated openness-environment result is consistent with HGT being opportunistic rather than ecologically directed. This is consistency with that mechanism, not evidence that establishes it. [src: pangenome_openness]

A separate analysis found that variable metabolic pathways strongly predicted openness, with rho=0.327 and p=7.2e-71. [src: discoveries] The association strengthened after controlling for genome count, reaching partial rho=0.530 and p=2.83e-203. [src: discoveries] This **supports** a metabolic-determinant model in which variation in pathway repertoires captures genome-content dynamics that are not explained by environment or phylogeny alone. [src: discoveries] The [[summaries/pathway_capability_dependency__REPORT]] evidence **supports** this association: across 2,810 GTDB species with at least 10 genomes, the analysis used 80 GapMind pathways and found the same raw and genome-count-adjusted correlations. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] The pathway-openness association was positive in 13 of 18 genera and significant in 5, with the strongest within-genus correlations being rho=0.534 for Clostridium and rho=0.506 for Eubacterium. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] This **refines** the metabolic-determinant model by showing that the signal is not confined to a single pooled species comparison, while the absence of full phylogenetic independent contrasts leaves residual phylogenetic confounding possible. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] The report defines variable pathways as those present in 10-90% of genomes and openness as the fraction of accessory gene clusters. It treats genome count as a critical confounder, because species with more sequenced genomes may appear to have more variable pathways simply from sampling depth. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] Five genera were significant at the p<0.05 level: Clostridium, Eubacterium, Mesorhizobium, Pseudomonas and Streptomyces. Smaller per-genus sample sizes limited statistical power for many groups. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] Within-genus validation was restricted to genera with ≥20 species per genus. The report's reading, that the relationship reflects a general evolutionary dynamic rather than a clade-specific artifact, is a suggestion and not an established result. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] The report also infers from the partial rho=0.530 correlation that metabolic pathway gain and loss is a major driver of pangenome dynamics. That causal role remains a hypothesis rather than a directly tested finding. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] The report presents this analysis in a pathway-conservation-versus-openness figure and a pathway-conservation-by-genus figure. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] For the same 2,810-species analysis, the central digest reports that the signal holds across 18/21 phylogenetic groups tested. It also reports that amino acid biosynthesis pathways (leu, val, arg, lys, thr) show the largest accessory dependence gap (~0.14). [src: discoveries] This 18/21 tally does not match the report's genus-level result of positive in 13 of 18 genera and significant in 5. Neither source explains the difference, so the two tallies are kept as an unresolved discrepancy. [src: discoveries, pathway_capability_dependency]

The new [[summaries/metabolic_capability_dependency__REPORT]] evidence **supports and extends** this model from pathway variability to measured pathway realization: after aggregating 41 organisms to 22 unique species clades, so that Fitness Browser strains sharing one clade pangenome contributed a single openness value, latent capability rate—the fraction of complete pathways with low observed fitness dependence—correlated positively with pangenome openness (Spearman rho=0.69, p=0.0004, n=22 clades), and the report's openness-versus-latent-capability scatter figure shows the same association. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] The report reads this as consistent with the Black Queen framework at the species level; that reading is an interpretation of a 22-clade correlation, not a direct test. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] However, pathway-level conservation did not distinguish latent capabilities from active dependencies, so the result **refines** rather than replaces the openness model: the signal is more consistent with clade-level genome dynamics and community context than with a simple conservation difference for individual pathways. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] The comparison is also based on 48 fitness-tested organisms and should not be treated as equivalent to the broader 2,810-species analysis. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency; pathway_capability_dependency] Pangenome openness for that analysis was queried for 7,334 species; among well-sampled pathogens with >2,500 genomes, [[entities/klebsiella-pneumoniae]] was labeled most open (99.05% open; 0.95% core genes) and [[entities/mycobacterium-tuberculosis]] most closed (97.4% open; 2.6% core genes), so even the closed extreme of this metric retains a high open fraction. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]

The [[summaries/fitness_effects_conservation__REPORT]] evidence **refines** this model by showing that conservation is biologically informative but only weakly predictive of measured fitness: across approximately 194,000 genes from 43 bacteria, essential genes were 82% core whereas always-neutral genes were 66% core, a 16-percentage-point gradient. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] This supports a connection between gene-content status and functional activity, but does not establish conservation or fitness importance as a determinant of openness. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] It also **refines** the hypothesis that core/accessory classification can miss adaptation: condition-specific genes were 77.3% core versus 70.3% for genes without specific phenotypes, and 4,450 genes (2.7%) were neutral overall but critical in one condition; these ephemeral-niche genes were more common among core genes (3.0%) than auxiliary genes (1.7%) or singleton genes (1.6%). [src: fitness_effects_conservation] The fitness evidence is nevertheless limited by rich-media and standard-stress coverage, single-gene perturbations, primarily Proteobacteria representation, and possible poor transposon coverage for singleton genes. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

The same analysis further found that, among 225 species with at least 50 genomes and at least 3 variable pathways, binary pathway-completeness profiles formed a median of 4 metabolic ecotypes per species, with a maximum of 8. Ecotype count correlated with openness at raw Spearman rho=0.262, p=6.8e-05, and partial rho=0.322, p=8.0e-07 after controlling for genome count. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] This **supports** the interpretation that within-species metabolic diversity is associated with genome fluidity, while the fixed 50% clustering cut makes the ecotype count threshold-dependent. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] The report's executive summary describes within-species pathway variation as defining 2-8 metabolic ecotypes per species, and its ecotype-count-versus-openness figures show the association. The genome-count adjustment is meant to account for the sampling bias whereby more-sequenced species may yield more clusters. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] The central digest reports the same hierarchical-clustering result for 225 species (≥50 genomes, ≥3 variable pathways), with a median of 4 metabolic ecotypes, a maximum of 8, and partial rho=0.322, p=8.0e-07 with openness, and names [[entities/alistipes-onderdonkii]] (8) and [[entities/barnesiella-intestinihominis]] (8) as the top-ecotyped species. [src: discoveries] The new analysis **also supports** this interpretation at organism level: all 10 target species formed metabolic clusters, although the reported environment-cluster associations were significant only for *Salmonella enterica* and *Phenylobacterium* sp.; the observational design and lack of explicit phylogenetic correction limit causal interpretation. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]

Ecological breadth was also associated with pathway completeness across 1,872 bacterial species with complete pangenome, [[entities/gapmind]] pathway, and [[entities/alph-aearth]] embedding data, the subset with sufficient AlphaEarth coverage. [src: discoveries] Niche breadth measured as AlphaEarth embedding diversity predicted pathway completeness at r=0.392 and p=7.1e-70, which the digest labels the strongest signal; embedding variance predicted pathway completeness more strongly at r=0.412 and p=1.8e-77, and geographic distance alone gave r=0.360 and p=1.8e-58, weaker than the embedding-based metrics. [src: discoveries] These results **support** a connection between ecological breadth, metabolic repertoire completeness, and pangenome structure, but they do not by themselves establish that ecological breadth causes openness. [src: discoveries] The new analysis **refines** this interpretation by showing that metabolic ecotype diversity itself tracks openness after genome-count adjustment, without demonstrating that environmental niche breadth is the mechanism. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

The same 1,872-species digest analysis also tested openness directly: pangenome openness correlated weakly but significantly with pathway completeness (r=0.107, p=3.6e-6) and with pathway variability measured as standard deviation (r=0.066, p=0.004), and moderately with AlphaEarth niche breadth (r=0.324, p=5.6e-47), while core fraction correlated negatively with niche breadth (r=-0.445, p=1.4e-91). [src: discoveries] The digest describes the variability relationship as stronger even though its reported r is smaller, and its readings that accessory genes enable metabolic heterogeneity and that pangenome flexibility enables adaptation to diverse environments are interpretations of correlations rather than direct tests. [src: discoveries] The niche-breadth association **contradicts** reading the null environment-effect result as showing that openness is unrelated to ecology, subject to the AlphaEarth coverage limits below. [src: discoveries]

Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) gene content gives a phylogeny-dependent openness signal. Within phyla, openness correlated positively with AMR count in 8/10 tested phyla, strongest in Bacillota (rho=0.219, p=1.0e-16) and Bacillota_C (rho=0.374, p=3.1e-5), whereas the overall correlation was near zero (rho=0.006), which the report interprets as phylogeny dominating the signal. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] Separately, AMR variability weakly anti-correlated with openness (Spearman rho = -0.193, p = 2.2e-12), so species with more open pangenomes had slightly lower AMR variability, contrary to expectation. [src: amr_strain_variation, discoveries] The proposed explanation, not directly tested in this evidence, is that open-pangenome species accumulate more rare or singleton AMR genes below the 5% threshold, deflating a variability index that measures the 5-95% prevalence zone; the negative association may therefore partly reflect the metric definition. [src: amr_strain_variation, discoveries] The prophage–AMR co-mobilization analysis adds a related caution. Its H2 association does not establish phage-mediated AMR transfer, because species with open pangenomes may independently accumulate both prophages and AMR genes. Openness is therefore a candidate confounder of mobile-element associations, not an established mechanism. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]

Openness did not track between-species gene acquisition. The gene_function_ecological_agora project defines pangenome openness as within-species genome-set diversity, the fraction of non-core gene clusters across multiple genome assemblies of a single species (computed as 1 − no_core/no_gene_clusters from motupan output), with high openness meaning high strain-level variability. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] Its P4-D4 test cross-correlated per-species openness with the project's M22 recent-rank gain attribution at genus level and returned an informative null at atlas scale: across 894 genera, openness versus log recent gains gave Pearson r +0.017 and Spearman r −0.011, 95% CI (−0.08, +0.06); restricting to regulatory KEGG orthologs (KOs) gave Pearson +0.023 and Spearman −0.013, 95% CI (−0.08, +0.05), and metabolic KOs gave Pearson +0.010 and Spearman −0.017, 95% CI (−0.08, +0.05), both null; the four-panel validation figure shows the same Spearman r=−0.011 null. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] The report concludes that openness is not a valid cross-substrate validation of M22 because within-species strain diversity and lineage-level between-species gain attribution measure distinct phenomena; this **refines** the openness concept by separating it from deep-time acquisition rate, rather than showing that gene gain is irrelevant to openness. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

The choice of openness metric matters for the direction of functional associations. In the pgp_pangenome_ecology analysis, openness was measured as singleton fraction. On that measure, openness correlated **negatively** with plant growth-promoting (PGP) gene richness (Spearman ρ = −0.195, p = 2.0e-97, n = 11,272 species with ≥2 genomes). The report's scatter-and-boxplot figure of singleton fraction versus PGP gene richness shows the same negative correlation. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology] This runs opposite in direction to the positive pathway-variability and ecotype associations above. However, the openness metrics differ (singleton fraction versus fraction of accessory gene clusters), and so do the functional gene sets, so the results are not directly equivalent tests and do not establish a contradiction. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology, pathway_capability_dependency] As literature context, the Pseudomonas carbon-ecology report attributes to Silby et al. (2011) and Loper et al. (2012) the estimate that ~54% of the *P. fluorescens*-group pangenome encodes variable metabolic capabilities. That estimate is consistent with variable metabolism making up much of accessory content in that group, but it is a cited external estimate, not a corpus measurement. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

Several reports propose openness as a predictor without testing it. The microbeatlas metal-ecology report suggests estimating genus-level pangenome openness as the proportion of genes in the accessory genome. It would then use that estimate as a predictor of niche breadth alongside metal type diversity, on the hypothesis that a broader accessory genome may drive both traits. This was not tested. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] The Web of Microbes explorer proposes testing whether organisms with higher E/(E+I) ratios (more de novo production) have more open pangenomes or more accessory genes. It would relate this to its metabolic novelty rate, which ranges from 32.4% for GW456-L13 to 15.2% for FW507-8R2A. That association remains untested, and the denominator of the reported novelty percentages is unclear. [src: webofmicrobes_explorer]

## Sampling and Representation Limits

Only 6.8% of species, or 1,872 of 27,690, had sufficient AlphaEarth coverage for the ecological-breadth analysis. [src: discoveries] Consequently, the observed relationships may describe the subset of species with adequate environmental representation rather than the full catalog of 27,690 species. [src: discoveries]

The AlphaEarth dataset also contained 3,838 of 83,287 genomes with NaN dimensions, and 36.6% of genomes were clustered at coordinates containing more than 50 genomes of more than 10 species. [src: discoveries] These coverage and coordinate-crowding patterns can make apparent environmental breadth partly reflect uneven sampling or representation in embedding space. [src: discoveries]

The contrast between the null environment and phylogeny result and the strong metabolic-pathway result shows why openness analyses require explicit separation of biological predictors from sampling proxies. [src: discoveries] Genome count was an important adjustment variable because controlling for it changed the pathway association from rho=0.327 to partial rho=0.530. [src: discoveries] The new analysis **reinforces** this caution by noting that its species sample was restricted to taxa with both pangenome statistics and ecotype-analysis results, that openness is only one summary of pangenome structure, and that partial correlations may not fully disentangle confounded variables; limited genome counts may also reduce power for some species. [src: pangenome_openness] Its broader analysis required at least 10 genomes per GTDB species, while the ecotype analysis required at least 50 genomes and at least 3 variable pathways, making the latter especially vulnerable to sampling-selection effects. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] The report itself notes that species with more sequenced genomes are more likely to show pathway variation and more ecotypes. It adds that partial correlations controlling for genome count mitigate this bias but do not fully eliminate it. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] The fitness-linked analysis is additionally limited to 48 organisms among 293,000 genomes with pathway predictions, so its clade-level association may not generalize across taxa. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] The fitness-conservation result adds a related representation concern: singleton and novel genes may appear neutral because of poor transposon coverage rather than true absence of effects. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

The P4-D4 null is also substrate-limited. Of 18,989 qualified species representatives, 10,856 had at least three genomes, the minimum for meaningful within-species openness, and of 3,539 genera with at least one multi-genome species, 894 had at least three multi-genome species for the atlas-wide test; the corresponding output tables carry 3,539 and 894 rows. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] Clade-targeted tests were directionally positive but underpowered: Mycobacteriaceae × mycolic acid gave Pearson +0.46 and Spearman +0.41 across 10 genera, 95% CI (−0.43, +0.86), and Cyanobacteriia × photosystem II (PSII) gave Pearson +0.11 and Spearman +0.12 across 83 genera, 95% CI (−0.10, +0.34); both intervals span zero, and multi-genome coverage of only 289 species across 10 Mycobacteriaceae genera and 185 species across 83 Cyanobacteriia genera is the limiting factor. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

Openness can also inflate composite gene-content scores. A metal functional signature of 1,286 KEGG KO terms was used to score all 27,702 pangenome species. Without genome-size normalization, species with large open pangenomes (*K. pneumoniae*, *P. aeruginosa*) dominated the scores, which reflected genome size rather than metal biology. After normalization by total KEGG-annotated clusters, bioleaching genera as a group were not significantly enriched over background (Mann-Whitney p=0.17). [src: metal_fitness_atlas] This **supports** the caution that openness-correlated gene counts need normalization before they are read as functional specialization. The 27,702-species scoring catalog differs from the 27,690-species catalog cited for the AlphaEarth analysis, and neither source explains the difference. [src: metal_fitness_atlas, discoveries]

## Tensions

The two openness analyses provide conflicting evidence about environmental determinants: one reported no significant environment or phylogenetic relationship, whereas another found strong associations between variable metabolic pathways and openness and between ecological breadth and pathway completeness. [src: discoveries] This **tension** may reflect differences in predictor definition, genome-count control, species inclusion, or environmental coverage rather than a simple contradiction about whether environment matters. [src: discoveries] The digest itself contrasts the pathway-variability association with the pangenome_openness null and calls metabolic pathway variability a genuine predictor, but the two results use different predictor sets and are not directly equivalent tests. [src: discoveries] The pathway_capability_dependency report draws the same contrast. It cites the pangenome_openness null for openness against environment or phylogeny effect sizes, and states that its pathway-variability metric succeeds where broader ecological variables failed. That framing is the report's interpretation across different predictor sets, not a harmonized comparison. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] The digest's direct openness-niche breadth correlation (r=0.324) sharpens the tension, because it links openness to an environmental breadth measure, though only within the coverage-limited AlphaEarth subset. [src: discoveries] The new analysis **supports** the null side of this tension for its harmonized openness-versus-effect test, but does not test the pathway or AlphaEarth associations directly. [src: pangenome_openness]

The strongest current interpretation is therefore that metabolic pathway variability is a reproducible candidate determinant or proxy of openness, while direct environmental and phylogenetic effects remain specification-dependent. [src: discoveries] The AlphaEarth associations should be treated as coverage-limited and not as population-wide causal estimates because only 6.8% of species had sufficient coverage. [src: discoveries] The HGT and core/accessory explanations remain hypotheses requiring functional and transfer-specific tests, not established consequences of the null correlations. [src: pangenome_openness] The ecotype-openness association **supports** metabolic diversification as an additional correlate, but its dependence on clustering thresholds and genome-count eligibility prevents it from establishing causality. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] The positive latent-capability association **further supports** this correlate while leaving unresolved whether openness promotes fitness-neutral capabilities, whether shared ecological context produces both, or whether both are affected by sampling and community interactions. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] The fitness study **supports** a functional distinction between core and accessory genes but **refines** any interpretation that conservation alone explains pangenome openness: its conservation gradient was statistically robust yet weak, and core genes also showed condition-specific and opposing fitness effects. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

Phylogenetic scale also changes the answer. The near-zero overall AMR-openness correlation (rho=0.006) alongside positive within-phylum correlations in 8/10 phyla **refines** the null phylogeny-effect result: pooling across phyla can mask lineage-level associations, so a null pooled correlation does not establish absence of within-lineage association, a pattern also tracked in [[concepts/phylogenetic-confounding-of-pangenome-associations]]. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] Similarly, the atlas-scale openness-versus-M22 null coexists with directionally positive but underpowered clade-targeted tests, leaving open whether openness tracks gene acquisition within specific lineages. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

## Relation to Pangenome Integration

These findings **refine** [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] by showing that pangenome synthesis should connect gene-content openness to variable metabolic pathways, pathway completeness, ecological breadth, and geographic representation rather than treating openness as an environment-independent species trait. [src: discoveries] They also **connect** to [[concepts/environment-embedding-geography]], because environmental embeddings and geographic range were used as predictors of pathway completeness, and to [[concepts/pangenome-openness-determinants]] only as the present concept's scope rather than as a separate target. [src: discoveries] The fitness comparison **further supports** integrating conservation and perturbation-derived functional evidence, while keeping the weak conservation-fitness relationship distinct from direct tests of openness. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

## Open Directions

- Recalculate openness, variable-pathway content, and pathway completeness across the full 27,690-species catalog using coverage-aware regression and explicit genome-count adjustment; test whether the metabolic association remains when the 1,872-species AlphaEarth subset is expanded or weighted for sampling coverage. [src: discoveries]
- Repeat the environment-versus-phylogeny comparison with the same species set, predictor definitions, and covariate structure used in the variable-pathway analysis; test whether the null correlations rho=-0.05 and rho=0.03 persist under harmonized modeling. [src: discoveries]
- Model AlphaEarth missingness and coordinate crowding explicitly, including the 3,838 genomes with NaN dimensions and the 36.6% of genomes in densely shared coordinates; test whether pathway-completeness associations survive inverse-coverage weighting or leave-one-coordinate-out validation. [src: discoveries]
- Partition variable metabolic pathways into core metabolic, secondary metabolic, and transport categories using pathway-level annotations; test which categories account for the partial rho=0.530 association with openness. [src: discoveries]
- Compare species-level openness with independent sampling-depth measures and geographic-range estimates; test whether the correlations for niche breadth, embedding variance, and geographic range reflect biological ecological breadth or uneven genome and environment sampling. [src: discoveries]
- Recompute metabolic ecotypes across alternative clustering distances and genome-count cutoffs, then test whether the partial rho=0.322 association survives phylogenetically independent or coverage-weighted models. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]
- Test whether the latent-capability rate correlation rho=0.69 persists after direct GapMind-to-fitness gene mapping, expanded fitness-condition coverage, and phylogenetically independent models; distinguish genome-dynamic effects from pathway-specific conservation. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]
- Stratify openness by functional categories, including mobile and defense-associated genes, and compare auxiliary fraction, Heap's law alpha, and pangenome fluidity across lifestyle groups; test whether the null broad environment result masks function-specific or lifestyle-dependent associations. [src: pangenome_openness]
- Link openness and functional categories to fitness breadth and condition-specific annotations across the 43-bacterium dataset; test whether the weak conservation-fitness gradient and core enrichment of ephemeral-niche genes persist after controlling for genome count, transposon callability, and phylogeny. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- Recompute AMR variability with an index that includes rare and singleton genes below the 5% threshold, then test whether the negative association with openness (rho=-0.193) persists or reflects the 5-95% prevalence-band definition. [src: amr_strain_variation, discoveries]
- Fit within-phylum and phylogenetically independent models of openness versus AMR count across all tested phyla, to test whether the near-zero pooled correlation and the 8/10 within-phylum positive correlations reconcile under explicit phylogenetic structure. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]
- Repeat the openness-versus-M22 recent-gain test within Mycobacteriaceae and Cyanobacteriia as multi-genome species accumulate, testing whether the directional positive clade-targeted correlations lift their confidence intervals off zero. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]
- Re-run the 2,810-species variable-pathway analysis with one documented phylogenetic grouping scheme, to explain why the digest's 18/21 group tally and the report's 13 of 18 genus tally differ. [src: discoveries, pathway_capability_dependency]
- Test the openness-niche breadth correlation (r=0.324) and core fraction-niche breadth correlation (r=-0.445) under genome-count adjustment and coverage weighting, to determine whether they reflect ecology or sampling in the 1,872-species subset. [src: discoveries]
- Compute singleton fraction and accessory-cluster fraction for the same species set, then re-test PGP gene richness against both. This would show whether the negative singleton-fraction association reflects metric choice or biology. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]
- Estimate genus-level accessory-genome proportion and add it to niche-breadth models alongside metal type diversity. This would test the untested proposal that accessory-genome breadth underlies both metal resistance and niche breadth. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]
- Repeat composite metal-signature scoring with and without genome-size normalization, with openness entered as a covariate. This would quantify how much of the unnormalized dominance by open-pangenome species reflects genome size. [src: metal_fitness_atlas]
