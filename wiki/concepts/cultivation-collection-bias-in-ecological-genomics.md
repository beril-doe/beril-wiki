# Cultivation and collection bias constrain ecological genomic inference

Ecological genomic inference is constrained when available genomes come disproportionately from culturable, described strains rather than the full diversity of environmental bacteria. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] The BacDive metal-validation project directly illustrates this limitation by linking curated isolation environments to genome-derived metal-tolerance scores through a species-level [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] bridge. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] The underlying analysis is documented in [[summaries/bacdive_metal_validation__REPORT]]. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

The MicrobeAtlas metal-ecology analysis **supports** this broader concern by showing that ecological-range inference is itself sensitive to representation: MicrobeAtlas niche breadth is a sequencing-effort proxy rather than confirmed ecological range, and is affected by sampling intensity, primer bias, geographic and temporal non-uniformity, and missing environments. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] Its genus-level analysis also found that sequenced-genome coverage correlated with inferred metal-type diversity (r = 0.35, p = 2.6×10⁻¹⁹), while single-genome genera had lower metal-type counts than multi-genome genera (Mann–Whitney p = 5.1×10⁻¹⁰). [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] Thus, both cultivated genome collections and environmental occurrence atlases can select which organisms and traits appear ecologically informative. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

The plant microbiome analysis **supports and extends** this concern to host-associated collections: plant-compartment labels were inferred primarily from NCBI `isolation_source` metadata, only 7,995 of 293,059 genomes (2.7%) had plant-associated annotations, and endophytes comprised only 29 species. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] BacDive identified 2,482 plant-related strains but contributed zero additional genome upgrades beyond `ncbi_env`. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] These figures show that both sparse metadata and the cultivation history of available genomes can constrain ecological classification before genomic comparisons begin. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The ecotype analysis **refines** this representation concern by showing that environmental metadata may be too incomplete or spatially indirect to explain genome-wide gene-content similarity: AlphaEarth embeddings covered only 28.4% of genomes, and geographic coordinates for host-associated bacteria may describe collection sites rather than their actual microenvironments. [src: ecotype_analysis] Across 172 species with sufficient data, phylogeny generally dominated environmental similarity as a predictor of gene-content similarity, while significant environmental effects were detected in only 12 species (7.0%) and no significant effect was detected in 156 species (90.7%). [src: ecotype_analysis] This does not contradict the evidence that collection and sequencing bias shape ecological inference; it indicates that weak environmental effects can reflect limited environmental coverage and metadata resolution, as well as genuinely lineage-dominated genome structure. [src: ecotype_analysis] The analysis is documented in [[summaries/ecotype_analysis__REPORT]]. [src: ecotype_analysis]

The plant analysis further **refines** this interpretation: across 607 root, rhizosphere, and phyllosphere species, compartment explained only approximately 6% of functional-marker variance after the refined analysis, and the original much larger effect lost 86% of its R² after excluding three genome-rich species per compartment. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The report attributes the initial effect mainly to taxonomic sampling and dominant rhizobial or Pseudomonas clades rather than a community-wide compartment signal. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] This supports the view that ecological labels and uneven taxonomic representation can make apparently strong environment–genome associations look broader than they are, while not proving that the residual compartment signal is purely an artifact. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The environmental reanalysis **supports** the clinical-sampling-bias observation but **refines** its interpretation: environmental species did not have stronger environment–gene-content partial correlations than human-associated species (one-sided Mann–Whitney U=1536, p=0.83), and the environmental-genome fraction was not associated with correlation strength (Spearman rho=-0.085, p=0.25). [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] Thus, the confirmed clinical bias does not account for the weak environment–gene-content signal under the reanalysis methodology. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] The report used genome-level harmonized classifications and majority voting across genome isolation sources, with 106 of 224 species majority human-associated, 47 majority environmental, and 71 Mixed/Other. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] This result is documented in [[summaries/ecotype_env_reanalysis__REPORT]]. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]

## Evidence from BacDive–pangenome matching

Of 97,334 BacDive strains, 42,227 strains (43.4%) matched 6,426 GTDB species, while 55,107 strains (56.6%) remained unmatched. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] Exact species-name agreement produced 33,535 matches (34.5%), and removing GTDB suffixes produced 8,692 additional matches (8.9%). [src: bacdive_metal_validation] Only 25,089 matched strains had isolation-source metadata, so both genome-score linkage and ecological metadata availability reduced the usable sample. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

This matching loss limits which cultivated strains can contribute to ecological validation and can make the retained sample unrepresentative of environmental diversity. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] The report specifically notes that BacDive represents culturable, described strains and may under-represent metal-tolerant extremophiles because of culture-collection bias. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] The unresolved 56.6% could partly reflect different species boundaries between GTDB and LPSN/DSMZ rather than absence of relevant genomes, making accession-level matching an important remedy. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

The MicrobeAtlas coverage result **refines** this interpretation: increasing the number of available genomes can reveal more metal types, but does not by itself establish that the added traits are distributed across the environment rather than concentrated in better-sampled lineages. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] In a 200-iteration rarefaction analysis representing each genus by one randomly sampled species, the metal-type association remained directionally consistent, but the median coefficient was +0.0147 rather than the full-data estimate of +0.0215; 89.5% of iterations had p < 0.05 and 57.5% were Bonferroni-significant at p < 0.0083. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] This supports coverage sensitivity as a measurement issue while showing that the observed association was not eliminated by that particular subsampling procedure. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

The plant analysis **supports** the same caution through a second representation mismatch: only 11.7% Jaccard overlap existed between 487 pangenome-derived plant genera and 438 MGnify rhizosphere genera. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The two resources measure different phenomena—mostly isolation metadata versus metagenomic detection—so their disagreement cannot be interpreted simply as ecological absence or presence. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] This strengthens the case for treating collection-based and occurrence-based catalogs as complementary, rather than interchangeable, samples of environmental diversity.

## Collection bias affects environmental validation

Among matched strains, heavy-metal contamination isolates had a median metal score of 0.240 and a mean of 0.236, compared with an environmental-baseline median of 0.187 and mean of 0.195. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] Their median delta was +0.053, Cohen's d was +1.00, and the Mann–Whitney p-value was 0.006 for n=10 isolates. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] Cohen's d was calculated as (mean_group - mean_baseline) / pooled_SD, with pooled_SD = sqrt((SD_group² + SD_baseline²) / 2). [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

The broader contamination gradient was heavy metal (+1.00) > waste/sludge (+0.57) > all contamination (+0.43) > industrial (+0.20). [src: bacdive_metal_validation] Waste/sludge isolates had n=305 and d=+0.57, all-contamination isolates had n=176 and d=+0.43, and industrial isolates had n=796 and d=+0.20; each comparison had p<0.0001. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] Host-associated isolates had n=12,086, d=+0.14, and p<0.0001, indicating that collection category can also track biological or genomic composition unrelated to environmental contamination. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

These results support [[concepts/environmental-resistome]] by showing that curated isolation environments can validate genome-based metal-tolerance predictions, but the strength of that validation depends on which environments and organisms enter the culture collection. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] The heavy-metal result is especially uncertain because n=10 matched isolates produced an observed d=1.00, only slightly above the approximately d=0.93 minimum detectable effect size for 80% power against an environmental baseline of approximately 5,000 strains. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

The MicrobeAtlas analysis **supports but qualifies** this interpretation: across 606 genera, metal-type diversity was associated with broader inferred niche breadth after phylogenetic adjustment, but the association was correlational and could reflect genome size, metabolic versatility, biofilm capacity, or gene acquisition rather than a causal effect of metal resistance. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] Controlling for genome size retained the metal-type effect (β = +0.0218, SE = 0.0061, p = 3.6×10⁻⁴), while log(genome size) also remained associated (β = +0.0263, SE = 0.0069, p = 1.5×10⁻⁴). [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] This reinforces the need to treat contamination-category differences as ecological validation signals rather than direct causal demonstrations.

The plant study **refines** the interpretation of marker-based ecological validation by showing that genome presence does not establish activity or phenotype. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] In its refined plant-associated panel, 878 of 1,115 species (78.7%) were classified as dual-nature because they carried both beneficial and pathogenic marker types, while all four neutral controls in an 18-organism validation panel were misclassified. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The report therefore treats categorical marker cohorts as a coarse screen and emphasizes continuous scores and experimental or transcriptomic validation. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] This is directly analogous to the BacDive limitation: a curated environmental label or genomic marker can provide validation evidence without independently demonstrating realized ecological function.

## Taxonomic composition and residual bias

Within Pseudomonadota, contamination isolates had metal scores delta=+0.040 above environmental isolates with p<0.001, using n=85 contamination and n=5,655 environmental isolates. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] Within Actinomycetota, the corresponding delta was +0.035 with p<0.001, using n=62 contamination and n=772 environmental isolates. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] These results support a contamination-associated signal beyond broad phylum composition in the two most-sampled phyla. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

The signal was not significant within Bacillota, where delta=-0.012, p=0.285, n=19 contamination and n=492 environmental isolates, or Bacteroidota, where delta=-0.008, p=0.456, n=5 contamination and n=156 environmental isolates. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] These null results cannot distinguish biological differences from limited statistical power because contamination-isolate sample sizes were small. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

Host-associated strains had a positive but small deviation, with d=+0.14 and p<0.0001. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] The report attributes this pattern potentially to genome-size and taxonomic confounding because host-associated records were dominated by Pseudomonas, Klebsiella, and Acinetobacter, which have large genomes and more KEGG-annotated gene clusters. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] Genome-size normalization reduced but did not eliminate this possible confounding, so normalization alone does not establish that isolation environment caused the score difference. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

The MicrobeAtlas study **refines** this taxonomic caution by showing that genus aggregation can make a broad genus score represent different species occupying different habitats rather than one organism being a generalist. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] Its strict 5% within-environment prevalence analysis reduced the PGLS sample from 606 to 379 genera and changed the metal-type coefficient from +0.0215 (p = 1.5×10⁻⁴) to +0.0166 (p = 0.092), although the direction was preserved. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] Ecological conclusions therefore depend on both taxonomic coverage and the detection threshold used to define environmental occurrence. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

The plant study **supports** this warning about taxonomic composition: its initial compartment effect was largely attributable to genome-rich species and dominant rhizobial or Pseudomonas clades, while the refined compartment signal was small. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Likewise, of 5,671 plant-enriched eggNOG groups, 5,341 (94.2%) were significant before phylum control, but only 50 retained significance after phylum-level control; the report interprets the near-saturation of raw associations mainly as taxonomic compositional divergence rather than plant-specific enrichment. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] This demonstrates that large collections can increase statistical certainty without removing lineage-composition bias.

## Phenotype records are an additional bottleneck

Only 24 matched strains had metal-utilization records covering iron, manganese, arsenate, chromate, cobalt, or zinc. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] Eight of those results were positive and 16 were negative. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] Positive utilizers had lower metal scores in the exploratory comparison, but the result was inconclusive because Mann–Whitney p=0.14 and Cohen's d=-0.57 with only 24 records. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

This underpowered phenotype comparison shows that linking genomes to collection metadata does not guarantee an independent phenotype validation. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] The limitation refines [[concepts/multi-omics-integration]]: genome-derived scores and curated phenotype records can be connected, but sparse and selectively recorded phenotypes may prevent reliable assessment of agreement. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

The environmental atlas provides a related validation constraint: in its 133-sample community analysis, only 16.8% of reads were joinable to genus-level GTDB metal-diversity data, so community-weighted metal-type means could be biased if uncovered genera have systematically different resistance profiles. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] The study therefore recommends testing the correlation between per-sample covered-read fraction and the community-weighted mean before interpreting site-level patterns. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

The plant analysis **supports** this bottleneck from a different angle: BacDive metabolite-utilization data agreed with GapMind pathway predictions at 83.1% consistency, but a GapMind core-level completeness score was 0% across compartments and may have been too stringent for broad taxonomic comparisons. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Thus, even when independent records are available, apparent disagreement can reflect assay scope, pathway thresholds, and taxonomic coverage rather than ecological biology alone.

## Interpretation and tensions

The contamination gradient supports the hypothesis that genome-based Metal Fitness Atlas scores capture real ecological metal-adaptation signal, but the heavy-metal estimate is imprecise because only n=10 isolates matched species with scores. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] This evidence is stronger at the larger contamination-category sample sizes than for the heavy-metal category itself, where the effect barely exceeded the power threshold. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

The result also needs to be distinguished from the finding that metal-tolerance genes were 88% core in the metal-specificity project: within-species conservation can coexist with between-species differences in the total number of metal-tolerance genes. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] The proposed explanation that species with larger core genomes encoding more metal-tolerance functions are more likely to occur in contaminated environments is an interpretation rather than a direct causal test. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

Compared with the Oak Ridge field-abundance analysis, which found a suggestive but non-significant correlation between laboratory metal tolerance and field abundance of rho=0.50, p=0.095, n=11 genera, the BacDive analysis provides larger-scale support for the same hypothesis through contamination-category effect sizes of d=+0.43 to +1.00. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] The comparison does not remove collection bias because the BacDive heavy-metal result still uses only n=10 isolates. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

The MicrobeAtlas groundwater analysis **supports** an environmental-occurrence relationship but **does not resolve** this collection-bias problem: metal-type diversity correlated with groundwater prevalence (Spearman ρ = +0.112, p = 0.0019), whereas groundwater-specific fold-enrichment was null (ρ = +0.042, p = 0.242). [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] This distinction argues against treating either curated isolation source or broad environmental detection as uniquely identifying a metal-contamination mechanism. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

The ecotype result **refines** this interpretation by showing that a weak whole-genome environmental signal cannot by itself distinguish absent ecological adaptation from inadequate environmental representation: the analysis used partial correlations, which assume linear relationships between distance matrices, and found no significant environmental effect for 90.7% of species. [src: ecotype_analysis] The reanalysis **supports** the robustness of the environmental-versus-human-associated null within one consistent method: Environmental species had median partial correlation 0.051 versus 0.084 for human-associated species, while the Mixed/Other median was 0.109; the environmental group was not stronger despite the confirmed clinical sampling bias. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] However, absolute correlations are not comparable across the two analyses: the reanalysis median across 183 species was 0.081 versus 0.003 originally, characterized as a 27x difference, because it used all embedded genomes, including up to 3,505 genomes per species, rather than diversity-maximizing downsampling capped at 250 genomes and also used different genome sets. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] Environmental species also had a 21% NaN rate versus 7% for human-associated species, so unequal filtering does not explain the absence of a stronger environmental signal and may, if anything, bias toward finding one. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] The original proposed follow-up—testing functional subsets such as COG categories rather than whole-genome gene content—therefore remains relevant because environmental adaptation may be concentrated in specific genes and missed by genome-wide summaries. [src: ecotype_analysis]

The reanalysis further **refines** the role assigned to clinical sampling bias: it confirms that environmental metadata and genome composition remain difficult to interpret, but indicates that the clinical imbalance alone does not explain the weak correlation. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] Possible explanations involving geographic epidemiological structure in human-associated species, greater statistical power in species with more genomes, and heterogeneous Mixed/Other sampling campaigns remain hypotheses rather than demonstrated mechanisms. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]

The plant study introduces a related **tension** with interpretations that treat broad plant-associated marker patterns as community-wide ecological specialization. Its refined PERMANOVA found a statistically significant compartment effect (pseudo-F = 23.2, p = 0.001) but R² = 0.071, while excluding three genome-rich species reduced the earlier R² = 0.527 to R² = 0.072. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The first result supports a real compartment-associated signal; the second supports the competing explanation that the original magnitude was largely driven by taxonomic sampling. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] These findings should not be reconciled by averaging: the effect is statistically detectable, but its community-wide magnitude remains sensitive to representation and model refinement.

## Open Directions

- Match BacDive GCA accessions directly to pangenome genome_ids through `kbase_ke_pangenome.genome`, then test whether recovered strains change the contamination effect estimates and the 56.6% unmatched fraction. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- Integrate ENIGMA CORAL community data from the Oak Ridge metal-contaminated site and compare community-level metal-tolerance profiles with BacDive isolation-source predictions to test whether the culture-collection signal generalizes to field communities. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- Stratify matched BacDive strains by metal-tolerance gene families and contamination environment, using metals with sufficient representation including iron and manganese, to test whether specific functions predict specific environments. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- Expand BacDive phenotype extraction beyond the `metabolite_utilization` table to include MIC and growth-inhibition data, then test whether independently measured tolerance agrees with genome-derived scores. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- Reanalyze contamination effects within taxonomic strata and after genome-size normalization, using the small Bacillota and Bacteroidota groups to determine whether their null results reflect biology or limited power. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- Build multi-primer, multi-region occurrence datasets and perform species-per-genus rarefaction to test whether the metal-type/niche-breadth association persists after detection probability and pangenome coverage are jointly equalized. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]
- For community-level validation, relate covered-read fraction to weighted metal-diversity scores and compare genus-level predictions with independent metal measurements across sites. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]
- Recalculate environmental–gene-content associations using direct environmental metadata and alternative embedding distances, then test COG functional subsets and ecotype-cluster gene content to determine whether environmental effects are masked by incomplete AlphaEarth coverage or whole-genome aggregation. [src: ecotype_analysis]
- Compare downsampled and full-genome extraction on the same species, controlling for genome count, to resolve the 27x partial-correlation discrepancy; repeat the test with structured ENVO terms and functional subsets. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]
- Expand plant-associated phylogenetic-tree coverage beyond 18 of 65 candidate species and repeat within-species host/subclade tests, while matching collection metadata to genome identifiers, to determine whether apparent host specialization generalizes beyond the currently testable lineages. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
- Compare pangenome-derived plant association with MGnify detection after genus-level rarefaction and matched taxonomic coverage, then test whether compartment effects persist when genome-rich species and uneven environmental sampling are controlled. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
- Validate plant-associated beneficial and pathogenic marker sets with transcriptomics or experiments, and test whether continuous scores outperform categorical labels under matched genome-size and taxonomic controls. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
