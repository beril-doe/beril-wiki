# Dual-nature plant-associated bacteria and the validity of marker-based cohorts

Dual-nature plant-associated bacteria carry both plant growth promoting (PGP) and pathogenic marker genes. The central digest reports that 60-85% of plant-associated bacteria are dual-nature [src: discoveries], and the underlying project reports the same range. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Later controls and validation weaken that headline: the rate depends on the marker panel and the scoring scheme, the categorical cohort labels do not discriminate at species level [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes], and the rate varies with genome size. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] A refined 17-marker panel still gives a high rate (78.7% among plant-associated species), and the report reads that as biologically real. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The evidence is genomic marker presence only, so it is not expression or experimental evidence. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

## Literature Context

The candidate papers support the idea that a binary beneficial/pathogenic label is hard to maintain, but they do not measure how common dual-nature bacteria are. A review argues that for many plant-associated microorganisms pathogenicity is not fixed. It varies along a gradient with host physiology, microbial strain, environmental conditions, resident microbiota and time [PMID 43055730](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43055730/). The review describes four reference points on this continuum: near-deterministic pathogens, context-dependent pathogens, opportunistic pathogens, and pathobiome- or dysbiosis-associated disease states. It also says that species-level or pathovar-level detection often misses real differences in virulence and host range [PMID 43055730](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43055730/). The abstract gives no prevalence figures.

Two experimental papers bear on the corpus in different ways. A study of Salicaceae (willow and poplar) xylem endophytes found Bacillus, Pseudomonas and Erwinia with plant growth-promoting traits and strong antagonism against vascular pathogens, including Xylella fastidiosa and Brenneria salicis. Co-inoculation reduced pathogen load and symptoms in tobacco and mitigated symptoms in willow. The authors call these endophytes context-dependent allies [PMID 42130174](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42130174/). This is a culture-based and genome-mining study, and the abstract gives no sample size.

A separate comparative genomics study of rice-associated Burkholderia looked at three strains: biocontrol B. vietnamiensis J14EpLeaf2 and pathogenic B. gladioli A1EpSeed5 and B. cepacia J14Eple. The pathogens had larger genomes (8.36-8.46 Mb) than the 6.95 Mb genome of B. vietnamiensis. They were also richer in mobile genetic elements and in CAZymes (carbohydrate-active enzymes) for plant cell wall degradation. B. gladioli had a complete T3SS, whereas B. cepacia lacked structural T3SS genes [PMID 42278262](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42278262/). Only three strains were compared. The abstract also notes that the predicted protein-protein interaction networks need experimental validation. LC-MS confirmed IAA, ornibactin and AHL production in B. vietnamiensis, which gives direct chemical support for its growth-promoting and biocontrol role [PMID 42278262](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42278262/).

The corpus's central claim is that 60-85% of plant-associated bacteria carry both PGP and pathogenic markers, with 78.7% (878/1,115) dual-nature under the refined 17-marker panel. This is novel as a genome-scale rate, because none of the candidates quantifies prevalence. The qualitative message is consistent with the continuum review. That message is that markers alone cannot assign a lifestyle, and that categorical labels discriminate poorly at fine resolution [PMID 43055730](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43055730/). The corpus's finding that all 14 beneficial and pathogenic ground-truth species were called dual-nature, and that all 4 neutral controls were misclassified, fits the review's strain-level caution.

The Burkholderia study partly qualifies the corpus. In three strains, genome size and effector or secretion repertoire differed between pathogenic and beneficial lifestyles [PMID 42278262](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42278262/). This is consistent with the corpus's continuous pathogenicity ratio, which separated beneficial and pathogenic species only weakly (p = 0.027, N = 7 vs 7). It is also consistent with the corpus's genome-size gradient, in which dual-nature prevalence was 54% in the bottom quartile. The Burkholderia result does not establish that genome size drives the lifestyle difference. The Xylella result in the Salicaceae study relates to the corpus's proposed Xylella fastidiosa validation organism. Expression-level evidence for co-regulation of the two marker sets remains untested in these papers and in the corpus.

## The headline rate

Classifying 25,660 species into beneficial, pathogenic, dual-nature and neutral cohorts showed that most plant-associated species carry both marker types. [src: discoveries] The project reports 65–85% per compartment. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The 30 genus dossiers showed near-universal carriage of both marker types among plant-associated genera. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The authors read this as a challenge to binary beneficial/pathogenic classification, and as a sign that plant-microbe interactions are context-dependent. [src: discoveries, plant_microbiome_ecotypes] They name host genotype, environmental conditions and community composition as determinants of the outcome. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] They link the pattern to Drew et al. (2021) and Etesami (2025), and call their work the first genome-scale quantification across 25,660 species. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] One example is the type III secretion system (T3SS). It delivers pathogenic effectors in Pseudomonas syringae and facilitates beneficial nodulation signaling in Rhizobium. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The report concludes that genomic markers alone are insufficient to call a bacterium beneficial or pathogenic. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

## How panel and scoring choice change the rate

The rate depends strongly on the scheme.
- The binary NB02 method calls a species dual-nature if it has at least one PGP and one pathogenic marker. It classifies 15,474/25,660 species (60.3%) as dual-nature. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
- The refined 17-marker panel with KEGG module gating gave 78.7% dual-nature after ubiquitous bacterial functions were removed. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
- NB07 composite scoring weights PGP markers 40%, core fraction 20%, complementarity 15%, metabolic breadth 10% and pathogen penalty 15%, with adaptive thresholds at the median of non-zero scores. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] It classifies 38.0% of species as neutral, 29.5% pathogenic, 25.2% dual-nature and 7.3% beneficial. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The report attributes the 35 percentage point gap between schemes to the difference between carrying both marker types and scoring highly on both axes. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] NB02 is more inclusive. NB07 is more discriminating but more assumption-laden. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The NB07 rate also depends on arbitrary scoring weights. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

### Refined plant-specific panel

The Phase 1 91-marker panel included ubiquitous bacterial functions and classified even Escherichia and Salmonella as 100% dual-nature. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The refinement removed 6 ubiquitous categories (flagella, chemotaxis, T6SS, biofilm, quorum sensing, T2SS), which had only 1.2–2.4× enrichment in plant versus non-plant species, and left 17 plant-specific markers. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The Phase 2 panel has 9 PGP and 8 pathogenic markers and uses KEGG module completeness gating for multi-gene systems. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

Across all 25,660 species, the refined panel classified 12,463 (48.6%) as dual-nature, 6,272 (24.4%) as pathogenic, 3,394 (13.2%) as beneficial and 3,531 (13.8%) as neutral. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Among the 1,115 plant-associated species, 878 (78.7%) were dual-nature, 108 (9.7%) pathogenic, 94 (8.4%) beneficial and 35 (3.1%) neutral. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Despite the stricter panel, dual-nature prevalence among plant-associated species increased from 60.3% to 78.7% (878/1,115 species). [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The report interprets this as biologically real, not an artifact of over-broad markers. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] This is the report's interpretation. The negative-control and ground-truth results below limit it.

### Marker generality

Several markers are ubiquitous across bacteria and not plant-specific. These are flagella, chemotaxis, biofilm, quorum sensing and secretion systems. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The inclusive NB02 rate reflects general functions such as flagella, chemotaxis and the type VI secretion system (T6SS). [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Chemotaxis and flagella contribute substantially to the dual-nature call. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] In dual-nature species, the pathogenic markers most often found were T6SS products (64%), T2SS (55%), T3SS products (50%) and chemotaxis (52%). [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The most common PGP markers were quorum sensing (49%), phenazine (34%) and flagella (40%). [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

Reclassifying T3SS, T6SS and T2SS as colonization markers changed 16.4% of dual-nature species to PGP-only. Plant-associated species still stayed 86.0% dual-nature, so the finding does not rest only on secretion-system annotations. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The report concludes that NB02's 60.3% substantially overestimates genuine plant-adapted duality. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

## Controls and ground-truth validation

Negative controls reduce the specificity of the Phase 1 markers. Non-plant Escherichia, Salmonella and Clostridioides were 100% dual-nature, and Staphylococcus was 98%. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] See [[entities/escherichia-coli]], [[entities/salmonella-enterica]] and [[entities/staphylococcus-aureus]] for related organisms.

With the refined panel the negative controls improved. Escherichia was 25% PGP-only, down from ubiquitous dual-nature in Phase 1. Salmonella was 0% PGP-only, Staphylococcus 18% and Mycobacterium 6%. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Positive controls were mostly beneficial or dual-nature: Rhizobium 97%, Bradyrhizobium 100%, Azospirillum 100% and Mesorhizobium 99%. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] In the PGP-versus-pathogen score validation, B. subtilis, R. leguminosarum, B. japonicum and S. meliloti cluster in the expected quadrants. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] See [[entities/bradyrhizobium]].

The earlier 92.7% genus-level agreement was circular, because cohorts are defined from marker presence. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The project replaced it with a curated panel of 18 model organisms: 7 beneficial, 7 pathogenic and 4 neutral. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] All 18 were present in the refined cohort data. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Categorical assignment was uninformative at species level. All 14 beneficial and pathogenic species were assigned dual-nature. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] All 4 neutral controls were misclassified [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]:
- E. coli K-12 as dual-nature. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
- Mycobacterium tuberculosis as dual-nature. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] See [[entities/mycobacterium-tuberculosis]].
- Deinococcus radiodurans as beneficial. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] See [[entities/deinococcus-radiodurans-atcc-13939]].
- Thermus thermophilus as pathogenic. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

An earlier 77.8% relaxed accuracy (14/18) was rejected as tautological. Counting dual-nature as correct for both classes cannot fail for dual-nature assignments. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The report attributes the neutral misclassifications to marker generality, not to a failure to detect plant adaptation. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The only discriminating signal was continuous. The pathogenicity ratio n_pathogen / (n_pgp + n_pathogen) had a median of 0.50 in beneficial species and 0.60 in pathogenic species (Mann-Whitney U = 9, p = 0.027, N = 7 vs 7). [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The report calls this a small effect at a small sample size, though statistically consistent. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] It advises treating the four-class labels as a coarse screen, with the continuous ratio discriminating within the dual-nature class, not as a species-level classifier. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] For a stronger follow-up panel, it proposes adding the near-pure beneficial Methylobacterium extorquens and the near-pure pathogen Xylella fastidiosa. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] See [[entities/methylobacterium-extorquens]].

## Genome size and expression caveats

Dual-nature prevalence among plant-associated species was 54% in the bottom genome-size quartile and 87% in the top three quartiles. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] About 33% of dual-nature species shift to neutral under genome-size normalization. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Normalization does not eliminate the dual-nature pattern. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The 78.7% headline therefore masks a genome-size gradient, which the report calls observational and not model-dependent. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] See [[concepts/genome-sampling-depth-genome-size-and-phylogeny-as-confounders-of-gene-content-and-pangenome-associations]]. Marker presence does not confirm simultaneous expression. Transcriptomic or experimental validation is needed to separate lifestyle flexibility from annotation artifacts, such as T6SS mediating inter-bacterial competition. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The report proposes RNAseq under beneficial versus pathogenic conditions to test whether the two gene sets are co-expressed or differentially regulated. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] See also [[summaries/plant_microbiome_ecotypes__REPORT]] and [[concepts/adversarial-review-pre-registration-and-multiple-testing-discipline]].

## Open Directions

- Add near-pure beneficial and near-pure pathogenic organisms to the validation panel. This would test whether any marker panel can separate the classes.
- Enlarge the ground-truth panel beyond 7 vs 7, and re-test the pathogenicity ratio with genome size and phylogeny as covariates. This would show whether p = 0.027 survives.
- Run RNAseq or use existing transcriptomes for dual-nature species under beneficial and pathogenic conditions, to separate real lifestyle flexibility from annotation artifacts.
- Compare dual-nature rates under several alternative scoring weights for NB07. This would measure how much the 25.2% depends on arbitrary weights.
- Re-run the non-plant negative controls and the neutral ground-truth species with the refined 17-marker panel, to see whether the misclassification of neutral species persists.
