# Conserved Within-Species Functions Can Coexist with Between-Species Functional Divergence

Species can conserve particular functions across their own members while differing from other species in the breadth of those functions. In the metal-tolerance analyses, this layered model explains how relatively conserved metal-tolerance functions can coexist with species-level differences in genome-derived scores and environmental distributions. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] The [[summaries/metal_fitness_atlas__REPORT]] **refines** this model by separating broadly required core cellular functions from specialized accessory resistance functions: across 22 organisms and 14 metals, metal-important genes were 87.4% core versus 76.9% for baseline genes (OR=2.08, p=4.3e-162). [src: metal_fitness_atlas]

## Evidence from Metal-Tolerance Scores

The [[summaries/bacdive_metal_validation__REPORT]] tested whether isolation environments provide ecological validation for genome-based metal-tolerance scores by linking [[entities/bacdive]] strains to [[entities/gtdb]] pangenome species. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] Among 97,334 BacDive strains, 42,227 (43.4%) matched 6,426 GTDB species, and 25,089 matched strains had isolation-source metadata. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

Heavy-metal contamination isolates had a median metal score of 0.240 and a mean of 0.236, compared with an environmental-baseline median of 0.187 and mean of 0.195. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] Their median delta was +0.053, with Cohen's d = +1.00 and Mann-Whitney p=0.006 for n=10 isolates. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] The contamination gradient was heavy metal d=+1.00, waste/sludge d=+0.57, all contamination d=+0.43, and industrial environments d=+0.20, with n=10, n=305, n=176, and n=796 isolates, respectively. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] These environment-associated differences **support** between-species divergence in aggregate metal-tolerance capacity, even when individual functions may be conserved within species. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

The new MicrobeAtlas analysis **supports** this between-species component at broader ecological scale: among 606 bacterial genera with AMR data, metal-type diversity was positively associated with inferred niche breadth in phylogenetic generalized least-squares (PGLS), with β = +0.021, SE = 0.0056, and p = 1.5×10⁻⁴, while total AMR cluster count and core AMR fraction were not significant. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] This **refines** the score-based interpretation by indicating that breadth across metal types, rather than simply more resistance genes, distinguishes broad-niche genera, although the genus-level association is correlational and does not establish causality. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

The score was normalized as metal clusters divided by annotated clusters, which controls for genome size but does not eliminate the possibility that metal-tolerance functions correlate with total metabolic complexity. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] This denominator is therefore not necessarily neutral: differences in annotation breadth can alter both the apparent functional numerator and the reference set used for normalization. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] Organisms with larger genomes or more broadly annotated gene clusters may receive different normalized scores because genome size, annotation coverage, and functional complexity are correlated. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

The MicrobeAtlas result **supports** an association beyond genome size but does not remove the same interpretive concern: after controlling for genome size, metal-type diversity remained associated with niche breadth at β = +0.0218, SE = 0.0061, p = 3.6×10⁻⁴, while log(genome size) also had a positive association of β = +0.0263, SE = 0.0069, p = 1.5×10⁻⁴. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] Thus, both studies require separation of metal-function effects from broader genomic and annotation complexity rather than treating a normalized or diversity score as a standalone causal measure. [src: bacdive_metal_validation, microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

The [[entities/metal-fitness-atlas]] **supports** the existence of between-species functional breadth while sharpening this caveat: its atlas combined 559 experiments across 31 organisms and 16 metals, but metal coverage was uneven and concentrations were not normalized by dose or tolerance threshold. [src: metal_fitness_atlas] Its genome-size-normalized repertoire score did not predict metal fitness, and the report therefore favors regulatory or expression-based models over simple presence/absence comparisons. [src: metal_fitness_atlas]

## Conserved and Specialized Functional Layers

The gene-resolution [[summaries/metal_cross_resistance__REPORT]] **supports** this layered interpretation. Across 8,162 metal-important genes, general-stress, metal-shared, and metal-specific tiers had mean pangenome core fractions of 92.0%, 91.0%, and 89.8%, respectively; the percentages fully core at at least 95% were 57.2%, 50.4%, and 45.7%. [src: metal_cross_resistance] Thus, broadly conserved functions coexist with a less-conserved layer of specialized metal functions that can differentiate species. [src: metal_cross_resistance]

The cross-resistance analysis identified 318 ortholog groups that were metal-shared in at least 2 organisms, with conserved families spanning cell-envelope, energy-metabolism, DNA-repair, and ion-homeostasis functions. [src: metal_cross_resistance] It therefore **refines** the score-based interpretation by separating general-stress, metal-shared, and metal-specific architectures rather than treating all metal-associated functions as equivalent. [src: metal_cross_resistance]

The metal-fitness atlas **supports** the general-stress component but **refines** the expected conservation pattern. Metal-important genes were more core than baseline in 21 of 22 organisms, while essential-metal tolerance genes for Fe, Mo, W, Se, and Mn had a mean core-fraction delta of +0.148 versus +0.081 for toxic metals (one-sided Mann-Whitney U: U=39, p=0.015). [src: metal_fitness_atlas] The strongest reported enrichments included manganese (+0.198, all 30 important genes core), zinc (+0.151), molybdenum (+0.148), tungsten (+0.145), and iron (+0.116), whereas cadmium had delta=-0.010 (p=0.92) and uranium had delta=+0.035 (p=0.34). [src: metal_fitness_atlas] This **supports** conserved cellular robustness, but the uneven coverage of rare metals means that some cross-species patterns largely reflect DvH and psRCH2 biology. [src: metal_fitness_atlas]

The MicrobeAtlas analysis **supports** this distinction at the genus level: metal-type diversity had intermediate phylogenetic signal, Pagel's λ = 0.335, compared with λ = 0.441 for core AMR fraction and λ = 0.260 for total AMR cluster count, whereas niche breadth had λ = 0.787. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] This pattern is consistent with conserved ecological and functional structure coexisting with more labile resistance repertoires, but the report attributes the architecture to a mixture of vertical inheritance and horizontal gene transfer rather than directly measuring within-species conservation. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

The metal-fitness atlas further **supports** the proposed two-tier model: its interpretation assigns most observed metal-important genes to conserved core functions such as envelope processes, DNA repair, central metabolism, protein quality control, and general stress response, while accessory efflux, sequestration, and detoxification functions form a specialized layer. [src: metal_fitness_atlas] It identified 149 conserved candidates without full functional annotation—89 truly unknown, 43 DUF/UPF, and 17 with partial hints—so the proposed species-divergent layer remains partly unresolved functionally. [src: metal_fitness_atlas]

The report proposes that metal-tolerance genes may be 88% core while species still vary in the total number of metal-tolerance genes represented in their core genomes. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] This hypothesis reconciles within-species conservation of shared functions with between-species divergence in the number and breadth of encoded functions, but the project did not directly test causality between core-genome size, metal-tolerance score, and contamination occupancy. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

## Genome Size, Annotation Breadth, and Ecological Interpretation

The genome-size mechanism is especially important for interpreting the smaller host-associated effect. Host-associated isolates had a metal-tolerance effect size of d=+0.14 with p<0.0001, whereas contamination categories ranged from d=+0.43 to d=+1.00. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] The report attributes the host-associated elevation as likely reflecting genome-size confounding because host-associated BacDive records were dominated by [[entities/pseudomonas]], [[entities/klebsiella]], and [[entities/acinetobacter-baumannii]] pathogens, which have larger genomes and more KEGG-annotated gene clusters. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] Genome-size normalization reduced but did not eliminate this possible effect. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] The smaller host-associated association therefore does not establish the same ecological interpretation as the stronger contamination-associated signals. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

The contamination results **support** an ecological metal-adaptation signal, but they do not show that it is independent of genome size, annotation breadth, or correlated metabolic complexity. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] The report proposes that species with larger core genomes encoding more metal-tolerance functions could score higher and be more likely to occur in contaminated environments. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] This remains a hypothesis rather than a direct causal test: normalized scores may combine metal adaptation with genome-scale and annotation-scale differences. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

A robust interpretation should distinguish three quantities: the absolute number of metal-tolerance clusters, the total number of annotated clusters used as the denominator, and the residual association with isolation environment after accounting for genome size and annotation breadth. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] This **refines** the use of the [[entities/metal-fitness-atlas]] score from a standalone ecological proxy toward a quantity requiring covariate-aware validation. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

The MicrobeAtlas study **supports** the need for this separation: the metal-type association persisted after adding pangenome coverage and in a three-covariate model including genome size and the number of sampled species per genus, but pangenome coverage itself was uneven and correlated with inferred metal-type diversity at r = 0.35 with p = 2.6×10⁻¹⁹. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] Its strict 5% prevalence analysis retained a positive coefficient but was not significant (β = +0.0166, SE = 0.0099, p = 0.092), showing that detection and sampling choices can affect the apparent ecological breadth. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

## Phylum-Stratified Support and Limits

The contamination signal persisted within [[entities/pseudomonadota]], where contamination isolates had metal scores delta=+0.040 above environmental isolates (p<0.001; n=85 contamination and n=5,655 environmental), and within [[entities/actinobacteria]], where the delta was +0.035 (p<0.001; n=62 and n=772, respectively). [src: bacdive_metal_validation] These results **support** functional divergence beyond broad phylum composition in the two most-sampled phyla, but they do not by themselves remove genome-size or annotation-breadth confounding. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

The signal was not significant within [[entities/bacillota-b]], where delta=-0.012 and p=0.285 (n=19 contamination and n=492 environmental), or [[entities/bacteroidota]], where delta=-0.008 and p=0.456 (n=5 and n=156, respectively). [src: bacdive_metal_validation] These null results cannot distinguish biological differences from limited statistical power because contamination-isolate sample sizes were small. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

## Tensions

There is a validation-scale **tension** between the positive BacDive association and the cross-resistance study's null species-scale test. BacDive found Cohen's d = +1.00 for heavy-metal contamination in n=10 isolates and a pangenome-scale Metal Fitness Atlas result of Cohen's d = +1.0 across 42K strains. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] In contrast, the cross-resistance study found no correlation between multi-metal tolerance scores and BacDive metal-environment isolation at Fitness Browser species scale (Spearman rho approximately -0.02, p > 0.8); after matching and collapsing strains, it retained 20 independent species and judged the test underpowered. [src: metal_cross_resistance] These results do not establish whether the discrepancy reflects scale, matching, phenotype definition, or sampling. [src: metal_cross_resistance]

The metal-fitness atlas **supports** the conservation side of this tension through its 87.4% core fraction, but it also **refines** the ecological interpretation: the broad metal-important definition (fit < -1 OR n_sick ≥ 1) captures general stress functions as well as metal-specific resistance, and the simple repertoire score failed to predict fitness. [src: metal_fitness_atlas] Thus, core conservation does not by itself validate normalized environmental scores or imply that gene presence predicts metal tolerance. [src: metal_fitness_atlas]

The MicrobeAtlas result **supports** the positive ecological-association side but does not resolve this tension: metal-type diversity predicted broader inferred niche breadth after phylogenetic adjustment, yet the analysis used genus-level occurrence proxies rather than direct metal-exposure phenotypes. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] Its groundwater validation was also mixed: metal-type diversity correlated with groundwater prevalence (Spearman ρ = +0.112, p = 0.0019), but groundwater-specific fold-enrichment was null (ρ = +0.042, p = 0.242). [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] These findings therefore strengthen the broader-range association without demonstrating a uniquely metal-contamination-driven mechanism. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

The metal-cross-resistance study **supports** the conservation component through broadly positive gene-level responses across 28 organisms, but its organisms were not phylogenetically independent. [src: metal_cross_resistance] Its gene-level conservation analysis does not directly measure within-species environmental occupancy, so it strengthens the proposed architecture without establishing the causal link left open by the BacDive study. [src: metal_cross_resistance] PGLS (phylogenetic generalized least squares) or independent contrasts would strengthen the conservation claim. [src: metal_cross_resistance]

The heavy-metal estimate is also imprecise: n=10 matched isolates produced an observed d=1.00 that barely exceeded the approximately d=0.93 minimum detectable effect size reported for 80% power with n=10 heavy-metal isolates versus approximately 5,000 environmental-baseline strains. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] The contamination gradient therefore supports the layered model, but the strongest estimate requires replication and explicit adjustment for genome size, annotated-cluster count, taxonomy, and sampling source. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

The BacDive bridge creates an additional evidence limitation. Of 97,334 strains, 55,107 (56.6%) were unmatched, primarily because GTDB species boundaries differed from LPSN/DSMZ-based naming; the observed pattern may therefore not represent all described or environmental bacteria. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] BacDive represents culturable, described strains rather than the full diversity of environmental bacteria and may under-represent metal-tolerant extremophiles. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] This incomplete [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] bridge can also change which genome-size and annotation-breadth classes enter the analysis. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

The interpretation connects to [[concepts/environmental-resistome]] because species-level conservation and divergence can be obscured when strain-level variation and environmental sampling are treated as interchangeable. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

## Open Directions

- Match BacDive GCA accessions directly to [[entities/kbase-ke-pangenome]] genome IDs and test whether the environmental score gradient persists after recovering part of the 56.6% unmatched set; test whether matching failure is associated with genome size or isolation environment. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- Compare within-species strain variation and between-species score differences using accession-linked pangenome genomes, and test whether species-level divergence explains more variance than strain-level heterogeneity. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- Refit the BacDive environment comparisons with genome size, annotated-cluster count, taxonomic group, and culture-source category as covariates; separate absolute metal-tolerance cluster counts from denominator effects and test whether the contamination association remains. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- Compare absolute metal-tolerance cluster counts with normalized scores across the same matched strains and test whether ecological rankings change when annotation breadth is held constant. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- Expand matched metal phenotypes beyond the 24 existing utilization records and current metabolite-utilization records with MIC and growth-inhibition data; test whether measured phenotypes and specific metal-tolerance gene families track normalized scores independently of genome and annotation breadth. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- Integrate [[entities/enigma-coral]] community data from the Oak Ridge metal-contaminated site and compare field distributions or abundance associations with genome-size-adjusted, species-level pangenome scores. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- Apply phylogenetic independent contrasts or PGLS to the 28-organism cross-resistance dataset and compare gene-tier conservation with species-level environmental scores. [src: metal_cross_resistance]
- Map cross-resistance gene signatures with KEGG/PFAM across 27K species and test whether pangenome-scale validation resolves the species-scale null result. [src: metal_cross_resistance]
- Reanalyze the MicrobeAtlas association with finer environmental categories, alternative phylogenetic trees, and genus-level rarefaction curves; test whether metal-type diversity predicts direct metal-exposure phenotypes rather than sequencing-effort proxies. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]
- Reanalyze the Metal Fitness Atlas after excluding genes important under non-metal stresses, normalize exposure by concentration relative to organism-specific tolerance thresholds, and test whether core conservation and species-level ecological associations remain. [src: metal_fitness_atlas]
- Functionally characterize the 149 conserved metal-fitness candidates using PaperBLAST, InterPro, and structural prediction, then test whether candidates explain within-species conservation or between-species metal-response differences. [src: metal_fitness_atlas]
