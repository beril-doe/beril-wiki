# Resistance-Island Co-inheritance and Mechanistic Linkage

Resistance islands are recurring groups of antimicrobial-resistance (AMR) genes that co-occur within strains, providing a way to study whether resistance mechanisms are inherited as linked modules rather than independently. In the analyzed pangenome resource, co-occurrence indicates genomic linkage or shared lineage history, but it does not by itself establish co-selection, functional synergy, or physical location on a particular mobile element. [^amr_strain_variation]

## Evidence from Within-Species AMR Variation

The study detected 1,517 resistance islands across 705 species, representing 54% of the species analyzed. [^amr_strain_variation] Islands had a mean size of 6.2 genes, a median size of 4 genes, a maximum size of 43 genes, and a mean phi coefficient of 0.827. [^amr_strain_variation] The phi coefficient measures pairwise co-occurrence, so the mean value indicates very tight co-inheritance among genes assigned to these islands. [^amr_strain_variation]

Of the 1,517 islands, 1,343 (88%) contained genes from multiple resistance mechanisms. [^amr_strain_variation] Efflux pumps occurred in 954 islands and enzymatic inactivation occurred in 698 islands, making them the most common components reported in the analysis. [^amr_strain_variation] The complete mechanism counts were 1,026 for Other/Unclassified, 954 for Efflux, 698 for Enzymatic inactivation, 694 for Oxidoreductase, 502 for Regulatory, 341 for Beta-lactamase, 293 for Target modification, and 137 for Cell wall modification. [^amr_strain_variation]

The prevalence of multi-mechanism islands supports the interpretation that resistance genes can be inherited as coordinated defense modules spanning multiple drug classes. [^amr_strain_variation] This evidence **supports** [cofitness-network-architecture](cofitness-network-architecture.md) by showing that strong AMR gene co-occurrence can form a structured network of tightly linked modules rather than only isolated pairwise associations. [^amr_strain_variation] The result remains an association: genes may be linked on the same mobile genetic element or maintained by lineage history without providing functional synergy or being jointly selected. [^amr_strain_variation]

The broader pangenome atlas **refines** this interpretation by showing that AMR is generally more accessory than the surrounding genome: only 30.3% of AMR genes were core versus 46.8% for the pangenome baseline, while the auxiliary genome was 2.2x enriched for AMR (33.6% versus 15.3%). [^amr_pangenome_atlas] In a paired test of 4,252 species with at least 5 AMR clusters, 63.7% had AMR less core than their species baseline (Wilcoxon p=1.1e-130, mean difference -0.102). [^amr_pangenome_atlas] These results **support** the possibility that some resistance islands represent mobile or variably inherited modules, but they do not establish physical linkage for the statistically defined islands. [^amr_pangenome_atlas]

The prophage-AMR analysis **refines** this distinction by providing partial genomic-context evidence without demonstrating mechanistic mobilization. Across 1,953 genomes sampled from 100 species, 20,073 of 36,041 AMR gene instances (55.7%) were on contigs carrying strict prophage markers, but only 1,991 (5.5%) were within 5 genes of a marker and the median nearest-marker distance was 34 genes. [^prophage_amr_comobilization] AMR genes within 10 genes were 67.6% accessory versus 65.5% for distal genes; the odds ratio was 1.10 with one-sided p=0.005 and bootstrap 95% confidence interval [1.024, 1.185], a statistically significant but modest 2.1 percentage point difference. [^prophage_amr_comobilization] The threshold-dependent odds ratios—0.78 at 3 genes, 0.92 at 5 genes, 1.10 at 10 genes, 1.19 at 15 genes, and 1.28 at 50 genes—together with a median species-level odds ratio of 0.85 show heterogeneous proximity effects rather than uniform physical co-inheritance. [^prophage_amr_comobilization] This **supports** the existing interpretation that accessory status and statistical co-occurrence are insufficient to establish resistance-island structure or phage-mediated transfer.

## Relationship to Phylogeny and Acquisition

AMR repertoire similarity was significantly associated with phylogenetic similarity in 701 of 1,261 species (55.6%) tested using Mantel tests at FDR < 0.05, where FDR is the false-discovery rate. [^amr_strain_variation] The median Mantel r for all AMR genes was 0.247, and 87.8% of species showed positive correlation between ANI distance and AMR Jaccard distance. [^amr_strain_variation] These results indicate that resistance-island distributions can reflect stable lineage-associated inheritance in addition to recent horizontal acquisition. [^amr_strain_variation]

Non-core, putatively acquired AMR genes had a median Mantel r of 0.222, compared with 0.117 for core, intrinsic genes; the paired t-test gave t = -8.35, p = 7.0e-16, and n = 489. [^amr_strain_variation] This finding **supports** the hypothesis that acquired resistance elements can become stably maintained and vertically transmitted within lineages. [^amr_strain_variation] However, the comparison is partly constrained by the low Jaccard-distance variance of near-universal core genes, so the stronger non-core signal cannot be interpreted as a purely biological measure of island inheritance. [^amr_strain_variation]

The atlas **supports** the intrinsic-acquired distinction at larger scale: beta-lactamases were 54.9% core, whereas regulatory genes were 6.5% core; blaTEM, tet(C), and ant(2'')-Ia were 0% core, while intrinsic efflux pumps such as emhABC were >95% core. [^amr_pangenome_atlas] This pattern is consistent with vertically inherited intrinsic resistance being more stable than many acquired genes, but the atlas also cautions that AMRFinderPlus includes stress-response genes alongside classical antibiotic-resistance genes. [^amr_pangenome_atlas]

The environmental-resistome analysis **refines** this lineage-focused interpretation: clinical species had a median of 5 AMR gene clusters versus 2 for soil, aquatic, and host-associated species, while accessory AMR comprised 68% in clinical species versus 43% in soil species. [^amr_environmental_resistome] Environment effects also remained significant within 5 of 6 major phyla and in 20 of 141 testable families, supporting an ecological contribution beyond phylogeny while leaving most families insufficiently resolved. [^amr_environmental_resistome] These are species-level ecological associations, not evidence that the observed resistance islands are physically linked or co-selected.

The atlas **supports** this ecological qualification: human/Clinical species carried 10.6 AMR clusters per species, compared with 4.6 for Soil/Terrestrial, 3.9 for Aquatic, and 3.0 for Animal, with a significant Kruskal-Wallis test (H=440, p=7.0e-93). [^amr_pangenome_atlas] Clinical AMR was less core (30.8%) than soil AMR (58.1%) or plant AMR (63.1%), consistent with greater representation of acquired resistance in clinical settings, although the comparison remains observational. [^amr_pangenome_atlas] Among 2,684 species with at least 3 genomes and AlphaEarth embeddings, environmental diversity predicted AMR count (Spearman rho=0.466, p=1.6e-144), while environmental diversity and AMR core fraction were negatively correlated (rho=-0.173, p=1.8e-19); these associations do not identify island-level co-selection. [^amr_pangenome_atlas]

At the species level, the prophage-AMR analysis **supports** a broader mobile-element or acquisition-context explanation while retaining the phylogenetic caveat: prophage marker density correlated with AMR repertoire breadth (Spearman rho=0.572, p<10^-300, n=4,770 species), with partial Spearman rho=0.464 after controlling for genome count (p=1.0×10^-253). [^prophage_amr_comobilization] The association was significant across Pseudomonadota (rho=0.54), Bacillota_A (rho=0.55), Bacillota (rho=0.40), Bacteroidota (rho=0.59), and Actinomycetota (rho=0.29). [^prophage_amr_comobilization] This cross-phylogenetic pattern is consistent with prophage-mediated transfer or shared recombination potential, but it does not distinguish those mechanisms from species with open pangenomes independently accumulating both prophages and AMR genes. [^prophage_amr_comobilization]

## Mechanistic Interpretation and Limits

The 88% prevalence of multi-mechanism islands suggests the hypothesis that resistance modules may provide broad protection against multiple drug classes or may be co-maintained because their genes share genomic context. [^amr_strain_variation] The environmental analysis **supports** ecological co-exposure as one plausible contributor to that maintenance: metal resistance represented 45.0% of AMR in aquatic species and 44.0% in soil species, whereas target modification represented 43.6% in human-gut species. [^amr_environmental_resistome] However, that analysis is observational and does not establish island-level co-selection; the available co-occurrence analysis likewise does not distinguish among physical linkage, ecological co-exposure, clonal expansion, co-selection, and direct functional interaction. [^amr_strain_variation] Consequently, the evidence supports mechanistic linkage as a testable model, but does not establish that the linked genes act synergistically. [^amr_strain_variation]

The prophage analysis **supports** this caution: prophage density tracks AMR breadth at species scale, but proximity to prophage markers has only a modest and heterogeneous relationship with AMR accessory status. [^prophage_amr_comobilization] Prophage markers were identified by keyword and Pfam matching in bakta_annotations rather than dedicated prophage-prediction tools, and gene distances were parsed from ordinal gene positions rather than base-pair coordinates; false-positive markers and inaccurate distances therefore limit mechanistic inference. [^prophage_amr_comobilization] The 100-species proximity analysis also sampled 20 genomes per species rather than all 293K genomes. [^prophage_amr_comobilization] The report's attempted fitness comparison could not be tested because the BERDL fitness browser contains RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing) data for only 48 model organisms with poor overlap to the GTDB species analyzed. [^prophage_amr_comobilization]

The atlas **refines** the mechanism evidence by showing that 18,448 of 83,008 AMRFinderPlus hits (22.2%) were Other/Unclassified and that mechanism labels were assigned by keyword matching against product descriptions rather than CARD ARO terms. [^amr_pangenome_atlas] Thus, both island-level mechanism composition and any inferred cross-mechanism linkage should be treated as annotation-sensitive; the atlas also reports that 55.4% of sparsely annotated one-source clusters were singletons versus 34.6% of two-source clusters. [^amr_pangenome_atlas]

The report specifically identifies genomic-context mapping to plasmids, chromosomes, integron boundaries, and insertion sequences as a next step for resolving the mechanism of island formation and maintenance. [^amr_strain_variation] Such analyses would connect the statistical islands to physical genomic structures and help distinguish horizontally transferred cassettes from chromosome-linked lineage signatures. [^amr_strain_variation]

## Relation to Other Concepts

This concept **refines** [resistance-island-coinheritance](resistance-island-coinheritance.md) by separating the observed statistical co-inheritance of resistance genes from the stronger, unproven claim of functional co-selection. [^amr_strain_variation] It also **supports** [environmental-resistome](environmental-resistome.md) because non-core AMR genes showed a stronger phylogenetic signal than core AMR genes, while retaining the report's statistical caveat. [^amr_strain_variation] The findings connect to [environmental-resistome](environmental-resistome.md) because environmental or host-associated selection could contribute to island maintenance, but the present co-occurrence results do not establish that causal pathway. [^amr_strain_variation] The environmental-resistome study **supports** this ecological link at the broader species level while showing that mechanism composition varies by environment, without resolving island-level causation. [^amr_environmental_resistome] The pangenome atlas **supports** the same distinction by linking clinical AMR enrichment to lower core fractions while warning that taxonomic, annotation, and sampling biases limit causal interpretation. [^amr_pangenome_atlas]

The prophage-AMR study **supports** [phage-defense-syndromes-and-arms-race](phage-defense-syndromes-and-arms-race.md) by adding a cross-species association between prophage-marker density and AMR breadth, while its modest proximity effect and marker-identification caveats argue against treating prophage markers as proof of resistance-island mobilization. [^prophage_amr_comobilization] It also **refines** [pangenome-integration](pangenome-integration.md) by linking AMR clusters, prophage markers, gene neighborhoods, species-level pangenomes, and taxonomy, while exposing the need for sequence-resolved validation. [^prophage_amr_comobilization]

## Open Directions

- Use the 1,517 island records together with genome assemblies and genomic-context mapping to plasmids, chromosomes, integron boundaries, and insertion sequences; determine which islands are physically linked and which are lineage-level co-occurrence patterns. [^amr_strain_variation]
- Apply geNomad or PHASTER to BERDL genomes and use scaffold sequences for base-pair-resolution distances; test whether strict prophage proximity, rather than contig co-occurrence, predicts AMR accessory status. [^prophage_amr_comobilization]
- Combine island membership with curated exposure metadata and phylogeny-aware models; test whether multi-mechanism islands are co-selected under shared antimicrobial environments rather than merely inherited together. [^amr_strain_variation]
- Integrate resistance-island genes with virulence-factor profiles and metabolic pathway variation; test whether island composition predicts host-associated phenotypes or fitness beyond lineage background. [^amr_strain_variation]
- Apply perturbation or competition experiments to representative multi-mechanism islands; test whether linked resistance genes produce additive, synergistic, or independent protection across drug classes. [^amr_strain_variation]
- Use the reported phi coefficients with fitness and genomic-context data in predictive models; test whether high co-inheritance predicts future AMR gene co-acquisition. [^amr_strain_variation]
- Revisit fitness-cost comparisons as fitness-browser coverage expands, and separate prophage, plasmid, and integrative-conjugative-element mobilization in the same genomes. [^prophage_amr_comobilization]
- Stratify physical-island and co-selection analyses by the environmental categories and mechanism profiles reported in the 293K-genome resistome dataset; test whether clinical, soil, and aquatic contexts predict distinct island architectures after phylogenetic control. [^amr_environmental_resistome]
- Reclassify island genes with CARD ARO mappings and test whether the clinical-versus-environmental core-fraction contrast persists after separating antibiotic resistance from metal and other stress-response genes. [^amr_pangenome_atlas]
