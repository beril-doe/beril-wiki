# Scale-Dependent Mobile-Element Associations and Mobilization Inference

Mobile-element associations can change with the genomic scale at which they are measured: local gene proximity, contig-level co-occurrence, and species-level repertoire breadth do not provide equivalent evidence for mobilization. The [prophage_amr_comobilization__REPORT](../summaries/prophage_amr_comobilization__REPORT.md) shows a modest and heterogeneous local association between antimicrobial-resistance (AMR) genes and prophage markers, but a substantially stronger species-level association between prophage-marker density and AMR repertoire breadth. [^prophage_amr_comobilization] The [amr_strain_variation__REPORT](../summaries/amr_strain_variation__REPORT.md) **refines** this framework by showing that AMR genes also form tightly co-inherited, frequently multi-mechanism resistance islands, without establishing which mobile element mobilizes them. [^amr_strain_variation] The [amr_pangenome_atlas__REPORT](../summaries/amr_pangenome_atlas__REPORT.md) **supports** treating accessory AMR as a broad pangenome pattern rather than a prophage-specific signature: across 27,690 pangenome species, AMR was depleted from the core genome and enriched in the auxiliary genome, while clinical AMR was less core than soil or plant AMR. [^amr_pangenome_atlas] The [amr_cofitness_networks__REPORT](../summaries/amr_cofitness_networks__REPORT.md) **refines** this scale framework by showing that AMR genes also occupy large, organism-shaped fitness-support networks; these are evidence of shared fitness phenotypes, not evidence that a mobile element physically links or transfers the genes. [^amr_cofitness_networks]

## Evidence Across Scales

### Gene and contig scale

Across the full pangenome inventory, 83,008 AMR gene clusters and 3,465,244 broad prophage marker clusters were identified, including 1,261,929 strict prophage-marker clusters. [^prophage_amr_comobilization] Prophage markers were 83.8% accessory and 53.8% singleton, whereas AMR genes were 69.7% accessory and 36.1% singleton. [^prophage_amr_comobilization]

The pan-bacterial AMR atlas independently found 83,008 AMRFinderPlus hits on gene-cluster representatives across 27,690 species, spanning 82,908 distinct clusters and 14,723 species; 53.2% of species carried at least one AMR hit. [^amr_pangenome_atlas] It **supports** the existing accessory-genome interpretation: only 30.3% of AMR genes were core versus 46.8% for the pangenome baseline, and the auxiliary genome was 2.2x enriched for AMR (33.6% versus 15.3%). [^amr_pangenome_atlas] In 4,252 species with at least 5 AMR clusters, 63.7% had AMR less core than their species baseline (Wilcoxon p=1.1e-130, mean difference -0.102). [^amr_pangenome_atlas] This broad conservation signal does not identify the mobile carrier, so it **refines** rather than replaces the local prophage evidence.

Among 36,041 AMR gene instances from 1,953 genomes sampled across 100 species, 20,073 (55.7%) occurred on contigs carrying strict prophage markers. [^prophage_amr_comobilization] The same analysis found that 12,026 (33.4%) were within 50 genes of a prophage marker, 7,137 (19.8%) were within 20 genes, 3,731 (10.4%) were within 10 genes, and 1,991 (5.5%) were within 5 genes; the median distance to the nearest prophage marker among co-localized AMR genes was 34 genes. [^prophage_amr_comobilization]

At a 10-gene threshold, AMR genes near prophage markers were 67.6% accessory, compared with 65.5% for distal AMR genes. [^prophage_amr_comobilization] Fisher's exact test gave an odds ratio of 1.10, one-sided p=0.005, and a bootstrap 95% confidence interval of [1.024, 1.185]. [^prophage_amr_comobilization] The statistically significant association was therefore modest, corresponding to a 2.1 percentage point difference in accessory fraction. [^prophage_amr_comobilization]

The local association was threshold-dependent rather than uniformly positive: the odds ratio was 0.78 at 3 genes, 0.92 at 5 genes, 1.10 at 10 genes, 1.19 at 15 genes, and 1.28 at 50 genes. [^prophage_amr_comobilization] Only 33 of 74 testable species had species-level odds ratios above 1, and the median species-level odds ratio was 0.85. [^prophage_amr_comobilization] This **refines** a simple proximity-based mobilization interpretation because broader windows produced stronger aggregate associations while the species-level pattern remained inconsistent. [^prophage_amr_comobilization]

The reversal at very close range may reflect the presence of core phage components immediately adjacent to phage structural genes, whereas broader windows may capture genomic islands containing both prophage remnants and laterally transferred genes. [^prophage_amr_comobilization] These mechanisms were interpretations rather than direct tests of mobilization. [^prophage_amr_comobilization]

The AMR strain-variation analysis **supports** the importance of genomic linkage beyond prophage-specific proximity: it detected 1,517 resistance islands across 705 species, with a mean size of 6.2 genes, a median size of 4 genes, a maximum size of 43 genes, and a mean phi coefficient of 0.827. [^amr_strain_variation] Here phi measures pairwise co-occurrence, so the result indicates tight co-inheritance, not demonstrated transfer. [^amr_strain_variation] Of these islands, 1,343/1,517 (88%) contained genes from multiple resistance mechanisms; efflux pumps occurred in 954 islands and enzymatic inactivation in 698. [^amr_strain_variation] This **refines** interpretation of prophage-proximal AMR because tightly linked AMR modules may reside on resistance islands associated with plasmids, chromosomes, or other mobile elements, and co-occurrence does not establish co-selection or functional synergy. [^amr_strain_variation]

The atlas **refines** the core/accessory comparison by showing that conservation depends on resistance biology: beta-lactamases were 54.9% core, intrinsic emhABC efflux pumps were >95% core, while regulatory genes were 6.5% core and blaTEM, tet(C), and ant(2'')-Ia were 0% core. [^amr_pangenome_atlas] Thus, an AMR gene's accessory status can indicate acquired resistance, but accessory status alone cannot establish prophage association or mobilization.

The cofitness analysis **supports** separating functional association from physical linkage. Across 28 organisms, 801 AMR genes with fitness data, and independent component analysis (ICA), which decomposes condition-dependent fitness profiles into modules, 769 (96%) AMR genes had at least one extra-operon cofitness partner at |r| > 0.3; the mean support-network sizes were 233 genes at |r| > 0.3, 110 at |r| > 0.4, and 71 at |r| > 0.5. [^amr_cofitness_networks] AMR-containing ICA modules had a median of 46 genes versus 27 for non-AMR modules (MWU p = 1.7×10⁻⁸), and 208/209 AMR gene-module assignments were in cross-organism conserved module families. [^amr_cofitness_networks] These findings identify broad shared-fitness neighborhoods rather than contig adjacency, resistance-island membership, or a demonstrated mobile carrier. [^amr_cofitness_networks]

### Species and repertoire scale

Across 4,770 species, prophage marker density was positively associated with AMR repertoire breadth, with Spearman rho=0.572, p<10^-300, and n=4,770 species. [^prophage_amr_comobilization] A log-log regression had a slope of 0.823 (SE=0.018), p<10^-300, and R²=0.30; the report states that a 10-fold increase in prophage density predicts a ~6.6-fold increase in AMR breadth. [^prophage_amr_comobilization] After controlling for genome count, the partial Spearman correlation remained rho=0.464 with p=1.0×10^-253. [^prophage_amr_comobilization]

The species-level association was significant across the five reported major phyla: Pseudomonadota (rho=0.54), Bacillota_A (rho=0.55), Bacillota (rho=0.40), Bacteroidota (rho=0.59), and Actinomycetota (rho=0.29). [^prophage_amr_comobilization] This cross-phylogenetic consistency **supports** an association that is not explained solely by the reported phylum groupings, but it does not establish phage-mediated AMR transfer. [^prophage_amr_comobilization]

The environmental-resistome analysis **supports** the existing alternative that shared ecological or sampling-associated propensity can contribute to species-level AMR breadth: across 14,723 species and 293K genomes, clinical species had a median of 5 AMR gene clusters versus 2 for soil, aquatic, and host-associated species, and environment effects persisted in 5 of 6 tested major phyla and in 20 of 141 testable families. [^amr_environmental_resistome] It **refines** interpretation of the prophage-density correlation because clinical and human-gut species had predominantly accessory AMR, whereas soil and aquatic species had more metal-resistance AMR; these environment-linked repertoires could covary with prophage density without demonstrating phage mobilization. [^amr_environmental_resistome]

The atlas **supports** this ecological-confounding alternative: human/Clinical species carried 10.6 AMR clusters per species versus 4.6 for Soil/Terrestrial, 3.9 for Aquatic, and 3.0 for Animal, with Kruskal-Wallis H=440 and p=7.0e-93. [^amr_pangenome_atlas] Clinical AMR was 30.8% core versus 58.1% for soil and 63.1% for plant AMR. [^amr_pangenome_atlas] Among 2,684 species with at least 3 genomes and AlphaEarth embeddings, environmental diversity correlated with AMR count (Spearman rho=0.466, p=1.6e-144) and negatively with AMR core fraction (rho=-0.173, p=1.8e-19). [^amr_pangenome_atlas] These associations strengthen the need to control for environment and sampling before interpreting prophage density as a mobilization driver.

The AMR strain-variation analysis **supports** retaining lineage and ecological confounding as alternatives: among 37,444 AMR gene-species records, 51.3% were rare, 41.3% variable, and 7.5% fixed, while 701/1,261 species (55.6%) showed significant AMR–ANI distance correlation at FDR < 0.05. [^amr_strain_variation] Its host-associated species carried more AMR genes per genome than terrestrial or aquatic species under both approximate environment-classification approaches, with Kruskal-Wallis p < 0.05. [^amr_strain_variation] These results **refine** the species-level prophage interpretation: lineage structure and host-associated sampling can organize AMR breadth independently of direct phage-mediated transfer.

The cofitness analysis **supports** retaining condition-dependent shared dispensability as another alternative to mobilization. Among AMR-containing support neighborhoods, flagellar motility, flagellum assembly, chemotaxis, and amino-acid biosynthesis were enriched in multiple organisms, but the analysis could not distinguish shared condition-dependent dispensability from shared regulation. [^amr_cofitness_networks] Its support-network organization was more organism-specific than mechanism-specific: mean GO-term Jaccard similarity was 0.375 for different AMR mechanisms within an organism versus 0.207 for the same mechanism across organisms (MWU p = 4.3×10⁻¹³). [^amr_cofitness_networks] This **refines** the interpretation of species-level prophage associations by showing that organismal regulatory and metabolic architecture can structure AMR-related functional associations without identifying a transfer route. [^amr_cofitness_networks]

The species-level result is compatible with two non-exclusive explanations: prophages may mobilize resistance genes through specialized or generalized transduction, and species with high recombination potential may independently acquire genes from multiple mobile elements. [^prophage_amr_comobilization] Because the analysis is correlational, the result cannot distinguish direct phage-mediated transfer from shared ecological, genomic, or recombination-related propensity. [^prophage_amr_comobilization] The environmental-resistome evidence **supports** retaining this distinction rather than treating accessory AMR as a specific signature of prophage transfer. [^amr_environmental_resistome] The resistance-island evidence further **supports** this caution because co-inherited multi-mechanism islands were widespread, but their genomic carrier and transfer mechanism were not resolved. [^amr_strain_variation] The cofitness evidence **supports** the same caution at a functional scale: cofitness indicates shared fitness phenotypes, not direct transcriptional control or physical mobilization. [^amr_cofitness_networks]

## What Each Scale Can Support

Contig-level co-occurrence establishes that AMR genes and strict prophage markers can occupy the same contig in the analyzed genomes, but it does not by itself establish that a prophage carries, excises with, or transfers an AMR gene. [^prophage_amr_comobilization] Gene-window results provide a scale-sensitive association whose magnitude changes from an odds ratio of 0.78 at 3 genes to 1.28 at 50 genes, so the selected distance threshold materially affects the inferred relationship. [^prophage_amr_comobilization]

Species-level density and repertoire correlations capture broader accumulation patterns than local neighborhoods and produced the strongest association in this report. [^prophage_amr_comobilization] However, those correlations cannot separate phage mobilization from plasmid or integrative-conjugative-element mobilization, shared recombination capacity, or other causes of joint AMR and prophage accumulation. [^prophage_amr_comobilization] This **complements** the environmental-resistome finding that ecological environment is associated with AMR diversity and core/accessory composition, while its observational design likewise cannot establish causation. [^amr_environmental_resistome]

The atlas **supports** interpreting species-level breadth as a composite of resistance biology, taxonomy, and environment: AMR clusters were enriched in defense mechanisms and inorganic-ion transport, and the five most abundant families included mercury- and arsenic-resistance genes as well as beta-lactamases. [^amr_pangenome_atlas] Because AMRFinderPlus includes stress-response genes alongside classical antibiotic-resistance genes, broad AMR counts may not measure antibiotic resistance narrowly. [^amr_pangenome_atlas]

Resistance-island co-inheritance provides an intermediate module-level signal: it identifies linked AMR mechanisms but does not identify the carrier element or prove co-selection. [^amr_strain_variation] This **refines** the interpretation of contig and species-scale associations by separating evidence for linkage from evidence for mobilization.

Cofitness provides a distinct functional scale between individual genes and organism-wide repertoires. Its large support neighborhoods and conserved ICA modules **support** condition-linked functional coupling, but the enrichment for flagellar and biosynthetic functions may reflect genes that are jointly dispensable in shaken laboratory cultures or supplemented media rather than genes that are co-regulated or co-mobilized. [^amr_cofitness_networks] Network size was not associated with AMR fitness cost (Spearman rho = −0.006, p = 0.87, N = 769), so functional-network breadth does not provide a proxy for the cost or transferability of resistance. [^amr_cofitness_networks]

This distinction **complements** [environmental-resistome](environmental-resistome.md), where AMR distributions are interpreted across genomic and ecological contexts, and it **refines** [pangenome-integration](pangenome-integration.md) by showing that integrated pangenome signals depend on whether evidence is summarized at the neighborhood, contig, or species level. [^prophage_amr_comobilization] It also connects to [phage-defense-syndromes-and-arms-race](phage-defense-syndromes-and-arms-race.md) because keyword and Pfam-based prophage-marker identification may include phage-defense systems and may miss divergent prophages. [^prophage_amr_comobilization]

## Measurement and Inference Limits

Distances were calculated from ordinal gene positions parsed from gene_id formats rather than base-pair coordinates, so the reported gene distances may differ from true genomic distances. [^prophage_amr_comobilization] Prophage markers were identified by keyword and Pfam matching in bakta_annotations rather than by dedicated prophage-prediction tools such as PHASTER or geNomad, which may cause false positives, including phage-defense systems, and may miss divergent prophages. [^prophage_amr_comobilization]

The co-localization analysis sampled 20 genomes per species for the 100-species analysis rather than examining all 293K genomes, so the local association may change under exhaustive genome-level analysis. [^prophage_amr_comobilization] Core and accessory labels depended on species-level pangenome calling with motupan, meaning that the same gene can have different conservation status in different species. [^prophage_amr_comobilization]

The environmental-resistome analysis further cautions that NCBI sampling overrepresents clinical isolates, majority-vote environment labels collapse within-species variation, 18.7% of AMR clusters lacked mechanism assignments, and its clinical-environment association was observational. [^amr_environmental_resistome] These limitations **refine** the interpretation of species-level mobile-element associations by adding environment classification, coverage, and annotation as possible confounders. [^amr_environmental_resistome] The AMR strain-variation analysis likewise found that 52.7% of genomes had no classifiable isolation_source and that dedicated environment curation was needed for ecotype analysis. [^amr_strain_variation]

The atlas adds that only 7,838 of 14,723 AMR-carrying species (53.2%) received a non-Other/Unknown environment classification, while the Other/Unknown bin contained 46.8% of species; AlphaEarth embeddings covered only 28% of genomes. [^amr_pangenome_atlas] This **supports** treating environmental AMR–prophage comparisons as coverage-limited. Its mechanism classification used keyword matching rather than CARD ARO terms, leaving 18,448 hits (22.2%) Other/Unclassified. [^amr_pangenome_atlas]

AMR detection in the strain-variation analysis relied on the AMRFinderPlus database, so novel resistance mechanisms absent from that database were missed. [^amr_strain_variation] This **refines** the interpretation of resistance-island counts and AMR breadth because apparent absence or rarity can reflect annotation scope as well as biology. The atlas likewise reported that 51.5% of hits were detected by HMM, 22.7% by BLASTP, 13.0% by EXACTP, 9.7% by PARTIALP, and 3.0% by ALLELEP, while singleton inflation may reflect annotation artifacts. [^amr_pangenome_atlas]

Cofitness interpretation has parallel limitations: cofitness is not equivalent to co-regulation, and the analysis treated missing fitness values as zero in z-score space through `np.nan_to_num`, which approximates but does not equal pairwise-complete Pearson correlation. [^amr_cofitness_networks] At |r| > 0.3, the mean support network contained 233 genes and therefore included many weak associations; confirmation at |r| > 0.4 was needed. [^amr_cofitness_networks] The 28 organisms were lab-adapted, phylogenetically biased, included many Pseudomonas organisms, and had limited ecological diversity. [^amr_cofitness_networks] These limitations **support** treating cofitness as functional context rather than evidence for a mobile carrier or ecological mobilization mechanism. [^amr_cofitness_networks]

The proposed fitness comparison could not be tested because the BERDL fitness browser contained RB-TnSeq, random barcode transposon sequencing, data for only 48 model organisms, with poor overlap with the GTDB pangenome species analyzed here. [^prophage_amr_comobilization] Whether prophage-proximal AMR genes have distinct fitness costs therefore remains unresolved. [^prophage_amr_comobilization] The atlas **refines** this gap rather than closing it: among 37 Fitness Browser organisms with AMR genes, AMR genes had median fitness -0.007 versus -0.012 for the non-AMR baseline, but the predominantly environmental organisms and laboratory conditions do not test recently acquired mobile resistance in clinical pathogens. [^amr_pangenome_atlas]

## Open Directions

- Apply geNomad or PHASTER to BERDL genomes and compare dedicated prophage calls with keyword/Pfam calls to quantify false positives, missed divergent prophages, and the contribution of phage-defense systems to the observed associations. [^prophage_amr_comobilization]
- Recompute AMR–prophage distances from scaffold sequences at base-pair resolution and test whether the threshold-dependent odds ratios persist when ordinal gene-position errors are removed. [^prophage_amr_comobilization]
- Analyze all available genomes from Klebsiella pneumoniae, Acinetobacter baumannii, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, and Escherichia coli to determine whether the local and species-level associations are robust to the 20-genomes-per-species sampling design. [^prophage_amr_comobilization]
- Partition prophage, plasmid, and integrative-conjugative-element signals and fit comparative models that test whether prophage density predicts AMR breadth independently of other mobile elements. [^prophage_amr_comobilization]
- Map the 1,517 resistance islands to plasmids, chromosomes, integron boundaries, insertion sequences, and prophage calls to determine which carriers explain tight AMR co-inheritance. [^amr_strain_variation]
- Revisit the fitness comparison as fitness-browser coverage expands, testing whether prophage-proximal and distal AMR genes differ in RB-TnSeq fitness effects. [^prophage_amr_comobilization]
- Stratify the mobile-element analysis by environment and clinical representation, then test whether prophage density predicts AMR breadth after accounting for environment, phylogeny, genome count, and other mobile elements. This would address the environmental confounding identified in the 14,723-species resistome analysis. [^amr_environmental_resistome]
- Replace keyword-based AMR mechanism labels with CARD ARO mappings and test whether prophage-density and resistance-island associations persist after separating antibiotic resistance from metal and other stress-response genes. [^amr_pangenome_atlas]
- Use the full Fitness Browser pangenome link table and antibiotic-stress conditions to test whether the apparently low laboratory cost of AMR genes generalizes to mobile, clinical-associated resistance and differs by prophage proximity. [^amr_pangenome_atlas]
- Perform fitness-matched permutations of the AMR cofitness neighborhoods, recompute cofitness separately in antibiotic and standard-growth conditions, and test flagellar knockouts and other conditionally dispensable gene classes; this would distinguish shared dispensability from regulatory coupling before using cofitness as functional support for mobilization hypotheses. [^amr_cofitness_networks]
