# Gene Essentiality Across Conditions and Predictors

Gene essentiality is dependence of growth on a gene’s function under a defined condition. This synthesis integrates transposon sequencing (TnSeq, which infers importance from mutant abundance), flux balance analysis (FBA, a constraint-based metabolic-modeling method), deletion phenotypes, annotation, pangenome conservation, orthology, pathway completeness, fitness modules, functional-dark-matter prioritization, metabolic capability versus dependency, genotype–condition prediction, metal fitness, respiratory wiring, and ecological context. Direct perturbation and measured growth provide the strongest evidence; conservation, annotation, modeling, and ecological associations are predictors or hypotheses rather than interchangeable definitions of essentiality. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer]

The [[summaries/acinetobacter_adp1_explorer__REPORT]] compares [[entities/tnseq]], [[entities/flux-balance-analysis]], annotation, and pangenome status in *Acinetobacter baylyi* ADP1. [[summaries/adp1_deletion_phenotypes__REPORT]] adds a 2,034-gene deletion analysis across 8 carbon sources, while [[summaries/adp1_triple_essentiality__REPORT]] compares up to 5,852 genes across FBA, RB-TnSeq (random-barcode transposon sequencing), complete-gene knockout phenotypes, mutant growth, and proteomics. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes] [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] The wider corpus adds annotation-gap, metabolic, ecological, metal, module, antimicrobial-resistance, and dark-gene analyses. [src: annotation_gap_discovery] [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] [src: berdl_data_atlas] [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] [src: costly_dispensable_genes] [src: counter_ion_effects] [src: essential_genome] [src: essential_metabolome] [src: field_vs_lab_fitness] [src: fitness_effects_conservation] [src: fitness_modules] [src: functional_dark_matter] [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma] [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] [src: metal_fitness_atlas] [src: metal_specificity] [src: module_conservation] [[summaries/amr_cofitness_networks__REPORT]] and [[summaries/amr_fitness_cost__REPORT]] extend condition-specific interpretation across 28 and 25 organisms. ## Condition-Specific Essentiality and Fitness

Essentiality changed with medium: 499 genes were essential on minimal media versus 346 on LB, and FBA flux classes changed between rich and minimal media for 177 of 866 genes, or 20%. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] The KBase Data Lakehouse atlas inventories 27,410,721 FitnessBrowser measurements and genes tested under an average of 121–187 conditions, although this inventory does not itself establish essentiality. [src: berdl_data_atlas]

The ADP1 deletion collection **supports** condition dependence: across 8 carbon sources, mean growth ratios ranged from 0.41 on urea to 1.36 on quinate; 625 genes, or 31% of the complete matrix, had condition-specificity scores ≥ 1.0. The urease complex was required on urea, aromatic degradation and [[entities/pqq-biosynthesis]] on quinate, and the [[entities/glyoxylate-shunt]] on acetate. Five principal components (PCA, a method summarizing correlated variation into orthogonal components) captured 82% of variance; the highest pairwise condition correlation was r = 0.58 and the median was r = 0.25. [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes]

The triple-essentiality analysis **supports** the same pattern: across 8 carbon sources, 333 of 478 genes (70%) showed condition-specific defects, 10 (2%) were defective across all 8, and 135 (28%) showed no defect; mean pairwise defect correlation was 0.38, ranging from -0.03 to 1.0. The Q25 threshold classified 72% of genes as defective; defect rates were 73%, 74%, and 69% in the FBA-essential, FBA-variable, and FBA-blocked groups. The expected rate under independent conditions was 1 − 0.75⁸ = 90%, but aggregation depends on inter-condition correlation. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]

Condition-specific effects are not predominantly accessory: strongly condition-specific genes were 77.3% core versus 70.3% for genes without such phenotypes (OR=1.78, p=1.8e-97), and 4,450 genes (2.7%) were neutral overall but critical in one condition. These ephemeral-niche genes were 3.0% of core genes, 1.7% of auxiliary genes, and 1.6% of singleton genes. This supports the hypothesis that core genes can be conditionally important but does not establish that all core genes are broadly adaptive. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

Among 757 experiments, 337 (44.5%) were field and 420 (55.5%) lab. Among 2,725 non-essential genes with fitness and pangenome links, genes important at fitness < -2 were 83.6% core for field-stress and 82.4% for field-core, versus 81.4% for lab-nutrient, 81.5% for lab-other, 73.4% for lab-antibiotic, and 71.2% for heavy-metals; field-stress, field-core, and lab-nutrient enrichment was significant after BH-FDR (Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate) correction. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]

Capability–dependency analyses **support** condition-specific importance but **refine** capacity–dependence claims. One analysis identified 1,695 complete pathway-organism pairs: 267 (15.8%) latent, 547 (32.3%) intermediate, and 881 (51.9%) active; pathway category predicted class strongly (χ²=163.6, df=4, p=2.5×10⁻³⁴). Carbon-source pathways were latent in 217 of 892 cases (24.3%), versus 48 of 735 amino-acid-biosynthesis cases (6.5%), making carbon pathways 3.7× more likely to be latent. Latent fractions ranged from 0–31.6% across organisms, averaged 15.0%, and ranged from 4.7% to 21.1% across 16 threshold combinations (SD = 5.9 percentage points). [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]

A stricter four-way analysis of 161 organism–pathway pairs across 7 model bacteria and 23 GapMind pathways found 57 (35.4%) Active Dependency, 66 (41.0%) Latent Capability, 24 (14.9%) Incomplete but Important, and 14 (8.7%) Missing. Its composite importance score weighted essentiality at 40%, fitness breadth at 30%, and fitness magnitude at 30%. Mapping used Fitness Browser-native KEGG annotations through besthitkegg-to-keggmember-to-EC-to-KEGG-map-to-GapMind pathway links. All 66 Latent Capability pairs became fitness-important under at least one condition type, with nitrogen limitation, stress, and carbon limitation most frequent, although median-based subset thresholds can reclassify pairs by construction. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

The Web of Microbes bridge **supports** using metabolic context to test condition-specific fitness but **qualifies** production-to-dependency inference. For *Pseudomonas* FW300-N2E3, 19 produced metabolites matched Fitness Browser carbon- or nitrogen-source experiments; 5 were de novo products and 14 amplified metabolites. [src: webofmicrobes_explorer] The archived snapshot recorded production without organism consumption: controls had D for detected in starting medium (742 observations) and N for not detected (1,023); organism observations had I for increased (1,338), E for emerged de novo (1,155), and N for no significant change (7,509). E and I were mutually exclusive across 10,744 observations, and no organism had a decrease or consumption action. [src: webofmicrobes_explorer] Among ENIGMA isolates in R2A, E/(E+I) ranged from 15.2% to 32.4%, from 16 of 55 changes in *Bacillus* FW507-8R2A to 34 of 83 in *Pseudomonas* GW456-L13; this is a proposed metabolic-novelty phenotype, not an established essentiality predictor. [src: webofmicrobes_explorer]

## Prediction Targets and Evaluation Boundaries

Essentiality analysis does not evaluate one universal task: lethal-versus-dispensable classification, quantitative growth impairment among viable mutants, continuous fitness ranking, and condition-specific defects have different targets and evaluation boundaries. The ADP1 triple-essentiality analysis demonstrates that changing the target changes the apparent performance of FBA, TnSeq, knockout assays, and proteomics. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]

For lethal-versus-dispensable classification, refined FBA-versus-complete-knockout comparisons covered 724 genes in rich media, with recall = 60.8%, precision = 64.0%, specificity = 79.7%, F1 = 0.624, and Cohen’s kappa = 0.486; the minimal-media comparison covered 833 genes, with recall = 65.6%, precision = 69.2%, specificity = 78.9%, F1 = 0.673, and Cohen’s kappa = 0.493. These results **support** moderate FBA concordance at the lethal boundary but do not generalize to quantitative growth defects among TnSeq-dispensable genes. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]

The quantitative-growth analysis was restricted to 478 genes that were TnSeq-dispensable and had FBA and mutant-growth data. FBA class was not associated with growth-defect status: χ² = 0.93, p = 0.63, 2 df; defect rates were 73.1% (57/78) for FBA-essential, 73.5% (150/204) for FBA-variable, and 69.4% (136/196) for FBA-blocked genes; the Kruskal–Wallis test on mean growth rates was H = 1.67, p = 0.43. The null association persisted at Q10, Q15, Q20, Q25, Q30, Q35, and Q40, whose chi-squared p-values were 0.985, 0.887, 0.405, 0.629, 0.313, 0.058, and 0.048, respectively. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]

Continuous fitness retained ranking information better than binary essentiality fraction for knockout-lethality prediction: inverted fitness produced AUC (Area Under the ROC Curve, a threshold-independent classification measure) = 0.700 in rich media and 0.725 in minimal media, versus essentiality-fraction AUC = 0.344 and 0.403. This **refines** interpretation of [[concepts/condition-specific-fitness]] because reducing continuous outcomes to essential-versus-dispensable labels loses information. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]

RB-TnSeq and complete-gene knockout assays disagreed strongly when both were treated as binary essentiality tests in rich media. Across RB-TnSeq thresholds of 0.01, 0.025, 0.05, 0.10, and 0.20, Cohen’s kappa was -0.139, -0.122, -0.081, -0.024, and -0.014. At the 0.05 threshold, 1,933 genes were compared: recall = 7.9%, precision = 5.8%, specificity = 82.8%, F1 = 0.067, and kappa = -0.081; both-essential numbered 18 genes (0.9%), both-dispensable 1,411 (73.0%), knockout-essential/TnSeq-dispensable 211 (10.9%), and knockout-dispensable/TnSeq-essential 293 (15.2%). [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]

The report interprets this discordance as consistent with transposon insertion measuring fitness cost while potentially preserving partial function, whereas complete deletion measures lethality. Partial or truncated protein production, read-through transcription, retained functional domains, and condition aggregation are proposed mechanisms, not resolved demonstrations. Rich-media knockout data covered 2,953 genes, including 346 essential and 2,607 dispensable genes; the merged minimal-media set covered 3,092 genes, including 499 essential genes, with minimal-media calls prioritized and rich-media calls used as fallback. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]

Proteomics supplied an independent continuous evidence axis rather than a condition-matched causal test. Across 2,288 genes measured over 7 *Acinetobacter* strains, essential genes (n = 464) had mean log2 expression = 28.43 ± 2.94 versus 25.73 ± 2.96 for dispensable genes (n = 1,824), a difference of 2.70 log2 units or 6.5-fold; Mann–Whitney U p = 9.91×10⁻⁵⁹. Expression correlated with knockout essentiality at Pearson r = 0.345 (p = 5.32×10⁻⁶⁵) and Spearman ρ = 0.338 (p = 3.28×10⁻⁶²), with ROC AUC = 0.743. Averaging across 7 strains rather than the exact knockout condition makes this supporting evidence, not condition-matched causation, and **complements** [[concepts/multi-omics-integration]]. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]

## Fitness Importance, Conservation, and Accessory Essentiality

The conservation result is a weak but reproducible gradient. Across approximately 194,000 genes from 43 bacteria, essential genes were 82% core (n=27,693), genes often sick in >10% of experiments 78% core (n=15,989), mixed genes 70% core (n=20,739), sometimes-sick genes 72% core (n=25,201), always-neutral genes 66% core (n=94,889), and sometimes-beneficial genes 70% core (n=9,705). By strongest measured effect, essential genes were 82.2% core, genes with min_fit < -3 were 77.7% core, and genes with min_fit from -1 to 0 were 66.4% core. Fitness breadth had a positive but weak association with conservation (Spearman rho=0.086, p=8.1e-230): essential genes were 82% core, genes affecting 20+ experiments 79%, genes affecting 6–20 experiments 73%, genes affecting 1–5 experiments 71%, and genes with 0 experiments 66%. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

These values **support** a relationship between functional importance, breadth of measured effects, and conservation, but the 16-percentage-point difference between 82% core among essential genes and 66% among always-neutral genes limits conservation as a standalone predictor. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

A separate linkage analysis **supports** the direction while using different filters: among 148,826 protein-coding genes from 33 organisms, essential genes were 86.1% core versus 81.2% for non-essential genes, with a median odds ratio of 1.56; 18 of 33 organisms showed significant enrichment after BH-FDR correction. Essentiality was inferred from RB-TnSeq under the library-construction and growth conditions represented in the Fitness Browser. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] Of 44 of 48 Fitness Browser organisms mapped to pangenome species clades, the analysis produced 177,863 gene-to-cluster links with 100.0% median protein identity and 94.2% median gene coverage. Thirty-four organisms had at least 90% coverage, and 33 entered downstream analysis because Dyella79 was excluded for a locus-tag mismatch. Four organisms—Cola, Kang, Magneto, and SB2B—were unmatched because their species had too few genomes in GTDB for pangenome construction. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]

Of the linked genes, 145,821 (82.0%) were core and 32,042 (18.0%) auxiliary; 7,574 singleton genes were a subset of the auxiliary category. Strongest enrichment signals were *Methanococcus maripaludis* S2 (OR=5.21), *Ralstonia syzygii* PSI07 (OR=3.41), and *Marinobacter adhaerens* (OR=3.08). [src: conservation_vs_fitness]

Essential genes can therefore be strain-specific, auxiliary, or highly divergent. The essential-auxiliary category contained 3,683 genes: 13.4% were enzymes and 38.2% hypothetical, with leading subsystems including ribosomes, DNA replication, type 4 secretion, and plasmid replication. These profiles support strain-specific variants of conserved machinery or mobile-element-associated functions, but do not demonstrate compensation for missing core functions. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]

The broader inventory contains 1,011,650,903 KBase pangenome gene rows spanning 293,059 genomes, 27,690 GTDB species clades or pangenomes, and 132.5M gene clusters. A separate 48-organism analysis found 41,059 of 221,005 genes essential (18.6%), with organism-level rates from 12.2% in Pedo557 to 29.7% in Magneto. Across 17,222 ortholog families, 859 (5.0%) were universally essential, 4,799 (27.9%) variably essential, and 11,564 (67.1%) never essential. [src: berdl_data_atlas] [src: discoveries] [src: essential_genome]

Universally essential genes were 91.7% core, variably essential genes 88.9%, never-essential genes 81.7%, and orphan essential genes 49.5%; counts were 6,963, 90,844, 47,140, and 4,683. Of 17,222 ortholog families, 839 of 859 universally essential families were strict single-copy families with copy ratio ≤1.5 and no non-essential paralogs. Essentiality penetrance and core fraction had weak positive correlation (rho=0.123, p=1.6e-17). [src: essential_genome]

## Conservation, Annotation, and Functional Novelty

Essential genes were more annotation-rich: 33% had COG assignments versus 5% of dispensable genes, and 92% had KEGG KO assignments versus 53%; approximately 8% lacked KO assignments and were candidate novel essential functions. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] AlphaFold analysis found median MSA depth 15,308 for core clusters versus 5,299 for auxiliary+singleton and 5,527 for auxiliary non-singleton clusters; across 38,051,842 bridged pairs, MSA depth and domain-hit count had Spearman ρ=0.7563. Yet 415,603 core clusters had MSA depth <10 and 68.9% were hypothetical, and only 29.3% of 132,531,501 clusters bridged to AlphaFold. [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]

Direct linkage **refines** the annotation gradient: essential-core contained 22,751 genes and was 41.9% enzymes and 13.0% hypothetical; essential-auxiliary contained 3,683 genes and was 13.4% enzymes and 38.2% hypothetical; essential-unmapped contained 1,259 genes and was 18.2% enzymes and 44.7% hypothetical. Essential-core was enriched in Protein Metabolism by +13.7 percentage points, Cofactors/Vitamins by +6.2%, Cell Wall by +3.9%, and Fatty Acid biosynthesis by +3.1%, and depleted in Carbohydrates by -7.9%, Amino Acids by -5.6%, and Membrane Transport by -4.0%, relative to non-essential genes. Essential-auxiliary genes included ribosomes, DNA replication, type 4 secretion, and plasmid-replication functions; essential-unmapped genes included divergent ribosomal proteins L34, L36, S11, and S12, translation factors, transposases, and DNA-binding proteins. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]

A poorly conserved essential class remains: 7,084 essential genes had no detectable ortholog in any other Fitness Browser organism, 58.7% were hypothetical versus 8.2% of universally essential genes, and 8,297 hypothetical essential genes represented 20.2% of all essentials. Of these, 3,912 had orthologs and were predictable by module transfer, whereas 4,385 orphan genes were not. A further analysis transferred 1,382 function predictions to hypothetical essential genes from fitness-module context in non-essential orthologs. [src: essential_genome]

The functional-dark-matter analysis found 57,011 dark genes (24.9% of 228,709 genes), including 9,557 essential dark genes; 39,532 (69.3%) had pangenome links, 12,686 were accessory, and 511 were both accessory and strongly fitness-active. [src: functional_dark_matter] The truly-dark analysis **sharpens** this result: of 57,011 dark genes, 33,105 were reclassified by Bakta, 6,427 remained hypothetical in both pipelines, and 17,479 lacked pangenome links. Among strongly phenotyped genes, stress conditions were reported at 28.7% versus 43.2% for annotation-lag genes (OR = 0.53, p < 0.001); the results section reported stress at 43.3% versus 54.7%, carbon-source conditions at 13.7% versus 21.7%, and motility at 1.2% versus 3.2%. [src: truly_dark_genes]

Among 39,532 pangenome-linked dark genes, persistent hypothetical genes had an essential fraction of 18.0% versus 13.4% for annotation-lag genes, core fraction of 43.1% versus 72.7%, ortholog presence of 29.3% versus 63.7%, and median ortholog breadth of 1 versus 4 organisms. Median length was 121 versus 194 amino acids and mean GC content was 0.542 versus 0.584; length d = −0.432, core OR = 0.284, essential OR = 1.420, GC d = −0.395, ortholog-presence OR = 0.236, and ortholog-breadth d = −1.072, with corresponding p-values < 1e-100, < 1e-100, < 1e-10, 2e-115, < 1e-100, and < 1e-100. Thus the aggregate 49.5% core fraction for orphan essentials is refined by a shorter, narrower-breadth persistent-dark subset, although short genes are also harder to annotate and measure. [src: truly_dark_genes] [src: essential_genome]

Most persistent-dark genes still had sequence-level clues: 79.4% had UniRef50 links, 84.7% database cross-references, 4.0% Pfam hits, and 4.6% KEGG KOs; eggNOG-mapper supplied partial signal for 43.5%, while 17.0% had COG categories and 55.4% of those assignments were category S, function unknown. Only 246 genes (3.8%) had no clues; 3,867 (60.2%) had minimal sequence identifiers, 711 (11.1%) had partial Pfam, COG, or eggNOG function, and 1,603 (24.9%) had phenotype or module evidence without functional annotation. [src: truly_dark_genes]

The module-conservation result **supports** core enrichment at the module level: across 1,116 ICA modules in 32 organisms, module genes were 86.0% core versus 81.5% for all genes (OR=1.46, p=1.6e-87); among 974 modules with at least 3 mapped genes, 577 (59%) were >90% core, 349 (36%) mixed, and 48 (5%) <50% core. Module-family breadth did not predict conservation (Spearman rho=-0.01, p=0.914). Thirty-eight accessory module families had <50% core genes; horizontal transfer or niche-specific operons remain hypotheses. [src: module_conservation]

## Predictor Agreement, Metabolic Capacity, and Ecological Context

Among 866 genes with FBA and TnSeq calls, 639 (73.8%) were concordant and 227 discordant. Among 478 TnSeq-dispensable genes, FBA class was not associated with quantitative growth-defect status (χ²=0.93, p=0.63, 2 df; Kruskal–Wallis H=1.67, p=0.43); refined FBA-versus-knockout comparisons gave rich-media F1=0.624 and kappa=0.486 across 724 genes, and minimal-media F1=0.673 and kappa=0.493 across 833. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]

RB-TnSeq thresholds contradicted knockout essentiality in rich media: at 0.05, recall was 7.9%, precision 5.8%, specificity 82.8%, F1 0.067, and kappa -0.081 across 1,933 genes; kappa ranged from -0.139 to -0.014 across thresholds 0.01–0.20. FBA missed complex-level essentiality: 10/13 Complex I subunits had quinate-specific defects despite 1.76× higher predicted flux and 0% predicted essentiality, while 30/51 network genes lacked FBA mappings. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

The respiratory study **refines** this model–measurement discordance. Quinate required Complex I, acetate required Complex I plus cytochrome bo3, ACIAD3522, and additional components, lactate specifically required cytochrome bo3, glucose had no specifically required respiratory component, and urea was generally demanding. Complex I growth ratios were 0.37 on quinate, 1.44 on glucose, and 0.49 on acetate; ACIAD3522 was dispensable on quinate and glucose but had a growth ratio of 0.013 on acetate, where the report describes it as lethal. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]

ADP1 has three parallel NADH dehydrogenases: 13-subunit proton-pumping Complex I, single-subunit non-proton-pumping [[entities/ndh-2]], and single-subunit ACIAD3522, an NADH-FMN oxidoreductase. NDH-2 is ACIAD_RS16420 with KO K03885; it is TnSeq-dispensable, absent from the deletion collection, standalone rather than operonic, and core. FBA predicts zero NDH-2 flux on standard carbon sources because growth optimization routes NADH through Complex I. Complex I, NDH-2, and ACIAD3522 had mean protein levels of 27.6 at the 66th percentile, 27.0 at the 59th percentile, and 26.2 at the 48th percentile, respectively, against a genome median of 26.4; the spread was 1.4 units. After retaining organisms with 1–2 NDH-2 hits and excluding those with more than 2, 5 of 14 organisms had validated NDH-2, and their mean Complex I aromatic deficit was −0.297 versus −0.156 without validated NDH-2 (p=0.52). [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]

Fitness modules were stable at 17–52 modules per organism across 32 bacteria; 94.2% had significantly elevated within-module cofitness, mean |r|=0.34 versus background 0.12, and mean genomic-adjacency enrichment was 22.7×. Ortholog transfer achieved 95.8% precision and 91.2% coverage for gene-level KO prediction, whereas Module-ICA had <1% KO-level precision but captured process-level co-regulation. With 20% of KEGG-annotated genes withheld, ortholog transfer achieved 95.8% strict precision, 91.2% coverage, and 0.934 F1; domain prediction achieved 29.1%, 66.6%, and 0.401; Module-ICA achieved <1% and 23.3%; and cofitness voting achieved <1% and 73.0%. [src: discoveries] [src: fitness_modules]

GapMind completeness and Fitness Browser measurements were compared through proxy mappings, so Active Dependency, Incomplete but Important, Latent Capability, and Missing are pathway-level evidence rather than direct gene-level essentiality calls. The metal atlas likewise found that a 1,286-KEGG-KO presence signature did not predict metal fitness. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] [src: metal_fitness_atlas]

GapMind found 17 of 18 amino-acid biosynthesis pathways complete in all 7 successfully mapped organisms; serine biosynthesis was complete in 6 of 7, with DvH the sole exception. The seven-organism sample contained 6 organisms with 18/18 pathways and DvH with 17/18 (94.4%), but only 7 of 45 organisms mapped successfully. [src: essential_metabolome] The larger analysis found 267 of 1,695 complete pathway-organism pairs latent, especially carbon pathways, while amino-acid biosynthesis was usually active. DvH’s apparent serine auxotrophy remains a hypothesis requiring testing. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]

Across approximately 293,000 genomes, the newer pathway analysis assessed 80 GapMind pathways—18 amino-acid biosynthesis and 62 carbon-source utilization pathways. Core-only versus all-gene completeness differed by 0.146 for leucine and valine, 0.141 for arginine, 0.140 for lysine, and 0.140 for threonine biosynthesis; corresponding all-gene/core-only values were 0.614/0.468, 0.614/0.468, 0.613/0.472, 0.804/0.664, and 0.803/0.663. These results are consistent with accessory contributions and possible community sharing, but do not demonstrate metabolite exchange or Black Queen dynamics. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

The capability–conservation relationship is not simple. Among 755 active dependencies, mean pathway conservation was 0.829; among 508 intermediate pathways it was 0.907; and among 248 latent capabilities it was 0.869. The active-versus-latent test gave Mann–Whitney U p=0.94 and rank-biserial r=0.052. Across 2,810 GTDB species with at least 10 genomes, variable pathway count correlated with pangenome openness at raw Spearman rho=0.327, p=7.2e-71, and partial Spearman rho=0.530, p=2.83e-203 after controlling for genome count. The signal was positive in 13 of 18 genera and significant at p<0.05 in 5 of 18: Clostridium, Eubacterium, Mesorhizobium, Pseudomonas, and Streptomyces; strongest within-genus correlations were rho=0.534 for Clostridium and rho=0.506 for Eubacterium. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

All 10 target species formed metabolic clusters with silhouette scores >0.2, ranging from 0.35 for PALSA-747 sp. to 0.89 for *Salmonella enterica*. Cluster membership was associated with isolation environment in *Salmonella enterica* (χ²=1570.2, df=25, p<0.0001) and *Phenylobacterium* sp. (χ²=12.2, df=1, p=0.0005), but not in *Stutzerimonas*, *Alteromonas*, *Pelagibacter*, or *Prochlorococcus*. Strongest pathway heterogeneity occurred in valine/leucine biosynthesis (0.60), tryptophan biosynthesis (0.51), and lysine/threonine biosynthesis (0.49). These associations lack explicit phylogenetic correction and may reflect metadata or annotation limitations. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]

Among 225 species with at least 50 genomes and at least 3 variable pathways, binary completeness profiles across 80 pathways produced a median of 4 metabolic ecotypes per species and a maximum of 8 in *Alistipes onderdonkii* and *Barnesiella intestinihominis*. Ecotype count correlated with pangenome openness at raw Spearman rho=0.262, p=6.8e-05, and partial rho=0.322, p=8.0e-07 after controlling for genome count. Genome fluidity is a plausible explanation, but sampling bias remains. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

The ENIGMA genotype–phenotype analysis **supports** interaction-aware models but **contradicts** expectations of general genotype-to-strain-fitness predictability. Across 46,389 genome × condition pairs from 727 genomes and 363 conditions, binary-growth AUC was 0.620 under genus-blocked holdout; amino acids, nucleosides, and carbon sources performed better (AUC 0.775, 0.780, and 0.695) than metals, antibiotics, and nitrogen (0.605, 0.619, and 0.435). KO × condition interactions increased mean AUC from 0.620 to 0.653 and improved 80 of 106 held-out genera; 95 of 343 individually testable conditions achieved AUC > 0.75. Continuous µmax, lag, and max_A had negative held-out R². [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma]

GapMind achieved AUC 0.646, accuracy 78.8%, and coverage 24.3% on 118 testable pairs; matched-condition Carbon Source Phenotypes transfer achieved AUC 0.800 and accuracy 76.8% at 23% coverage, with internal five-fold AUC 0.858. Approximately 76% of conditions lacked either coverage source, and performance fell to approximately AUC 0.63. Gene content therefore predicts some capabilities but does not establish within-strain essentiality. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma]

## Tensions

The two conservation–essentiality integrations report different essential-gene core fractions: 82% (and 82.2% under strongest-effect grouping) across approximately 194,000 genes from 43 bacteria, versus 86.1% among 148,826 genes from 33 organisms. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] [src: conservation_vs_fitness] This methodological tension involves datasets, organism filters, pangenome mappings, and denominators; the estimates must not be averaged.

ADP1 analyses associate essentiality with core status and richer annotation, whereas 415,603 core clusters had MSA depth <10 and 68.9% were hypothetical. Core enrichment also coexists with 66% core status among always-neutral genes, 24.4% positive-fitness core genes versus 19.9% accessory genes, and function-specific reversals. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] [src: alphafold_msa_annotation] [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

The direct linkage adds a sampling tension: essential genes were 86.1% core versus 81.2% for non-essential genes, but clades with only 2 genomes can have trivially high core fractions and pangenome coverage varies. The main *Escherichia coli* clade was absent because it contained too many genomes; Keio, an *E. coli* BW25113 strain, mapped to the small s__Escherichia_coli_E clade at only 26.1% coverage. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]

Persistent hypothetical genes were less core and had narrower ortholog breadth than annotation-lag genes, but had a higher essential fraction, 18.0% versus 13.4%. This does not overturn the aggregate conservation–essentiality gradient; it shows that essentiality can persist in lineage-restricted genes and that short-gene measurement and annotation biases must be separated from biological novelty. [src: truly_dark_genes]

Module genes were 86.0% core versus 81.5% for all genes, but zero essential genes appeared in modules because essential genes are invisible to insertion-based ICA. This is a measurement tension rather than a biological contradiction. [src: module_conservation]

Pathway-level conservation conflicts with the gene-level gradient: latent complete pathways had mean conservation 0.869 versus 0.829 for active dependencies, with p=0.94. The newer analysis found Active Dependencies at mean core completeness 0.986 versus 0.975 for Latent Capabilities in only 7 matched model organisms, while its larger analysis links pathway variability—not pathway dependency—to openness. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

Broad metal-important genes were 87.4% core versus 76.9% baseline, whereas DvH heavy-metal genes were 71.2% core and not significantly enriched after correction. Metal-specific genes were 84.8% core pooled, less enriched than general sick genes at 90.2%, while metal+stress genes were 94.3% core. These results should not be averaged because definitions, thresholds, organism sets, and stress histories differ. [src: metal_fitness_atlas] [src: field_vs_lab_fitness] [src: metal_specificity]

The respiratory study adds a model–measurement tension: Complex I was required on quinate despite theoretical quinate production of 0.57 NADH per carbon, while it was dispensable on glucose at 1.50 NADH per carbon; FBA predicted zero NDH-2 flux because it optimizes ATP yield, and the cross-species NDH-2 comparison found no significant compensatory pattern (p=0.52). [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] [src: discoveries]

Web of Microbes production matches cannot establish uptake, pathway completeness, or gene essentiality: the snapshot has no organism consumption actions, and 107 formula-only matches expanded to 900 candidate molecules. Likewise, only 859 of 17,222 ortholog families were universally essential, while 4,799 were variably essential and 11,564 never essential; 17 of 18 amino-acid pathways were complete in all 7 mapped organisms, yet DvH lacked predicted serine biosynthesis. [src: webofmicrobes_explorer] [src: discoveries] [src: essential_metabolome] [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]

ADP1 showed moderate FBA–knockout concordance, whereas annotation-gap analysis found 42.5% baseline FBA accuracy across 574 organism–carbon-source combinations; FBA lacked mappings for 30/51 aromatic-network genes and predicted 0% Complex I essentiality despite 1.76× higher aromatic flux. Module-ICA had <1% strict KO precision despite strong cofitness, versus 95.8% for ortholog transfer; these are different prediction targets rather than a biological contradiction. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] [src: annotation_gap_discovery] [src: discoveries] [src: fitness_modules]

The counter-ion analysis found 39.8% overlap between metal-important and NaCl-stress genes, while removing shared-stress genes preserved core enrichment for 12 of 14 metals and the original 87.4% core, OR=2.08 conclusion. A separate analysis found 14.7% of metal-important genes sick under osmotic stress; the 2.7× discrepancy reflects stricter thresholds and different organism sets. [src: counter_ion_effects] [src: metal_specificity]

## Interpretation and Limits

Direct TnSeq calls, measured condition differences, and quantified FBA–TnSeq comparisons provide the strongest evidence. No gene had measurements across all six database modalities, and incomplete overlap limits separation of biological essentiality from coverage, annotation, model, and measurement effects. Deletion measurements were single-timepoint growth ratios across only 8 carbon sources; 499 essential genes and 316 incomplete-data genes were excluded from the complete matrix. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes]

Fitness measurements were biased toward rich media and standard stresses, ecological niches were underrepresented, single-gene knockouts did not capture epistasis, and the 43-bacterium cohort was primarily Pseudomonadota. Singleton or novel genes may lack fitness data because of poor transposon coverage rather than true neutrality. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] Only 48 organisms were fitness-tested among 293,000 genomes with pathway predictions; latent-capability rates were threshold-sensitive, and pathway-level conservation cannot detect partial erosion or gene redundancy. The newer study was limited to 7 matched organisms for Tier 1 comparison, 80 pathways, KEGG-based mappings, and genome-count sampling bias. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

The conservation linkage extended taxonomic breadth but retained sampling limits: 44 of 48 organisms were linked, 34 had at least 90% coverage, and only 33 were used downstream; four organisms were unmatched because their GTDB species clades had too few genomes. Ten organisms were excluded from Phase 2 because they had <90% DIAMOND coverage, and Dyella79 was excluded because the Fitness Browser table used N515DRAFT_* locus tags while protein sequences used ABZR86_RS*, producing a 0% join rate. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]

The essential-gene set is an upper bound because missing transposon insertions can reflect short genes, low-complexity regions, or scaffold-edge locations rather than essentiality; essential genes were slightly shorter on average, consistent with insertion bias. RB-TnSeq essentiality was typically measured in rich-media library-construction conditions, so stress-only essential genes were not captured. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] [src: essential_genome]

Pangenome coverage limits conservation comparisons. Clades containing only 2 genomes can have trivially high core fractions, because presence in both equals 100% core. BBH orthology is conservative and can miss paralogs, gene fusions, and distant homologs, so some apparent orphan essentials may have undetected divergent orthologs. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] [src: essential_genome]

The Web of Microbes source was a 2018 archived snapshot containing 37 organisms across 5 ENIGMA-funded projects, 589 metabolites, and 10,744 action observations; 332 metabolites, or 56.4%, were unidentified with an `Unk_` prefix. It came from a single laboratory, included only 20 experimental organisms, may not represent the current GNPS2-hosted resource, and lacked consumption data. [src: webofmicrobes_explorer]

The truly-dark study found that 17,479 of 57,011 dark genes lacked pangenome links, leaving a 31% coverage gap; at the observed 16.3% truly-dark rate among linked genes, approximately 2,841 additional truly dark genes may exist among the unlinked set, including 2,208 with strong fitness phenotypes. Bakta may produce false-negative functional calls, BBH orthology covered only 32 of 48 Fitness Browser organisms, GC deviation is an imperfect HGT proxy, short genes are harder both to annotate and measure, and polar effects may contribute to strong phenotypes. [src: truly_dark_genes]

The respiratory study is limited because NDH-2 has no growth data; its stoichiometric explanation uses theoretical rather than measured flux; only 4 organisms lacked NDH-2 in the cross-species comparison; annotation variation may miss orthologs; ACIAD3522 may have another function; text matching produced false-positive NDH-2 calls; and the planned pangenome KO co-occurrence analysis was not performed. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]

Other limits include failed organism-to-clade linkage caused by boolean-string taxids, Fitness Browser taxonomic bias, missing or inconsistent environment metadata, AlphaEarth coverage of 83K/293K genomes (28%), and limited NMDC validation. The ENIGMA CORAL survey contained 6,705 genomes, 15,015 genes, 4,346 field samples across 596 Oak Ridge locations, and 213,044 ASVs, but no DvH gene-level fitness data. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] [src: functional_dark_matter] [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]

The ENIGMA dataset contained 27,632 curves from 123 strains, 195 molecules, 303 plates, and 7.57M timepoints; 15,227 (55.1%) showed no growth, 9,861 (35.7%) were fit-ok, and approximately 9% failed fitting. Only 275 of 486 strain × condition Fitness Browser anchors showed measurable growth, a strain-name collision produced 1,751 spurious clinical genomes, and verified GTDB linkages fell from 32 to 20. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma]

The pitfalls audit adds operational limits: KBase Data Lakehouse is migrating from Delta to Iceberg; tenant and database names require explicit resolution; REST requests can return 504, 524, 503, or empty responses; direct Spark SQL is preferred; numeric fields and Spark DECIMAL values require explicit casting; billion-row pangenome tables require key filters and server-side aggregation; a 1 GB Spark Connect result cap makes large `.toPandas()` collections unsafe; and disabling automatic broadcast joins caused an NB10 job joining 13.7M rows with 18,989 species-taxonomy rows to hang for 17+ minutes. Short ENIGMA strain names are not globally unique, MetaPhlAn3 cohort names require synonymy reconciliation, and erroneous joins can create extreme apparent abundance differences or incorrect genus matches. [src: pitfalls]
