# Study and batch confounding can reverse environmental generalization

Environmental associations estimated across heterogeneous studies can reflect study, batch, classifier, or unmeasured sample-processing structure rather than portable environmental effects. The [euk_in_prok_correlates__REPORT](../summaries/euk_in_prok_correlates__REPORT.md) provides a direct example: eukaryotic read fractions appeared environmentally predictable under random cross-validation but failed to generalize when entire studies were held out. Missing laboratory metadata further prevents direct separation of ecological effects from extraction, filtration, host-depletion, library-preparation, and related processing effects. [^euk_in_prok_correlates]

## Core claim

When environmental categories are strongly nested within studies, an environmental predictor can act as a proxy for study-specific protocols, populations, sequencing workflows, or unmeasured sample-processing factors. Random train/test splits allow closely related samples from the same study to occur in both partitions, whereas study-held-out validation tests whether an association transfers to a new batch or study. This makes [study-batch-confounding-of-environmental-associations](study-batch-confounding-of-environmental-associations.md) a direct extension of [environment-embedding-geography](environment-embedding-geography.md) and [callability-limited-comparative-inference](callability-limited-comparative-inference.md). [^euk_in_prok_correlates]

The [ecotype_env_reanalysis__REPORT](../summaries/ecotype_env_reanalysis__REPORT.md) **supports** the distinction between an observed environmental signal and a transferable environmental effect, while **refining** it beyond study-held-out read-fraction prediction. In a genome-level comparison, environmental species had a median environment–gene-content partial correlation of 0.051, versus 0.084 for human-associated species; the one-sided Environmental > Human-associated test gave U=1536 and p=0.83. [^ecotype_env_reanalysis] The continuous fraction-of-environmental-genomes analysis likewise found no relationship with correlation strength (rho=-0.085, p=0.25). [^ecotype_env_reanalysis] Thus, the clinical composition bias was real but did not explain the weak environmental gene-content association, consistent with the possibility that environmental structure can be genuine yet non-portable or poorly aligned with the measured biological outcome. [^ecotype_env_reanalysis]

The original [ecotype_analysis__REPORT](../summaries/ecotype_analysis__REPORT.md) **supports** this qualification at broader species scale: among 172 species with sufficient data, the median environment–gene-content partial correlation was 0.0025 versus 0.0143 for phylogeny, with phylogeny dominating in 60.5% of species; significant positive, negative, and null environmental effects occurred in 7.0%, 2.3%, and 90.7% of species, respectively. [^ecotype_analysis] This **refines** the page's interpretation: weak whole-genome environmental association is not by itself evidence of batch confounding, because environmental adaptation may act on specific gene subsets, while it also supports the hypothesis that AlphaEarth embeddings do not capture all ecologically relevant variation. [^ecotype_analysis]

## Evidence from eukaryotic read fractions

The analysis quantified GOTTCHA2 relative eukaryotic abundance across 2,759 NMDC ReadbasedAnalysis runs from 9 studies. [^euk_in_prok_correlates] GOTTCHA2 detected eukaryotic reads in 77% of the runs, with a median eukaryotic fraction of 2.7%, a mean of 13.3%, and 20% of runs exceeding 20% eukaryotic reads. [^euk_in_prok_correlates] These fractions were classifier- and database-dependent: Kraken2 and Centrifuge produced approximately 0 domain-level Eukaryota signal in this deployment, whereas GOTTCHA2 was plastid- and eukaryote-aware. [^euk_in_prok_correlates] The GOTTCHA2 values should therefore be interpreted as relative or ordinal contamination measures rather than calibrated absolute fractions. [^euk_in_prok_correlates]

The apparent environmental structure was substantial: aquatic freshwater samples had 99.5% eukaryotic detection and a plastid share of 1.00, terrestrial soil had 55.7% detection and a plastid share of 0.43, and plant-root samples had 100% detection and a plastid share of 0.03. [^euk_in_prok_correlates] Matrix differences were significant in a Kruskal–Wallis test with H=77.8 and p=1.3×10⁻¹⁷, and all pairwise matrix contrasts were significant after BH-FDR, where FDR means false discovery rate. [^euk_in_prok_correlates] These results establish strong observed environmental differences, but they do not identify which differences arise from sample ecology and which arise from unrecorded laboratory processing. [^euk_in_prok_correlates]

Each biome was approximately 80–100% nested within a single study, preventing a clean separation of environment from study or batch in cross-study univariate comparisons. [^euk_in_prok_correlates] A gradient-boosted model using study_id alone achieved R²=0.24 under random cross-validation, while an environment model achieved R²=0.35 under the same validation scheme. [^euk_in_prok_correlates] Under GroupKFold, meaning cross-validation in which complete studies are held out as groups, the environment model achieved R²=−0.30 out of study, and the model combining environment with sequencing achieved R²=−0.39. [^euk_in_prok_correlates] The out-of-study environment model therefore performed worse than predicting the mean, while its out-of-study detection AUC was 0.56, approximately chance. [^euk_in_prok_correlates]

This **contradicts** the interpretation that the stronger random-validation environment score demonstrates broadly transferable environmental prediction. [^euk_in_prok_correlates] The contrast supports the hypothesis that the pooled cross-study signal was substantially batch-driven and encoded study composition or processing history rather than a portable environmental effect. [^euk_in_prok_correlates] Missing protocol fields prevent direct adjustment for wet-lab variables that may differ systematically among studies. [^euk_in_prok_correlates]

Adding sequencing platform and depth did not improve study-held-out prediction. [^euk_in_prok_correlates] Measured sequencing depth was negatively associated with eukaryotic fraction, with Spearman ρ=−0.29, p=5.2×10⁻⁷, and n=292 runs with measured depth, but this association was not batch-controlled. [^euk_in_prok_correlates] Measured-depth runs came from a handful of non-soil studies, while the dominant NEON soil study recorded no depth; the depth result is therefore suggestive rather than evidence of a controlled sequencing-depth effect. [^euk_in_prok_correlates]

## Why within-study analysis changes, but does not settle, the conclusion

A batch-controlled analysis of 1,186 runs from the dominant NEON soil metagenome study used one sampling program with a constant protocol and batch. [^euk_in_prok_correlates] Within that study, local vegetation was associated with eukaryotic fraction at H=119.1 and p=7.6×10⁻²¹ across 11 `env_local_scale` levels, and geography was associated across 47 sites at H=310.4 and p=2.4×10⁻⁴⁶. [^euk_in_prok_correlates] A model using local environment and geography achieved within-study five-fold R²=+0.17 ± 0.06, contrasting with cross-study out-of-study R²=−0.30. [^euk_in_prok_correlates]

This **supports** a genuine fine-scale environmental signal when batch is held approximately constant and **refines** [environment-embedding-geography](environment-embedding-geography.md): environmental metadata can be predictive, but apparent portability depends on validation and sampling structure. [^euk_in_prok_correlates] It does not establish that the result is free of protocol confounding or causal, because local environment and geography may still track sub-batches such as sampling campaigns. [^euk_in_prok_correlates] Nor does it establish portability to aquatic or host-associated collections. [^euk_in_prok_correlates]

The ecotype reanalysis **supports** this portability qualification but does not reproduce the original absolute correlation scale: its all-species median partial correlation was 0.081, compared with 0.003 in the original analysis, a reported 27x difference. [^ecotype_env_reanalysis] The reanalysis used all genomes with embeddings, including up to 3,505 genomes per species, rather than diversity-maximizing downsampling with a maximum of 250 genomes; the report states that the absolute values are not comparable, while the within-method environmental-versus-human comparison remains valid. [^ecotype_env_reanalysis] This **refines** the page's methodological warning: validation structure matters, but genome selection and extraction methodology can also change the apparent strength of an environmental association.

The original ecotype analysis **supports** the same caution but adds a different whole-genome result: it analyzed 13,381 genomes across 224 species and produced correlations for 172 species, finding that phylogeny generally dominated gene-content similarity and that environmental effects were often statistically non-significant. [^ecotype_analysis] Its environmental embeddings covered only 28.4% of genomes, and geographic coordinates were often missing or imprecise; partial correlations also assume linear relationships between distance matrices. [^ecotype_analysis] These limitations **refine** the interpretation of a null environmental effect: the result is consistent with weak or poorly measured ecological structure, but does not distinguish that explanation from genuine subset-specific adaptation or unmeasured confounding. [^ecotype_analysis]

## Metadata, classifier, and join limitations

NMDC did not populate DNA-extraction kit, size fractionation or filtration, host-depletion method, or library-preparation fields, and `has_filtration` was all false in the processing metadata. [^euk_in_prok_correlates] These unmeasured factors can remain residual explanations for apparent environmental differences even after measured sequencing variables are included. [^euk_in_prok_correlates] Classifier and reference-database compatibility must therefore be audited before comparing contamination or taxonomic fractions across studies; the GOTTCHA2-specific measurement scale **supports** this qualification. [^euk_in_prok_correlates]

The analysis used `workflow_run_id` rather than the biosample level because 1,067 of 2,759 runs were pooled from multiple biosamples, avoiding pseudo-replication from biosample-level joins. [^euk_in_prok_correlates] Metadata linkage connected workflow runs to biosamples and studies through NMDC tables and linked 99%+ of classified runs. [^euk_in_prok_correlates] For pooled runs, environmental and collection metadata came from a representative biosample selected by `MIN(biosample_id)`; heterogeneous pooled biosamples could therefore introduce predictor label noise. [^euk_in_prok_correlates] The report characterizes this as a conservative bias that can weaken associations rather than manufacture them. [^euk_in_prok_correlates] This **refines** [pooled-run-pseudoreplication-and-metadata-label-noise](pooled-run-pseudoreplication-and-metadata-label-noise.md) and [environment-embedding-geography](environment-embedding-geography.md). [^euk_in_prok_correlates]

The ecotype reanalysis further **supports** controlling unequal observation counts: environmental species had a 21% NaN partial-correlation rate, compared with 7% for human-associated species, so environmental species were disproportionately removed by missing-correlation filtering. [^ecotype_env_reanalysis] Because this filtering would, if anything, favor finding a stronger environmental signal, its failure to do so does not support clinical sampling bias as the explanation for the null comparison.

The original ecotype analysis **refines** this metadata concern from another angle: AlphaEarth embeddings were available for only 28.4% of genomes, and host-associated geographic coordinates may represent collection sites rather than the organisms’ actual microenvironments. [^ecotype_analysis] It therefore suggests the hypothesis that incomplete or ecologically misaligned environmental metadata can attenuate environmental associations even when study or batch is not the primary explanation. [^ecotype_analysis]

## Implications for environmental inference

Random cross-validation should not be treated as evidence of environmental generalization when samples from the same study or batch can occur in both training and test partitions. [^euk_in_prok_correlates] Study-held-out GroupKFold or within-study contrasts are more appropriate tests of whether an association survives batch changes. [^euk_in_prok_correlates] Absent extraction, filtration, size-fractionation, host-depletion, and library-preparation metadata should be treated as unresolved confounders, not as evidence that protocol effects are absent. [^euk_in_prok_correlates]

The ecotype result **refines** this implication by showing that correcting the environmental-versus-human comparison for genome-level classification and using a consistent within-method analysis still produced no stronger environmental association: environmental species had median partial correlation 0.051, while human-associated species had 0.084, with p=0.83 for Environmental > Human-associated. [^ecotype_env_reanalysis] Embedding similarity therefore need not represent ecologically relevant variation; the report notes that environmental embeddings had a 3.4x geographic-signal ratio versus 2.0x for human-associated embeddings, while the captured environmental variation did not strongly predict bacterial gene content. [^ecotype_env_reanalysis]

The original ecotype analysis **supports** prioritizing functional subsets over whole-genome association: it proposes testing COG functional categories, including V-Defense and L-Mobile, and comparing gene content within identified ecotype clusters. [^ecotype_analysis] This **refines** the implication that a weak genome-wide signal should not be interpreted as absence of ecological adaptation, particularly when the environmental representation is incomplete or the relevant effects are locus-specific. [^ecotype_analysis]

## Tensions

The cross-study analysis shows strong environmental differences, including environment-model R²=0.35 under random cross-validation, while study-held-out validation gives R²=−0.30 and detection AUC=0.56. [^euk_in_prok_correlates] The within-NEON analysis nevertheless gives positive predictive performance, with R²=+0.17 ± 0.06. [^euk_in_prok_correlates] These findings are not mutually exclusive: fine-scale environmental structure may be real while the pooled cross-study association is not portable because study, batch, and unmeasured protocol variables are entangled. [^euk_in_prok_correlates] Constant protocol and batch reduce confounding but do not remove possible sub-batch confounding from sampling campaigns. [^euk_in_prok_correlates]

The ecotype reanalysis **refines** this tension rather than resolving it: environmental species did not show stronger environment–gene-content correlations than human-associated species, despite a confirmed clinical sampling bias. [^ecotype_env_reanalysis] Its absolute median correlation differed from the original analysis because genome inclusion and downsampling differed, so the reanalysis cannot be used to compare absolute effect sizes across methods; its within-method group comparison remains valid. [^ecotype_env_reanalysis]

The original ecotype analysis introduces a related scale and interpretation tension: it reports a median environment partial correlation of 0.0025 versus 0.0143 for phylogeny and no significant environmental effect in 90.7% of species, whereas the ecotype reanalysis reports an all-species median partial correlation of 0.081 versus 0.003 in the original analysis. [^ecotype_analysis][^ecotype_env_reanalysis] These values must not be reconciled by averaging: the reanalysis attributes the discrepancy to different genome inclusion and downsampling, and the original analysis also had only 28.4% embedding coverage. [^ecotype_analysis][^ecotype_env_reanalysis] The evidence therefore supports a robust caution about portability and measurement coverage, but does not establish whether the remaining weak signal is primarily ecological, methodological, or confounded.

## Open Directions

- Recover DNA-extraction kit, filtration, size-fractionation, host-depletion, and library-preparation records from laboratory information systems, then fit hierarchical or batch-stratified models to test which protocol variables explain residual eukaryotic-fraction variation. [^euk_in_prok_correlates]
- Reanalyze the 2,759 runs with classifier-compatible reference databases and a common taxonomic estimator, testing whether environmental associations persist after database effects are controlled. [^euk_in_prok_correlates]
- Use additional NMDC studies with overlapping biome categories and study-held-out GroupKFold to test whether prediction remains above the original cross-study R²=−0.30. [^euk_in_prok_correlates]
- Reanalyze the 1,186-run NEON subset with sampling-campaign identifiers, restricted permutations, or mixed-effects models, testing whether the within-study R²=+0.17 ± 0.06 survives sub-batch control. [^euk_in_prok_correlates]
- Separate pooled and unpooled workflow runs, retain biosample-level protocol and environmental labels, and compare run-level models with measurement-error or partial-pooling models to quantify how the 1,067 pooled runs affect environmental estimates. [^euk_in_prok_correlates]
- Expand the analysis to studies with recorded sequencing depth and laboratory metadata, then use GroupKFold by study to ask whether protocol-aware models generalize beyond the dominant NEON soil study. [^euk_in_prok_correlates]
- Perform a paired downsampled-versus-full-genome extraction using the same species and environment labels to determine why the original and reanalysis median partial correlations were 0.003 and 0.081, respectively, without treating the 27x difference as a biological effect. [^ecotype_env_reanalysis]
- Add genome count as a covariate and repeat the environmental-versus-human comparison with structured ENVO terms and functional gene subsets, testing whether sampling depth, ontology resolution, or whole-genome Jaccard distances mask environment-specific associations. [^ecotype_env_reanalysis]
- Reanalyze the 172-species ecotype dataset with alternative embedding distances and direct environmental metadata, then test COG categories such as V-Defense and L-Mobile and compare gene content within ecotype clusters to determine whether subset-level effects are hidden by whole-genome distances. [^ecotype_analysis]
