# Coverage and Study Bias in Microbial Phenotype Databases

Coverage and study bias in microbial phenotype databases can make apparently broad phenotype–genotype associations reflect uneven sampling, sparse feature measurements, and phylogenetic composition rather than independent biological relationships. Phylogenetic confounding occurs when an apparent association between a phenotype and a genomic or ecological trait is produced primarily by lineage composition rather than by a consistent relationship within lineages. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] The [ecotype_analysis__REPORT](../summaries/ecotype_analysis__REPORT.md) provides complementary comparative evidence: across 172 bacterial species, phylogeny generally predicted whole-genome gene-content similarity more strongly than environmental similarity, while environmental effects were weak or non-significant. [^ecotype_analysis] This **supports** treating lineage composition as a primary confounder in broad phenotype associations, while **refining** the interpretation by suggesting that ecological adaptation may act on specific gene subsets rather than genome-wide content. [^ecotype_analysis]

The [ecotype_functional_differentiation__REPORT](../summaries/ecotype_functional_differentiation__REPORT.md) **supports and refines** this subset-level interpretation: PCA and KMeans clustering identified valid gene-content ecotypes in 12 of 15 sampled species, and 170 of 257 COG-category tests were significant after BH-FDR correction. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] Adaptive categories had larger mean effects than housekeeping categories (0.0136 versus 0.0064; Mann–Whitney U p = 2.53 x 10^-6), although effects were small and the analysis lacked within-species phylogenetic controls. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] Thus, weak genome-wide environmental associations need not imply that phenotype-relevant functional differences are absent, but functional differentiation itself remains potentially confounded by lineage or demographic structure. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation]

The [plant_microbiome_ecotypes__REPORT](../summaries/plant_microbiome_ecotypes__REPORT.md) **extends** this concern from phenotype and environmental databases to integrated plant-association analyses. Of 293,059 GTDB r214 genomes, only 7,995 (2.7%) had plant-associated annotations; compartment labels were inferred primarily from variable NCBI isolation_source metadata. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Its refined compartment analysis found a statistically significant but weak functional separation: PERMANOVA R² = 0.071, while location-only db-RDA R² = 0.060, with 84% of the PERMANOVA R² attributable to centroid shifts and 16% to dispersion. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The earlier R² = 0.527 fell to R² = 0.072 after excluding three genome-rich species per compartment, an 86% loss. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] This **supports** the existing warning that broad associations can be driven by taxonomic composition and sampling structure rather than community-wide ecological effects.

## Coverage of the BacDive–Pangenome Bridge

The [bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance__REPORT](../summaries/bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance__REPORT.md) provides a case study linking [bacdive](../entities/bacdive.md) phenotypes to genome-based metal-tolerance scores from the [metal-fitness-atlas](../entities/metal-fitness-atlas.md). The bridge contained 97,334 BacDive strains, of which 37,368 (38.4%) matched a pangenome species and metal score, covering 5,647 unique GTDB species; 3,994 species had at least 5 phenotype features and were used for the main comparative analyses. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] Species-name matching recovered 5,647 of 27,702 GTDB species (38.4%), and GCA accession matching was not implemented. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] Coverage varied substantially among the ten tested phenotype features: 43,378 strains had isolation-source data, 29,784 had metabolite-breadth data, 5,254 had H₂S-production data, and 1,980 had acetate-utilization data. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] Across the features, matched strains ranged from 22,581 to 475 and species with metal scores ranged from 4,531 to 422. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

These differences produce very different effective sample sizes and species compositions. The H₂S comparison illustrates the problem: only 8 H₂S-negative species were present in the matched set, making its observed effect size of d = -0.87 unreliable and likely inflated by small-sample bias. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] BacDive testing was also biased toward well-studied organisms, including Pseudomonas and Escherichia coli, whereas poorly studied species had sparser phenotype data. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

The [ecotype_analysis__REPORT](../summaries/ecotype_analysis__REPORT.md) identifies a related coverage limitation: AlphaEarth environmental embeddings covered only 28.4% of genomes, and geographic coordinates were often missing or imprecise. [^ecotype_analysis] This **supports** interpreting weak environmental associations as potentially coverage-limited rather than as evidence that environment is biologically irrelevant. [^ecotype_analysis] The [plant_microbiome_ecotypes__REPORT](../summaries/plant_microbiome_ecotypes__REPORT.md) **refines** this point: among 65 plant-associated species with at least 20 genomes, only 18 had phylogenetic-tree distance data, and only 17 met the final testability criteria. Five of those 17 passed Bonferroni correction, but the effect across all 65 candidates cannot be estimated until tree coverage improves. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The new ecotype-functional analysis **extends** this annotation-coverage concern from environmental metadata to functional phenotypes: approximately 38% of gene clusters had COG annotations, leaving 62% unannotated, so ecotype-specific adaptive genes may be missed or results biased toward better-characterized functions. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] Its significant differentiation in S (unknown function) in 11 of 12 species, with mean effect size 0.0392, indicates that precisely the least interpretable functional category can contribute strongly to observed differences. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] This **supports** treating database coverage and annotation coverage as distinct, compounding sources of missingness rather than assuming that measured phenotype breadth represents total functional breadth.

Plant-associated data add a second, independent coverage mismatch. Only 11.7% Jaccard overlap existed between 487 pangenome-derived plant genera and 438 MGnify rhizosphere genera because isolation metadata and metagenomic detection measure different processes. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The plant study also found that 5,341 of 5,671 eggNOG groups were significant in raw plant-versus-non-plant tests, but treated this near-saturation as evidence of broad taxonomic composition rather than plant-specific enrichment. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] After phylum-level control, 50 remained significant, and COG3569 occurred in 54% of plant-associated species versus 12% of non-plant species, with phylogenetically controlled OR = 6.01. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] This **supports** the existing distinction between database coverage and biological signal: apparent enrichment requires controls for taxonomic and genome-size composition.

The MicrobeAtlas analysis **refines** this interpretation by showing that environmental-range proxies have their own coverage and detection biases: Levins' B_std is based on sequencing detections across 13 environment categories, and genus aggregation can combine different species occupying different habitats rather than demonstrate that one organism is a generalist. [^microbeatlas_metal_ecology] In the strict 5% within-environment prevalence analysis, the PGLS sample fell from 606 to 379 genera and the positive metal-type association changed from β = +0.0215, p = 1.5×10⁻⁴ to β = +0.0166, p = 0.092, while the correlation between original and strict B_std was 0.680. [^microbeatlas_metal_ecology] This **supports** treating sparse environmental detections as an analogue of sparse phenotype measurements: direction can persist while inferential strength depends on coverage.

## Lineage Composition and Phenotype Associations

The strongest univariate association was between Gram stain and metal tolerance: Gram-negative species had higher scores than Gram-positive species, with Cohen’s d = -0.61, p < 1e-60, and n = 3,272 species. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] Seven of 10 tested features passed FDR correction, where FDR means false discovery rate: Gram stain (d = -0.610, q = 4.0e-60), oxidase (d = +0.530, q = 1.3e-24), motility (d = +0.345, q = 7.2e-23), urease (d = -0.175, q = 9.1e-06), enzyme breadth (rho = -0.058, q = 8.2e-04), nitrate reduction (d = +0.100, q = 7.4e-03), and catalase (d = +0.104, q = 4.1e-02). [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] H₂S production did not pass correction (d = -0.867, q = 7.3e-02), while metabolite breadth (rho = -0.013, q = 4.7e-01) and acetate utilization (d = +0.005, q = 7.9e-01) were not significant. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

Class-stratified analysis could not test the Gram-stain association within taxonomic classes because the signal was almost entirely between lineages, especially between Gram-positive Actinomycetes and Gram-negative Proteobacteria. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] This supports treating the apparent association as lineage-confounded, while remaining compatible with the hypothesis that Gram-negative cell-envelope architecture influences metal-cation uptake. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] The ecotype analysis **supports** this lineage-first interpretation at a broader genomic scale: the median partial correlation between environmental similarity and gene-content similarity was 0.0025 versus 0.0143 for phylogenetic similarity, and phylogeny dominated in 60.5% of species. [^ecotype_analysis] The MicrobeAtlas study **refines** it by finding strong phylogenetic conservation for niche breadth itself, with Pagel's λ = 0.787 for Levins' B_std and λ = 0.909 for habitat range across 1,264 genera. [^microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

The plant study **supports** the same lineage-first interpretation while showing why it should not be generalized to every functional subset. Refined compartment effects were small, and root communities were dominated by Rhizobium, Mesorhizobium, and Bradyrhizobium while phyllosphere communities were dominated by Sphingomonas and Methylobacterium. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Of 14 refined plant-association markers, only 8 remained significant in genus-clustered models, and within-genus label shuffling retained only nitrogen fixation, ACC deaminase, and T3SS. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Thus, apparent plant-compartment marker associations are mostly genus-scale or composition-sensitive, although the surviving markers remain candidates for within-lineage testing.

Urease-positive species had lower metal-tolerance scores, with d = -0.18 and p < 1e-5, reversing the prediction that urease-associated nickel handling would confer nickel tolerance. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] The effect was driven by Actinomycetes, where d = -0.59 and p < 1e-16, and disappeared within Gammaproteobacteria, where d = +0.08 and was not significant, and Bacilli, where d = +0.06 and was not significant. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] This supports phylogenetic confounding rather than a universal urease effect. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

Catalase showed a Simpson’s-paradox pattern: the overall association was positive, with d = +0.10, whereas catalase-negative species scored higher within Actinomycetes (d = -0.62, p < 1e-5), Gammaproteobacteria (d = -0.49, p = 0.004), and Betaproteobacteria (d = -0.51, p = 0.006). [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] The overall positive association therefore reflected between-class composition rather than a consistent within-class relationship. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

Oxygen tolerance provided little evidence for a biologically meaningful association: among 3,751 species with oxygen-tolerance data, the anaerobe–aerobe difference was negligible (d = -0.016, p = 0.55). [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] Facultative anaerobes had the highest mean score (0.221), compared with aerobes (0.216) and anaerobes (0.215), although the three-group Kruskal–Wallis test was marginally significant (H = 8.53, p = 0.014). [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

## Model Evidence for Coverage-Driven Interpretation

Taxonomy alone explained 35.4% of metal-tolerance variance, phenotype features alone explained 16.3%, and taxonomy plus phenotype yielded R² = 0.345, slightly below taxonomy alone, with delta R² = -0.009. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] Adding `n_metal_clusters`, the number of metal-resistance gene clusters, increased the full model to R² = 0.633. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] This supports [environmental-resistome](environmental-resistome.md) by showing that genome-encoded resistance repertoire added predictive information that the measured classical phenotypes did not add after taxonomy was included.

In 5-fold phylogenetic-blocked cross-validation, the gene-count-only model had R² = 0.063 and RMSE = 0.045; the phenotype-only model had R² = 0.163 and RMSE = 0.043; the taxonomy-only model had R² = 0.354 and RMSE = 0.038; the taxonomy-plus-phenotype model had R² = 0.345 and RMSE = 0.038; and the full model had R² = 0.633 and RMSE = 0.028. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] Every model used n = 3,994 species, with 1, 13, 3, 16, and 17 features, respectively. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

The full [xgboost](../entities/xgboost.md) model’s SHAP feature importance, where SHAP means Shapley additive explanation, placed taxonomic class/order codes and `n_metal_clusters` among the top predictors, while phenotype features contributed minimally after taxonomy was included. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] This supports interpreting the measured phenotypes primarily as phylogenetic proxies rather than independent predictors of the composite metal-tolerance score. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] Taxonomy can predict a composite score because lineage captures correlated genomic and physiological differences, but predictive performance does not establish that a measured phenotype causes or independently explains metal tolerance. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

The ecotype analysis **refines** this coverage-driven interpretation rather than establishing a general absence of environmental effects. Among 172 species, a significant positive environmental effect occurred in 12 species (7.0%), a significant negative effect in 4 species (2.3%), and no significant effect in 156 species (90.7%). [^ecotype_analysis] The analysis tested whole-genome gene-content similarity, whereas environmental adaptation may be concentrated in particular gene subsets. [^ecotype_analysis] Consistent with that possibility, all 12 species with valid ecotypes had at least one differentiated COG category, while adaptive categories were more strongly differentiated than housekeeping categories. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] However, KMeans assumes spherical clusters, HDBSCAN was unavailable, and no core-genome phylogenetic controls were applied, so this evidence does not by itself separate ecological adaptation from demographic structure. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation]

The plant study **supports and qualifies** this subset-level interpretation. Its refined analysis found 878 of 1,115 plant-associated species (78.7%) classified as dual-nature under stricter marker definitions, but the result was sensitive to genome size: the dual-nature rate was 54% in the lowest genome-size quartile versus 87% in the top three quartiles, and approximately 33% of dual-nature species shifted to neutral after an earlier genome-size normalization. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] In a curated panel, all 14 known beneficial or pathogenic organisms were classified as dual-nature and all four neutral controls were misclassified. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] This **supports** treating marker-based phenotype classes as coverage- and definition-sensitive screens rather than validated phenotype labels.

The MicrobeAtlas PGLS provides a related example in which a coverage-sensitive ecological trait nevertheless retained an association after phylogenetic adjustment: among 606 genera, metal type diversity remained positive after adding pangenome coverage, with β = +0.0204, SE = 0.0057, p = 3.4×10⁻⁴ in the simple model and β = +0.0224, SE = 0.0067, p = 8.3×10⁻⁴ in the multi-predictor model. [^microbeatlas_metal_ecology] This **supports** using explicit coverage covariates and phylogenetic models rather than treating database breadth as either purely technical or purely biological. [^microbeatlas_metal_ecology] However, the association was correlational, and the report could not distinguish metal-resistance effects from reverse acquisition or shared factors such as genome size, metabolic versatility, or biofilm capacity. [^microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

## Direct-Validation Coverage Limits

The direct Fitness Browser–BacDive validation contained 12 organisms representing 6 unique species: Cupriavidus basilensis, Methanococcus maripaludis, Ralstonia solanacearum, Pseudomonas simiae, Azospirillum brasilense, and Pseudomonas fluorescens. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] All Gram-typed organisms were Gram-negative, preventing within-set testing of the strongest Gram-stain association. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] All urease-typed organisms were urease-negative despite being routinely tested against nickel. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] The single anaerobe, Methanococcus maripaludis, had only 1 metal tested versus 4–5 for aerobes, so n = 1 was not interpretable. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

The direct validation therefore lacked balanced coverage for the key phenotype contrasts. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] These limitations connect to [cultivation-collection-bias-in-ecological-genomics](cultivation-collection-bias-in-ecological-genomics.md) and [environmental-resistome](environmental-resistome.md). [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] The Metal Fitness Atlas scores were genome-based predictions rather than direct metal-tolerance measurements, so the associations remain phenotype-to-genome correlations rather than phenotype-to-phenotype validation. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] This refines the concerns in [circularity-in-metabolic-model-validation](circularity-in-metabolic-model-validation.md) and [evidence-triangulation-for-functional-annotation](evidence-triangulation-for-functional-annotation.md) about distinguishing mechanism from correlated representation. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

The plant study **supports** this direct-validation warning: GapMind pathway predictions agreed with BacDive metabolite-utilization data at 83.1%, but the GapMind core-level completeness score was 0% across compartments and may be too stringent for broad taxonomic comparisons. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Its complementarity analysis was also structurally underpowered, with 0 PGP-dominant, 3 pathogen-dominant, and 66 dual-or-mixed genera among 69 NMDC co-occurring genera. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Agreement between databases therefore increases confidence in measurement compatibility without establishing that the inferred pathway or phenotype is expressed in the ecological context.

MicrobeAtlas adds a second direct-validation coverage constraint: in its community-level groundwater analysis, only 16.8% of reads were joinable to genus-level GTDB metal-diversity data, leaving approximately 83% uncovered. [^microbeatlas_metal_ecology] This **supports** the existing concern that apparent environmental associations can depend on which organisms have usable annotations, and it motivates testing whether covered-read fraction correlates with community-weighted resistance estimates. [^microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

## Tensions

Seven of ten phenotype features were significant after FDR correction, but phenotype features alone explained 16.3% of variance, taxonomy alone explained 35.4%, and adding phenotype features to taxonomy reduced the model to R² = 0.345. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] This is a tension between widespread univariate significance and limited evidence that the phenotypes add independent predictive information after taxonomic structure is accounted for. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

The univariate Gram-stain association supports the hypothesis that cell-envelope architecture may influence metal tolerance, while lineage structure prevents separating that mechanism from taxonomic composition. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] Likewise, the overall positive catalase association contradicts the negative within-class associations in Actinomycetes, Gammaproteobacteria, and Betaproteobacteria; the corpus therefore does not support one catalase direction across lineages. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

The ecotype analysis found that phylogeny dominated gene-content similarity in 60.5% of species, but environment dominated in 39.5%; significant environmental effects were detected in only 16 of 172 species. [^ecotype_analysis] This **refines** rather than contradicts the phenotype database result: lineage is often dominant at genome-wide scale, but the nonzero environmental-dominant subset and the possibility of subset-specific adaptation caution against treating weak whole-genome environmental effects as universal evidence of no ecological structuring. [^ecotype_analysis] The ecotype-functional study further **supports** functional subset differentiation, but its lack of phylogenetic controls creates a tension: COG differences may represent adaptive ecotypes or lineage-linked gene-content structure. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation]

The plant study creates a related tension. It found small compartment effects after adversarial correction, while also finding strong apparent plant-associated marker and gene-family differences before and after some controls. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The retained signals may reflect genuine plant-associated functions, but the 86% loss of the original compartment R² after removing genome-rich species and the limited phylogenetic-tree coverage prevent separating ecological adaptation from taxonomic sampling across the full cohort. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The composite-score analysis cannot establish metal-specific mechanisms: per-metal scores are needed to test whether catalase predicts copper, urease predicts nickel, or H₂S predicts zinc, copper, and cadmium tolerance. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] This refines the connection to [condition-specific-fitness](condition-specific-fitness.md), because a phenotype may have a specific effect under one metal condition without predicting a composite score across metals. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

The MicrobeAtlas result creates a related coverage tension rather than resolving the database-bias problem: metal type diversity remained associated with niche breadth after phylogenetic adjustment and coverage control, but the association weakened and lost significance under strict within-environment prevalence filtering (β = +0.0166, p = 0.092). [^microbeatlas_metal_ecology] Thus, the result **supports** a potentially reproducible ecological signal while **retaining** the competing explanation that detection and sampling coverage influence its strength. [^microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

## Open Directions

- Apply GCA accession matching to BacDive and GTDB records, then quantify whether recovered species alter feature-level coverage and the Gram-stain, urease, and catalase associations. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]
- Use PGLS, meaning phylogenetic generalized least squares, or phylogenetic PCA on the matched 3,994-species set to test whether phenotype associations persist after phylogenetic signal is removed. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]
- Reweight or stratify analyses by phenotype-test coverage and taxonomic representation, then test whether model rankings and exact effect estimates change when well-studied lineages cannot dominate. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]
- Replace the composite score with per-metal scores and use within-class regression to test whether catalase predicts copper, urease predicts nickel, and H₂S predicts zinc, copper, or cadmium tolerance. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]
- Add BacDive machine-learning-predicted phenotypes and compare them with experimentally recorded phenotypes to determine whether expanded coverage introduces model-dependent bias. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]
- Assemble urease-positive and urease-negative organisms from the same taxonomic classes and test them under nickel and other metals with RB-TnSeq, meaning random barcode transposon sequencing, to distinguish nickel-specific effects from general lineage differences. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]
- Expand direct Fitness Browser–BacDive validation across Gram-positive and Gram-negative organisms, urease-positive organisms, multiple oxygen-tolerance states, anaerobes, and balanced metal panels to determine whether database associations reproduce in directly measured metal-fitness data. [^bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]
- Reanalyze the 172-species ecotype set with alternative environmental distances and direct metadata, then test COG functional categories and ecotype-cluster gene content to determine whether the weak whole-genome environmental signal masks subset-specific associations. [^ecotype_analysis]
- Scale the ecotype-functional analysis from the 15-species sample to all 456 eligible species, overlay core-genome phylogenies, and compare KMeans with HDBSCAN to test whether COG differentiation persists after cluster geometry and lineage structure are addressed. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- Characterize the unannotated S-category genes with AlphaFold or domain analysis and relate them to phenotype databases, testing whether annotation missingness is concentrated in ecotype-specific functions. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- Estimate rarefaction curves across multiple species-per-genus thresholds in MicrobeAtlas and test whether metal-type coefficients stabilize as pangenome coverage increases. [^microbeatlas_metal_ecology]
- Recompute community-weighted metal diversity after relating covered-read fraction to the estimate, and subdivide heterogeneous aquatic categories into marine, freshwater, and saline environments. [^microbeatlas_metal_ecology]
- Fit a sparse or subsampled full PGLMM using GTDB-tree covariance to test whether plant-compartment and host associations persist after lineage structure is modeled, and expand tree coverage for the 47 currently unrepresented candidate species. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
- Compare pangenome-derived and MGnify-derived plant associations after harmonizing genus, genome, and environmental detection units; test whether the 11.7% Jaccard overlap reflects sampling coverage, taxonomic resolution, or genuine ecological differences. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
- Validate plant marker panels with transcriptomics and condition-specific experiments, testing whether dual-nature marker sets are co-expressed rather than merely co-occurring in larger genomes. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
