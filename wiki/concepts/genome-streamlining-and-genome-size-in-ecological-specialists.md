# Genome streamlining and genome size in ecological specialists

This page covers genome streamlining and genome size in ecological specialists. Deep-clay Bacillota_B genomes are about 1 Mbp larger than soil relatives, so streamlining is not universal in subsurface lineages. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] The report on discoveries states that the Patescibacteria/CPR streamlining pattern does not generalize to cultivable subsurface Firmicutes. [src: discoveries] Across genera, specialists have smaller genomes, so conserved gene sets look denser per Mb and housekeeping controls are not true nulls. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] The metal-gene report reads this as a pervasive streamlining effect, and genome size is the only true confounder it finds. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] The Bacillota_B evidence is a within-phylum comparison with a small anchor group (n=10). [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] Part of the niche-breadth evidence is labelled exploratory. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology]

## Literature Context

The candidate papers mostly support the idea that ecological specialism goes with smaller genomes. In plant-associated bacteria, host specificity across 329 tree species was negatively correlated with bacterial genome size and local host abundance, and the authors tie this to genome streamlining [PMID 38844351](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38844351/). Cryoconite specialists with smaller genome size and lower gene redundancy were more abundant under high radiation stress, which the authors read as streamlined genomes being better adapted [PMID 37848997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37848997/). In 317 cyanobacterial MAGs, genome minimization (<3 Mbp) was linked to dominance under phosphorus scarcity [PMID 41271554](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41271554/). A free-living Polynucleobacter population had a small genome (2.1 Mb) and a passive lifestyle. The authors warn against generalizing from this one lineage [PMID 22448227](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22448227/). Streamlining also appears in Endozoicomonadaceae symbionts, where a sister clade shows reduced genome size and fewer orthogroups, consistent with host specialization [PMID 42338645](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42338645/). It appears in Antarctic endophytic fungi too, with reduced genome size (~26.3 Mb on average) and small secretomes [PMID 41921590](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41921590/). A pesticide-gradient study suggests that selective pressure may push bacteria to streamline their average genome size [PMID 39786931](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39786931/). Within Thermosipho, genome size varied among species, and the more widely distributed generalist T. africanus had the largest genomes and the most carbohydrate metabolism genes, although all genomes showed streamlining [PMID 30239713](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30239713/). The tomato russet mite shows the same pattern in an animal, with an extremely reduced environmental-response gene repertoire in line with specialization theory [PMID 33095158](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33095158/).

The corpus's genus-level finding is consistent with this literature. Specialists carry smaller genomes, so conserved gene sets such as ribosomal proteins, amino acid biosynthesis and DNA repair become denser per Mb (β from −0.029 to −0.034). The corpus reads these as a streamlining baseline rather than true nulls. The Thermosipho result fits its carbohydrate reading. Generalists with larger genomes carry more carbohydrate genes, which matches the opposite signs of per-Mb density and GapMind breadth. The Thermosipho paper does not test that reconciliation, and the corpus itself calls it exploratory. The papers above do not test housekeeping controls as a baseline. That framing, and the coreness-matched permutation (emp_p = 0.298), have no counterpart in the candidates. They are best treated as a methodological extension.

The corpus's deep-clay Bacillota_B result sits in tension with a simple streamlining-by-specialism reading. Anchor genomes (n=10) were about 1 Mbp larger than soil relatives (4,110,038 bp versus 3,046,124 bp). None of the candidates examines subsurface Firmicutes, so this within-phylum expansion is novel relative to them. The candidates are also mostly correlative, and several are single-lineage or abstract-level findings. They show that streamlining is common among specialists across lineages and habitats, not universal. The report's own reading is that the Bacillota_B expansion reflects a lineage-specific exception, and that conclusion remains unresolved.

## Subsurface specialists can have larger genomes

Deep-clay [[entities/bacillota-b]] genomes were larger than soil-baseline genomes, the opposite of the streamlining prediction. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] Mean genome size was 4,110,038 bp in the deep-clay anchor (n=10) and 3,046,124 bp in the soil baseline (n=62), with Cohen's d +1.39 and Mann–Whitney p 0.025. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] The anchor includes Mont Terri Opalinus and Ca. Desulforudis audaxviator organisms. [src: discoveries] CheckM-rescaled size was 4,323,230 versus 3,233,715, with d +1.37 and p 0.013. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] The discoveries digest gives the same comparison as 4.3 Mbp versus 3.2 Mbp, with the anchor 35% larger. [src: discoveries] Mean eggNOG ortholog-group (OG) content was 2,630 versus 2,106 (d +1.30, p 0.022). [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] The CheckM-rescaled values were 2,771 versus 2,233 (d +1.32, p 0.009). [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory, discoveries] All four size and OG-count metrics were significantly larger in the anchor (p ≤ 0.025), with Cohen's d ≥ +1.30. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

Two controls limit the interpretation. GC content did not differ: 48.84% in the anchor versus 47.76% in the baseline (d +0.21, p 0.44). [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] Mean CheckM completeness was also similar: 94.7% versus 94.3% (d +0.08, p 0.93). [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory, discoveries] The report therefore concludes that the size difference cannot be a MAG-quality artefact. It states that deep-clay Bacillota_B encode ~1 Mbp more genetic material at the same QC (quality control) level. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

The report proposes, but does not do, a COG (clusters of orthologous groups) and KEGG-pathway breakdown of the extra gene fraction. It names mobile-element-borne accessory operons, sporulation regulons and anaerobic-respiration accessories as possible mechanisms. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

### Relation to the streamlining literature

The report argues this does not truly contradict Tian et al. (2020). That "small is mighty" finding was specific to Patescibacteria/CPR (candidate phyla radiation), with ≤1 Mbp genomes and an episymbiotic lifestyle. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] The report says Tian notes that non-CPR phyla retain larger genomes. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] See [[entities/patescibacteria]]. The report reads its result as consistent with Tian's framing: streamlining is CPR-specific, and cultivable subsurface Firmicutes show the opposite pattern. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] The discoveries digest frames streamlining as a Patescibacteria/CPR-specific adaptation to episymbiotic life on a host. It says cultivable subsurface Firmicutes show the opposite pattern under the same nominal subsurface oligotrophy. [src: discoveries] The report describes free-living anaerobic Bacillota_B as a niche where deep-clay specialization correlates with gene-content expansion, not reduction. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

The report also reads the expansion as consistent with the self-sufficiency hypothesis of Beaver & Neufeld (2024). It cites Becraft et al. (2021), who found a self-sufficient *Ca.* Desulforudis audaxviator genome retaining full N-fixation, amino-acid biosynthesis and C-fixation without streamlining. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory, discoveries] Bacillota_B in deep-clay habitats encode ~25% more orthogroups than soil congeners, which the report presents as extending a single-lineage finding to a within-phylum comparison. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] It calls this a statistically supported refutation of "subsurface = streamlining" within cultivable Firmicutes. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] See [[concepts/biosynthetic-self-sufficiency-as-a-deep-subsurface-adaptation]] and [[entities/candidatus-desulforudis-audaxviator]].

A separate report cites other critiques of streamlining as a universal subsurface adaptation. Props et al. 2019 documents expansion and positive selection in oligotrophic engineered cooling water. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] Cortez et al. 2022 shows streamlining is acidophile-specific. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] In the opposite direction, Chaudhari et al. (2024) demonstrated streamlining in Parcubacteria moving from soil to groundwater, with smaller genomes and higher pseudogene fractions in groundwater variants. [src: oak_ridge_cultivation_gap]

## Genome size in marine and soil specialists (cited literature)

The metal-gene report cites several studies that link specialism to smaller genomes. These are literature citations, not measurements by the report. Rastogi et al. (2023) found that marine bacterioplankton generalists (B = 11.5) and specialists (B = 8.9) differ by ~29% in Levins' niche breadth. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] Specialist niches were shaped by deterministic selection (54.1%), versus 62.8% stochastic assembly for generalists. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] Leu et al. (2022) reported average genome sizes of 3.0–4.5 Mb for free-living oligotrophic specialists and 4.5–5.2 Mb for particle-associated generalists, a 24–35% size difference. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] Generalists carry 2–5-fold more CAZymes (carbohydrate-active enzymes) and 8+ substrate-class transporters per genome. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] Lauro et al. (2009) found comparable size reductions in SAR11 and *Prochlorococcus* specialists, with gene loss concentrated in regulation and secondary metabolism. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] Szukics et al. (2024), using a modified Levins' B in soil Thaumarchaeota, found the generalist-to-specialist transition occurs at a 6-fold higher rate than the reverse. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology]

## Streamlining inflates per-Mb density of conserved gene sets

Across prokaryotic genera, specialists carry smaller genomes, so near-universal gene sets look denser per Mb. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] The metal-gene report states this as ecological specialists reducing their genomes across the board, so any conserved gene set becomes denser per Mb. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] The three pre-specified negative controls (ribosomal proteins, amino acid biosynthesis, DNA repair) all showed highly significant negative associations with niche breadth, not the near-zero values anticipated. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] Their β values ranged from −0.029 to −0.034, which established a streamlining baseline rather than true nulls. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] They show the streamlining signature at the same or greater magnitude as the metal-gene set: ribosomal proteins β = −0.029, amino acid biosynthesis β = −0.034 and DNA repair β = −0.033. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] The report states that these are streamlining indicators, not true negative controls, because a valid negative control needs a per-Mb density that does not scale with specialisation. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] The report concludes that the appropriate null for the metal-gene signal is the constitutive housekeeping β (≈ −0.029 to −0.035), not zero. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology]

### Where the metal-gene signal sits

The metal-gene association (β = −0.021) ranks 11th of 19, roughly tied with lipid metabolism. It is 30–60% weaker than the housekeeping baseline, not anomalously strong. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] The report gives the same gap as P1 β = −0.021 versus housekeeping β ≈ −0.029 to −0.035. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] A coreness-matched permutation (NB20; 1,000 KO sets matched on count and coreness decile) gave a null β median of −0.018 (range −0.039 to +0.012). The observed β = −0.021 gave emp_p = 0.298. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] The overall association is thus not distinguishable from coreness-matched alternatives. The report places it within the pervasive streamlining landscape rather than a metal-specific phenomenon. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] The report states that this permutation does not support uniqueness of the metal gene set among conserved gene categories. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] It locates the distinctiveness of the metal gene set in its internal functional split, not its overall magnitude. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology]

The report offers an interpretation of the weaker signal. Specialists do not compact all genes equally. They may retain higher per-Mb metal-gene density than uniform compaction would give, consistent with selective retention of metal-processing functions. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] The report states this as a genuine content signal: specialists are specifically enriched in metal-processing genes above what genome-size-driven compaction predicts. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] It reads the 30–60% gap as metal genes being less compacted than housekeeping genes, consistent with selective retention. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] This is an interpretation, not a tested result. The report says the housekeeping comparison is mechanistically motivated and addresses a different question from the coreness permutation, so emp_p = 0.298 does not negate the housekeeping gap. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] It also says the functional landscape is correlational: the evidence supports metal-content enrichment above compaction, but causality remains unresolved. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] The report proposes to separate streamlining-driven from metal-content-specific effects by modelling niche breadth on a compaction index built from true-negative category densities, plus metal-gene density residualised against that index. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] One related null: across 293,059 prokaryotic genomes, the core fraction at the 95% prevalence threshold was 0 for all five metal functional categories, because no metal KO met the near-universal threshold. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] The report's NB23 analysis draws from this that coreness cannot explain functional specificity. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology]

### Genome size as a covariate

In the conditional PGLS (phylogenetic generalized least squares) model, the baseline β was −0.0207 (p = 2.1×10⁻⁸). [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] Adding genome size gave β = −0.0110 (p = 0.006), a −46.7% true attenuation. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] The report classes this as robust, below its 50% threshold (0.021 → 0.011). [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] It reads the surviving significance as showing the association is not simply a proxy for the small-genome specialist pattern. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] It also reads the result as small-genome specialists investing a disproportionately high fraction of their genomes in metal processing, beyond what genome size alone predicts. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] The attenuation is not eliminated by explicit genome-size correction. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] The report says 46.7% is within the expected range for streamlining indicators, not an outlier needing special explanation. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] Adding annotation depth instead amplified β to −0.0304 (p = 7.9×10⁻¹¹), a +46.7% suppressor effect. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] The report reads this as amplification, not attenuation, because genera with richer annotations have fewer apparent metal-gene specialists. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] It calls genome size the only true confounder. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] The annotation-depth covariate n_ko_ann_z and the KO-breadth covariate ko_breadth_z are perfectly collinear, since both derive from n_ko_primary, so either gives the same conditional result. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology]

The permutation comparison limits this reading. The observed 46.7% attenuation fell within the permuted 95% CI (confidence interval) [43.9%, 318.2%] (permuted median 94.9%). [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] The report calls this only weak evidence because the interval is wide. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology]

### Carbohydrate metrics point in opposite directions

Two carbohydrate-related measures have opposite signs against niche breadth. Carbohydrate KO density per Mb was negatively associated with niche breadth in NB18 (β = −0.026, p = 1.3 × 10⁻⁹). [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] GapMind carbon breadth was positively associated in microbeatlas_metal_ecology NB07 (β = +0.142, SE = 0.033, p = 1.9 × 10⁻⁵). [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] The report calls these geometrically consistent. Per-Mb density is a ratio with genome length in the denominator, so streamlining raises density even as the absolute carbohydrate gene complement falls. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] GapMind pathway completeness is a qualitative repertoire metric, counting whether full metabolic pathways are present, and it grows with generalism. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] Specialists therefore show high carbohydrate KO density per Mb and low GapMind breadth, and generalists the converse. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] This reconciliation is exploratory, compares independently computed cross-study metrics, and needs an integrated genome-size-partitioned analysis. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] See [[entities/gapmind]].

## Genome size in cultured versus MAG cohorts

In the Oak Ridge cultivation-gap report, cultured genomes average 5.8 Mbp and MAGs (metagenome assembled genomes) about 1.5 Mbp (median). [src: oak_ridge_cultivation_gap] The report attributes the MAG median to CPR dominance. [src: oak_ridge_cultivation_gap] It names the 4-fold genome size difference as the dominant driver of the bias distribution. [src: oak_ridge_cultivation_gap] It did not run a genome-size-normalized analysis, such as comparing KOs within size-matched phyla, that would separate cultivation selection from streamlining. [src: oak_ridge_cultivation_gap] See [[concepts/genome-size-and-taxonomic-composition-confounding-of-cultured-versus-mag-functional-comparisons]].

## Tensions

- Larger genomes in specialists versus smaller ones: deep-clay Bacillota_B genomes are larger than soil relatives [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory], while across genera, specialists have smaller genomes and denser conserved gene sets. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] The reports differ in scope: one is a within-phylum comparison, the other a genus-level niche-breadth analysis. Neither report resolves this.
- Whether the metal-gene signal is special: the coreness-matched permutation finds the overall β within the null (emp_p = 0.298) [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology], while the report reads the 30–60% gap from housekeeping as a genuine content signal. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] The report says the two address different questions. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology]

## Open Directions

- Break down the extra Bacillota_B gene fraction by COG and KEGG pathway to see whether it is mobile-element accessory operons, sporulation regulons or anaerobic-respiration accessories. This would close the open question of where the extra ~1 Mbp sits.
- Repeat the per-Mb niche-breadth analysis within Bacillota_B alone, so the genus-level streamlining baseline can be compared with the within-phylum expansion.
- Use a larger or independent deep-clay anchor set than n=10 to test whether the size difference holds.
- Rerun the genome-size attenuation permutation with a more informative design, since the current CI [43.9%, 318.2%] is too wide to be decisive.
- Fit the proposed compaction-index model to partition the metal-gene β into streamlining-driven and metal-content-specific parts, which would test the selective-retention interpretation.
- Run a genome-size-partitioned analysis of carbohydrate KO density and GapMind breadth together, and a size-matched-phyla cultured-versus-MAG comparison, to close the gaps the reports leave.
