A target-priority score ranks taxa by how strongly they are implicated in disease, but it says nothing about what the community loses when that taxon is removed. In the IBD phage-targeting work the dominant such cost axis is explicit: the analysis identifies a **bile-acid 7α-dehydroxylation network** — the conversion of primary bile acids into secondary, unconjugated forms — comprising [[entities/flavonifractor-plautii]], [[entities/eggerthella-lenta]] and [[entities/enterocloster-bolteae]] as its principal per-target ecological-cost axis in paired [[entities/hmp2]] samples [src: ibd_phage_targeting].

## Per-target annotation axes a headline score does not carry

The cross-project digest argues that pathobiont-targeting interventions need three axes of per-target annotation alongside the headline Tier-A priority score: the iron/AIEC (adherent-invasive *E. coli*) versus other Crohn's-disease specialization mechanism, the bile-acid coupling cost, and whether an association is mediated by species abundance or by strain content [src: discoveries]. This is a design caveat rather than a measurement: it states what a ranking must be annotated with, not a result about any one taxon [src: discoveries].

The first axis is a question of targeting granularity: distinguishing an iron/AIEC-mediated specialization mechanism from other Crohn's-disease mechanisms informs whether an intervention should target an entire species broadly or specifically its AIEC subset [src: discoveries]. Framed that way, the mechanism question developed in iron acquisition and pathobiont expansion becomes an operational choice about target resolution, and it **refines** the translation problem framed in [[concepts/phage-therapy-evidence-translation]] by making target breadth, not just target identity, the decision [src: discoveries].

## The bile-acid 7α-dehydroxylation network

The second axis is instantiated concretely. *F. plautii*, *E. lenta* and *E. bolteae* are reported as active [[entities/bile-acid-7alpha-dehydroxylation]] taxa, while [[entities/mediterraneibacter-gnavus]] and [[entities/escherichia-coli]] are not; because of this, depleting active 7α-dehydroxylators is expected to shift the bile-acid pool toward inflammatory primary tauro-conjugated forms [src: discoveries]. That last step is an inference from substrate–product structure rather than a measured intervention outcome, so it should be read as the hypothesis that removing an active dehydroxylator reverses the direction of the pool it maintains — a prediction that connects target choice to the inflammatory metabolite classes catalogued in [[concepts/metabolite-class-signatures-of-gut-inflammation]] [src: discoveries].

The network assignment rests on correlational evidence. In 468 paired CSM* HMP2 samples, *F. plautii*, *E. lenta* and *E. bolteae* show the predicted substrate–product signature: negative [[entities/spearman-correlation]] with primary tauro-conjugated bile acids (substrates: cholate at ρ=−0.26 for *F. plautii*) and positive correlation with secondary unconjugated bile acids (products: lithocholate at ρ=+0.18 for *E. bolteae*, +0.15 for *F. plautii*; deoxycholate +0.17 for *E. bolteae*) [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. These are observational sample-level correlations within one cohort rather than enzymatic assays, which places the membership call at the level of a detectable substrate–product signature, as the project itself describes it [src: ibd_phage_targeting].

The negative side of the comparison carries its own information: *M. gnavus* and *E. coli* show the opposite correlation pattern, confirming they are **not** in the 7α-dehydroxylation network [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. This **supports** the digest's split of the same five taxa into active and inactive dehydroxylators [src: discoveries], and it means the bile-acid coupling cost is an axis that discriminates among candidate targets rather than a blanket penalty on pathobiont depletion [src: discoveries]. It does not license the stronger reading that depleting an inactive taxon leaves bile-acid metabolism untouched: exclusion from this network means the direct substrate–product coupling was not detected, and no source here tests indirect effects of removing *M. gnavus* or *E. coli* on the pool [src: ibd_phage_targeting].

Within the project, this axis displaced an earlier one. The prior "metabolic-coupling cost" annotation was based on species-level co-occurrence, and the paired sample-level test failed to identify a metabolic intermediate that supports cross-feeding — lactate has opposite signs between *A. caccae* and the pathobionts, with only 7 strict triangles total — so the bile-acid network was adopted in its place as the more mechanistically grounded cost annotation [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. The replacement is a change of evidence class, from co-occurrence to a sample-level substrate–product signature, not a contradiction between two findings [src: ibd_phage_targeting].

## Open Directions

- Test the depletion prediction directly rather than by correlation: in a gnotobiotic or *ex vivo* community, remove an active dehydroxylator (*F. plautii*, *E. lenta* or *E. bolteae*) and measure whether the bile-acid pool shifts toward primary tauro-conjugated forms, closing the gap between a substrate–product correlation and a causal ecological cost [src: ibd_phage_targeting].
- Rank the three annotation axes against each other by scoring the same candidate target list on iron/AIEC mechanism, bile-acid coupling and species-abundance-versus-strain-content mediation, and checking whether they reorder targets concordantly or pull in different directions — the digest requires all three but does not say which binds [src: discoveries].
- Resolve the AIEC-subset question at strain resolution: stratify the *E. coli* signal into AIEC and non-AIEC strain content within the same cohort to establish whether species-level depletion is warranted, which is the choice the mechanism axis is meant to decide [src: discoveries].
- Measure whether depleting a taxon outside the network (*M. gnavus* or *E. coli*) perturbs the bile-acid pool indirectly, since the opposite-sign correlations establish only that these taxa lack the direct substrate–product signature [src: ibd_phage_targeting].
- Replicate the reported 468 paired CSM* correlations in an independent cohort; that specific quantitative support comes from a single paired metagenome–metabolome comparison, even though the project frames its bile-acid result as a multi-line convergence narrative [src: ibd_phage_targeting].
