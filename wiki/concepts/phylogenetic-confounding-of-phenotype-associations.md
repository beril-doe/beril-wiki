# Phylogenetic Confounding of Microbial Phenotype Associations

Phylogenetic confounding occurs when an apparent association between a microbial phenotype and a genomic or ecological trait is produced primarily by differences in lineage composition rather than by a consistent relationship within lineages. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

The [[summaries/bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance__REPORT]] provides a direct example using [[entities/bacdive]] phenotypes and genome-based metal-tolerance scores from the [[entities/metal-fitness-atlas]]. The bridge contained 97,334 BacDive strains, of which 37,368 (38.4%) matched a pangenome species and metal score, covering 5,647 unique GTDB species. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] Among these, 3,994 species had at least 5 phenotype features and were used for the main comparative analyses. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

## Evidence from Metal-Tolerance Phenotypes

Gram-negative species had higher metal-tolerance scores than Gram-positive species, with Cohen's d = -0.61, p < 1e-60, and n = 3,272 species. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] This was the largest effect among the tested phenotype features, but class-stratified analysis could not test the association within taxonomic classes because the signal was almost entirely between lineages, especially between Gram-positive Actinomycetes and Gram-negative Proteobacteria. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] The result is therefore compatible with a mechanistic role for the Gram-negative outer membrane in restricting metal-cation uptake, but the observed association is statistically confounded with phylogeny. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

Seven of 10 tested features passed FDR correction, where FDR means false discovery rate: Gram stain (d = -0.610, q = 4.0e-60), oxidase (d = +0.530, q = 1.3e-24), motility (d = +0.345, q = 7.2e-23), urease (d = -0.175, q = 9.1e-06), enzyme breadth (rho = -0.058, q = 8.2e-04), nitrate reduction (d = +0.100, q = 7.4e-03), and catalase (d = +0.104, q = 4.1e-02). [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] H₂S production did not pass correction (d = -0.867, q = 7.3e-02), while metabolite breadth (rho = -0.013, q = 4.7e-01) and acetate utilization (d = +0.005, q = 7.9e-01) were not significant. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

The urease result demonstrates why an aggregate association should not be interpreted as a universal phenotype mechanism. Urease-positive species had lower metal-tolerance scores (d = -0.18, p < 1e-5), reversing the prediction that urease-associated nickel handling would confer nickel tolerance. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] The effect was driven by Actinomycetes (d = -0.59, p < 1e-16) and disappeared within Gammaproteobacteria (d = +0.08, ns) and Bacilli (d = +0.06, ns), supporting phylogenetic confounding rather than a general urease effect. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

Catalase showed a Simpson's-paradox pattern, in which the aggregate direction differed from the direction within several lineages. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] The overall association was positive (d = +0.10), whereas catalase-negative species scored higher within Actinomycetes (d = -0.62, p < 1e-5), Gammaproteobacteria (d = -0.49, p = 0.004), and Betaproteobacteria (d = -0.51, p = 0.006). [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] The overall positive association therefore reflected between-class composition rather than a consistent within-class relationship. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

By contrast, oxygen tolerance provided little evidence for a biologically meaningful association. Among 3,751 species with oxygen-tolerance data, the anaerobe-aerobe difference was negligible (d = -0.016, p = 0.55). [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] Facultative anaerobes had the highest mean score (0.221), compared with aerobes (0.216) and anaerobes (0.215), although the three-group Kruskal-Wallis test was marginally significant (H = 8.53, p = 0.014). [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

## Predictive Models Separate Lineage from Phenotype

Taxonomy alone explained 35.4% of metal-tolerance variance, phenotype features alone explained 16.3%, and taxonomy plus phenotype yielded R² = 0.345, slightly below taxonomy alone; the phenotype-plus-taxonomy change was delta R² = -0.009. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] Adding the number of metal-resistance gene clusters, `n_metal_clusters`, increased the full model to R² = 0.633, indicating that genome-encoded resistance repertoire was the strongest predictor in this analysis. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] This supports [[concepts/environmental-resistome]] by showing that the resistance-gene repertoire added predictive information that the measured classical phenotypes did not add after taxonomy was included. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

In 5-fold phylogenetic-blocked cross-validation, the gene-count-only model had R² = 0.063 and RMSE = 0.045; phenotype only had R² = 0.163 and RMSE = 0.043; taxonomy only had R² = 0.354 and RMSE = 0.038; taxonomy plus phenotype had R² = 0.345 and RMSE = 0.038; and the full model had R² = 0.633 and RMSE = 0.028. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] Every model used n = 3,994 species, and the models used 1, 13, 3, 16, and 17 features, respectively. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

The full [[entities/xgboost]] model's SHAP feature importance, where SHAP means Shapley additive explanation, placed taxonomic class/order codes and `n_metal_clusters` among the top predictors. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] Phenotype features contributed minimally after taxonomy was included, supporting the interpretation that the measured phenotypes were primarily phylogenetic proxies rather than independent predictors of the composite metal-tolerance score. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

## Implications for Microbial Association Studies

A significant phenotype–trait association across species is not sufficient evidence for a phenotype mechanism when the phenotype is unevenly distributed across lineages. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] The urease and catalase results specifically support testing whether an association persists within taxonomic groups or after an explicit phylogenetic correction. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] This refines [[concepts/phylogenetic-confounding-of-pangenome-associations]] by showing the same inferential problem for measured microbiological phenotypes rather than only for pangenome features. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

The phenotype results also expose a distinction between predictive performance and biological interpretation. Taxonomy can predict a composite score because lineage captures correlated genomic and physiological differences, but that predictive value does not establish that the measured phenotype causes or independently explains metal tolerance. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] The genome-based Metal Fitness Atlas scores are predictions rather than direct metal-tolerance measurements, so these results remain phenotype-to-genome correlations rather than phenotype-to-phenotype validation. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] This caveat connects to [[concepts/circularity-in-metabolic-model-validation]] and [[concepts/evidence-triangulation-for-functional-annotation]], where independent measurements are needed to distinguish genuine mechanism from correlated representation. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

## Limits of the Direct Validation

The direct Fitness Browser–BacDive validation contained 12 organisms representing 6 unique species: Cupriavidus basilensis, Methanococcus maripaludis, Ralstonia solanacearum, Pseudomonas simiae, Azospirillum brasilense, and Pseudomonas fluorescens. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] All Gram-typed organisms were Gram-negative, preventing within-set testing of the strongest association. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] All urease-typed organisms were urease-negative yet were routinely tested against nickel, and the single anaerobe, Methanococcus maripaludis, had only 1 metal tested versus 4–5 for aerobes, so n = 1 was not interpretable. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

BacDive testing was biased toward well-studied organisms, including Pseudomonas and Escherichia coli, which had many phenotype tests, whereas poorly studied species had sparse data. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] Species-name matching recovered 5,647 of 27,702 GTDB species (38.4%), and GCA accession matching was not implemented, so the bridge may omit additional links. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] These coverage limitations connect to [[concepts/cultivation-collection-bias-in-ecological-genomics]] and [[concepts/metadata-resolution-and-within-species-heterogeneity]]. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

## Tensions

The univariate Gram-stain association supports the hypothesis that cell-envelope architecture may influence metal tolerance, while the lineage structure of the comparison prevents separating that mechanism from taxonomic composition. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] Similarly, the overall positive catalase association contradicts the negative within-class associations in Actinomycetes, Gammaproteobacteria, and Betaproteobacteria, so the corpus does not support a single direction for catalase across lineages. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

The composite-score analysis also cannot establish metal-specific mechanisms: per-metal scores are needed to test whether catalase predicts copper, urease predicts nickel, or H₂S predicts zinc, copper, and cadmium tolerance. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] This tension links the phenotype-association problem to [[concepts/condition-specific-fitness]], because a phenotype may have a specific effect under one metal condition without predicting a composite score across metals. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]

## Open Directions

- Match BacDive records to GTDB species using GCA accessions, then repeat the species-level association and test whether the Gram-stain, urease, and catalase effects change after expanded coverage. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]
- Apply PGLS, meaning phylogenetic generalized least squares, or phylogenetic PCA to the matched phenotype and metal-score data, and ask whether any phenotype retains an association after phylogenetic signal is removed. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]
- Replace the composite Metal Fitness Atlas score with per-metal scores and use within-class regression to test whether catalase predicts copper tolerance, urease predicts nickel tolerance, and H₂S predicts zinc, copper, or cadmium tolerance. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]
- Assemble urease-positive and urease-negative organisms from the same taxonomic classes and test them under nickel and other metals with RB-TnSeq, meaning random barcode transposon sequencing, to distinguish nickel-specific effects from general lineage differences. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]
- Expand the direct Fitness Browser–BacDive validation with Gram-positive organisms, urease-positive organisms, and multiple anaerobes, then test whether phenotype associations replicate in directly measured metal-fitness data rather than genome-based scores. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]
