# Unequal evidence callability can make ecological contrasts untestable

## Core idea

Ecological comparison requires comparable evidence on both sides of a contrast. When pathway, organism, or phenotype calls exist for only one source or group, the comparison can become untestable rather than merely statistically underpowered. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] “Callability” means that a compound could be linked to an organism-level utilization prediction or measured fitness result through the queried resources; it does not mean that the compound is biologically absent from an uncalled group. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

Metabolic-utilization inference is also shaped by whether a compound has a resolvable structure, database representation, reaction linkage, literature support, and genome-level annotation. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] “Organism-dark” therefore marks a resource-defined coverage boundary and discovery signal, not evidence that a compound is unknown to science or unusable by microorganisms. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] This **extends** [[concepts/functional-dark-matter]] from uncharacterized genomic functions to compounds that cannot currently be connected to organisms, pathways, or genetic determinants, and **refines** [[concepts/metabolic-model-gapfilling]] by separating missing pathway links from compounds lacking usable database representation. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

This distinction connects [[summaries/enigma_carbon_census_1__REPORT]] to [[concepts/occurrence-versus-catabolic-activity]], [[concepts/sampling-depth-and-downsampling-effects]], and [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]]. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The [[summaries/ecotype_env_reanalysis__REPORT]] **refines** this principle beyond compound utilization: an ecological environment-versus-gene-content contrast can remain weak even after clinical sampling bias is explicitly measured, so unequal representation should be tested as a possible confounder rather than assumed to explain the result. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]

## The ENIGMA evidence funnel and its dark boundary

The ENIGMA Carbon Census evaluated 83 enrichment compounds: 59 from SSO groundwater and 24 from necromass. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] All 83 were structure-resolved with InChIKeys through PubChem, 54 were linked to KEGG, and 9 were initially callable through either an ENIGMA-isolate utilizer call or a Tier-1 measured RB-TnSeq carbon-source fitness experiment. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The resulting funnel was 83 compounds → 83 structure-resolved → 54 KEGG-linked → 9 callable → 74 organism-dark. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The 74/83 dark compounds, or 89% of the census, were not linkable to organism-level utilization through the queried BERDL and curated resources. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The dark fraction was similar between sources: 53/59 groundwater compounds, or 90%, and 21/24 necromass compounds, or 88%, were organism-dark. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] This **supports** the interpretation that the observed gap tracks chemical representation and annotation coverage more strongly than sampling source, but does not establish that source has no biological effect. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The dark set is heterogeneous: 33 compounds were KEGG-linked but lacked a reaction in the queried genomes, 29 were fully orphan compounds with no KEGG link, 6 were biosynthesis-known but catabolism-unknown, and 6 were represented only by generic reactions. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The 29 fully orphan compounds are the hardest discovery targets because they lack both a KEGG link and a genome-level reaction connection; they were disproportionately necromass-derived, comprising 13/24 necromass compounds versus 16/59 groundwater compounds. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] This **refines** organism-darkness by distinguishing missing pathway links from missing chemical identifiers or database entries. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The six biosynthesis-known dark compounds—Tyramine, guanidineacetic acid, cinnamic acid, caffeic acid, palmitic acid, and farnesol—should not be treated as equivalent to fully orphan compounds because external MIBiG and biosynthetic-literature consultation may clarify their biology. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] This **supports** [[concepts/evidence-triangulation-for-functional-annotation]]: different dark buckets require different evidence and should not be collapsed into one unknown class. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

## Evidence asymmetry and chemical-space coverage

The 8 ENIGMA-isolate-callable compounds were salicylic acid, 3-hydroxybenzoic acid, 4-hydroxybenzaldehyde, phthalic acid, terephthalic acid, Phenylethylamine, xanthine, and Abscisic acid. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Lauric acid was additionally callable through measured fitness in a reference bacterium but had no ENIGMA-isolate utilizer rows. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The isolate-callable compounds were concentrated in Shikimates/Phenylpropanoids: salicylic acid, 3-hydroxybenzoic acid, 4-hydroxybenzaldehyde, phthalic acid, and terephthalic acid were in that class; Phenylethylamine and xanthine were in Alkaloids; and Abscisic acid was in Terpenoids. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The class-count test gave χ²=5.07, p=0.53, df=6, with Shikimates 5/25, Alkaloids 2/26, Terpenoids 1/17, Fatty acids 0/10, Polyketides 0/2, AA/Peptides 0/2, and mixed 0/1. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Adding measured-fitness lauric acid did not change the verdict. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The result suggests the hypothesis that compounds in better-represented chemical classes are more likely to acquire callable utilization evidence, but the census did not formally support that hypothesis. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The null result does not establish equal callability across classes because the callable sample was small and confounded with annotation coverage. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] It is directionally useful for enrichment design but underpowered and confounded with annotation coverage. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

Callable compounds had median Complexity 133 versus 207 for dark compounds, with p=0.034; median MolecularWeight 152 versus 179, with p=0.066; and median HeavyAtomCount 11 versus 13, with p=0.057. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] They also showed directionally higher polarity, with TPSA 57 versus 41 and hydrogen-bond donors 2 versus 1. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] These comparisons were descriptive rather than calibrated inference because only n=9 callable compounds were available and the Mann–Whitney p-values were uncorrected. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The pattern supports the hypothesis that simpler, more familiar, or more database-represented compounds are easier to connect to utilization evidence, but it does not distinguish biological bioavailability from an annotation-coverage ceiling. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Simple, common, pollutant-adjacent aromatics may be overrepresented among callable compounds because they are also more likely to appear in KEGG, ModelSEED, and genome-depot annotations. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] This **supports** [[concepts/environmental-resistome]] and [[concepts/structural-annotation-gap]] by showing that computational visibility may reflect schema and annotation coverage rather than biological importance. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

## Source contrasts and evidence tiers

The dark fraction was similar for the two source groups: 53/59 groundwater compounds, or 90%, and 21/24 necromass compounds, or 88%, were organism-dark. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Thus, source labels did not supply balanced compound-level evidence for testing whether groundwater and necromass compounds differed in organismal utilization architecture. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The imbalance was sharper for source-tracking hypothesis H3: only 2 of the 8 ENIGMA-isolate-callable compounds were necromass-sourced, and both were phthalate-class aromatics with Actinomycetota-heavy utilizers. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Lauric acid was necromass-sourced, but its call came only from measured fitness in a reference bacterium, with no ENIGMA-isolate rows and no field data. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Consequently, the project reported an SSO field-occurrence atlas rather than a statistical groundwater-versus-necromass source contrast. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

This is an evidence-coverage limitation, not evidence that the two sources have equivalent biology. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The available callable set was too small and compositionally confounded to separate source effects from compound-class and annotation-coverage effects. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] A source contrast is testable only when both source groups have sufficient, comparable calls for the same type of biological evidence. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Otherwise, an observed difference can reflect resource visibility, chemical-class composition, or assay availability rather than ecology. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The census therefore supports the conclusion that H3 was untestable and confounded, rather than supporting or rejecting a groundwater-versus-necromass utilization difference. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] H4 illustrates the same principle: it was strongly supported for the 8 ENIGMA-isolate-callable compounds but null for the other 75 compounds lacking isolate-level placement. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The null result for those 75 compounds is a missing-evidence result, not a biological finding that they lack phylogenetically structured utilizers. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The ecotype reanalysis **supports** the broader distinction between a measured null contrast and a callability failure, but also **refines** the role of sampling composition. Among 224 species selected with at least 20 genomes containing AlphaEarth embeddings and at least 30% coverage, 106 species (47%) were majority human-associated, 47 (21%) were majority environmental, and 71 (32%) were Mixed/Other. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] Environmental species had a median partial correlation of 0.051, compared with 0.084 for human-associated species and 0.109 for Mixed/Other species; the one-sided Environmental > Human-associated test gave U=1536 and p=0.83. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] The continuous environmental-fraction analysis likewise found rho=-0.085 and p=0.25, while the human-associated-fraction analysis gave rho=0.030 and p=0.69. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]

Thus, the clinical sampling bias was real but did not explain the weak environment–gene-content relationship. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] This **contrasts** with any interpretation that unequal clinical representation alone accounts for the null ecological signal: within one consistent reanalysis methodology, environmental species did not show stronger correlations. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] However, 10/47 environmental species (21%) had NaN correlations, compared with 13/66 Mixed/Other species (20%) and 7/100 human-associated species (7%); the environmental group was therefore more heavily filtered, so the result remains a callability-sensitive comparison rather than proof that all ecological contrasts are equally observable. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]

## Operational definitions can change the result

Xanthine exposed a category error in the representation-to-function pipeline. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Allowlisted reaction R02107, xanthine→urate, was treated as carbon-catabolic even though it represents purine nitrogen acquisition rather than carbon catabolism. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Removing R02107 from the carbon allowlist reduced the effective carbon-callable set from 9 to 8, although committed tables still contained xanthine because they were not regenerated. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

This **supports** [[concepts/pathway-versus-reaction-evidence-resolution]] and [[concepts/composite-functional-annotation]]: chemical identity and database presence require directional reaction validation before establishing biological utilization. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The dark boundary is filter-dependent: the 8 ENIGMA-isolate calls used a genome-prevalence-<10% signature-reaction filter retaining reactions assigned to KEGG degradation maps or a 3-reaction curated allowlist, whereas lauric acid was independently callable through measured fitness and was not subject to this filter. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] A different reaction filter could change which compounds are considered callable or dark. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The ecotype reanalysis **further refines** this methodological point: its all-genome extraction produced a median partial correlation of 0.081 across 183 species, compared with 0.003 in the original downsampled analysis, described as a 27x difference. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] The report states that absolute correlations are not comparable across those methods, while the within-method Environmental-versus-Human-associated comparison remains valid. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] This shows that evidence callability and extraction design can alter the apparent strength of an ecological contrast even when the contrast itself is retained. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]

## Why environmental occurrence cannot repair missing catabolic evidence

The environmental atlas covered 86 implicated genera using 3825 taxonomy-bearing NMDC metagenomes and 302 Planet Microbe marine runs. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] In NMDC, 83/86 genera were detected in 1719 metagenomes, and 99% of samples were labeled through two independent ontology systems. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The SSO field atlas detected 62 implicated utilizer genera at genus resolution, with top field prevalences approximately 0.7–0.9; 3-hydroxybenzoic-acid utilizers reached 0.90 field prevalence. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

These observations provide organismal abundance or occurrence, not evidence that the organisms catabolize the census compounds, because no environmental dataset measured those compounds. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The atlas therefore cannot substitute for missing utilization calls when the question concerns source-specific degradation capacity. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Broad occurrence, including strong periphyton representation of Burkholderiales/Comamonadaceae, cannot establish compound degradation or activity. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] This **refines** [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]] and **supports** [[concepts/environment-embedding-geography]] by separating environmental presence from evidence for the focal metabolic phenotype. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

Periphyton-sited enrichment is nevertheless a practical sampling hypothesis: listed genera reached approximately 96–97% prevalence in periphyton, with mean relative abundance approximately 0.005–0.009. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] These data can prioritize candidate source communities, but environmental occurrence alone cannot rescue a missing compound-to-pathway link or validate utilization of a particular dark compound. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The ecotype reanalysis **supports** this separation of occurrence from functional relevance: environmental embeddings had a 3.4x geographic-signal ratio versus 2.0x for human-associated embeddings, yet the environmental signal did not predict stronger gene-content correlations. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] The report offers, as a hypothesis rather than an established mechanism, that embeddings may capture regional epidemiological structure in human-associated species rather than ecological differences. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]

## From resource-defined darkness to experiments

The 74 dark compounds define a ranked experimental target space rather than merely missing annotations. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Wet-lab enrichment was recommended to begin with the 29 fully orphan compounds, especially necromass-heavy alkaloids and terpenoids, while the 6 biosynthesis-known compounds should first receive MIBiG or biosynthetic-literature consultation. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

Class-level catabolic literature exists for compounds including monoterpenes and nicotine, while the project’s zero literature rescues resulted from a shallow PubMed-title-only screen. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Targeted PaperBLAST or abstract-level PubMed mining could therefore reclassify part of the dark set without new wet-lab observation, but this remains a hypothesis-generating route rather than evidence that the compounds are already biologically characterized. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The census connects compound identity, reaction annotation, measured fitness, taxonomy, and environmental occurrence across multiple resources in a tiered workflow. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] This **supports** [[concepts/evidence-triangulation-for-functional-annotation]] and reinforces [[concepts/evidence-triangulation-for-functional-annotation]] and [[concepts/metabolic-model-gapfilling]] as routes for converting resource-defined gaps into testable hypotheses. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

## Tensions

The census showed a directional concentration of callable compounds in simpler, commonly represented chemical classes, but H1 was not formally supported by the chi-square test. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] This tension should remain an annotation-coverage hypothesis rather than being resolved as either a true chemical-class gradient or its absence. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The principal tension is between biological darkness and database darkness. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The 29 fully orphan compounds were not linkable through the queried resources, but “organism-dark” does not mean unknown to science; the literature screen was shallow. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The result therefore supports a resource-limited discovery frontier while leaving open how much of the 74-compound dark set reflects absent biology, absent annotations, absent literature retrieval, or an overly restrictive reaction filter. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

A second tension is between environmental presence and functional evidence. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The atlas provides occurrence and abundance data for implicated genera, including the 86-genera, 3825-NMDC-metagenome, and 302-Planet-Microbe-run scope, but not compound-resolved catabolic activity. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Environmental occurrence can prioritize enrichment sources without establishing that observed organisms consume a particular dark compound. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

A third tension concerns environmental callability versus the interpretation of null ecological contrasts. The ecotype reanalysis found no stronger environment–gene-content association among environmental species despite confirming clinical sampling bias, with U=1536 and p=0.83 for Environmental > Human-associated. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] Yet environmental species also had a higher NaN rate, 21%, than human-associated species, 7%, and the report notes that the extraction methodology produced a 27x difference in overall median correlation relative to the original analysis. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] The result supports the within-method null comparison but leaves unresolved how much ecological signal is lost through species-level filtering, genome-count imbalance, and extraction choices. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]

## Open Directions

- Apply targeted PaperBLAST and abstract-level PubMed mining to the 74 organism-dark compounds, especially the 29 fully orphan compounds, asking how many are reclassified beyond the title-only screen. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]
- Apply MIBiG and biosynthetic-literature searches to Tyramine, guanidineacetic acid, cinnamic acid, caffeic acid, palmitic acid, and farnesol, asking whether biosynthetic evidence distinguishes catabolism-unknown from genuinely unlinked compounds. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]
- Re-run the census with complementary annotation pipelines and explicit provenance tracking, asking whether the 29 fully orphan compounds remain chemically unrepresented or become linkable through alternative databases. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]
- Regenerate committed tables after removing R02107 from the carbon allowlist, asking whether xanthine changes callable-set composition or downstream class comparisons. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]
- Recompute callable status under alternative catabolic-direction filters and compare dark sets, asking how much the 74-compound frontier depends on reaction-selection rules. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]
- Compare callable and dark sets with calibrated, multiple-testing-corrected physicochemical models using a larger compound collection, asking whether Complexity, MolecularWeight, HeavyAtomCount, TPSA, and hydrogen-bond donors predict annotation status independently of chemical class. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]
- Test the 74 dark compounds in targeted enrichment and measured-fitness experiments, beginning with the 29 fully orphan and 6 biosynthesis-known/catabolism-unknown compounds, asking which resource-defined gaps correspond to realized utilization. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]
- Re-run the groundwater-versus-necromass comparison after promoting dark compounds through targeted searches, matching compounds by chemical class and evidence tier to ask whether the source contrast remains. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]
- Combine periphyton enrichment with compound-resolved growth assays, genome sequencing, pathway reconstruction, and metatranscriptomic or metaproteomic assays, asking whether the Burkholderiales/Comamonadaceae reservoir actually transforms prioritized compounds. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]
- Apply study-aware mixed models or sample-level permutations to NMDC and SSO metadata, accounting for study structure, compositionality, zero-inflated abundance data, and sample-level environment labels, asking whether source-associated occurrence differences persist. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]
- Integrate chemical identity, reaction evidence, measured fitness, taxonomy, and environmental metadata through provenance-aware joins to ask which missing evidence layer most often blocks conversion from dark to callable status. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]
- Recompute the ecotype environment-versus-gene-content comparison with matched genome counts and explicit NaN diagnostics, asking whether unequal species-level callability changes the Environmental-versus-Human-associated contrast. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]
- Directly compare full-genome extraction with diversity-maximizing downsampling under identical species, distance, and correlation procedures, asking what produces the 27x difference in median partial correlation. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]
- Repeat the ecotype analysis using functional subsets such as transport and secondary-metabolism genes, structured ENVO terms, and genome count as a covariate, asking whether whole-genome distances mask environment-specific functional signals. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]
