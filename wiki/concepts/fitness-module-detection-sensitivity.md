# Fitness-Module Discovery Depends on Thresholds and Decomposition Constraints

Independent component analysis (ICA), a statistical decomposition method, can identify groups of genes with coordinated fitness patterns across experiments, but the resulting modules depend on membership thresholds, annotation settings, experiment count, experimental diversity, and the allowed number of components. [src: fitness_modules] ICA is therefore best interpreted as a process-level discovery tool rather than a direct assignment of precise molecular functions to individual genes. [src: fitness_modules]

## Threshold choice changes module quality

The initial D'Agostino K-squared membership approach produced modules containing 100-280 genes, with 59% enrichment and 1-17x correlation enrichment. [src: fitness_modules] Replacing that rule with an absolute component-weight threshold of |weight| >= 0.3 and limiting each module to a maximum of 50 genes produced biologically coherent modules, with 94% enrichment and 2.8x correlation enrichment. [src: fitness_modules] This shows that thresholding is not cosmetic: it changes module size and the strength of the recovered cofitness signal. [src: fitness_modules]

The final analysis identified 1,116 stable modules across 32 organisms, with each organism represented by at least 100 experiments. [src: fitness_modules] Module sizes had a median range of 7-50 genes. [src: fitness_modules] Of these modules, 94.2% showed significantly elevated within-module cofitness by Mann-Whitney U test at p < 0.05. [src: fitness_modules] Within-module mean |r| was 0.34 versus 0.12 in the background, corresponding to a 2.8x enrichment, while module genes showed 22.7x genomic-adjacency enrichment. [src: fitness_modules] The adjacency result supports the interpretation that many recovered modules reflect operon co-localization as well as correlated fitness behavior. [src: fitness_modules]

The conservation analysis **supports** this threshold-sensitivity interpretation: its upstream membership rule used |Pearson r| >= 0.3 and a maximum of 50 genes per module, so the resulting conservation profile is conditional on the same kind of membership constraints rather than an invariant property of the underlying fitness data. [src: module_conservation] Among 974 modules with at least 3 mapped genes, 577 (59%) were >90% core, 349 (36%) were 50-90% core, and 48 (5%) were <50% core; these classifications use convenient cutoffs that can also shape apparent module architecture. [src: module_conservation]

## Experimental depth and decomposition constrain reliability

The experiment-depth evidence **refines** the threshold result: organisms represented by fewer experiments produced weaker module structure, whereas broader experiment coverage supported more stable cofitness inference. [src: fitness_modules] The approximately 100-experiment inclusion threshold supports reproducible structure in the reported collection, but it is not a universal reliability cutoff. [src: fitness_modules] Caulo, despite having 198 experiments, showed only 2.9x correlation enrichment, demonstrating that meeting a nominal experiment-count threshold does not guarantee uniformly strong module recovery. [src: fitness_modules] Experiment number alone is therefore insufficient; organism-specific biology, condition composition, and experimental diversity may also affect module quality. [src: fitness_modules]

The analysis imposed a 40% component cap, meaning that the number of components could not exceed 40% of the number of experiments, to avoid FastICA convergence failures. [src: fitness_modules] This constraint improves computational stability but may cause some modules to be missed in organisms with few experiments. [src: fitness_modules] Limited depth can consequently weaken cofitness estimates while also restricting the number of components that can be stably extracted. [src: fitness_modules] Differences in depth and the component cap affect both sensitivity and comparability across organisms, so a weak or absent module should not automatically be interpreted as evidence of absent biological conservation. [src: fitness_modules]

The conservation-linked analysis further **supports** this qualification: pangenome mappings covered 29/32 organisms because Cola, Kang, and SB2B lacked sufficient GTDB genomes for pangenome construction, making its conservation estimates incomplete across the full module collection. [src: module_conservation] It also found no family-breadth/conservation association (Spearman rho=-0.01, p=0.914), redirecting interpretation away from module-family scope and toward gene-level conservation under a high approximately 81.5% all-gene core baseline. [src: module_conservation]

## Annotation thresholds do not equal discovery thresholds

Lowering the enrichment-overlap threshold from 3 to 2 and adding PFam domains increased the module annotation rate from 8% to 80%. [src: fitness_modules] The number of annotated modules increased from 92 to 890, and the resulting changes unlocked 7.6x more function predictions. [src: fitness_modules] PFam provided the broadest annotation coverage, whereas KEGG KOs were too gene-specific for module-level enrichment. [src: fitness_modules] Because PFam operates at the domain level, its broader coverage may overcount functional associations. [src: fitness_modules]

These annotation choices differ from the component-membership threshold: the |weight| >= 0.3 rule determines which genes enter a module, whereas the enrichment-overlap threshold determines how module content is associated with functional annotations. [src: fitness_modules] Thus, both module construction and downstream annotation settings can alter apparent biological yield without changing the underlying fitness data. [src: fitness_modules] The conservation analysis **supports** keeping these layers distinct: its core/accessory classifications describe mapped gene conservation, not functional annotation accuracy, and its accessory-module pattern is only a hypothesis about horizontally transferred or niche-specific units rather than direct evidence of either process. [src: module_conservation]

## Process-level modules are not gene-level function calls

In a held-out benchmark withholding 20% of KEGG-annotated genes, ortholog transfer achieved 95.8% strict precision, 91.2% coverage, and 0.934 F1. [src: fitness_modules] Domain-based prediction achieved 29.1% precision, 66.6% coverage, and 0.401 F1. [src: fitness_modules] Module-ICA achieved <1% strict precision and 23.3% coverage, while cofitness voting achieved <1% strict precision and 73.0% coverage. [src: fitness_modules] These results support using ortholog transfer for specific molecular-function prediction and ICA modules for biological-process context. [src: fitness_modules]

Module-ICA and cofitness had near-zero strict KEGG KO precision because KEGG KO groups are gene-level assignments, averaging approximately 1.2 genes per unique KO, whereas a module with 20 annotated members typically contained 20 different KOs. [src: fitness_modules] The 6,691 function predictions generated for hypothetical proteins should therefore be interpreted as process-level evidence rather than proof of a specific KO-defined function. [src: fitness_modules] Of those predictions, 2,455 were family-backed, representing 37% and carrying cross-organism conservation support, while 4,236 were module-only predictions. [src: fitness_modules] This distinction **supports** [[concepts/process-level-versus-gene-level-fitness-inference]] and [[concepts/gene-essentiality]]: the reliability of process-level module detection is not equivalent to the reliability of gene-level annotation. [src: fitness_modules]

The conservation result **refines** this distinction: zero essential genes appeared in the ICA modules because essential genes lack usable transposon-insertion fitness data, so the observed conservation profile describes the non-essential, insertion-callable portion of the genome rather than essential-gene modules. [src: module_conservation]

## Cross-organism stability is alignment- and depth-dependent

Cross-organism alignment produced 1.15M bidirectional-best-hit (BBH) pairs across 32 organisms and 13,402 ortholog groups. [src: fitness_modules] It identified 156 module families spanning 2+ organisms, including 28 spanning 5+, 7 spanning 10+, and 1 spanning 21 organisms. [src: fitness_modules] A total of 145 families had consensus functional labels, representing 93% of the families. [src: fitness_modules] These results provide evidence for conserved fitness regulons across diverse bacterial phyla, but BBH-based alignment represents conserved co-regulation patterns rather than proof that every family member has an identical molecular function. [src: fitness_modules]

The depth and component-cap evidence **qualifies** this cross-organism result: differences in experimental depth and extraction limits can influence which modules are detected in each organism, even when shared biology is present. [src: fitness_modules] Stable module families can reveal conserved process-level structure, but their membership, annotation, and cross-organism comparability depend on the analysis regime. [src: fitness_modules] The conservation analysis **supports** this qualification: module genes were 86.0% core versus 81.5% for all genes (OR=1.46, p=1.6e-87), a statistically strong but modest +4.5 percentage-point enrichment under the high baseline; family breadth itself was not predictive of conservation. [src: module_conservation] This finding **supports** [[concepts/cofitness-network-architecture]] by quantifying how module construction and experiment depth affect recovered network structure. [src: fitness_modules] It **refines** [[concepts/condition-specific-fitness]] by extending condition-dependent fitness analysis from individual genes to independently regulated modules, and it **supports** [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] by connecting module families to cross-organism ortholog groups.

The full project reports are available at [[summaries/fitness_modules__REPORT]] and [[summaries/module_conservation__REPORT]]. [src: fitness_modules] [src: module_conservation]

## Open Directions

- Re-run ICA across organisms while varying the |weight| threshold, 50-gene maximum, and 40% component cap; test which settings preserve the 94.2% significant-module rate, 2.8x correlation enrichment, and 22.7x genomic-adjacency enrichment. [src: fitness_modules]
- Use matched experiment subsets, bootstrap or split-sample ICA, and convergence diagnostics to quantify recovery below and above approximately 100 experiments, including Caulo's 198 experiments and 2.9x enrichment. [src: fitness_modules]
- Balance condition classes across organisms to test whether experimental diversity, rather than experiment count alone, explains module-reliability differences. [src: fitness_modules]
- Compare PFam, KEGG KO, and other annotation layers at fixed memberships to determine whether the increase from 8% to 80% reflects genuine coverage or domain-level overcounting. [src: fitness_modules]
- Benchmark module-level predictions against independently measured phenotypes or perturbation data to test whether process-level predictions improve interpretation despite <1% strict KEGG KO precision. [src: fitness_modules]
- Reconstruct cross-organism families under alternative orthology definitions and after downsampling every organism to the same experiment count; test whether the 156 families, including the family spanning 21 organisms, remain stable without assuming identical molecular functions. [src: fitness_modules]
- Recalculate module conservation under alternative membership and core/accessory thresholds, with essentiality-aware missingness and matched pangenome coverage, to test whether the 86.0% versus 81.5% enrichment and the rho=-0.01 family-breadth result persist. [src: module_conservation]
