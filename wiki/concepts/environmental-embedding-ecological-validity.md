# Ecological Validity of Environmental Embeddings

## Scope

This concept concerns whether environmental embeddings and metadata capture ecological variation at a resolution relevant to bacterial genome and gene-content comparisons. The [[summaries/ecotype_analysis__REPORT]] evaluated whether environmental similarity or phylogenetic similarity better predicted gene-content similarity across bacterial species. [src: ecotype_analysis]

A central measurement problem is collection-site versus organismal microenvironment mismatch: geographic coordinates may describe where a microbial sample was collected rather than the physical, chemical, or host-associated environment experienced by the organism. [src: ecotype_analysis] This mismatch can weaken tests of whether environmental similarity predicts bacterial gene-content similarity, especially for host-associated bacteria whose relevant microenvironment may be inside or on the host. [src: ecotype_analysis]

The [[summaries/env_embedding_explorer__REPORT]] **supports** this measurement concern by showing that AlphaEarth embeddings encode spatially autocorrelated context, but that the available coordinates and environmental labels are unevenly representative of ecological conditions. Across 83,287 genomes with embeddings, 38% were human-associated, while Soil, Marine, and Freshwater each represented 7%; this composition may make collection-site similarity a particularly weak proxy for organismal ecology in the combined dataset. [src: env_embedding_explorer]

## Key Evidence

The analysis extracted genome metadata, taxonomy, NCBI environmental and isolation-source metadata, pangenome composition, and gene-cluster presence/absence profiles from the [[entities/kbase-ke-pangenome]] database. [src: ecotype_analysis] It covered 13,381 genomes across 224 species and produced correlation results for 172 bacterial species with sufficient environmental and phylogenetic data. [src: ecotype_analysis] Environmental embeddings were obtained from [[entities/alph-aearth]]. [src: ecotype_analysis]

A partial correlation, defined here as the association between two variables after accounting for a third variable, had a median value of 0.0025 for environment and 0.0143 for phylogeny. [src: ecotype_analysis] Phylogeny dominated the gene-content signal in 60.5% of species, whereas environment dominated in 39.5% of species. [src: ecotype_analysis] Only 12 species (7.0%) showed a significant positive environmental effect, 4 species (2.3%) showed a significant negative environmental effect, and 156 species (90.7%) showed no significant environmental effect. [src: ecotype_analysis]

These results support the interpretation that vertical inheritance generally dominates genome-wide gene-content similarity, while environmental adaptation may be concentrated in specific gene subsets rather than reflected across the whole genome. [src: ecotype_analysis] They also refine [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]] by identifying a measurement problem: environmental predictors can be biologically misaligned with the organism even when geographic coordinates are available. [src: ecotype_analysis]

The [[summaries/ecotype_env_reanalysis__REPORT]] **supports** the original null interpretation but **refines** its explanation: among 224 species classified from genome-level isolation-source assignments, environmental species did not have stronger environment–gene-content correlations than human-associated species (one-sided Mann–Whitney U: U=1536, p=0.83). [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] The reanalysis therefore indicates that confirmed clinical sampling bias does not explain the weak signal, although its full-genome extraction and lack of downsampling produced a median partial correlation of 0.081 across 183 species versus 0.003 in the original analysis; the report states that these absolute values are not comparable across methodologies. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]

The embedding explorer **supports** the interpretation that AlphaEarth is not an ecologically neutral distance measure: mean cosine distance increased from 0.41 for pairs less than 100 km apart to 0.82 for pairs in the 10K–20K km bin, with a plateau at intercontinental scales. [src: env_embedding_explorer] Environmental samples showed a stronger gradient than human-associated samples, rising from 0.27 to 0.90 across the corresponding nearby-to-intercontinental comparison, versus 0.37 to 0.75 for human-associated samples. [src: env_embedding_explorer] This **refines** the ecological-validity question: embeddings capture geographic environmental structure, but that structure may not correspond to the organism’s relevant microenvironment or to gene-content differences.

## Ecological-Validity Limitations

[[entities/alph-aearth]] embeddings covered only 28.4% of genomes in the analysis, limiting the environmental signal available for comparison with gene content. [src: ecotype_analysis] Geographic coordinates in NCBI metadata were often missing or imprecise, further reducing the quality of environmental associations. [src: ecotype_analysis] For host-associated bacteria, coordinates may represent the sampling location while the biologically relevant environment is a host, tissue, body site, or other organism-proximal microenvironment. [src: ecotype_analysis] Thus, the absence of a significant difference between environmental and host-associated bacteria in environmental effects (p=0.66) does not establish that host-associated organisms lack environmental structuring. [src: ecotype_analysis]

The explorer **supports** these limitations with a larger audit: AlphaEarth embeddings covered 28.4% of 293,059 genomes, and 79,449 of the 83,287 embedded genomes had valid values across all 64 dimensions while 3,838 contained at least one NaN. [src: env_embedding_explorer] Among 83,286 genomes with coordinates, 50,109 (60.2%) were classified as Good, 30,469 (36.6%) as Suspicious cluster, and 2,708 (3.3%) as Low precision. [src: env_embedding_explorer] The suspicious-cluster heuristic can flag legitimate field sites, including Rifle, Colorado and Saanich Inlet, so coordinate quality is not equivalent to ecological validity. [src: env_embedding_explorer]

This evidence **supports** treating collection-site coordinates as potentially mismatched proxies for organismal exposure rather than as direct measurements of habitat conditions. [src: ecotype_analysis] It also **supports** [[concepts/environment-embedding-geography]] because incomplete embedding coverage, missing or imprecise coordinates, and collection-site bias can weaken associations between environmental distance and genomic variation. [src: ecotype_analysis] The explorer further **supports** this connection: isolation_source was available for 76,295 genomes (91.6%), host for 53,091 (63.7%), and each of env_broad_scale, env_local_scale, and env_medium covered fewer than half of the 83,287 genomes. [src: env_embedding_explorer] The result **supports** [[concepts/environmental-resistome]] by showing that metadata resolution and the mismatch between collection location and organismal microenvironment can limit ecological inference. [src: ecotype_analysis]

The reanalysis **supports** the p=0.66 null result with a more systematic genome-level classification, obtaining p=0.83 for Environmental > Human-associated. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] However, environmental species had a NaN partial-correlation rate of 10/47 = 21%, compared with 7/100 = 7% for Human-associated species, so filtering disproportionately removed environmental species; the report argues that this would, if anything, bias toward detecting a stronger environmental signal, which was not observed. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] This strengthens the conclusion that clinical composition alone is insufficient to explain the weak association, while not eliminating metadata and representation limitations.

The explorer **refines** the interpretation of representation bias rather than contradicting that result: keyword matching mapped 5,774 unique isolation_source values to 12 broad categories, captured 71% of genomes with a label, left 17% in Other, and left 12.5% Unknown because isolation_source was missing or null. [src: env_embedding_explorer] Thus, an environmental-only reanalysis is testable and warranted, but category harmonization and coordinate-quality decisions remain potential sources of ecological misclassification. [src: env_embedding_explorer]

The partial-correlation analysis used distances and assumed linear relationships, so it may miss nonlinear ecological effects. [src: ecotype_analysis] The analysis therefore suggests the hypothesis that AlphaEarth embeddings do not fully capture ecologically relevant environmental variation. [src: ecotype_analysis] This remains a hypothesis rather than an established limitation of all environmental embeddings because embedding coverage was incomplete and the analysis relied partly on imperfect geographic metadata. [src: ecotype_analysis] The reanalysis further suggests the hypothesis that embeddings may capture geographic epidemiological structure in human-associated species rather than ecological differences, but this proposed mechanism was not directly established. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]

The explorer **supports** the latter as a hypothesis, not as an established mechanism: human-associated samples had a flatter geographic embedding gradient than environmental samples, and human gut and clinical genomes were distributed across many UMAP clusters, potentially reflecting geographic substructure. [src: env_embedding_explorer] UMAP, a nonlinear dimensionality-reduction method, and DBSCAN, a density-based clustering method, are parameter-dependent; the explorer reports 320 DBSCAN clusters and cautions that apparent structure may not represent the true high-dimensional topology. [src: env_embedding_explorer]

The analysis **connects to** [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] because environmental tests were performed against pangenome gene-content similarity rather than a single phenotype. [src: ecotype_analysis] The weak environmental signal should therefore not be interpreted as definitive evidence that collection environments are irrelevant to gene content; a more defensible interpretation is that environmental similarity was measured imperfectly, particularly for host-associated organisms and samples with incomplete or imprecise metadata. [src: ecotype_analysis]

## Tensions

The analysis found that environmental similarity was generally weaker than phylogenetic similarity for explaining genome-wide gene-content similarity, while environment nevertheless dominated in 39.5% of species. [src: ecotype_analysis] This is a quantitative, within-analysis tension between a broad phylogenetic pattern and species-level exceptions, not evidence that environmental effects are absent and not a contradiction that can be resolved by averaging the signals. [src: ecotype_analysis]

The original and reanalysis partial-correlation medians also differ: 0.0025 for the original environment measure [src: ecotype_analysis] versus 0.081 across all 183 species in the reanalysis. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] This **contradicts** any direct comparison of their absolute effect sizes, but the reanalysis explicitly attributes the discrepancy to different genome sets, full-genome extraction, and downsampling procedures; its within-method Environmental versus Human-associated comparison remains interpretable. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]

The geographic embedding gradient creates a further interpretive tension with gene-content validity: embedding distance increases with geographic distance, but the original analysis found a median environment–gene-content partial correlation of 0.0025. [src: env_embedding_explorer; ecotype_analysis] This does not contradict the geographic signal; it **refines** what that signal can establish, because spatially structured satellite context need not be the organism-level environmental variable that predicts gene content. [src: env_embedding_explorer; ecotype_analysis]

## Open Directions

- Use the 13,381-genome metadata set with direct environmental metadata and alternative environmental embedding distances to test whether non-AlphaEarth representations improve gene-content associations after accounting for phylogenetic distance. [src: ecotype_analysis]
- Combine direct environmental metadata with embedding distances and partial-correlation or nonlinear distance-based methods to determine whether coarse descriptors or linear modeling hide environmental effects. [src: ecotype_analysis]
- Stratify the 172-species result by host-associated versus environmental lifestyle, replace collection coordinates with host, tissue, body-site, or other organism-proximal metadata where available, and test whether the environmental effect changes. [src: ecotype_analysis]
- Partition gene clusters into COG functional categories, including V-Defense and L-Mobile, and test whether category-specific associations with environment exceed the whole-genome signal. [src: ecotype_analysis]
- For species with identified ecotype clusters, compare gene content between clusters using organism-proximal environmental metadata to determine whether ecotype- or locus-level associations are missed by collection-site coordinates. [src: ecotype_analysis]
- Re-run the original and reanalysis workflows on matched genome sets, with and without diversity-maximizing downsampling, to identify the source of the 27x median-correlation discrepancy. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]
- Add genome count as a covariate and replace majority-vote labels with structured ENVO terms to test whether sampling depth or environmental ontology changes the within-method comparison. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]
- Re-run [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]] on environmental-only samples after refining suspicious coordinates with isolation_source homogeneity, testing whether the weak gene-content association persists when institutional-address and field-site classifications are separated. [src: env_embedding_explorer]
- Correlate embedding dimensions A00–A63 with latitude, temperature, precipitation, NDVI, and land-cover classifications, then test whether dimensions tied to measurable environmental variables predict accessory-gene similarity after controlling for phylogeny. [src: env_embedding_explorer]
