# Multi-gene fitness modules predict pangenome co-inheritance better than pairwise links

## Core claim

Laboratory-measured pairwise co-fitness showed only a weak positive relationship with gene co-occurrence across bacterial pangenomes, whereas coordinated multi-gene modules identified by independent component analysis (ICA) showed a stronger co-inheritance signal. [^cofitness_coinheritance] This **supports** [cofitness-network-architecture](cofitness-network-architecture.md) by indicating that multi-gene organization may be more informative than isolated pairwise links for understanding which genes travel together through a pangenome. [^cofitness_coinheritance]

The study integrated Fitness Browser co-fitness measurements with pangenome presence/absence data, phylogenetic distances, ICA modules, and SEED functional annotations; its full project summary is [cofitness_coinheritance__REPORT](../summaries/cofitness_coinheritance__REPORT.md). [^cofitness_coinheritance] The prevalence-ceiling analysis **refines** this integration: nearly universal genes provide too little presence/absence variation for reliable co-occurrence inference, so weak associations may reflect limited detectability rather than weak biological coupling. [^cofitness_coinheritance]

The linked [conservation_vs_fitness__REPORT](../summaries/conservation_vs_fitness__REPORT.md) analysis provides a complementary conservation benchmark: among 33 organisms, essential genes were 86.1% core versus 81.2% for non-essential genes, with a median odds ratio of 1.56; 18 of 33 organisms showed significant enrichment after Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate correction. [^conservation_vs_fitness] This **supports** the interpretation that fitness-linked genomic architecture is generally core-conserved, while refining it by showing that essentiality predicts only a modest shift against an already core-rich background. [^conservation_vs_fitness]

## Pairwise signal and the prevalence ceiling

Across 9 organisms, 2,253,491 cofit pairs were compared with 22,534,910 prevalence-matched random pairs. [^cofitness_coinheritance] The mean delta phi, defined as cofit-pair co-occurrence minus matched-random co-occurrence, was +0.011 across organisms, with 7 of 9 organisms showing positive effects and 8 of 9 significant at p<0.05 by two-sided Mann-Whitney testing. [^cofitness_coinheritance] The aggregate effect was delta=+0.003 with Mann-Whitney p=1.66e-29, but the Wilcoxon signed-rank test across organisms was not significant (W=9, p=0.13), demonstrating high inter-organism variance. [^cofitness_coinheritance]

The organism-level results ranged from Ddia6719 at +0.093 to Korea at -0.042, with +0.026 for pseudo3_N2E3, +0.009 for Phaeo, +0.006 for SyringaeB728a, +0.003 for Koxy, +0.002 for Smeli, +0.001 for Btheta, and -0.000 for Putida. [^cofitness_coinheritance] Korea’s negative result is difficult to interpret because 95.2% of its cofit pairs had NaN phi values, only approximately 8,000 of 166,601 pairs were computable, and both genes were present in 100% of 72 genomes for the affected pairs. [^cofitness_coinheritance]

A prevalence ceiling occurs when gene clusters occur in nearly every genome: the phi coefficient, measuring association between two binary presence/absence patterns, can then approach zero for both cofit and random pairs. [^cofitness_coinheritance] Most Fitness Browser genes mapped to core clusters (>95% prevalence), where phi approaches 0 for both groups. [^cofitness_coinheritance] Co-fitness strength was weakly anti-correlated with co-occurrence across 1.04M pairs (Spearman rho=-0.109, p<1e-300), a pattern attributed to strong co-fitness pairs often being core genes with near-universal prevalence. [^cofitness_coinheritance]

Only 0.7% of cofit pairs were genomically adjacent within 5 genes, and excluding adjacent pairs did not change the result pattern, so operon proximity did not explain the weak pairwise association. [^cofitness_coinheritance] The analysis therefore supports interpreting delta phi together with prevalence distributions, core-versus-auxiliary status, and the amount of computable data rather than treating raw phi as directly comparable across species. [^cofitness_coinheritance] Restricting primary comparisons to auxiliary pairs in which both clusters are below 95% prevalence would target the variable portion of the pangenome, although it would exclude genuinely coupled nearly universal genes. [^cofitness_coinheritance]

This limitation interacts with [phylogenetic-confounding-of-pangenome-associations](phylogenetic-confounding-of-pangenome-associations.md): cofit-pair phi was higher among near genomes than medium-distance genomes (mean=0.102 versus mean=0.067), most species lacked genomes in the far stratum (>0.05 branch distance), and phylogenetic stratification was available for 7 of 9 organisms. [^cofitness_coinheritance] Thus, insufficient prevalence variation and incomplete phylogenetic separation both constrain interpretation. [^cofitness_coinheritance] Near-clonal organisms nevertheless retained detectable signals: Ddia6719 had ANI 99.47% and delta=+0.093, while pseudo3_N2E3 had ANI 99.66% and delta=+0.026. [^cofitness_coinheritance]

## ICA modules show stronger co-inheritance

Across 195 ICA modules in 6 organisms, within-module co-occurrence had mean phi=0.229, compared with a prevalence-matched null mean of 0.177 from 1000 permutations, for delta=+0.053. [^cofitness_coinheritance] A total of 51/195 modules (26%) were significant at p<0.05, and 21/195 (11%) remained significant at q<0.05 after Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction, where FDR means false discovery rate. [^cofitness_coinheritance] This module-level result **supports** the conclusion that coordinated multi-gene structure captures a stronger pangenome inheritance signal than pairwise co-fitness, even when prevalence ceilings weaken individual-pair inference. [^cofitness_coinheritance]

The organism-level module results were Koxy: 44 modules, 14 (32%) significant, mean phi=0.079, null mean=0.040; Btheta: 36, 22 (61%), 0.192, 0.086; Putida: 38, 6 (16%), 0.266, 0.202; Korea: 29, 0 (0%), 0.357, 0.308; Phaeo: 37, 3 (8%), 0.108, 0.095; and pseudo3_N2E3: 40, 4 (10%), 0.444, 0.409. [^cofitness_coinheritance] Btheta showed the strongest module-level signal, whereas Korea had no significant modules because all Korea modules were greater than 90% core with prevalence near 1.0. [^cofitness_coinheritance] The results support [condition-space-dimensionality](condition-space-dimensionality.md) only indirectly: variation is organism-dependent, but environmental condition space was not tested as a causal explanation. [^cofitness_coinheritance]

The strongest enrichment occurred in accessory modules, defined as modules with less than 50% core genes: mean delta phi +0.108, with 8/11 (73%) significant at p<0.05 and 4/11 (36%) at FDR q<0.05. [^cofitness_coinheritance] Core modules, defined as more than 90% core genes, had mean delta +0.059, with 29/120 (24%) significant at p<0.05 and 13/120 (11%) at q<0.05. [^cofitness_coinheritance] Mixed modules, defined as 50–90% core genes, had mean delta +0.031, with 14/64 (22%) significant at p<0.05 and 4/64 (6%) at q<0.05. [^cofitness_coinheritance] The accessory-versus-core difference trended toward significance by Mann-Whitney testing (p=0.051). [^cofitness_coinheritance]

This accessory result **refines** rather than replaces the broader module picture. Across 1,116 ICA modules from 32 organisms, the independent [fitness_modules__REPORT](../summaries/fitness_modules__REPORT.md) analysis found 94.2% with significantly elevated within-module cofitness, mean within-module |r|=0.34 versus 0.12 in the background, and 22.7x genomic-adjacency enrichment. [^fitness_modules] Its held-out benchmark found Module-ICA had <1% strict precision for exact KEGG KO assignments, whereas ortholog transfer achieved 95.8% precision, 91.2% coverage, and 0.934 F1. [^fitness_modules] Modules are therefore supported as process-level, multi-gene units, but module membership does not establish an individual gene’s molecular function. [^fitness_modules]

Cross-organism alignment further supports the conservation aspect: 156 module families spanned 2+ organisms, including 28 spanning 5+, 7 spanning 10+, and 1 spanning 21 organisms; 145 families had consensus functional labels. [^fitness_modules] These are conservation patterns, not proof that every member has an identical molecular function. [^fitness_modules] Module membership is also not generally an accessory-genome phenomenon: module genes were 86.0% core versus 81.5% for all genes (OR=1.46, p=1.6e-87). [^module_conservation] Among 974 modules with >=3 mapped genes, 577 (59%) were >90% core, 349 (36%) were 50–90% core, and 48 (5%) were <50% core; the median module was 93.4% core. [^module_conservation]

The conservation-versus-fitness integration **supports** this predominantly core interpretation: 27,693 putative essential genes among 148,826 protein-coding genes across 33 organisms were 86.1% core, compared with 81.2% of non-essential genes, while essential-auxiliary genes numbered 3,683 and were 38.2% hypothetical. [^conservation_vs_fitness] Essential-core genes were 41.9% enzymes and 13.0% hypothetical, whereas essential-auxiliary genes were 13.4% enzymes and 38.2% hypothetical; this **refines** the module result by distinguishing a well-annotated conserved functional backbone from poorly characterized strain-specific essential functions. [^conservation_vs_fitness] The enrichment was modest overall (median odds ratio 1.56), and 18 of 33 organisms were significant after correction, so essentiality does not imply universal or perfectly conserved module membership. [^conservation_vs_fitness]

The COG functional-category comparison **supports and functionally refines** this core-versus-novel distinction: across 32 species spanning 9 phyla and 357,623 genes, core genes were concentrated relative to novel or singleton genes in conserved translation, nucleotide, coenzyme, amino-acid, and energy functions, while novel or singleton genes were enriched in mobile elements (COG L, +10.88%), defense mechanisms (COG V, +2.83%), and unknown function (COG S, +1.64%). [^cog_analysis] The COG L enrichment was consistent across 100% of species, whereas COG V was also consistent across 100% and COG S across 69%. [^cog_analysis] This **supports** interpreting predominantly core fitness modules as part of a conserved functional architecture, while suggesting that accessory-module co-inheritance may be especially relevant to mobile, defense, and niche-associated functions; it does not establish that any individual module has one of those functions. [^cog_analysis]

Thus, the stronger co-inheritance signal is concentrated most clearly in the accessory subset, while the conservation architecture of fitness modules is predominantly core. [^cofitness_coinheritance][^module_conservation] The cofitness analysis identifies 48 accessory modules, whereas the conservation analysis identifies 38 accessory module families; these are distinct module-level and family-level summaries, not a contradiction. [^cofitness_coinheritance][^module_conservation] The conservation-versus-fitness result further **supports** this distinction by showing that essential genes are preferentially core but that essential-auxiliary genes remain numerous and poorly characterized. [^conservation_vs_fitness] The evidence suggests that coordinated regulation or other functional coupling can leave a detectable inheritance signature, but does not establish that regulation causes co-inheritance. [^cofitness_coinheritance]

## Prevalence-aware interpretation and pangenome integration

The module contrast **supports** testing coordinated units alongside pairwise relationships: modules can retain detectable variation when individual pairs are weak, but Korea demonstrates that modules also lose detectability when all members are >90% core and prevalence is near 1.0. [^cofitness_coinheritance] Ddia6719’s delta=+0.093 and pseudo3_N2E3’s delta=+0.026 despite ANI values of 99.47% and 99.66% show that even near-clonal organisms can retain enough accessory variation to detect co-inheritance, although their high baseline phi values limit interpretation. [^cofitness_coinheritance]

The module-conservation analysis **supports** a related caution: family breadth was not associated with conservation (Spearman rho=-0.01, p=0.914), and the high all-gene core baseline of 81.5% limits absolute enrichment to the observed +4.5 percentage points. [^module_conservation] This suggests that module-family scope is less informative than gene-level conservation for explaining the genomic background against which co-inheritance is measured. [^module_conservation] The conservation-versus-fitness integration **supports** the same caution: pangenome core fractions are sensitive to clade size, and clades containing only 2 genomes can have trivially high core fractions because presence in both genomes equals 100% core. [^conservation_vs_fitness]

The study supports [pangenome-integration](pangenome-integration.md) by showing that linked functional-fitness and pangenome data can test whether laboratory relationships correspond to population-level gene co-occurrence, while prevalence is an explicit quality criterion for that cross-dataset analysis. [^cofitness_coinheritance] It also supports [cross-tenant-data-bridging](cross-tenant-data-bridging.md) because it joined Fitness Browser data, KBase pangenome clusters, phylogenetic distances, ICA modules, and SEED annotations through linked files and Spark extraction. [^cofitness_coinheritance] The conservation-versus-fitness integration **supports** this cross-dataset premise by mapping 44 of 48 Fitness Browser organisms to pangenome species clades and producing 177,863 gene-to-cluster links, but **refines** it with coverage limits: 33 organisms were used downstream, four organisms were unmatched because their species had too few GTDB genomes, and ten organisms were excluded for <90% DIAMOND coverage. [^conservation_vs_fitness]

The evidence does not establish that co-fitness causes co-inheritance: shared ancestry, prevalence structure, genome architecture, horizontal transfer, and other functional coupling can produce correlated presence patterns. [^cofitness_coinheritance] The narrower supported conclusion is that coordinated module membership was more strongly associated with pangenome co-occurrence than pairwise co-fitness, particularly for accessory modules, within a larger population of predominantly core co-regulated fitness modules. [^cofitness_coinheritance][^module_conservation]

The COG comparison **refines** the interpretation of horizontal transfer in this integration: the strongest cross-species novelty signal was COG L mobile-element enrichment, +10.88%, but the COG analysis was comparative functional annotation rather than a direct transfer test, and approximately 70% of genes had COG annotations. [^cog_analysis] Therefore, mobile-element enrichment is consistent with a mechanism that could help generate accessory co-inheritance, but it should not be treated as proof that the observed module associations were caused by horizontal transfer. [^cog_analysis]

## Open Directions

- Restrict pairwise and module analyses to auxiliary-only pairs in which both clusters are below 95% prevalence; test whether the module advantage and organism-level heterogeneity persist after removing the prevalence ceiling. [^cofitness_coinheritance]
- Recompute co-fitness from raw genefitness data for Ralstonia UW163 and Ralstonia GMI1000, then repeat prevalence-matched tests to determine whether their exclusion removed informative low-ANI diversity. [^cofitness_coinheritance]
- Resolve reference-genome mapping and apply stronger phylogenetic controls to test whether module-level delta phi remains higher than pairwise delta phi after ancestry is accounted for. [^cofitness_coinheritance]
- Combine prevalence-stratified phi estimates with reference-resolved phylogenetic distances to test whether co-fitness predicts co-occurrence after separately controlling for prevalence and shared ancestry. [^cofitness_coinheritance]
- Build module co-transfer networks and test cross-module prediction after matching module prevalence and auxiliary content, asking whether modules predict one another’s pangenome distributions beyond within-module co-occurrence. [^cofitness_coinheritance]
- Expand analyses to species with >30% auxiliary genes and existing co-fitness data, testing whether auxiliary-gene fraction and increased accessory variation improve pairwise and module-level detection. [^cofitness_coinheritance]
- Reanalyze the 974 mapped modules using the same prevalence-matched test, separating the 577 (59%) >90% core, 349 (36%) mixed, and 48 (5%) <50% core modules, to determine whether accessory enrichment persists across the 32-organism conservation set. [^module_conservation]
- Test whether the null family-breadth association (rho=-0.01, p=0.914) remains after matching module families for core fraction and prevalence, distinguishing gene-level conservation from module-scope effects. [^module_conservation]
- Apply prevalence-matched co-inheritance tests to the 156 cross-organism module families and ask whether family-level conservation predicts co-occurrence beyond organism, ancestry, and core-fraction controls. [^fitness_modules]
- Link accessory-module membership and COG L/V/S annotations to environmental metadata, testing whether mobile, defense, and unknown-function enrichments predict module co-inheritance after controlling for prevalence and phylogeny. [^cog_analysis]
- Extend the conservation-versus-fitness analysis from binary essentiality to condition-specific fitness, especially fitness < -2 under stress conditions, and test whether conditionally important genes show different module and core-fraction patterns. [^conservation_vs_fitness]
- Use Fitness Browser ortholog data to identify essential gene families conserved across species, correlate mean fitness effects with core-genome fraction, and characterize the 3,683 essential-auxiliary genes to test whether they compensate for missing core functions. [^conservation_vs_fitness]
