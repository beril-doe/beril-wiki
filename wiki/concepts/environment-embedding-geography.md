# Geographic and environmental structure in microbial genomic patterns

This concept examines how [[entities/alph-aearth]] satellite-derived embeddings, environmental metadata, metagenomes, and pangenome data encode geographic and environmental structure. Evidence is summarized in [[summaries/env_embedding_explorer__REPORT]], [[summaries/functional_dark_matter__REPORT]], [[summaries/gene_function_ecological_agora__REPORT]], [[summaries/genotype_to_phenotype_enigma__REPORT]], [[summaries/harvard_forest_warming__REPORT]], [[summaries/lab_field_ecology__REPORT]], [[summaries/lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas__REPORT]], [[summaries/metal_resistance_global_biogeography__REPORT]], [[summaries/microbeatlas_metal_ecology__REPORT]], [[summaries/phb_granule_ecology__REPORT]], [[summaries/plant_microbiome_ecotypes__REPORT]], [[summaries/prophage_ecology__REPORT]], [[summaries/pseudomonas_carbon_ecology__REPORT]], [[summaries/soil_frontier_genomics__REPORT]], [[summaries/soil_metal_functional_genomics__REPORT]], [[summaries/pitfalls]], and [[summaries/snipe_defense_system__REPORT]]. ## Dataset and coverage

The embedding project characterized 64-dimensional AlphaEarth embeddings for 83,287 genomes in the KBase pangenome database, representing 28.4% of 293,059 genomes; 79,449 had valid values across all dimensions and 3,838 contained at least one NaN. [src: env_embedding_explorer] The pitfalls audit **supports** treating this as a partial sampling frame: 3,838 of 83,287 genomes, or 4.6%, had at least one NaN, while coverage depends on valid latitude and longitude and is especially sparse for clinical isolates. [src: pitfalls]

The subset was strongly human-associated: human clinical genomes numbered 16,390 (20%), human gut 13,466 (16%), and human other 1,669 (2%), together comprising 38%; soil had 6,073 (7%), marine 5,850 (7%), and freshwater 5,840 (7%). Other contained 13,944 genomes (17%), including site-specific, generic, and uncaptured clinical labels. [src: env_embedding_explorer] The pitfalls audit found 52.7% unknown per-genome NCBI environment labels, or 94,957 of 180,025 genomes, while species-level majority-vote labels reached 91% coverage. [src: pitfalls]

The Pseudomonas analysis **supports** the importance of sampling composition while **refining** the scale at which carbon-pathway ecology can be tested: it included 12,732 genomes across 433 species clades, with *Pseudomonas* s.s. containing 19 species and 6,905 genomes and *Pseudomonas_E* 398 species and 5,687 genomes. Isolation-source metadata were classifiable for 8,171/12,732 genomes, or 64.2%, and *P. aeruginosa* comprised 6,760/12,732 genomes, or 53%. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] Among 54 free-living and plant-associated species, carbon profiles were associated with environment by 999 permutations (p = 0.006), but Random Forest balanced accuracy was 0.408 +/- 0.169 against a 0.250 chance baseline. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

This uneven sampling composition extends beyond genome embeddings. In 2,759 NMDC prokaryote-targeted metagenome runs, environmental metadata predicted eukaryotic read fraction across studies, but the association was largely confounded with study or batch. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] The functional-dark-matter collection contained 37/48 organisms from Pseudomonadota, and its top 500 candidates included 417 Gammaproteobacteria but no Archaea, Actinobacteria, or Epsilonproteobacteria. [src: functional_dark_matter]

Across 293,059 GTDB r214 genomes, only 7,995 genomes, or 2.7%, had plant-associated annotations covering 1,136 species; the endophyte category contained only 29 species, and BacDive identified 2,482 plant-related strains but added zero genome upgrades beyond ncbi_env. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] This **supports** explicit coverage stratification and **refines** environmental interpretation toward taxonomically and metadata-aware comparisons.

The prophage analysis **supports** this sampling-frame caution while **refining** it to mobile-module analysis. All 27,702 analyzed bacterial species carried prophage-associated clusters, totaling 4,005,537 clusters, but the calls came from eggNOG rather than dedicated prophage detection. Packaging and lysogenic regulation occurred in 100.0% of species, lysis in 99.9%, integration in 99.1%, anti-defense in 64.3%, head morphogenesis in 56.1%, and tail in 55.6%; only 34.9% carried all 7 modules. [src: prophage_ecology]

The global metal-resistance analysis **supports** the importance of environmental composition while **refining** geographic representativeness. Of 260,652 environmental MAGs extracted from MGnify, 30,497 remained after host-associated-biome filtering and 22,356 had usable coordinates; 2.8% carried at least one metal-resistance type. Soil had 5.8% prevalence, OR=5.05, q=1.8e-82; marine had 1.2%, OR=0.20, q=9.2e-80; and rhizosphere had 1.9%, OR=0.66, q=0.30 (NS). [src: metal_resistance_global_biogeography] This **supports** [[concepts/environmental-resistome]].

The MicrobeAtlas analysis **supports** environmental composition as a determinant while **refining** the question from biome prevalence to niche breadth. Across 464,000 16S amplicon samples, Levins' B_std niche breadth was phylogenetically conserved across 1,264 bacterial genera (Pagel's λ = 0.787, p = 7.9×10⁻¹⁰²), and habitat range was even more conserved (λ = 0.909, p = 1.4×10⁻¹⁵⁷). In 606 genera with AMR annotations, metal type diversity was associated with B_std in PGLS, phylogenetic generalized least squares (β = +0.021, SE = 0.0056, p = 1.5×10⁻⁴), whereas total AMR cluster count and core AMR fraction were not significant. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

The ecological-agora analysis **supports** environmental structure while **refining** its clade specificity: 35.6% of 90 Cyanobacteriia species were in a marine+sponge-dominant cluster, 34.0% of 50 Mycobacteriaceae species were in a gut+sludge cluster and 20.0% in another gut cluster, while 729 Bacteroidota species occupied marine and gut-associated clusters. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] The ENIGMA analysis similarly found that global Pseudomonas was 37.8% clinical, 12.9% soil/plant, and 9.4% aquatic, while all ENIGMA Pseudomonas belonged to the environmental Pseudomonas_E fluorescens/protegens clade; Rhodanobacter was 55% aquatic, 10% contaminated, and 0% clinical. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma]

The new soil-metal analysis **supports** the importance of environmental composition while **refining** the genomic signal from broad resistance prevalence to functional gene-content shifts. Across 51,748 soil samples and nine metals—[[entities/copper]], [[entities/cobalt]], [[entities/chromium]], [[entities/nickel]], [[entities/zinc]], [[entities/lead]], [[entities/arsenic]], [[entities/cadmium]], and [[entities/mercury]]—it identified 2,355 significant COG–metal associations at FDR < 0.05; COG means a Cluster of Orthologous Groups functional category and FDR means false discovery rate. Chromium and lead produced the strongest signals, with transporters including ABC and RND systems and biosynthesis genes dominating top hits. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics] In a copper-specific analysis of 116 COGs using 7,566 samples with nearby KBase genomes within 10 km, cell-division and nucleotide-transport categories BQ, FQ, and FK were the strongest positive associations, whereas energy-production categories DI and CE were the strongest negative associations. These observational associations **refine** the interpretation of environmental metal effects toward possible energetic trade-offs, not demonstrated mechanisms. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]

The same study reported a conditional db-RDA, distance-based redundancy analysis, R² = 0.799 and p = 0.005 using 999 permutations: after conditioning on batch and project effects, metal concentrations explained 80% of variance in community COG profiles. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics] This **supports** environmental structure but **refines** the comparison with prior predictive nulls: the value is conditional on project accession removal, and the unconditional metal-only R² was not reported. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics] Biome-stratified PGLS found distinct metal–COG relationships in soil, marine, and wastewater environments, **supporting** environment-specific responses rather than a universal resistance programme. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]

The new SNIPE analysis **supports** the importance of sampling composition while **refining** the environmental association to a mobile defense system. DUF4041/PF13250 occurred in 4,572 gene clusters across 1,696 species and 33 bacterial and archaeal phyla; 13.3% were core, 30.7% accessory, and 56.1% singleton, so 86.7% were accessory or singleton. [src: snipe_defense_system] Among species with AlphaEarth embeddings, SNIPE-bearing species numbered n=1,069 and non-SNIPE species n=13,977. Welch’s t-tests with Bonferroni correction found differences in 22 of 64 dimensions at p < 0.05; the largest effect was A19, Cohen’s d = 0.26 and p = 5.0e-14, with effect sizes |d| = 0.11–0.26. [src: snipe_defense_system] This **supports** a modest environmental shift rather than strict habitat segregation, but **refines** the conclusion because AlphaEarth covered only 28.4% of genomes and was biased toward environmental isolates with geographic metadata; no biological interpretation was assigned to A19. [src: snipe_defense_system]

The soil-frontier analysis **supports** the importance of coverage bias while **refining** the scale and direction of the gap. Across 5,441 soil samples with clay content, mine proximity, nighttime lights, uranium, and functional gene counts, the proposed clay shield was not supported globally: Soil & Climate, Geochemical, and Industrial models had out-of-sample R² = −0.205 ± 0.197, −0.331 ± 0.071, and −0.221 ± 0.042, respectively. Low- and high-clay cross-validation R² were −0.268 and −0.292, with a difference of 0.024 and 95% CI [−0.423, 0.161]; clay had importance approximately 0.14 but did not improve high-clay prediction. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] The result **refines** environmental-prediction claims because negative R² may reflect spatial autocorrelation, batch effects, train/test distributional shift, or high-leverage outliers rather than genuine global unpredictability.

The same study introduced the Genomic Discovery Index (GDI), defined as OTU Richness / (Mean Genome Completeness + 1), at 1° spatial bins. Forest had GDI = 902.36, cropland 890.82, grassland 503.42, and wetland 525.13; forest and cropland were therefore jointly highest-GDI biomes, whereas grassland and wetland were relatively well-mapped. Frontier areas with GDI > 1000 had mean pH = 6.74 versus 5.94 in mapped areas, a +0.8 pH unit gap. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] This **supports** [[concepts/functional-dark-matter]] and **refines** provenance interpretation: GDI conflates OTU richness with completeness and can equal 902 even when zero genomes are present, so the alkaline-soil gap may be a sequencing-effort effect rather than an assembly or annotation barrier. [src: soil_frontier_genomics]

## Environmental compartments, pathway content, and sampling scale

The Pseudomonas result **supports** environmental effects on pathway content but **refines** them toward deep-clade structure: among 7 *Pseudomonas* s.s. species and 189 *Pseudomonas_E* species with at least 5 genomes, 43 of 62 pathways differed after Mann–Whitney testing with Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate (FDR) correction (q < 0.05). Plant-derived sugar and sugar-alcohol pathways showed the largest losses in the *P. aeruginosa* group relative to the *P. fluorescens/putida* group, while amino-acid and core organic-acid pathways remained near-universal. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] The first five PCs captured 74.9% of variance, with PC1 explaining 31.2% and PC2 17.9%; environment-group distance was 2.054 between versus 1.890 within, p = 0.006, but four-class balanced accuracy was only 0.408 +/- 0.169. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

The plant analysis **supports** compartment structure but **contradicts** strong claims of broad species-level separation. In a refined 17-marker panel across 607 species, PERMANOVA gave pseudo-F = 23.2, p = 0.001, and R² = 0.071; PERMDISP gave F = 15.6 and p = 0.001, and db-RDA gave location-only R² = 0.060, p = 0.001. The earlier R² = 0.527 fell to R² = 0.072 after excluding three genome-rich species per compartment, an 86% loss. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Root-associated species were dominated by Rhizobium, Mesorhizobium, and Bradyrhizobium, phyllosphere species by Sphingomonas and Methylobacterium, and Pseudomonas_E was the only genus significantly represented across all compartments. ACC deaminase, T3SS, nitrogen fixation, and quorum sensing had OR = 69.3, 65.6, 14.5, and 24.1, respectively, but marker presence does not establish activity or phenotype. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The PHB analysis **supports** environmental context as a determinant of pathway distribution while **refining** environmental-breadth interpretation. Across 27,690 GTDB species, 21.9% carried phaC and 21.7% had a complete PHB pathway; prevalence was 44.0% in plant-associated species, 43.6% soil, 34.5% wastewater/engineered, 25.5% freshwater, 20.1% sediment, 18.7% marine, 11.1% human-associated, 7.4% human clinical, and 3.3% animal-associated. High-variability environments exceeded low-variability environments by 4.4×, 4.6×, 3.1×, and 1.4× across genome-size quartiles Q1–Q4, with p = 1.18 x 10^-11, 3.25 x 10^-47, 4.17 x 10^-70, and 3.21 x 10^-14. [src: phb_granule_ecology] Yet PHB-positive species had median embedding variance 0.3295 versus 0.2472 for PHB-negative species (Mann–Whitney U = 446,546, p = 1.88 x 10^-6), while genome size correlated with niche breadth at rho = 0.302 and p = 1.5e-43; after genome-size control, partial rho became -0.047 with p = 0.037, a 56.3% reduction and sign reversal. [src: phb_granule_ecology]

The prophage analysis **refines** this pathway-level picture. In 1,773 species, PERMANOVA on Bray-Curtis module composition found significant effects for genome-size quartile, environment, and family phylogeny, each p=0.01; genome size was dominant (F=212.99), followed by environment (F=30.04) and phylogeny (F=6.17), and genome size correlated with prophage cluster count at rho=0.717. [src: prophage_ecology] Nevertheless, environmental effects remained within every genome-size quartile, all Kruskal-Wallis p < 6.5e-78. Constrained permutations across 18,031 species enriched tail, head morphogenesis, and anti-defense in human-associated environments at log2(OR)=2.21, 1.98, and 1.70, with Z-scores 10.86, 10.00, and 8.76; human-clinical environments enriched anti-defense, tail, and head morphogenesis at log2(OR)=0.14, 0.77, and 0.70, while anti-defense was depleted in freshwater and animal-associated environments at log2(OR)=-0.74 and -0.24. [src: prophage_ecology]

The soil-metal study **supports** this compartment-specific pathway interpretation and **refines** it with a direct chemical gradient. Biome-stratified PGLS found different metal–COG relationships in soil, marine, and wastewater environments, while the copper analysis suggested positive associations for cell division and nucleotide transport and negative associations for energy-production categories. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics] However, the result is observational and does not establish that copper caused the shifts or that the energy categories represent a mechanistic trade-off. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]

The SNIPE result **supports** the mobile-module interpretation and **refines** environmental pathway evidence toward defense systems. The 86.7% accessory-plus-singleton fraction is consistent with frequent gain or loss and mobile defense-island carriage, while the 22/64 significant AlphaEarth dimensions show a modest environmental association rather than a named habitat effect. [src: snipe_defense_system] The corrected domain architecture is PF13250/DUF4041 plus PF13455/Mug113, not canonical GIY-YIG PF01541: zero surveyed clusters contained both DUF4041/PF13250 and PF01541, although 54 of 4,572 DUF4041 clusters carried the Mug113 description. [src: snipe_defense_system]

The plant pathway analysis found beneficial plant-growth-promotion clusters had a 64.6% core fraction versus 45.2% for pathogenic clusters and 46.8% genome-wide; the per-species Mann–Whitney U was 83,567,419, p = 3.38e-125, with bootstrap 95% CI [0.089, 0.106]. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] However, a study of ten Pseudomonas strains found biocontrol traits largely accessory, so the result is taxonomically scoped. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Species carrying singleton marker clusters were 16 times more likely to carry transposase or integrase singletons (Fisher OR = 15.95, p = 8.8e-20), but marker singletons had enrichment ratio 0.78 relative to the genomic average, with Wilcoxon p < 1e-300; plant-associated genera had median 3.7 versus 2.8 mobile elements per genome, p = 1.49×10⁻⁵. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

## Geographic distance-decay in embedding space

Across 50,000 sampled genome pairs with good coordinates, mean embedding cosine distance increased from 0.41 for <100 km across 231 pairs, to 0.51 for 100–500 km across 1,058, 0.56 for 500–1K km across 2,016, 0.66 for 1K–2K km across 3,779, 0.78 for 2K–5K km across 5,824, 0.80 for 5K–10K km across 20,935, and 0.82 for 10K–20K km across 16,107. [src: env_embedding_explorer] The relationship was strongest below 2,000 km and plateaued above 5,000 km, supporting environmental distance-decay rather than a simple raw-distance effect. [src: env_embedding_explorer]

The NMDC analysis **refines** this interpretation: within one constant-protocol soil study, local vegetation and geography remained associated with eukaryotic signal, whereas cross-study environment prediction had out-of-study R²=−0.30 and within-study five-fold R²=+0.17 ± 0.06. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] The pitfalls audit found a pooled near-versus-far ratio of 2.0x, versus 3.4x for environmental samples and 2.0x for human-associated samples. [src: pitfalls]

The global metal-resistance analysis **supports** spatial structure but **refines** it into a sampling-sensitive hypothesis. Of 289 5° grid cells with at least 5 MAGs, 11 hotspots had OR>2 and q<0.05 and 3 coldspots had OR<0.5 and q<0.05. The strongest hotspot was the Atacama/Andean region of Chile at lat=-25°, lon=-70°, with 21.8% prevalence, OR=9.83, q=7.6e-12, and n=101 MAGs. [src: metal_resistance_global_biogeography] These statistics describe the sampled archive, not a causal effect of proximity.

The MicrobeAtlas result **supports** geographic structure while **refining** it with phylogeny-aware comparisons: across 606 genera, metal type diversity—not total AMR cluster count or core AMR fraction—predicted niche breadth (raw r ≈ 0.21; PGLS β = +0.021). The association remained positive after pangenome coverage (β = +0.0204, SE = 0.0057, p = 3.4×10⁻⁴) and genome size (β = +0.0218, SE = 0.0061, p = 3.6×10⁻⁴), but strict 5% within-environment prevalence reduced the sample to 379 genera and gave β = +0.0166, p = 0.092. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

Harvard Forest **refines** distance-decay with a controlled single-site contrast. In 42 biosamples from a 25-year +5°C warming experiment, direct-sample taxonomy reproduced Actinobacteria-up/Acidobacteria-down in organic soil, while mineral soil showed no phylum-level changes after FDR correction. PERMANOVA attributed 7.6% of variance to warming (p=0.069), 30.6% to soil horizon (p=0.0002), and 41% to treatment × horizon (p=0.0002). [src: harvard_forest_warming]

Oak Ridge **supports** environmental context but **refines** laboratory-to-field transfer: across 108 [[entities/enigma-coral]] groundwater sites, [[entities/16s-amplicon-sequencing]] detected 14 of 26 Fitness Browser genera; Sphingomonas, Pseudomonas, and Caulobacter occurred at 93%, 91%, and 82% of sites. Five of 11 sufficiently prevalent genera had uranium associations after BH-FDR correction, in both directions, while the aggregate laboratory metal-tolerance score had rho=0.503, p=0.095, n=12. [src: lab_field_ecology]

The lanthanide atlas **supports** environmental association while **refining** its scope: xoxF occurred in 3,690 genomes versus 195 with mxaF, ratio 18.92 : 1, xoxF fraction 0.9498 with 95% CI [0.9425, 0.9558], and one-sided p = 7.6 × 10⁻²². Soil_sediment had 6.84% xoxF, OR = 1.92, p_BH = 6.1 × 10⁻³⁹; marine had 4.76%, OR = 1.31, p_BH = 7.8 × 10⁻⁷; REE-impacted samples had 10.81%, OR = 3.51, p_BH = 0.082, and REE-acid-mine-drainage contained only 37 MAGs. [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]

The soil-metal study **supports** the importance of spatial matching while **refining** the meaning of proximity-based associations. Its copper-specific analysis matched 7,566 soil samples to nearby KBase genomes using a 10 km criterion, but some genomes designated nearby may not have been co-located with the copper measurements. Sensitivity analyses at 5 km and 20 km are therefore needed before copper–COG attribution is treated as spatially robust. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]

The prophage study found partial Spearman rho=0.468 between environmental niche breadth and module count after genome-size control in 2,008 species, p=8.41e-110. In 6,365 NMDC samples, taxonomy-based burden inference had 87.2% median matching coverage and identified 57 significant module–abiotic correlations; the strongest included packaging with pH rho=0.519, all modules with pH rho=0.474, temperature rho=0.399, depth rho=0.361, and total nitrogen rho=0.333. [src: prophage_ecology] The pH result suggests, but does not establish, that alkaline environments favor lysogeny or that prophage burden responds to pH-linked conditions.

The SNIPE analysis **supports** this pattern of geographically structured but modest environmental association: the 22/64 significant AlphaEarth dimensions had effects of |d| = 0.11–0.26, with A19 the largest at d = 0.26. [src: snipe_defense_system] Because AlphaEarth dimensions derive from satellite imagery at sample coordinates and may capture vegetation, temperature, precipitation, and land cover, but A19 has no assigned biological interpretation, the result **refines** the prophage and metal findings as statistical environmental associations rather than mechanisms. [src: snipe_defense_system]

The soil study **contradicts** a strong interpretation of negative global prediction as a biological null. Its three model families all performed worse than the training mean, but the analysis did not distinguish distributional shift caused by spatial autocorrelation within GroupKFold folds, high-leverage outliers dominating test-fold mean squared error, and genuine unpredictability of functional gene counts from measured predictors. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] The soil-metal study adds a contrasting conditional association: metals explained 80% of residual community COG-profile variance in db-RDA, but the conditional R² = 0.799 cannot be compared directly with an unconditional biological effect until the metal-only R² is reported. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics] Thus the clay result is a global predictive null, not yet evidence that soil functional potential is biologically unrelated to measured stressors. [src: soil_frontier_genomics]

## Embedding structure and environment

UMAP, a nonlinear dimensional-reduction method, projected the embeddings into two dimensions, and DBSCAN, a density-based clustering method, identified 320 clusters. Many clusters were dominated by one harmonized environment category, while Air, Extreme, and Plant concentrated in a few clusters; Human gut and Human clinical genomes were distributed across many clusters, potentially reflecting geographic substructure. Phylum coloring showed taxonomic structure partly confounded with environment; Campylobacterota were predominantly gut-associated. [src: env_embedding_explorer]

The ecological-agora analysis **refines** this evidence with AlphaEarth k = 10 clusters: Cyanobacteriia concentrated in marine+sponge clusters, Mycobacteriaceae in gut-associated clusters, and Bacteroidota in both marine and gut clusters. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] ENIGMA found 8 metabolic guilds across 20 taxonomic orders from 7,167 KO presence/absence features across 123 strains; guilds aligned with, but were not identical to, taxonomy. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma]

The Pseudomonas analysis **refines** cluster interpretation: its four environment classes contained 51 species—soil 13, freshwater 13, plant_surface 19, rhizosphere 6—and the most discriminating pathways were D-serine (importance 0.132), arabinose (0.094), rhamnose (0.086), fucose (0.085), and xylose (0.070), but balanced accuracy was 0.408 +/- 0.169. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

Harvard Forest found carbon-cycling KOs enriched among heated-up DNA KOs in organic soil: 5 of 57 carbon-cycling hits versus 428 of 12,806 other hits, Fisher OR 2.78, one-sided p=0.042; approximately 39% of DNA KOs were organic-only, mineral-only, or sign-flipping. [src: harvard_forest_warming]

The plant analysis **refines** guild interpretation: after reducing 91 markers to 17 and applying KEGG module gates, 878 of 1,115 plant-associated species, or 78.7%, were dual-nature; 108 pathogenic, 94 beneficial, and 35 neutral. Broader binary presence classified 60.3% of 25,660 species as dual-nature, while composite scoring gave 38.0% neutral, 29.5% pathogenic, 25.2% dual-nature, and 7.3% beneficial. All 14 known beneficial or pathogenic species were assigned dual-nature and all four neutral controls were misclassified. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Host extraction identified 1,307 species across 19 hosts; rice contributed 286, Arabidopsis thaliana 184, wheat 122, and maize 98. Only 11.7% Jaccard overlap existed between 487 pangenome-derived plant genera and 438 MGnify rhizosphere genera; of 65 plant-associated species with at least 20 genomes, only 18 had tree-distance data, 5 of 17 testable species passed Bonferroni-corrected Fisher tests, and strongest host-by-subclade evidence occurred in Xanthomonas campestris and Xanthomonas vasicola. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The prophage analysis **supports** environmental structure beyond taxonomy but **refines** the ecological unit to modules and lineages. MMseqs2 at 70% AAI, amino-acid identity, grouped 38,085 TerL sequences from 11,789 species into 10,991 lineages; the largest had 1,094 members across 869 species and 6,921 lineages (63%) were singletons. Thresholds of 50% and 80% AAI gave 4,001 and 16,283 lineages. Among 824 lineages with at least 5 species, 325 were specialists and 499 generalists, but 0/500 tests showed significant environment-specific enrichment after FDR correction. [src: prophage_ecology]

The SNIPE result **supports** testing accessory defense systems in this framework but **refines** marker choice. The high-confidence SNIPE set is based on PF13250/DUF4041 with PF13455, whereas canonical PF01541 is not a reliable nuclease marker; 80.4% of DUF4041 clusters, or 3,675/4,572, were represented by T5orf172 or DUF4041 descriptions, while 897 included potentially noisy primary annotations such as histidine kinase (319) and seryl-tRNA aminoacylation (238). [src: snipe_defense_system] Three 558-aa DUF4041 proteins were detected in Klebsiella alongside 28 mannose PTS IID/ManZ proteins and 1 mannose PTS IIA/ManX protein, but no Klebsiella SNIPE or ManYZ fitness data were available; the co-occurrence is therefore a hypothesis about phage-entry ecology, not a demonstrated mechanism. [src: snipe_defense_system]

## Coordinate quality and metadata limitations

Among 83,286 genomes with coordinates, 50,109 (60.2%) were Good, 30,469 (36.6%) Suspicious cluster, and 2,708 (3.3%) Low precision. Suspicious clusters included potentially institutional addresses and legitimate field sites such as Rifle, Colorado (39.54, -107.78; 1,883 genomes), Saanich Inlet (48.36, -123.30; 1,529), Siberian soda lakes (52.11, 79.17; 812), Pittsburgh (40.44, -79.97; 630), and Lima (-12.0, -77.0; 1,641). [src: env_embedding_explorer]

Cleaned latitude/longitude were available for 83,286 genomes, geo_loc_name for 83,270, isolation_source for 76,295 (91.6%), host for 53,091 (63.7%), env_broad_scale for 34,800 (41.8%), env_local_scale for 31,541 (37.9%), and env_medium for 31,483 (37.8%). Keyword processing mapped 5,774 unique isolation_source values to 12 broad categories, capturing 71% of genomes with a label; 17% remained Other and 12.5% Unknown. [src: env_embedding_explorer]

The pitfalls audit **supports** these provenance concerns: ncbi_env is entity–attribute–value data requiring joins through genome.ncbi_biosample_id and ncbi_env.accession, attribute filtering, and pivoting; missing classifications cannot be interpreted as biological absence. [src: pitfalls] The global metal analysis found a 30.8% per-sample coordinate gap, although all 532 MAG grid cells represented in its coordinate dataset had ENA coverage. [src: metal_resistance_global_biogeography]

The soil-metal study **supports** provenance-aware environmental joins and **refines** the spatial problem to the genome–sample matching step. The copper analysis used a 10 km proximity criterion to associate 7,566 soil samples with nearby KBase genomes, but nearby genomes may not be co-located with the measured soil samples; 5 km and 20 km sensitivity analyses are required. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]

MicrobeAtlas found pangenome coverage correlated with metal type diversity at r = 0.35, p = 2.6×10⁻¹⁹; single-genome genera had lower metal type counts than multi-genome genera (Mann–Whitney p = 5.1×10⁻¹⁰), and only 16.8% of reads joined genus-level AMR data. Rarefaction retained a positive direction in all 200 iterations, but only 57.5% were Bonferroni-significant at p < 0.0083; strict prevalence was non-significant at p = 0.092. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

The prophage burden bridge assumed genus-level conservation and had 87.2% median matching coverage; only 28% of genomes had AlphaEarth embeddings, producing a biased subsample. [src: prophage_ecology] NMDC workflow results must be linked at workflow_run_id level: 1,067 of 2,759 runs were pooled from multiple biosamples, so biosample joins could create pseudo-replication. GOTTCHA2 provided usable eukaryotic signal, whereas Kraken2 and Centrifuge were prokaryote-restricted in that deployment. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] Functional-dark-matter mapping covered only 5 of 6 carrier genera, and 441 of 449 exploratory tests were significant at FDR < 0.05. [src: functional_dark_matter]

The ecological-agora study found 12 of 32 genus-level mismatches caused by ENIGMA strain-name collisions, including one introducing 1,751 spurious clinical genomes; GTDB-Tk and genus checks reduced verified linkages from 32 to 20. Harvard Forest's abiotic_features field was all zeros because of an NMDC parsing artifact, leaving +5°C as the only environmental contrast. Oak Ridge was limited by genus-level 16S resolution, temporal mismatch, aggregation, and uncontrolled pH, dissolved oxygen, carbon, and other covariates. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora; genotype_to_phenotype_enigma; harvard_forest_warming; lab_field_ecology]

The plant study found 12 of 22 marker Pfams absent from bakta_pfam_domains despite abundance in interproscan_domains; the ten non-zero Bakta Pfams returned only 10–35% of InterProScan counts. Versioned identifiers recovered 19,364 hits across 7,962 species for ten marker Pfams. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Pseudomonas source classification had approximately 6.7% unknown and approximately 29.1% other; majority voting can obscure generalists, GapMind omits genus-specific capabilities including toluene, naphthalene, and benzoate degradation, and phylogenetic non-independence was not explicitly controlled. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

SNIPE **refines** these provenance limitations with marker-specific annotation uncertainty. eggNOG Pfam annotations may miss divergent homologues; only 54/4,572 DUF4041 clusters had Mug113 co-annotation, and DUF4041 may occur in non-SNIPE proteins. [src: snipe_defense_system] AlphaEarth coverage was 28.4% and biased toward environmental isolates with coordinates; the planned COG V defense-gene density join could not be completed because it required Spark Connect for a 93M × 132M row join. NMDC analysis returned only 2 samples for the queried ecosystem format, and PhageFoundry had no Klebsiella fitness/TnSeq tables. [src: snipe_defense_system]

The soil-frontier analysis **supports** the need for provenance-aware environmental joins but **refines** the GDI interpretation. GDI is a novel index without published precedent; because its denominator is Mean Genome Completeness + 1, zero completeness can produce a high value, and richness can dominate the index. The recommended validation is to report richness and completeness separately, correct for uniform 16S sequencing depth, bootstrap biome-level confidence intervals, and control pH discovery bias for the number of 16S samples in each pH bin. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] Forest GDI = 902.36 versus cropland GDI = 890.82 differs by 1.3%, so the values are not meaningfully distinguishable without uncertainty estimates. [src: soil_frontier_genomics]

The soil-metal report adds an independent multiple-testing and confounding limitation. Its 2,355 discoveries among 3,915 implied tests—nine metals × 435 COGs—represent a 60% discovery rate, but co-contamination makes tests non-independent; Benjamini–Hochberg FDR may therefore be anti-conservative under positive correlation, and the true FDR may be higher than reported. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics] Effect sizes were not systematically reported, and associations with rho < 0.05 may be statistically significant without substantial biological effect. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]

## Relationship to environment–gene-content analysis

The prior [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]] analysis found AlphaEarth environment similarity to be a weak predictor of gene content, with median partial correlation 0.0025. The 38% human-associated composition may dilute that relationship, but this does not establish that environmental similarity strongly predicts gene content. [src: env_embedding_explorer]

Pseudomonas **supports** a more limited proposition: carbon profiles contained an ecological signal but were dominated by subgenus structure. Pathway richness was higher in free-living and plant-associated species than host-associated species, with median 57 pathways complete in more than 50% of genomes versus 55; within *Pseudomonas_E*, plant-associated species averaged 56.7, free-living 56.1, and host-associated 55.2. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] MicrobeAtlas similarly found metal type diversity, but not total AMR burden or core AMR fraction, associated with niche breadth after phylogenetic adjustment; strict prevalence reduced the effect to β = +0.0166, p = 0.092. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

The soil-metal result **supports** a more conditional version of environment–gene-content association. It found 2,355 significant COG–metal associations across 51,748 soil samples and a conditional db-RDA R² = 0.799 after batch and project conditioning, while biome-stratified PGLS found distinct relationships in soil, marine, and wastewater. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics] These results **refine** the weak whole-genome AlphaEarth correlation by showing that measurable environmental associations may occur for selected functional categories and chemical gradients, but observational design, co-contamination, conditional variance accounting, incomplete effect sizes, and spatial matching prevent a causal or universal interpretation. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]

Functional-dark-matter tests covered 151 accessory dark-gene clusters across 31 species; 10 of 137 environmental-category tests and 1 of 67 AlphaEarth tests were significant at FDR < 0.05. Nitrogen-source carriers correlated with total nitrogen (ρ = +0.109, n = 1,231, FDR = 2.3e-4) and ammonium nitrogen (ρ = +0.231, n = 1,230, FDR = 8.0e-16), pH carriers with pH (ρ = +0.157, n = 4,366, FDR = 7.4e-25), and anaerobic carriers negatively with dissolved oxygen (ρ = -0.298, n = 272, FDR = 1.5e-6). [src: functional_dark_matter]

The plant analysis found 5,341 of 5,671 eggNOG ortholog groups significant in raw plant-versus-non-plant tests, but only 50 enriched families retained significance after phylum control. COG3569 had Fisher OR = 8.92 and phylogenetically controlled OR = 6.01, q = 7.7e-242, occurring in 54% of plant-associated species versus 12% of non-plant species. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The proposed test remains whether environmental samples with similar AlphaEarth embeddings share more accessory genes after controlling for phylogeny. [src: env_embedding_explorer] The pitfalls audit requires genome_id rather than gtdb_taxonomy_id, shared representations such as COG, KEGG orthologs, or Pfam, genus checks, and assembly accessions such as GCF_*. [src: pitfalls]

The prophage result **refines** this test by showing that environmental similarity may predict selected mobile modules even where whole-lineage similarity does not. Across 15 phylogenetically stratified species, 44/95 module-species tests (46.3%) showed higher within-module co-occurrence than expected, with mean contig co-localization 0.769; packaging, lysis, and lysogenic regulation were more co-localized, while integration and anti-defense were more dispersed. [src: prophage_ecology] The SNIPE result **supports** extending this design to accessory defense islands: 86.7% of DUF4041 clusters were accessory or singleton, but domain incompleteness and sampling bias require PF13250-plus-PF13455 searches and phylogeny-aware validation. [src: snipe_defense_system]

ENIGMA separated predictable capability from quantitative performance: across 46,389 genome × condition pairs from 727 genomes and 363 conditions, binary growth AUC was 0.620 under genus-blocked validation, while KO × condition interactions increased mean AUC to 0.653 in 80 of 106 held-out genera. Amino acids and nucleosides had AUC 0.775 and 0.780, metals 0.605, antibiotics 0.619, and nitrogen 0.435; continuous µmax, lag, and yield-related max_A had negative R² under genus-blocked holdout. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma]

Plant complementarity **contradicted** a strong co-occurrence model: corrected pair-level complementarity was slightly below random, Cohen’s d ≈ −0.4 and permutation p < 0.001; prevalence-weighted d = −0.39. BacDive and GapMind agreed at 83.1%, but GapMind core completeness was 0% across compartments and the NMDC C-score analysis had 0 PGP-dominant, 3 pathogen-dominant, and 66 dual-or-mixed genera among 69. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The soil-frontier result **refines** the proposed environment-to-gene-content test by adding a soil-specific null and a coverage-bias metric. Clay content was a consistent feature with importance approximately 0.14 but did not improve high-clay prediction, while GDI identified jointly high-discovery-gap forest and cropland biomes and a +0.8 pH difference between frontier and mapped areas. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] These results motivate separating true functional unpredictability from spatial/model failure and separating sampling gaps from assembly or annotation gaps before interpreting environmental gene-content associations. [src: soil_frontier_genomics]

## Tensions

Embeddings show clear geographic distance-decay, yet environment similarity had median partial correlation 0.0025 with gene content. [src: env_embedding_explorer] These measures differ, and the latter is affected by human-associated composition, metadata, coordinate quality, and phylogeny; the pitfalls audit found a 2.0x pooled near-versus-far ratio, 3.4x for environmental samples, 2.0x for human-associated samples, and 52.7% unknown labels. [src: pitfalls]

Pseudomonas carbon profiles were environmentally associated (p = 0.006), but balanced accuracy was 0.408 +/- 0.169 and PCA primarily separated *Pseudomonas* s.s. from *Pseudomonas_E*. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] PHB showed raw breadth association rho = 0.106, p = 1.77 x 10^-06, but genome-size-controlled partial rho = -0.047, p = 0.037. [src: phb_granule_ecology]

The soil-metal study adds a tension between strong conditional multivariate association and uncertain biological attribution. Its db-RDA gave conditional R² = 0.799 and p = 0.005 after project and batch conditioning, but the unconditional R² for metals alone was not reported; chromium, copper, lead, and zinc co-vary in many industrial soils, so individual COG–metal associations may reflect generic multi-metal contamination rather than metal-specific responses. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics] The 60% discovery rate among 3,915 implied tests may also be inflated because positively correlated tests can make Benjamini–Hochberg FDR anti-conservative. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]

A second soil-metal tension concerns spatial attribution. The copper analysis used 7,566 samples with nearby genomes within 10 km, but proximity does not guarantee co-location; therefore the copper–COG result is not yet directly comparable with coordinate-based distance-decay evidence until 5 km and 20 km sensitivity analyses are completed. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]

Prophage module effects remained significant within genome-size quartiles, but genome size dominated PERMANOVA (F=212.99), calls were annotation-based, only 28% of genomes had embeddings, and NMDC burden was genus-inferred. [src: prophage_ecology] Human-associated module enrichments contrasted with the absence of significant TerL-lineage enrichment in 0/500 tests, supporting modular exchange but not independent adaptation of complete phage lineages. [src: prophage_ecology]

MicrobeAtlas produced a primary PGLS effect β = +0.021, p = 1.5×10⁻⁴, but strict prevalence gave p = 0.092 and groundwater-specific fold enrichment was null (ρ = +0.042, p = 0.242). [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] NMDC cross-study prediction had R²=−0.30 versus within-study R²=+0.17 ± 0.06. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] The global metal hotspot at Atacama/Andean Chile had 21.8% prevalence and OR=9.83, but does not establish stable regional biogeography independent of sampling. [src: metal_resistance_global_biogeography]

Plant compartment PERMANOVA had refined R² = 0.071, while the earlier R² = 0.527 collapsed to 0.072 after removing genome-rich species. Plant-associated mobilome burden was higher (3.7 versus 2.8), but marker singletons were depleted (enrichment ratio 0.78), and corrected complementarity was negative (d ≈ −0.4). [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

SNIPE adds a related tension: its environmental association is statistically detectable in 22 of 64 AlphaEarth dimensions, but the largest effect is modest (d = 0.26), AlphaEarth covers only 28.4% of genomes, and no dimension has a named biological interpretation. [src: snipe_defense_system] Its 86.7% accessory-plus-singleton distribution supports mobile defense-island turnover, but only 54/4,572 DUF4041 clusters carry Mug113 co-annotation and DUF4041 can occur outside SNIPE, so observed prevalence and environmental pattern may reflect incomplete or noisy domain detection. [src: snipe_defense_system] This **refines** rather than resolves the broader tension between environmental association and causal gene-content prediction.

The soil-frontier analysis adds a direct tension between a predictive null and a biological-null interpretation. All three model families had negative out-of-sample R², and low-clay prediction was not better than high-clay prediction: the difference was 0.024 with 95% CI [−0.423, 0.161]. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] This **supports** the conclusion that the clay shield was not demonstrated at global scale, but it **contradicts** treating the result alone as evidence of genuine biological unpredictability because spatial distributional shift, outlier leverage, and model failure were not separated. [src: soil_frontier_genomics]

A second soil tension concerns discovery gaps. Forest GDI = 902.36 and cropland GDI = 890.82 were identified as jointly highest, but their difference is 1.3% and lacks bootstrap confidence intervals. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] The +0.8 pH gap between frontier and mapped areas may indicate under-sampling of alkaline microbiomes, but reverse causality remains possible because fewer alkaline samples may have been sequenced; GDI can also be high when completeness is zero. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] This **refines** the existing provenance tension: environmental under-representation is measurable, but its biological versus sampling origin remains unresolved.

The ecological-agora, ENIGMA, Harvard Forest, Oak Ridge, and lanthanide results retain their respective tensions: cluster alignment is not causal; global Pseudomonas representation differs from ENIGMA's environmental clade; treatment alone explained 7.6% (p=0.069) versus treatment × horizon 41%; laboratory metal tolerance was non-significant in field abundance; and REE-impacted samples did not pass FDR while REE-AMD contained only 37 MAGs. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora; genotype_to_phenotype_enigma; harvard_forest_warming; lab_field_ecology; lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]

The pitfalls audit found K=4 LDA ecotypes with a 33-species Tier-A list, but held-out-species Jaccard values 0.230 for E1 and 0.064 for E3; independent within-substudy evidence reduced the list to 3 candidates. [src: pitfalls] This **contradicts** treating the unvalidated list as stable and supports held-out-feature clustering and independent validation.
