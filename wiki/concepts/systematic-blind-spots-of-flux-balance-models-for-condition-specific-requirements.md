# Systematic blind spots of flux balance models for condition-specific requirements

Flux balance analysis (FBA) models of Acinetobacter baylyi ADP1 miss condition-specific gene requirements in several systematic ways: medium assumptions, cofactor supply, iron, respiratory capacity and threshold effects. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality, aromatic_catabolism_network, respiratory_chain_wiring] The evidence comes from one organism across four sources, with direct enrichment statistics for aromatic degradation and mostly explanatory reasoning for the respiratory and cofactor points. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality, aromatic_catabolism_network] The page stops at growth-defect quantification among dispensable genes. See [[entities/flux-balance-analysis]] and [[entities/acinetobacter-baylyi-adp1]].

## Literature Context

The four candidate papers are genome-scale metabolic model studies in other organisms. Each of them tests or discusses gene essentiality predictions. They do not address Acinetobacter baylyi ADP1, and none of them studies aromatic catabolism, Complex I or NDH-2. They give context for the general limits of model-based essentiality prediction. They cannot confirm the ADP1-specific blind spots directly.

The Yeast 6 consensus network reports improved sensitivity, specificity and predictive values for gene essentiality under glucose-limited aerobic conditions when it is analyzed with FBA, compared with Yeast 5. It also reports improved prediction of auxotrophy. The same update expanded biochemical coverage and corrected earlier inaccuracies [PMID 23935056](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23935056/). The abstract does not attribute the gain to any single change. It is consistent with the hub's view that missing reaction content limits prediction, but it does not isolate that cause. In P. putida KT2440, auxotrophy was correctly predicted in 75% of cases. That study concluded that network structure is the main determinant of prediction accuracy and that biomass composition has negligible influence [PMID 18974823](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18974823/). It also used the reconstruction to identify knowledge gaps. This agrees with the hub's claim that the ADP1 gaps lie in the network, such as the 30 of 51 quinate-specific genes without reaction mappings. The ADP1 aromatic-degradation gap is attributed instead to medium assumptions, so the P. putida result is only partly parallel.

Two other papers show that environment-specific context is a recognized difficulty. For Mycobacterium tuberculosis, limited knowledge of the in-host environment complicates modeling. A condition-specific biomass reaction derived from transcript data predicted nutrient uptake accurately. The authors say that gene essentiality "can also be predicted but accurate predictions remain difficult to obtain" [PMID 30123778](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30123778/). For Clostridium difficile, the authors note that a standard model cannot account for metabolic changes across environments. They integrated transcriptomic and codon-usage data and reported 92.3% accuracy against experimental essentiality data for R20291 homologs [PMID 28209199](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28209199/). These results support the general point that fixed model assumptions limit condition-specific prediction. The hub's suggestion to update ADP1 media assumptions from measured composition follows the same logic, though that ADP1 test has not been done.

The corpus's more specific claims go beyond these papers. These are the under-prediction of aromatic degradation genes (9 of 11 discordant, OR=9.7, q=0.012), the missing Complex I threshold effect, and the NDH-2 routing under growth-rate optimization. None of the candidates reports such results, so they are novel relative to this set. They also rest on one organism and, for the respiratory points, mostly on explanatory reasoning. The candidate papers neither contradict nor test them.

## Aromatic catabolism and medium assumptions

Aromatic degradation genes are the clearest blind spot: they are systematically under-predicted by FBA in ADP1. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] In the original study, 9 of 11 aromatic degradation genes were FBA-discordant (OR=9.7, q=0.012). [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] The refined analysis reports OR = 9.70, q = 0.012, and says 9 of 11 were discordant. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] This analysis used RAST function annotations with 100% gene coverage and Fisher's exact test with BH-FDR (Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate) correction, among 157 discordant genes. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] Aromatic degradation is named as the main source of FBA discordance. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]

Almost all of these genes are FBA-blocked, with zero predicted flux, yet their deletion impairs growth. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] A directional analysis found enrichment for FBA under-prediction (OR = 12.0, q = 0.004). [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] The refined study confirms under-prediction for genes that are blocked in FBA but KO-essential (KEGG ortholog) or show growth defects. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] The discordant genes include the beta-ketoadipate pathway enzymes 4-carboxymuconolactone decarboxylase and beta-ketoadipate enol-lactone hydrolase. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] See [[entities/ketoadipate-pathway]].

The report attributes this to environmental assumptions: the model assumes minimal media without aromatic compounds, so flux is blocked. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] It offers two explanations for the observed growth defects. Experimental media may contain trace aromatics, for example from yeast extract or agar impurities. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] Alternatively, the genes may have moonlighting functions. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] The report reads this as a systematic gap in model assumptions, not in network topology. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] This is a hypothesis the report has not tested. It recommends adding trace aromatic compounds to the media definition, testing whether the beta-ketoadipate discordance resolves, and updating assumptions from measured media composition. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]

Lipid metabolism contrasts with aromatic catabolism. It was depleted among discordant genes (OR = 0.34, q = 0.042), with only 7 of 46 lipid genes discordant. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] The report suggests the model handles lipid pathways better than aromatic catabolism. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]

## Cofactor supply, iron and genes without reaction mappings

Many condition-specific genes cannot be tested by FBA because they have no reaction mappings. Of 51 quinate-specific genes, 30 (59%) have none. [src: discoveries] These include pyrroloquinoline quinone (PQQ) biosynthesis, iron acquisition, transcriptional regulators and respiratory-chain components. [src: discoveries] The aromatic_catabolism_network report gives the same 30/51 count and says cofactor supply chains and regulatory genes are invisible to the model. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] It calls this a specific class of model incompleteness: cofactor supply chains, iron homeostasis and respiratory-chain capacity constraints. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] PQQ biosynthesis, iron acquisition, transcriptional regulation and newly identified Complex I accessory factors lie outside the model's scope. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] The report proposes that adding PQQ biosynthesis, iron homeostasis and respiratory-chain capacity constraints could improve prediction of condition-dependent essentiality. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Two proteins, ACIAD3137 and ACIAD2176, lack FBA reaction mappings and assigned metabolic functions. [src: discoveries] See [[entities/aciad3137-and-aciad2176]].

## Respiratory capacity and threshold effects

FBA sees increased respiratory demand but not the resulting bottleneck. The ADP1 model predicts 1.76× higher Complex I flux on aromatic substrates (0.55 vs 0.31) but 0% Complex I essentiality. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network, discoveries] The Complex I support requirement was not predicted by the model. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Full TCA-cycle (tricarboxylic acid) oxidation of beta-ketoadipate-derived succinyl-CoA and acetyl-CoA generates NADH (reduced nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) at multiple steps, which requires Complex I under high flux. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

The report gives two reasons for the miss. First, FBA optimizes growth rate and can redistribute flux through alternative pathways. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Second, losing any one subunit abolishes Complex I function entirely, a threshold effect that FBA's linear programming cannot represent. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network, discoveries] See [[entities/respiratory-complex-i-nadh-ubiquinone-oxidoreductase]].

NDH-2 shows the same pattern. FBA predicts zero flux through NDH-2 on all standard carbon sources and routes all NADH through Complex I. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] It also predicts zero flux through ACIAD3522 on all standard media, because growth-rate optimization favors Complex I for its higher ATP (adenosine triphosphate) yield per NADH. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] The report calls this the fundamental reason FBA fails to predict condition-specific respiratory requirements. FBA assumes the optimal pathway is always used and misses capacity constraints that force suboptimal alternatives at high flux. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] On lactate, Complex I is only mildly important (0.77), which the report suggests means NDH-2 partially compensates. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] See [[concepts/substrate-dependent-complex-i-requirement-and-ndh-2-compensation]] and [[entities/alternative-nadh-dehydrogenase-ndh-2]].

NDH-2 is TnSeq-dispensable but missing from the deletion collection, so it has no growth data. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] The central prediction, that NDH-2 compensates on glucose, therefore cannot be tested directly with this dataset. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] The report proposes an NDH-2 knockout as the definitive test of whether NDH-2 deletion makes Complex I essential on glucose. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]

## Open Directions

- Add trace aromatic compounds to the ADP1 minimal-media definition, using measured media composition, and test whether the aromatic-degradation and beta-ketoadipate discordance resolves. This would separate the media explanation from moonlighting.
- Add PQQ biosynthesis, iron homeostasis and respiratory-chain capacity constraints to the ADP1 model and check whether condition-dependent essentiality predictions improve. This addresses the 30/51 quinate-specific genes without mappings.
- Build an NDH-2 deletion mutant in ADP1 and test whether Complex I becomes essential on glucose. This would close the gap left by missing NDH-2 growth data.
- Model Complex I as an all-or-nothing multi-subunit unit, to test whether the threshold effect explains the 0% essentiality prediction.
