# Fitness costs and conditional benefits of antimicrobial-resistance genes

Antimicrobial-resistance (AMR) genes can impose a measurable baseline burden while becoming beneficial under antibiotic exposure, creating a distinction between constitutive cost and condition-specific protection. This concept synthesizes the evidence in [[summaries/amr_fitness_cost__REPORT]] and connects it to [[concepts/condition-specific-fitness]], [[concepts/environmental-resistome]], and [[concepts/pangenome-integration]]. [src: amr_fitness_cost]

## Baseline fitness cost

Using genome-wide transposon fitness data from the [[entities/kescience-fitnessbrowser]] compendium, the analysis identified 1,352 AMR genes across 43 organisms and tested 25 organisms with sufficient data for per-organism effects. [src: amr_fitness_cost] The perturbation method was RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing), which measures knockout fitness relative to the pool average rather than an absolute selection coefficient. [src: amr_fitness_cost]

Under non-antibiotic conditions, AMR-gene knockouts had systematically higher fitness than non-AMR-gene knockouts, indicating that AMR genes impose a relative metabolic burden in the measured strains. [src: amr_fitness_cost] A DerSimonian-Laird random-effects meta-analysis across 25 organisms estimated a pooled AMR-versus-background shift of **+0.086 [95% CI: +0.074, +0.098], z = 14.3, p ~ 0**; all 25 of 25 organisms showed a positive shift. [src: amr_fitness_cost] The median per-organism Cohen's d was **0.18**, heterogeneity was **I² = 54.3%**, and Cochran's **Q = 52.54, p = 0.0007**. [src: amr_fitness_cost] Six organisms were nominally significant at p<0.05, and two were significant after false-discovery-rate correction at q<0.05: acidovorax_3H11 and Koxy. [src: amr_fitness_cost]

The pooled **+0.086** effect is a relative difference, not evidence that AMR-gene knockouts have beneficial absolute growth. [src: amr_fitness_cost] Absolute AMR-gene knockout fitness averaged **−0.024**, whereas the non-AMR knockout background averaged approximately **−0.11**; a one-sample Wilcoxon test of AMR-gene knockout fitness against zero gave **p = 0.999**. [src: amr_fitness_cost] Thus, the result supports a relative-cost interpretation: removing AMR genes reduced the burden relative to the background, while the measured AMR knockouts themselves were not shown to have positive absolute fitness. [src: amr_fitness_cost]

## Conditional benefit under antibiotic exposure

The baseline burden was partially decoupled from antibiotic-dependent importance. [src: amr_fitness_cost] Across any-antibiotic experiments, **57.0%** of AMR genes showed a fitness flip toward greater importance under antibiotic exposure, with **N = 797**, mean flip **+0.045** defined as non-antibiotic fitness minus antibiotic fitness, and Wilcoxon signed-rank **p = 0.0001**. [src: amr_fitness_cost]

The antibiotic-dependent shift varied by resistance mechanism. [src: amr_fitness_cost] Broad-spectrum efflux genes had a mean flip of **+0.094**, compared with **−0.001** for enzymatic-inactivation genes, with Mann-Whitney U **p = 0.007**. [src: amr_fitness_cost] This supports the hypothesis that broad-spectrum efflux systems can protect against multiple antibiotic conditions, whereas enzymatic inactivation is more dependent on exposure to a matching antibiotic. [src: amr_fitness_cost]

A class-matched validation included **157 gene-antibiotic pairs** across four resistance classes, with a mean flip of **+0.113**, a flip rate of **54.8%**, and Wilcoxon **p = 0.14**. [src: amr_fitness_cost] Chloramphenicol resistance genes showed the strongest validation, with **6/6 (100%)** showing the expected flip. [src: amr_fitness_cost] Beta-lactam resistance contributed **105 pairs across 10 organisms** and had a **50%** flip rate. [src: amr_fitness_cost] The non-significant class-matched result and the significant any-antibiotic result indicate that the conditional-benefit pattern is stronger in the broader analysis than in the smaller matched validation. [src: amr_fitness_cost]

## Mechanism, conservation, and cost

Baseline fitness cost did not differ significantly among efflux (**N=254**), enzymatic inactivation (**N=304**), metal resistance (**N=144**), and unknown (**N=74**) mechanisms. [src: amr_fitness_cost] The Kruskal-Wallis test gave **H = 0.65, p = 0.89**, and the Jonckheere-Terpstra test for the predicted ordering efflux > enzymatic > metal > unknown gave **z = 0.23, p = 0.41**; no pairwise comparison survived false-discovery-rate correction. [src: amr_fitness_cost] These results contradict the prediction that resistance mechanism alone determines baseline fitness cost in this dataset. [src: amr_fitness_cost]

Mechanism did, however, predict pangenome conservation status. [src: amr_fitness_cost] A chi-squared test found a strong association between mechanism and conservation status (**χ² = 69.3, p = 1.4×10⁻¹³**). [src: amr_fitness_cost] Metal-resistance genes were accessory in **44%** of cases, compared with **13%** of efflux genes and **16%** of enzymatic-inactivation genes. [src: amr_fitness_cost] The result therefore refines the relationship between mechanism and persistence: mechanism predicted where AMR genes occurred in the pangenome, but not their baseline fitness cost. [src: amr_fitness_cost]

Core or intrinsic AMR genes (**N=638**) and accessory or acquired AMR genes (**N=163**) had virtually identical fitness distributions, with both means at **−0.024**, Cohen's **d = 0.002**, and Mann-Whitney U **p = 0.33**. [src: amr_fitness_cost] The broader stratification analysis likewise found no conservation difference (**p = 0.33**), no Tier 1-versus-Tier 2 difference (**p = 0.26**), and no antibiotic-versus-metal resistance-type difference (**p = 0.87**). [src: amr_fitness_cost] This null result supports the possibility that horizontal transfer preferentially retains cost-optimized genes or that compensation occurs rapidly after acquisition, but the evidence is strongest for well-sampled species. [src: amr_fitness_cost]

## Essentiality and interpretation limits

Only **4.6%** of AMR genes were absent from the fitness matrices and therefore putatively essential, compared with an approximately **14%** background essential rate estimated in a prior analysis using a different organism set. [src: amr_fitness_cost] Tier 1 genes from bakta_amr (**N=110**) and Tier 2 keyword-annotated genes (**N=691**) had indistinguishable fitness distributions (**KS p = 0.17**), although the broader assembly contained **178 Tier 1** and **1,174 Tier 2** genes. [src: amr_fitness_cost] These findings connect the AMR-cost result to [[concepts/gene-essentiality]] while leaving open the possibility that essential AMR genes, if systematically more costly, are underrepresented in the measured fitness distributions. [src: amr_fitness_cost]

The approximately **+0.086** common cost may reflect residual metabolic overhead after compensatory evolution in lab-adapted RB-TnSeq strains rather than the unmitigated cost of newly acquired resistance; this is an explanation consistent with the data, not a directly measured compensatory-evolution process. [src: amr_fitness_cost] All **25** tested organisms were lab-adapted strains, so the estimate may underestimate costs in natural populations. [src: amr_fitness_cost] Tier 2 contained **86%** of the AMR genes and relied on keyword matching from Bakta annotations, which may include non-AMR genes such as general efflux transporters; the Tier 1 sensitivity analysis gave consistent results. [src: amr_fitness_cost] Transposon insertions can also have polar effects on downstream genes in operons, potentially confounding AMR-gene fitness measurements. [src: amr_fitness_cost]

Core/accessory labels used a **≥95%** prevalence threshold, while most Fitness Browser species had few GTDB genomes: the median was **9**, with a range of **2–399**. [src: amr_fitness_cost] A gene present in all **9** sampled genomes could therefore be labeled core at the available sampling depth but become accessory with broader sampling, making the core-versus-accessory null result especially cautious for species with fewer than **20** genomes. [src: amr_fitness_cost] The product classifier did not handle fosfomycin or tellurite resistance annotations, leaving approximately **25 genes** in the unknown mechanism category; reclassification would move them to enzymatic inactivation and metal resistance, respectively. [src: amr_fitness_cost]

## Tensions

The main tension is between the strong any-antibiotic signal and the weaker class-matched validation. [src: amr_fitness_cost] The any-antibiotic analysis found a **57.0%** flip rate for **N = 797** genes with **p = 0.0001**, whereas the class-matched analysis found a **54.8%** flip rate for **157** pairs with **p = 0.14**. [src: amr_fitness_cost] This does not establish opposing biological effects, because the analyses differ in sample size and matching design; it identifies a need to determine whether broad condition coverage or antibiotic-class specificity best explains the stronger result. [src: amr_fitness_cost]

A second tension concerns the absence of baseline cost differences among mechanisms alongside the strong mechanism-by-conservation association. [src: amr_fitness_cost] Mechanism was not associated with baseline cost (**H = 0.65, p = 0.89**) but was strongly associated with conservation status (**χ² = 69.3, p = 1.4×10⁻¹³**). [src: amr_fitness_cost] The current evidence therefore supports different roles for mechanism in fitness and pangenome distribution rather than a single mechanism-to-cost-to-retention pathway. [src: amr_fitness_cost]

## Open Directions

- Use organism-stratified models on Cup4G11 (**77** AMR genes) and BFirm (**50** AMR genes) to test mechanism effects while controlling for genetic background. [src: amr_fitness_cost]
- Subclassify efflux pumps into narrow-spectrum drug pumps and general RND systems such as AcrAB-TolC, then test whether constitutively expressed systems have lower baseline costs. [src: amr_fitness_cost]
- Replace averages across non-antibiotic experiments with condition-specific analyses of metal, osmotic, and carbon-limitation stresses to test whether the baseline-cost estimate changes by environmental condition. [src: amr_fitness_cost]
- Cross-reference the **144** metal-resistance genes with fitness measurements and the metal fitness atlas to test whether genes costly under standard conditions are protective under metal stress. [src: amr_fitness_cost]
- Extend the analysis from **25** Fitness Browser organisms to all **293K** BERDL genomes by predicting AMR cost from gene-cluster conservation patterns and testing whether the laboratory-derived relationship generalizes across the broader genome collection. [src: amr_fitness_cost]
