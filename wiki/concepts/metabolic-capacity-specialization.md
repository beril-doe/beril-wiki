# Utilization capacities are phylogenetically concentrated and specialist-dominated

The ENIGMA Carbon Census found that callable carbon-utilization capacities were concentrated in a small number of genomes and taxonomic groups rather than broadly distributed across the surveyed genome set. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] This pattern refines [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] by showing that integrating compound, pathway, genome, and taxonomy evidence can reveal strong specialization even when many genomes are screened. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

## Evidence for specialist-dominated capacity

The analysis considered 8 ENIGMA-isolate-callable capacities across genomes carrying at least 1 capacity. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Mean versatility was approximately 1.19 capacities per genome, the median was 1, and the maximum was 4 capacities per genome. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Among 3109 genomes, 675 carried exactly 1 of the 8 aromatic or alkaloid capacities, whereas 18 carried at least 3, with a maximum of 4 capacities per genome. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] These counts support the interpretation that most genomes represented single-capacity specialists, while broad-capacity generalists were uncommon. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The callable capacities were salicylic acid, 3-hydroxybenzoic acid, 4-hydroxybenzaldehyde, phthalic acid, terephthalic acid, Phenylethylamine, xanthine, and Abscisic acid. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The isolate-callable set was concentrated in Shikimates/Phenylpropanoids, which contributed salicylic acid, 3-hydroxybenzoic acid, 4-hydroxybenzaldehyde, phthalic acid, and terephthalic acid, while Phenylethylamine and xanthine were in Alkaloids and Abscisic acid was in Terpenoids. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Xanthine was subsequently recognized as a carbon-category error because reaction R02107 represents purine nitrogen acquisition rather than carbon catabolism, leaving an effective carbon-callable set of 8 rather than 9. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The capability/dependency comparison **refines** this specialist interpretation by separating encoded pathway completeness from measured fitness dependence. Among 1,695 complete pathway-organism pairs, 267 (15.8%) were latent, with carbon-source utilization pathways more often latent than amino-acid biosynthesis pathways (24.3% versus 6.5%). [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] Thus, a callable or complete utilization pathway need not be a laboratory-measured dependency; the latent fraction varied from 4.7% to 21.1% across tested thresholds, so the magnitude is classification-sensitive. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] A separate comparison of 161 organism–pathway pairs classified 66 (41.0%) as Latent Capability and found that all 66 became fitness-important under at least one condition type, further **supporting** the interpretation that encoded utilization capacity can be specialized yet conditionally realized rather than constitutively required. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] See [[summaries/metabolic_capability_dependency__REPORT]] for the full comparison.

## Taxonomic concentration

Generalist chassis were concentrated in Paraburkholderia, with 5 genomes, Burkholderia, with 4, and Hydrogenophaga, with 2. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] High-versatility genera included Polaromonas, Alcaligenes, Burkholderia, Paraburkholderia, Comamonas, and Hydrogenophaga. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Utilizer placements were concentrated in Pseudomonadota and Burkholderiales, with compound-specific contributions from Pseudomonadales and Sphingomonadales. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

Within particular genera, capacities could be highly conserved even though capacities were sparse across the full genome collection. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Castellaniella and Hylemonella showed approximately 100% capacity for 3-hydroxybenzoic acid, Novosphingobium and Sphingobium showed approximately 92–100% capacity for 4-hydroxybenzaldehyde, and Paenarthrobacter showed 100% capacity for phthalic acid. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Comamonas showed 92% capacity for 3-hydroxybenzoic acid and 85% capacity for 4-hydroxybenzaldehyde. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] These within-lineage patterns support [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]] by indicating that utilization capacity can be concentrated within particular ecological or taxonomic reservoirs rather than evenly distributed among genomes. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The conservation–fitness integration **supports** the broader interpretation that lineage concentration can coincide with conservation of required functions: across 33 organisms, essential genes were 86.1% core versus 81.2% for non-essential genes, with a median odds ratio of 1.56, and 18 of 33 organisms showed significant enrichment after Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate correction. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] This is evidence for modest within-species core conservation of viability-associated genes, not direct evidence that the eight callable capacities are essential or that their taxonomic concentration is caused by essentiality. Essential-auxiliary genes nevertheless comprised a distinct category, including strain-specific variants of core machinery and mobile-element-associated functions, so the result **refines** the hypothesis that specialist capacities may occur in accessory genomic compartments rather than implying that all accessory capacities are utilization traits. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]

The metabolic-capability study **supports** the broader possibility of lineage-structured metabolic specialization: all 10 tested species formed metabolic clusters, but cluster–isolation-environment associations were significant only for *Salmonella enterica* and *Phenylobacterium* sp., not for four marine organisms. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] This **refines** the ecological-reservoir interpretation: within-species metabolic structure is detectable, but its environmental meaning is observational and can depend on metadata resolution, ecological scale, and phylogenetic structure. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] Across 225 species with at least 50 genomes and at least 3 variable pathways, pathway-completeness clustering produced a median of 4 metabolic ecotypes per species and a maximum of 8, while ecotype count correlated with pangenome openness at partial Spearman rho=0.322, p=8.0e-07 after controlling for genome count. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] This **supports** the claim that specialization is not limited to the eight callable compounds, while the fixed clustering threshold and incomplete phylogenetic control leave its magnitude provisional. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

The cross-species COG analysis provides broader functional context that **supports** this lineage-concentrated interpretation: novel or singleton genes were enriched in mobile-element, defense, and unknown-function categories, while core genes were relatively enriched in conserved metabolic and housekeeping functions. [src: cog_analysis] This suggests the hypothesis that some specialist utilization capacities reside in accessory or recently acquired genomic material, but the COG analysis did not directly map those categories to the eight callable capacities, so it **refines** rather than directly demonstrates the mechanism of specialization. [src: cog_analysis] The pathway-capability analysis **supports** that hypothesis at pathway scale: core-only versus all-gene GapMind completeness showed accessory-dependent gaps of 0.146 for leucine and valine biosynthesis, 0.141 for arginine, 0.140 for lysine, and 0.140 for threonine. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] These differences indicate accessory contributions to pathway completeness, but do not demonstrate metabolite exchange or directly identify the genes responsible. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

## Co-occurrence does not imply a portable module

The broad cross-module modularity hypothesis was not supported beyond a shared aromatic funnel. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Within-aromatic capacity pairs had a median Haldane-corrected odds ratio of 1.12 and a median Jaccard index of 0.026, whereas within-other pairs had a median odds ratio of 7.43 with median Jaccard values driven by near-zero counts. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Cross-block pairs had a median odds ratio of 2.28, with 4/15 confidence intervals entirely above 1 and a median Jaccard index approximately 0. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The strongest cross-block signals involved Phenylethylamine and aromatic-funnel acids: 4-hydroxybenzaldehyde plus Phenylethylamine had an odds ratio of 2.84, a 95% confidence interval of 1.49–5.40, q=0.035, and n11=11, while phthalic acid plus Phenylethylamine had an odds ratio of 3.37, a 95% confidence interval of 1.46–7.75, q=0.071, and n11=6. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Despite these signals, their Jaccard index remained approximately 0.04, and only 25 of 3109 genomes, or 0.8%, carried both an aromatic and a non-aromatic capacity. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The combined result supports phylogenetic concentration rather than a portable, broadly co-assembled capacity set. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] This distinction connects to [[concepts/cofitness-network-architecture]] and [[concepts/module-level-coinheritance]], because statistical association between capacities need not indicate widespread physical or functional co-assembly. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The broader pathway analysis **supports** this interpretation of non-portable specialization: across 2,810 GTDB species, variable pathway count was positively associated with pangenome openness after controlling for genome count (partial Spearman rho=0.530, p=2.83e-203), and the signal was significant in 5 of 18 genera. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] This links pathway diversity to genome fluidity, but does not establish that the callable capacities themselves are transferred or co-inherited as modules. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

## Evidence boundaries

The evidence for specialization is strongest for the 8 ENIGMA-isolate-callable compounds, for which 569 utilizer prediction rows were produced across the callable set. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] GTDB placement included 494 strain records representing 359 distinct strains, comprising 64 high-certainty records based on gene-complete Tier-2 pathways, 387 medium-certainty records based on Tier-2 pathways, and 43 medium-certainty records based on Tier-3 signature enzymes. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] H4 was strongly supported for the 8 ENIGMA-isolate-callable compounds but was null for the other 75 compounds lacking isolate-level placement. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

Certainty is not directly comparable across compounds with different signature lengths because it is based on the raw number of carried signature reactions divided by required reactions. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Terephthalic acid had 34/34 high-certainty records, but this was identified as a single-reaction-signature artifact rather than strong evidence of pathway completeness. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The specialization pattern should therefore be interpreted as a property of the callable evidence set and its annotation rules, not as a complete census of all environmental carbon-utilization capacity. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The COG result likewise concerns functional categories across core and novel genes rather than direct pathway-completeness measurements, reinforcing this evidence boundary. [src: cog_analysis] The GapMind comparison further covered 80 pathways—18 amino acid biosynthesis and 62 carbon-source utilization pathways—but excluded cofactor biosynthesis, lipid metabolism, and secondary metabolism, so it **refines** rather than replaces the callable-compound boundary. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

The conservation–fitness result also has important boundaries: essentiality was inferred from RB-TnSeq, a random-barcode transposon sequencing approach, under library construction and growth conditions, and the report identifies gene-length, coverage, clade-size, and mapping limitations. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] Thus, its core-enrichment signal **supports** a conservation context for specialization but cannot establish condition-independent necessity for callable utilization pathways. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] See [[summaries/conservation_vs_fitness__REPORT]] for the full integration and limitations.

The environmental atlas detected 62 of the implicated utilizer genera at genus resolution, with top field prevalences approximately 0.7–0.9, and 3-hydroxybenzoic-acid utilizers reached 0.90 field prevalence. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] These observations identify potential reservoirs for specialist capacities, but the atlas measured organismal occurrence or abundance rather than catabolic activity. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Periphyton was especially informative as a freshwater-biofilm environment, where listed Burkholderiales and Comamonadaceae genera reached approximately 96–97% prevalence with mean relative abundance approximately 0.005–0.009. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

For the broader functional comparison, see [[summaries/cog_analysis__REPORT]].

## Open Directions

- Combine the 3109-genome capacity matrix with GTDB phylogeny and a phylogenetically controlled permutation or trait-evolution model to test whether the observed specialist distribution exceeds lineage-based null expectations. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]
- Recompute capacity co-occurrence after removing xanthine/R02107 and regenerating committed tables, then compare odds ratios and Jaccard indices with fitness-matched null models to determine which associations persist after the carbon-category correction. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]
- Link the 569 utilizer prediction rows to the 3825 taxonomy-bearing NMDC metagenomes and use sample-aware models to test whether high-versatility genera are enriched in periphyton relative to soil, freshwater, sediment, and plant environments. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]
- Experimentally test specialist and generalist isolates on multiple callable compounds using matched enrichment and growth assays to ask whether predicted capacity breadth corresponds to realized carbon utilization. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]
- Extend the analysis to the 74 organism-dark compounds using targeted PaperBLAST, abstract-level literature mining, and pathway reconstruction to determine whether the apparent specialization persists after annotation gaps are reduced. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]
- Map callable utilization pathways onto core, novel, and singleton gene classes in the COG dataset, then test with phylogenetically controlled models whether specialist capacities are disproportionately associated with mobile-element or defense-enriched genomic compartments. [src: cog_analysis]
- Pair GapMind completeness with direct gene assignments and condition-matched fitness assays to test whether lineage-concentrated callable capacities are realized, latent, or conditionally dependent. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]
- Apply phylogenetically controlled models to the 2,810-species pathway dataset and test whether the pathway-variation/open-pangenome association persists after independent contrasts rather than genome-count adjustment alone. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]
- Validate the 4-per-species ecotype summary across clustering cutoffs and with condition-matched fitness data to distinguish robust metabolic structure from threshold- and sampling-dependent partitions. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]
- Intersect callable-capacity genes with Fitness Browser essentiality and condition-specific fitness, then test whether specialist capacities are preferentially core, auxiliary, or conditionally important after controlling for phylogeny and gene-length bias. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]
