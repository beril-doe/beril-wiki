# AMR ecotypes and environmental association

This page covers AMR ecotypes and environmental association: whether strains within a species cluster into AMR-profile ecotypes and whether these track environment, host or isolation source. The evidence is one report, amr_strain_variation. Clustering finds ecotypes in a minority of species, but the test of their link to environment is underpowered, so the association stays unproven. [src: amr_strain_variation] See also [[summaries/amr_strain_variation__REPORT]] and [[concepts/within-species-variation-of-antimicrobial-resistance-gene-content]].

## Literature Context

Several candidate papers bear on whether within-species lineages carry distinct genomic profiles tied to host, source or niche. The strongest is an ecological genomics study of *Escherichia coli* from diverse hosts and environments. It used comparative genomics and parallelised pangenome-wide association studies (900 experiments), and it challenges the "everything is everywhere" idea. It reports adaptive genomic signatures associated with host species, physiology and ecology, and it links these to the emergence of antimicrobial resistance [PMID 41408707](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41408707/). In *Listeria monocytogenes*, hypervirulent clones such as CC1 were strongly associated with dairy products. Hypovirulent clones (CC9, CC121) were associated with meat products and showed a higher prevalence of stress resistance and benzalkonium chloride tolerance genes [PMID 31171794](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31171794/). Smaller or descriptive studies point the same way. Elephant isolates of *Streptococcus agalactiae* (24 isolates from three German zoos) diverged from isolates of other hosts and clustered by zoo and MLST profile, with several genes found only in the elephant host [PMID 40906522](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40906522/). A *Campylobacter lari* collection of 158 isolates from human, food, animal, waterbird and environmental sources showed high genetic diversity, but its abstract reports no source-specific AMR clustering [PMID 36354326](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36354326/). In *Weissella* (347 genomes), most genomes lacked detectable AMR or virulence genes, so ecotype structure in AMR would be hard to resolve there [PMID 42439591](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42439591/).

At the community level, antibiotic exposure selects for resistant bacteria and increases opportunities for horizontal gene transfer in the gut [PMID 25271726](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25271726/). In coastal ecosystems, resistome assembly differs by niche: horizontal gene transfer dominated in water, and vertically inherited ARGs dominated in sediments [PMID 43014933](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43014933/). In a lake food web, anthropogenic disturbance was generally associated with higher ARG and VFG abundance and diversity [PMID 42722277](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42722277/). These are metagenomic studies of environmental niches, not strain-level ecotype tests.

The corpus finding that 190 of 974 eligible species (19.5%) form at least 2 AMR ecotypes is consistent with the general view that lineages within a species differ in accessory content in ways linked to niche. The *E. coli*, *Listeria* and *S. agalactiae* papers support that view [PMID 41408707](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41408707/), [PMID 31171794](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31171794/), [PMID 40906522](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40906522/). The corpus's own environment test is underpowered. Only 2 species were testable, and 52.7% of genomes lack a classifiable isolation source. So the corpus does not confirm or contradict these studies on ecotype–environment linkage. The visual structuring in the *K. pneumoniae*, *S. aureus* and *S. enterica* UMAP plots is only suggestive. The species-level result is that host-associated species carry more AMR genes per genome. It fits the disturbance-linked and host-niche patterns in the environmental studies, though those use different data and scales [PMID 42722277](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42722277/). The corpus's pan-species ecotype screen with UMAP and DBSCAN is not matched by any candidate paper, so that approach is novel relative to this evidence. Single-species pangenome-wide association, as in *E. coli*, suggests a route to testing associations once metadata are curated.

## Do ecotypes exist?

Of 974 species with at least 15 genomes eligible for clustering, 190 (19.5%) form at least 2 distinct AMR ecotypes. The method was UMAP (uniform manifold approximation and projection) plus DBSCAN clustering of AMR Jaccard distances. [src: amr_strain_variation] The median silhouette score was 0.620, which the report reads as good cluster quality. [src: amr_strain_variation] The report states that distinct AMR subtypes occur in 19.5% of species. [src: amr_strain_variation] It calls this consistent with the clinical/community-associated split in species such as *K. pneumoniae*, citing Holt et al. 2015, where genomic analysis found distinct lineages with different AMR and virulence profiles tied to different ecological niches. [src: amr_strain_variation] See [[entities/klebsiella-pneumoniae]] and [[entities/umap]].

## Do ecotypes track environment?

The report could not test this well. Per-genome metadata are sparse: 52.7% of genomes have no classifiable isolation_source. Only 2 species had enough within-species environmental diversity for chi-squared testing under strict expected-frequency criteria. [src: amr_strain_variation] The report says this does not mean ecotypes are unrelated to environment. The test is underpowered with current metadata. [src: amr_strain_variation] Case-study UMAP plots for *K. pneumoniae*, *S. aureus* and *S. enterica* show visible environmental structuring. [src: amr_strain_variation] This is visual evidence only, not a statistical result. See [[entities/staphylococcus-aureus]] and [[entities/salmonella-enterica]]. *E. coli* was excluded from the case studies because it exceeds the 500-genome computational cap. [src: amr_strain_variation] See [[entities/escherichia-coli]].

The report proposes UMAP and heatmap figures with clinical metadata overlays for six case-study species: *K. pneumoniae*, *E. coli*, *S. aureus*, *P. aeruginosa*, *S. enterica* and *A. baumannii*. [src: amr_strain_variation] It also proposes linking ecotypes to virulence factor profiles and metabolic pathway variation from other KBase Data Lakehouse analyses. [src: amr_strain_variation]

## Species-level environment signal

At the species level, host-associated species consistently carry more AMR genes per genome than terrestrial or aquatic species. This held under both a rule-based keyword classification approximating BacDive categories and an NCBI keyword-based annotation (Kruskal-Wallis, p < 0.05). [src: amr_strain_variation] Both methods agree in direction, with human-clinical isolates showing the highest AMR burden. [src: amr_strain_variation] See [[concepts/environmental-and-clinical-distribution-of-antimicrobial-resistance]].

## Metadata limits

The two classifiers differ in coverage. The NCBI keyword classifier assigns environments to 91% of species (1,190/1,307). [src: amr_strain_variation] The BacDive approximation classifies 35% (459/1,307). [src: amr_strain_variation] Both are approximate, and the report says dedicated metadata curation would improve ecotype analyses. [src: amr_strain_variation] See [[concepts/sampling-bias-and-metadata-sparsity-in-amr-resistome-surveys]], [[concepts/harmonizing-free-text-isolation-source-and-environment-metadata]], [[entities/bacdive]] and [[entities/ncbi-env-environmental-metadata]].

## Open Directions

- Curate isolation-source metadata for the 190 ecotype-forming species, then rerun the chi-squared tests. This would close the power gap that left only 2 testable species.
- Produce the proposed UMAP and heatmap figures with clinical metadata overlays for the six case-study species. This would turn the visual impression of structuring into tested associations.
- Handle *E. coli* with subsampling or a higher genome cap, so the species with the most genomes is not left out.
- Link ecotypes to virulence factor profiles and metabolic pathway variation. This would show whether ecotypes reflect wider lineage differences.
