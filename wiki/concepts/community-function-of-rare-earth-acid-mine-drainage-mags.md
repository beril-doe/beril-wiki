# Community function of rare-earth acid mine drainage MAGs

The lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas report gives a first descriptive characterization of 37 metagenome assembled genomes (MAGs) from rare-earth-element acid mine drainage (REE-AMD) water. These MAGs are led by acidophilic, metal-tolerant lineages and carry stress-response genes. Canonical methylotrophs do not dominate them, and xoxF appears in only a few MAGs. [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas] The evidence is descriptive only: it comes from one bioproject with n=37. [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas] See also [[summaries/lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas__REPORT]] and [[concepts/environmental-association-of-lanthanide-dependent-methanol-oxidation-genes]].

## Literature Context

Two candidate papers describe acid mine drainage (AMD) microbiology, and both bear on the stress-response theme of this page. [PMID 25764459](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25764459/) reports metagenomes from a low-temperature (6-10°C) AMD stream biofilm at pH 2.5-2.7 in the Kristineberg mine, northern Sweden. The community had low diversity and was dominated by organisms similar to Acidithiobacillus ferrivorans. Its metagenomes carried genes predicted to encode pH homeostasis and metal resistance, together with cold-adaptation functions. They also indicated a primarily autotrophic lifestyle driven by the oxidation of ferrous iron and inorganic sulfur compounds, plus nitrogen fixation capacity. These are predictions from metagenome sequence, not measured activities.

[PMID 24801164](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24801164/) took a functional metagenomic approach to the Tinto River, a natural AMD environment, and screened for arsenic resistance. Thirteen functionally active genes were identified. They fell into transport, stress response, DNA damage repair, phospholipid biosynthesis, amino acid biosynthesis and RNA-modifying enzymes. Most (11) encoded proteins not previously linked to heavy-metal resistance, or hypothetical or unknown proteins. The ClpB chaperone and the RNA-modifying enzymes increased cell survival under heat shock, acid pH and UV radiation. These assays were done in Escherichia coli, so they show what the genes can do in a heterologous host. They do not show that the same functions confer resistance within the native AMD community.

The corpus's REE-AMD MAG characterization is consistent with this picture in a qualitative way. The hub reports that the 37 MAGs are led by acidophilic, metal-tolerant lineages such as Acidocella, Acidiphilium, Thiomonas and Metallibacterium. It also reports widespread DNA-repair genes (RecN in 33 MAGs, RadA in 32), acid-resistance genes (FtsH zinc metalloprotease in 31) and MerR-family regulators (30). This agrees with the Kristineberg finding that AMD metagenomes encode pH-homeostasis and metal-resistance functions [PMID 25764459](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25764459/). It also agrees with the Tinto River finding that stress-response and DNA-repair categories feature among genes tied to resistance phenotypes [PMID 24801164](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24801164/). The corpus infers function only from gene presence, which is the same limit as in the Kristineberg predictions.

The corpus's central claims about methylotrophy have no counterpart in these candidates. Neither paper examines xoxF, lanmodulin or REE-driven methanol oxidation. The reported scarcity of methylotrophy markers (xoxF in only 4/37 MAGs, and 0/37 for bakta-validated lanmodulin and xoxJ) is therefore novel relative to these two papers. It can be neither supported nor contradicted by them. The same applies to the uncharacterised f__REEB76 clade. The corpus evidence also remains descriptive, from a single bioproject with n=37, and the two literature papers come from different sites, with different metal chemistry and a different temperature regime. Any comparison should be read as thematic rather than quantitative.

## Taxonomy

The 37 MAGs come from samples tagged "rare earth elements-acid mine drainage (REEs-AMD) contaminated river water". They are taxonomically diverse and not dominated by canonical methylotrophs. [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas] The community is led by acidophilic and metal-tolerant lineages: Acidocella, Acidiphilium, Thiomonas and Metallibacterium. It also includes several Burkholderiaceae_A/_B genera (Limnohabitans, Rhodoferax_A, Trinickia), Chitinophagaceae, Acidimicrobiia, Chloroflexota and Cyanobacteriota. [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas] The set includes a previously uncharacterised clade, f__REEB76 / g__REEB76, discovered from these samples and named accordingly. [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas] The report describes f__REEB76 as uncharacterised and proposes studying its phylogenomic placement, predicted metabolism and xoxF/lanmodulin presence. [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas] The report reads the community profile as consistent with a hostile, carbon-poor, low-pH water that selects for chemolithotrophic acidophiles. [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]

## Scarcity of methylotrophy markers

Only 4/37 REE-AMD MAGs carry any xoxF. [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas] Bakta-validated lanmodulin is present in 0/37 MAGs [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas], and bakta-validated xoxJ is present in 0/37. [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas] The report concludes that in real REE-rich acidic mine drainage the dominant biology is acid-stress and metal-resistance defense, not methanol oxidation, and that methylotrophs are present but rare. It says this complicates the narrative that REE-impacted environments are full of REE-utilising microbes. [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]

## Caveats

The REE-AMD anchor is small (n=37), comes from a single bioproject, and is descriptive only. Inferential claims about REE-impacted environments need a larger, more independent collection. [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]

## Open Directions

- Assemble a larger, independent collection of REE-impacted metagenomes. This would test whether the stress-dominated, methylotroph-poor pattern holds beyond one bioproject.
- Characterize the f__REEB76 clade. Place it phylogenomically, predict its metabolism, and check for xoxF or lanmodulin.
- Examine the 4 xoxF-carrying MAGs. Identify their lineages and test whether they are the rare methylotrophs.
