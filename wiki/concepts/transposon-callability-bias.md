# Transposon Callability Bias in Essentiality Inference

Transposon callability bias is the risk that missing transposon insertions are interpreted as evidence of gene essentiality even when technical or sequence features prevented insertion or detection. [src: essential_genome]

The [[summaries/essential_genome__REPORT]] analysis used RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing) to infer essentiality across 221,005 genes from 48 bacteria. [src: essential_genome] Its essential-gene definition is an upper bound because genes without transposon insertions may lack insertions because of small size, AT-rich sequence, or scaffold-edge effects rather than true essentiality. [src: essential_genome] The [[summaries/conservation_vs_fitness__REPORT]] analysis **supports** this upper-bound interpretation: its independent RB-TnSeq essentiality calls likewise treated missing fitness data cautiously, noting that short genes, low-complexity regions, and scaffold edges can prevent informative insertions. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] The [[summaries/fitness_effects_conservation__REPORT]] analysis **supports** retaining this caution because near-zero fitness among novel singleton genes may reflect poor transposon coverage rather than true neutrality. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

## Consequences for Essentiality Estimates

The study classified 41,059 of 221,005 genes as essential, corresponding to 18.6% of all genes, but callability failures could inflate this estimate if technically inaccessible genes are counted as essential. [src: essential_genome] Essentiality rates ranged from 12.2% in Pedo557 to 29.7% in Magneto, and those differences may therefore combine biological variation with organism- or sequence-specific differences in transposon callability. [src: essential_genome]

Essential genes had a median length of 675 bp, compared with 885 bp for non-essential genes, and 17.8% of essential genes were shorter than 300 bp. [src: essential_genome] This length distribution is consistent with the report's warning that small genes may be especially vulnerable to false essentiality calls, although the summary does not establish that short genes caused the observed enrichment. [src: essential_genome] The conservation-versus-fitness analysis **supports** retaining this concern: it found essential genes to be slightly shorter on average and explicitly identified insertion bias as a possible limitation. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] The costly-versus-dispensable analysis **refines** this concern by showing that costly+dispensable genes are also shorter than costly+conserved genes, with median lengths of 615 bp and 765 bp, respectively; however, that result does not demonstrate that short genes were falsely called essential. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] Its definition of burden as max_fit > 1 in any experiment also **supports** caution about single-measurement classifications, because a single experiment can classify a gene as burdensome and the result is sensitive to fitness-data noise. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

The new comparative analysis also **refines** interpretation of the estimate rather than eliminating the bias concern: among 33 organisms passing its integration filters, essential genes were 86.1% core versus 81.2% for non-essential genes, with a median odds ratio of 1.56; 18 of 33 organisms showed significant enrichment after Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate correction. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] This association indicates that many inferred essential genes are conserved, but it does not show that every call is biologically essential because the same analysis retained the insertion-coverage and condition limitations. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] The fitness-effects analysis **further refines** this interpretation: across approximately 194,000 genes from 43 bacteria, essential genes were 82% core while always-neutral genes were 66% core, a statistically robust but weak conservation gradient; therefore conservation is consistent with many calls being biologically meaningful but cannot by itself validate individual essentiality calls. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

The costly+dispensable analysis **supports** separating conservation from assay interpretation: across 142,190 genes from 43 bacteria, 5,526 were both costly and dispensable, and this group was enriched for mobile elements, poorly annotated and taxonomically restricted genes rather than core metabolic functions. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] This finding does not directly measure transposon callability, but it identifies a biologically distinct, short-gene-rich accessory population that should not be treated as evidence against technical bias in essentiality calls. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

RB-TnSeq defines essentiality under specific library-construction conditions, typically rich media, so it can also miss genes that are essential only under stress. [src: essential_genome] This creates a complementary false-negative problem: technical non-callability can make a gene appear essential, while condition-specific biology can make a genuinely important gene appear non-essential under the tested condition. [src: essential_genome] Evidence from [[summaries/core_gene_tradeoffs__REPORT]] **refines** this condition-dependence: 25,271 genes were classified as true trade-off genes because their fitness was below -1 in some conditions and above 1 in others, while 28,017 were Costly + Conserved, showing that conservation and laboratory fitness burden are not interchangeable measures of essentiality. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] The conservation-versus-fitness report **supports** extending this test to condition-specific fitness rather than binary essentiality. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] The fitness-effects analysis also found that genes with strong condition-specific effects were 77.3% core versus 70.3% without such annotations, **refining** the concern by showing that condition-specific signals are not confined to poorly conserved accessory genes. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] The costly+dispensable analysis **supports** this emphasis on context: 14.1% of costly+dispensable genes had condition-specific phenotypes, compared with 16.7% of costly+conserved genes and 2.7% of neutral+dispensable genes. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

## Interpretation Across Comparative Analyses

The report identified 859 universally essential ortholog families, 4,799 variably essential families, and 11,564 never-essential families among 17,222 ortholog families. [src: essential_genome] These categories should be interpreted alongside [[concepts/gene-essentiality]] because apparent universal or variable essentiality can reflect both biological dependence and uneven perturbation callability. [src: essential_genome]

The report found 7,084 essential genes with no detectable orthologs in any other Fitness Browser organism, including 4,385 hypothetical orphan essentials that were not predictable by the reported module-transfer method. [src: essential_genome] Conservative BBH (bidirectional best-hit) orthology can miss paralogs, gene fusions, and distant homologs, so some apparent orphan essentials may have undetected orthologs with diverged sequences. [src: essential_genome] This limitation links callability-aware essentiality interpretation to [[concepts/callability-limited-comparative-inference]] and [[concepts/homology-search-negative-evidence]]. [src: essential_genome]

The study's proposed interpretation of 859 universally essential families as a stringent experimentally defined core is therefore conditional on reliable insertion-based measurements across organisms. [src: essential_genome] The smaller set of 15 families essential in all 48 organisms reflects the requirement that transposon disruption be lethal in every tested organism, which is more stringent than computational conservation. [src: essential_genome] The core-gene trade-off analysis **supports** keeping this distinction explicit: core genes were 1.29 times more likely than non-core genes to be trade-off genes (odds ratio 1.29, p=1.2e-44), so conservation can coexist with condition-dependent laboratory burden rather than imply universal essentiality. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]

The costly+dispensable analysis **further supports** treating pangenome status as a contextual comparator rather than an essentiality validator: only 50.8% of costly+dispensable genes had SEED annotations and 20.0% had KEGG annotations, while 44.5% were orphans with no ortholog group. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] Its 90% identity DIAMOND threshold for linking Fitness Browser and pangenome genes may miss recently acquired genes with low sequence similarity, and its ortholog groups were assigned by bidirectional best hits across 48 organisms; these limitations **reinforce** the need for sensitive homology and synteny checks when interpreting apparent orphan or accessory genes. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

## Relation to Other Assay Limitations

Callability bias is distinct from [[concepts/essentiality-assay-discordance]]: the former concerns whether a perturbation can be observed or interpreted, whereas assay discordance concerns disagreement between essentiality measurements. [src: essential_genome] It also complements [[concepts/genetic-perturbation-coverage-bias]], because sequence accessibility and library construction can shape which genes receive informative perturbations. [src: essential_genome] The fitness-effects analysis **supports** this distinction between biological neutrality and missing measurements. Novel singleton genes had near-zero mean fitness under the tested laboratory conditions. The report reads them as largely invisible to lab-based fitness assays, neither beneficial nor detrimental, rather than as systematically beneficial or harmful. It also noted that poor transposon coverage could explain missing fitness data for these genes, so near-zero observed fitness should not automatically be treated as true neutrality. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

## Absence From Fitness Data as an Upper Bound

The conservation-versus-fitness analysis identified 27,693 putative essential genes, 18.6% of 148,826 protein-coding genes across 33 organisms, with a per-organism range of 12.9-28.9%. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] These figures come from a different organism set from the 48-organism essential-genome estimates above, and the page reports the two separately rather than reconciling them. [src: conservation_vs_fitness, essential_genome] The essential calls are callability inferences by construction. After checking all 45 tables in the Fitness Browser database, the project found no explicit essentiality column, insertion count or "has fitness data" flag. Putative essentials are therefore type='1' genes from the `gene` table that are absent from `genefitness`. That gives only an upper bound on true essentiality, not confirmed essential-gene calls. [src: pitfalls]

The conservation-versus-fitness report cites Goodall et al. (2018) as precedent for this concern. That study used TraDIS to define the *E. coli* K-12 essential genome and noted that gene-length biases can affect transposon-based essentiality calls. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] The report says its gene-length validation addresses the concern. However, that validation found putative essential genes slightly shorter on average, which the report reads as consistent with some insertion bias, not as ruling it out. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]

The same analysis found 1,259 essential-unmapped genes with no pangenome cluster match, and 44.7% of them were hypothetical. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] Their reported known functions include divergent ribosomal proteins (L34, L36, S11, S12), translation factors, transposases and DNA-binding proteins. The report interprets these as likely recently acquired or highly divergent variants of core functions; this is an interpretation, not a measurement. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] Like the orphan essentials above, these genes combine an insertion-absence call with a failed homology match, so both detection limits apply at once. [src: conservation_vs_fitness, essential_genome]

Gene length links callability to other gene properties. The field-versus-lab analysis notes that short genes get fewer transposon insertions while core genes tend to be longer, so length is confounded with both fitness measurement quality and core status. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness] The truly-dark-genes analysis **supports** and extends this confound. It compared truly dark genes with annotation-lag genes and found a median length of 121 versus 194 aa, with an effect size of d = −0.432. Short genes are harder to annotate, with fewer domains and fewer homologs. They are also harder to measure fitness for, because they have fewer TA sites. The report says this length difference may partially explain the other differences it observed. [src: truly_dark_genes]

## Systematic Exclusion of Putative Essentials From Downstream Analyses

Putative essentials have no `genefitness` rows, so analyses that query only that table silently omit them. The pitfalls digest reports that such a query misses ~14.3% of protein-coding genes, which it describes as having no transposon insertions and as 82% core. It warns that fitness-versus-conservation analyses must either add these genes from the `gene` table or acknowledge that the most extreme fitness category is missing. [src: pitfalls] Several projects report the same exclusion, listed below. [src: counter_ion_effects, metal_specificity, field_vs_lab_fitness]

- In the counter-ion analysis, ~14.3% of protein-coding genes lacked transposon insertions and were absent from both NaCl and metal fitness data. These genes were 82% core, and their exclusion affected the original and corrected conservation analyses equally. [src: counter_ion_effects]
- The metal-specificity analysis reported that ~14% of protein-coding genes (~82% core) were putatively essential and absent from fitness data. [src: metal_specificity]
- The field-versus-lab analysis excluded an additional 678 essential genes (80.1% core) because no transposon mutants, and hence no fitness data, were available. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]

Two metal reports each argue that this exclusion biases their own results conservatively. The metal fitness atlas states that putatively essential genes (~14.3% of protein-coding genes, ~82% core) lack transposon insertions and are absent from fitness data. Because these genes are overwhelmingly core, it argues that excluding them makes the observed core enrichment of metal fitness genes a conservative estimate. [src: metal_fitness_atlas] The metal-specificity analysis says the exclusion biases all categories toward core enrichment, so its true baseline core fraction is likely lower than 81% and its reported deltas are conservative. [src: metal_specificity] These direction-of-bias arguments are reasoned, not measured, and another study states a different baseline direction (see Tensions). They also treat the excluded set as essential, although absence from `genefitness` is only an upper bound on essentiality, and gene length is confounded with both callability and core status. [src: metal_fitness_atlas, metal_specificity, pitfalls, field_vs_lab_fitness]

The AMR fitness-cost analysis applies similar logic to a cost estimate. Its +0.086 is the pooled effect of a DerSimonian-Laird random-effects meta-analysis across 25 organisms, meaning that AMR gene knockouts are 0.086 fitness units less detrimental than the average gene knockout. The report notes that absolute AMR knockout fitness averages −0.024 while the non-AMR background averages around −0.11, so the cost is a relative difference from that background. [src: amr_fitness_cost] About ~4.6% of AMR genes are putatively essential and absent from the fitness matrices. If these are the most costly AMR genes, the report says the +0.086 estimate is a lower bound. [src: amr_fitness_cost] The report also argues that this low essential rate weighs against strong right-censoring bias. That is a reasoned bound, not a direct measurement of the missing genes' costs. [src: amr_fitness_cost]

Module-based analyses inherit the exclusion completely. No essential genes (0) appeared in any module because independent component analysis (ICA), which decomposes fitness variation into independent signals, requires fitness data. Modules therefore capture only the non-essential portion of the genome. [src: module_conservation] The discoveries digest reports the same null across the 1,116 fitness modules in 32 organisms, which ties callability directly to [[concepts/fitness-module-detection-sensitivity]]. [src: discoveries]

The functional-dark-matter analysis classified 9,557 (55%) of 17,344 scored dark genes as essential because no viable transposon mutants were available. [src: functional_dark_matter] These essential dark genes have zero rows in `genefitness` and are invisible to standard RB-TnSeq, although this does not mean they were absent from every scoring analysis. [src: functional_dark_matter] Their essential label rests on the same absence-of-insertions inference that the pitfalls digest treats as only an upper bound. [src: functional_dark_matter, pitfalls]

## Insertion Is Not Deletion

In [[entities/acinetobacter-baylyi-adp1]], a single-organism comparison set RB-TnSeq results against experimental knockout essentiality in rich media (N=1,933 genes). The TnSeq measure here is essentiality_fraction, which the report describes as a cross-condition fitness measure: continuous and poor for lethality. It is not an insertion-absence call like the others on this page. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] At an essentiality_fraction cutoff=0.05 against the rich-medium knockout data, 211 knockout-essential genes were called dispensable by TnSeq (mean essentiality_fraction = 0.0034), and 293 TnSeq-essential genes were knockout-dispensable. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] The report offers explanations but does not demonstrate them. It proposes that the first group tolerates partial function from transposon-disrupted alleles, through truncated proteins or read-through transcription. It proposes that the second group may be fitness-important but not lethal. It sums up the point as transposon insertion ≠ gene deletion. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]

Changing the threshold did not resolve this discordance. In the same rich-medium knockout comparison, five essentiality_fraction thresholds (0.01, 0.025, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20) were tested on a distribution with mean=0.026 and median=0.0, and none showed positive kappa against the knockout data. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] This **refines** the callability concern, with a caveat. Missing insertions can inflate absence-based essential-gene counts, while the ADP1 comparison suggests the hypothesis that disrupted alleles retaining partial function can make knockout-essential genes look dispensable. Because the ADP1 measure is cross-condition fitness compared with rich-medium knockouts, rather than insertion absence, this is a related failure mode, not a direct test of absence-based calls. It connects this page to [[concepts/essentiality-assay-discordance]]. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality, essential_genome]

Polar effects are a further measurement artifact. Transposon insertions can affect downstream genes in operons, which may confound fitness measurements for AMR genes. [src: amr_fitness_cost] Some strong phenotypes of truly dark genes may likewise reflect polar effects on downstream genes rather than the dark gene's own function. [src: truly_dark_genes]

## Tensions

Studies disagree about which way excluding putative essentials moves the baseline core fraction. The metal-specificity analysis states that the exclusion biases all categories toward core enrichment, so its true baseline core fraction is likely lower than 81%. [src: metal_specificity] The field-versus-lab analysis states that including its 678 excluded essential genes (80.1% core) would raise its overall baseline slightly. It adds that this would not change its condition-class comparisons, which are among non-essential genes only. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness] The two claims concern different organism sets and baselines, so they are recorded here as study-specific assertions, not reconciled. [src: metal_specificity, field_vs_lab_fitness]

## Open Directions

- Reanalyze the 41,059 essential genes using gene length, AT content, scaffold position, insertion density, and local library coverage to test which features predict apparent essentiality. [src: essential_genome]
- Compare insertion-depleted genes with independent CRISPRi knockdown or targeted-deletion results to estimate the fraction of calls attributable to technical non-callability. [src: essential_genome]
- Recalculate essentiality rates after excluding low-callability loci and ask whether the range from 12.2% in Pedo557 to 29.7% in Magneto narrows. [src: essential_genome]
- Test whether the 7,084 orphan essentials remain orphaned after sensitive homology searches and synteny-aware comparisons, distinguishing true lineage-specific genes from missed homologs. [src: essential_genome]
- Repeat RB-TnSeq under stress conditions and compare with rich-medium results to separate condition-specific essentiality from missed essentiality caused by the original assay environment. [src: essential_genome]
- Recompute the essential-versus-core enrichment after excluding low-callability loci, testing whether the median odds ratio of 1.56 persists across the 33 integrated organisms. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]
- Stratify the 25,271 trade-off genes and 28,017 Costly + Conserved genes by insertion callability, then test whether their condition-specific fitness patterns persist after excluding technically under-covered loci. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]
- Reanalyze singleton fitness neutrality after stratifying by insertion coverage and gene length, testing whether the near-zero mean fitness signal persists in well-covered loci. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- Test whether the 5,526 costly+dispensable genes are disproportionately low-callability because of short length, AT content, or mobile-element sequence, separating genuine fitness burden from perturbation-accessibility artifacts. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]
- Replace binary core/accessory status with pangenome prevalence and repeat the essentiality-conservation analysis, while using sensitive homology searches to assess the 44.5% orphan fraction among costly+dispensable genes. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]
- Split the ~14.3% of genes absent from `genefitness` by gene length and TA-site density, then recompute the metal core-enrichment deltas and baselines with and without them. This would test the 'conservative estimate' argument and settle the baseline-direction tension. [src: metal_fitness_atlas, metal_specificity, field_vs_lab_fitness, truly_dark_genes]
- Use the ADP1 rich-medium knockout data to test whether the 293 TnSeq-essential/knockout-dispensable genes are enriched for short or AT-rich loci. Also test whether the 211 knockout-essential/TnSeq-dispensable genes carry insertions concentrated near gene ends, as the partial-function hypothesis would predict. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]
- Flag genes upstream of essential or strong-phenotype genes within operons, then re-estimate AMR fitness costs and dark-gene phenotypes without them. This would bound the contribution of polar effects. [src: amr_fitness_cost, truly_dark_genes]
- Re-score the 9,557 essential dark genes and the 1,259 essential-unmapped genes with callability covariates. This would separate likely lineage-specific essentials from insertion-inaccessible loci before experimental prioritization. [src: functional_dark_matter, conservation_vs_fitness]
