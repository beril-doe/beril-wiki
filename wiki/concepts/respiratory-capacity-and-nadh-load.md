# Respiratory-chain dependence tracks reducing-equivalent load rather than substrate identity

Respiratory-chain dependence may be driven more by reducing-equivalent load—particularly NADH generated during substrate oxidation—than by whether a substrate is chemically aromatic. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] The [[summaries/aromatic_catabolism_network__REPORT]] supports this interpretation by combining condition-specific fitness data with metabolic flux analysis and by comparing aromatic and non-aromatic substrates. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] New ADP1 wiring evidence **supports** this interpretation by showing that carbon sources select qualitatively different respiratory configurations. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] The report proposes that these configurations come from NADH flux rate and capacity constraints rather than from total reducing-equivalent yield. That mechanism is still a hypothesis: it rests on theoretical pathway stoichiometry, not on measured flux distributions or direct NDH-2 growth data. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]

The [[summaries/metabolic_capability_dependency__REPORT]] **supports** separating encoded metabolic capability from experimentally measured dependency: across 161 organism–pathway pairs, complete pathways could be either fitness-important or fitness-neutral, and all 66 pairs classified as Latent Capability became fitness-important under at least one tested condition type. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] This **refines** the respiratory interpretation by showing that pathway presence or substrate category alone does not establish functional dependence; the relevant condition-specific reducing-equivalent burden still requires measurement. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] The condition-specific reclassification is partly sensitive to the report’s median-based importance threshold, so it is evidence for conditional dependence rather than independent confirmation of every individual pathway call. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

## Core interpretation

The report identifies [[entities/complex-i]] as the largest support subsystem in the ADP1 quinate-catabolism network: 21/51 quinate-specific genes (41%) belong to Complex I, or NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreductase, which the report calls the dominant support requirement for aromatic catabolism. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] This association initially appears substrate-specific because Complex I fitness is strongly impaired under aromatic conditions, but the strongest condition-level defects occur on acetate and succinate, which are non-aromatic substrates that also generate high NADH flux through the TCA cycle. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Thus, the evidence supports the hypothesis that respiratory-chain demand tracks reducing-equivalent production rather than aromatic chemistry itself. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

The cross-project digest **supports** reading Complex I as one part of a larger support network. It reports that the [[entities/beta-ketoadipate-pathway]] in ADP1 has 8 core genes, while 51 genes in total show quinate-specific growth defects. The digest calls this a 7:1 support-to-pathway ratio, but its own stated counts do not directly reproduce that ratio, so the ratio should be treated as a source-reported figure with an unresolved inconsistency. The digest groups the support genes into Complex I (21 genes, NADH reoxidation), iron acquisition (7 genes, Fe²⁺ for ring-cleavage dioxygenase), and [[entities/pqq-biosynthesis]] (2 genes, cofactor for quinate dehydrogenase). [src: discoveries] The deletion-phenotype analysis defines this quinate-specific set as 51 genes at spec > 0.5, z < -1, and notes that it extends beyond the core degradation pathway to include Complex I subunits. [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes] That analysis also suggests that aromatic catabolism places unique demands on the electron transport chain, but this is an inference rather than a measurement. [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes] PQQ biosynthesis genes are condition-specific for both quinate and glucose, consistent with PQQ-dependent dehydrogenases catalyzing the first step of both pathways. This shared signal **refines** an aromatic-specific reading, because the cofactor requirement is not unique to the aromatic substrate. [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes] [[entities/aciad3137]] (UPF0234/YitK) and [[entities/aciad2176]] (DUF2280) correlate at r > 0.98 with Complex I genes across 8 growth conditions. Neither has reaction mappings in flux-balance analysis (FBA, a growth-optimizing constraint-based model of metabolism), and neither has an assigned metabolic function. Their co-fitness with the nuo operon suggests, but does not establish, a physical or regulatory association with Complex I. [src: discoveries]

The new wiring analysis **refines** this interpretation. It reports that the branched respiratory chain of [[entities/acinetobacter-baylyi-adp1]] (62 genes across 8 subsystems) is wired in a condition-dependent manner, and that requirements differ by carbon source. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]
- Quinate requires only Complex I; [[entities/cytochrome-bo3]], cytochrome bd, succinate dehydrogenase (SDH) and all other components are dispensable. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]
- Acetate is the most demanding condition, requiring Complex I, cytochrome bo3, [[entities/aciad3522]] and additional components. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]
- Lactate requires cytochrome bo3; the Complex I effect is mild and cytochrome bd is dispensable. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]
- Glucose has no specifically required respiratory component (full redundancy). [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]
- Urea is generally demanding across the reported respiratory profile. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]

The cross-project digest **supports** the same reading for ADP1. It says each of ADP1's 8 carbon sources requires a distinct set of respiratory chain components. In the digest, quinate requires only Complex I, with all other components dispensable. Acetate requires Complex I, cytochrome bo3, ACIAD3522 and more, which makes it the most demanding condition. Glucose requires no specific component (full redundancy). The digest describes this as qualitatively different wiring per substrate class rather than a quantitative gradient. [src: discoveries] These qualitative configurations are consistent with a capacity-limited model in which the relevant determinant is how NADH is generated and reoxidized under each condition, not substrate identity alone. That model remains a proposed interpretation. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]

The report's per-substrate mechanistic models are interpretations layered on these phenotypes, not measured fluxes. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] For quinate, the report proposes that aromatic ring cleavage causes a TCA burst and a Complex I bottleneck. It treats cytochrome bo3 and cytochrome bd as dispensable because the bottleneck is NADH reoxidation, not the terminal oxidase step. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] For acetate, it proposes that direct TCA entry drives continuous NADH production, which requires all available dehydrogenases plus cytochrome bo3 as the primary terminal oxidase. ACIAD3522 loss is specifically lethal on acetate (0.013 growth ratio). [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] For lactate, it proposes that pyruvate entry gives moderate NADH flux and a specific cytochrome bo3 requirement. Complex I is only mildly important (0.77), and the report suggests, but does not demonstrate, partial NDH-2 compensation. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] For glucose, it proposes that [[entities/entner-doudoroff-pathway]] metabolism spreads NADH production across multiple steps, so NDH-2 capacity suffices and no specific respiratory component is required. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] Separately, the deletion-phenotype analysis lists lldR and cyoC/cyoD among the lactate-specific genes, associated with lactate regulation and cytochrome oxidase. This is consistent with a lactate-specific terminal-oxidase requirement. [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes]

The capability–dependency comparison **supports** this distinction at broader scale: pathway category predicted dependency class (χ²=163.6, df=4, p=2.5×10⁻³⁴), but complete pathways could remain fitness-neutral, especially carbon pathways. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] The newer analysis **supports** the same distinction with a separate four-way classification of complete and incomplete pathways against fitness importance, including 57 Active Dependency and 66 Latent Capability pairs among 161 combinations. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] Neither analysis identifies respiratory load directly, but both reinforce that chemical or genomic pathway identity is an imperfect proxy for condition-specific physiological demand. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

## Evidence from transferred fitness data

Ortholog-transferred data from the [[entities/kescience-fitnessbrowser]] contains 12,241 entries covering 2,005 genes and 13 conditions. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Complex I orthologs have mean fitness values of -1.35 on aromatic conditions and -0.77 on comparison conditions, with Mann-Whitney p < 0.0001. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] This result supports a strong condition-dependent Complex I effect, but it does not by itself establish that aromatic substrates are the causal driver. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

Per-condition results refine that association: the largest Complex I defects relative to background are -1.55 on acetate and -1.39 on succinate. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Because acetate and succinate are non-aromatic substrates that generate high NADH flux through the TCA cycle, these observations support reducing-equivalent load as an alternative explanation for the apparent aromatic association. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] The report states that its cross-species data show Complex I is dispensable on glucose and lactate, where NDH-2 suffices, but essential on substrates that generate high TCA cycle flux (aromatics, acetate, succinate). [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] It proposes that ADP1's quinate-specificity of Complex I likely reflects an alternative NADH dehydrogenase, [[entities/ndh-2]], that compensates on simpler substrates. This compensation explanation is inferred, not directly established. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] The cross-project digest repeats this reframing and recasts the "aromatic support network" as a "high-NADH-flux bottleneck network". It still treats NDH-2 compensation as a likely explanation, not a demonstrated one. [src: discoveries]

The wiring analysis **qualifies** that compensation hypothesis rather than confirming it. Complex I growth ratios are 0.37 on quinate, 1.44 on glucose, and 0.49 on acetate, while ACIAD3522 has a growth ratio of 0.013 on acetate. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] In the wiring table, Complex I (NDH-1) has 13 subunits and is proton-pumping (4 H⁺/NADH). [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] ACIAD3522, an NADH-FMN oxidoreductase, has 1 subunit and is not proton-pumping. Its growth ratios are 1.39 on quinate, 1.39 on glucose, and 0.013 on acetate. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] The report cautions that ACIAD3522 may not be a true NADH dehydrogenase in the respiratory sense, because an NADH-FMN oxidoreductase could serve other metabolic functions. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] NDH-2 is present as ACIAD_RS16420 (KO K03885), a standalone core-genome gene outside respiratory operons. It is dispensable in transposon-insertion sequencing (TnSeq) but missing from the deletion collection, so it has no growth data. The FBA model predicts zero flux through NDH-2 on all standard carbon sources and routes all NADH through Complex I. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] The digest describes NDH-2 as a 1-subunit, non-pumping enzyme that is a predicted, not measured, backup for glucose. [src: discoveries] The digest frames the three enzymes as a division of labor: Complex I is quinate-essential and glucose-dispensable, while ACIAD3522 is acetate-lethal (0.013) and quinate-fine (1.39). Given the caveat about ACIAD3522's function, this division of labor among respiratory dehydrogenases is a hypothesis. [src: discoveries, respiratory_chain_wiring] The report then filtered likely NDH-2 false positives, keeping organisms with 1-2 hits and treating those with >2 hits as likely misannotated Complex I subunits. After filtering, 5 of 14 organisms had validated NDH-2. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] Those organisms showed larger Complex I aromatic deficits (mean = -0.297) than organisms without NDH-2 (mean = -0.156). This is the opposite of the compensation prediction, and the difference is not significant (p = 0.52). [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] The digest reports that the initial analysis (10/14 organisms with NDH-2, apparent support for compensation) was driven by misannotated Complex I subunits leaking through the text filter in 5 organisms, so the apparent support was an annotation artifact. [src: discoveries] The ADP1 wiring pattern may therefore be species-specific rather than a general rule. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]

## Evidence from metabolic flux and gene phenotypes

Flux-balance analysis (FBA), a growth-optimizing constraint-based model of metabolism, predicts 1.76× higher Complex I flux on aromatic substrates than on the comparison condition, with fluxes of 0.55 versus 0.31. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] This supports increased respiratory demand during aromatic growth, but the same model predicts 0% essentiality for Complex I. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] The contrast indicates that modeled flux capacity and gene essentiality are not equivalent: the model can redistribute flux through alternative routes, whereas disruption of a multi-subunit complex can eliminate complex function. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] The report gives two reasons for the miss. First, FBA optimizes for growth rate and can redistribute flux through alternative pathways. Second, its linear programming framework does not capture the threshold effect that occurs when losing any single subunit eliminates Complex I function. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] The digest **extends** this model gap: of the 51 quinate-specific genes, 30 (59%) have no FBA reaction mappings. As a result, PQQ biosynthesis, iron acquisition, transcriptional regulators, and respiratory chain components are invisible to the metabolic model. [src: discoveries]

Observed phenotypes provide direct support for a complex-level dependency: 10/13 Complex I operon subunits independently produce quinate-specific growth defects. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] The report proposes a mechanism: succinyl-CoA and acetyl-CoA from β-ketoadipate are fully oxidized in the TCA cycle, generating NADH at multiple steps, and Complex I is needed to reoxidize that NADH under high flux. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] However, the quinate-specific phenotype does not distinguish aromatic substrate chemistry from the reducing-equivalent burden associated with processing that substrate. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

The new analysis **supports** the capacity interpretation but **refines** the mechanism: for each carbon source, theoretical stoichiometry gives a total NADH yield and a yield per carbon, which the report lists beside the Complex I growth ratio. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] The values are:
- Quinate: 4 NADH (0.57 NADH/carbon); Complex I growth ratio 0.37.
- Glucose: 9 NADH (1.50 NADH/carbon); growth ratio 1.44.
- Acetate: 3 NADH (1.50 NADH/carbon); growth ratio 0.49.
- Lactate: 5 NADH (1.67 NADH/carbon); growth ratio 0.77. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]

Quinate produces fewer NADH per carbon than glucose or acetate, yet Complex I is more essential on quinate. The report therefore proposes NADH production rate, rather than total yield, as the determinant. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring, discoveries] In this model, ring cleavage via the β-ketoadipate pathway yields succinyl-CoA and acetyl-CoA at the same time. That creates a concentrated TCA-cycle NADH burst that exceeds NDH-2's reoxidation capacity. Glucose instead spreads NADH production across Entner-Doudoroff and TCA steps, staying within NDH-2's capacity. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring, discoveries] This is a biochemical interpretation based on theoretical pathway stoichiometry, not measured flux distributions. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] FBA predicts zero flux through NDH-2 and ACIAD3522 on all standard media because growth optimization routes NADH through the ATP-favorable Complex I pathway, illustrating why capacity constraints and alternative suboptimal routes can be missed. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]

## Limits of the inference

The cross-species fitness evidence is not definitive for ADP1 because the transferred data mixes organisms with different respiratory-chain architectures. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] The conclusion that NADH load, rather than substrate identity, is the primary driver is therefore a hypothesis rather than an established general rule. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Direct Complex I fitness measurements on aromatic and non-aromatic substrates in the same organism would provide a stronger test.

The capability–dependency study **supports** this caution because its latent fraction varied from 0–31.6% across organisms and from 4.7% to 21.1% across tested classification thresholds; it also notes that Fitness Browser experiments are biased toward laboratory conditions. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] The newer analysis further **supports** this caution: only 7 of 48 Fitness Browser organisms had matching GapMind data, and its condition-specific latent reclassification used a median-based threshold that can cause pathways to cross the threshold by construction. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] Thus, an apparently latent respiratory or metabolic capability may reflect untested environmental conditions or calibration choices rather than true dispensability. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

The proposed compensation by NDH-2 also requires direct testing because the report recommends searching the ADP1 genome for NDH-2 and comparing its deletion phenotype on quinate versus glucose. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] The new evidence **refines** this open test by identifying NDH-2 in ADP1 while confirming that it is TnSeq-dispensable but absent from the deletion collection and has no direct growth data. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] Proteomics is consistent with a passive, flux-based model: Complex I, NDH-2, and ACIAD3522 had similar protein levels under standard growth conditions, with means of 27.6 (66th percentile), 27.0 (59th percentile), and 26.2 (48th percentile), respectively, against a genome median of 26.4. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] The report interprets this constitutive co-expression as wiring at the metabolic rather than the transcriptional level. These measurements are not condition-specific. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] The digest gives the same interpretation: the dehydrogenase that becomes limiting depends on the NADH flux rate from the carbon source, not on regulated expression. [src: discoveries]

Several predictions of the flux-rate model remain untested. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] The central prediction, that NDH-2 deletion makes Complex I essential on glucose, can only be tested experimentally with an ADP1 NDH-2 knockout. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] The model also predicts that quinate-grown cells have higher NADH/NAD⁺ ratios than glucose-grown cells, which metabolomics could test directly. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] Whether ADP1 upregulates Complex I on aromatic substrates and NDH-2 on glucose has not been established. The report proposes reanalyzing the Stuani et al. (2014) quinate-versus-succinate proteomics data for respiratory chain proteins to test this. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]

## Tensions

The earlier transferred-fitness interpretation reports Complex I as dispensable on glucose and lactate and treats this as consistent with NDH-2 compensation. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] The new ADP1-specific analysis reports a Complex I growth ratio of 1.44 on glucose, predicts zero NDH-2 flux under standard FBA conditions, and finds no significant cross-species compensation pattern (p = 0.52). [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] The digest adds that the cross-species result that first looked supportive was an annotation artifact of text-matched NDH-2 candidates. [src: discoveries] The ADP1 wiring analysis also reports a mild Complex I effect on lactate (0.77) rather than outright dispensability. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] These results do not establish that the earlier interpretation is false: they may reflect different fitness metrics, model assumptions, or species-level respiratory architectures. Resolving the tension requires matched ADP1 deletion assays and direct NADH/NAD⁺ measurements across carbon sources. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]

## Relation to other concepts

This concept **refines** [[concepts/condition-specific-fitness]] by separating the observed condition effect from the chemical identity of the substrate. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] It **supports** [[concepts/metabolic-model-gapfilling]] because the model captures increased Complex I flux but misses the corresponding essentiality and leaves respiratory-support functions incompletely represented. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] The wiring analysis **strengthens** this connection by showing that FBA preferentially routes NADH through the ATP-favorable pathway and can therefore miss condition-specific capacity constraints. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] The capability–dependency results **refine** this connection by demonstrating that completeness predictions and measured fitness dependence can diverge, with the divergence sensitive to pathway mapping and laboratory condition coverage. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] [src: pathway_capability_dependency] It also **connects** [[concepts/respiratory-capacity-and-nadh-load]] to [[entities/complex-i]] and [[entities/ndh-2]] as candidate components of the capacity limit. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

## Open Directions

- Measure Complex I and NDH-2 deletion fitness in ADP1 on quinate, acetate, succinate, glucose, and lactate, using matched growth assays to ask whether fitness loss follows NADH-generating load rather than aromatic substrate identity. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]
- Measure NADH/NAD⁺ ratios across those carbon sources and test whether an NDH-2 deletion changes the predicted condition-specific respiratory requirement. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]
- Expand the condition panel with benzoate, catechol, vanillate, iron limitation, and respiratory inhibitors, then recompute co-fitness and condition-specific fitness to separate aromatic chemistry, iron demand, and respiratory load. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]
- Add PQQ biosynthesis, iron homeostasis, and respiratory-chain capacity constraints to the ADP1 FBA model, then compare predicted essentiality and flux with the observed defects in 10/13 Complex I operon subunits. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]
- Compare Complex I retention and NDH-2 complements across aromatic-degrading species using pangenome data and KO-based searches to test whether respiratory architecture predicts transferability of the NADH-load hypothesis. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]
- Reanalyze pathway capability and fitness dependence with direct GapMind per-step gene assignments and matched non-laboratory conditions, testing whether respiratory-pathway completeness predicts NADH-load-dependent fitness rather than substrate category. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]
- Calibrate condition-specific pathway-importance thresholds against independently defined essentials, then test whether the resulting respiratory classifications reproduce NADH-load-dependent fitness in matched ADP1 assays. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]
