# Why transposon fitness and complete-knockout essentiality disagree

[[summaries/adp1_triple_essentiality__REPORT]] shows that transposon-based assays and complete-gene knockouts do not measure identical phenotypes. RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing) reports the fitness of insertion mutants, whereas a complete knockout tests whether removing the entire gene produces lethality under the assay conditions. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] This distinction places the result within [[concepts/gene-essentiality]] and [[concepts/condition-space-dimensionality]]. Evidence from [[summaries/fitness_effects_conservation__REPORT]] **supports** treating fitness as a graded phenotype: across approximately 194,000 genes from 43 bacteria, fitness importance and pangenome conservation were positively associated, but the relationship was weak, so fitness importance is not a binary or universal proxy for gene status. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] The synthesis **supports** this interpretation across 194,216 protein-coding genes from 43 diverse bacteria: essential genes were 82% core, whereas always-neutral genes were 66% core, demonstrating a quantitative but modest conservation gradient rather than a binary essentiality boundary. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] The linked conservation_fitness report **refines** that synthesis with a separate 33-organism analysis: essential genes were 86.1% core versus 81.2% for non-essential genes, with a median odds ratio of 1.56, and 18 of 33 organisms showed significant enrichment after Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate correction. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] This modest enrichment supports conservation as a graded correlate of viability requirements, not as an assay-independent definition of essentiality. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] Evidence from [[summaries/core_gene_tradeoffs__REPORT]] **further supports** this distinction: 25,271 genes, or 17.8% of those examined, were trade-off genes with fitness below -1 in some conditions and above 1 in others. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]

## Core evidence

At an RB-TnSeq essentiality threshold of 0.05 in rich medium, the comparison covered 1,933 genes. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] Only 18 genes (0.9%) were called essential by both RB-TnSeq and knockout assays, while 1,411 genes (73.0%) were called dispensable by both. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] The two major discordant groups were knockout-essential/RB-TnSeq-dispensable genes, comprising 211 genes (10.9%), and knockout-dispensable/RB-TnSeq-essential genes, comprising 293 genes (15.2%). [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]

Agreement was poor across all tested RB-TnSeq thresholds of 0.01, 0.025, 0.05, 0.10, and 0.20, for which Cohen’s kappa was -0.139, -0.122, -0.081, -0.024, and -0.014, respectively. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] At the 0.05 threshold, recall was 7.9%, precision was 5.8%, specificity was 82.8%, F1 was 0.067, and Cohen’s kappa was -0.081. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] The uniformly negative kappa values support systematic assay discordance rather than a disagreement caused by one arbitrary threshold, although the thresholds were not formally corrected for multiple testing. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]

The cross-organism pangenome analysis **supports** the direction of this result while limiting its generalization: it identified 27,693 putative essential genes among 148,826 protein-coding genes across 33 organisms, with essentiality inferred from RB-TnSeq under the library construction and growth conditions represented in the Fitness Browser. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] Essentiality calls therefore remain condition- and assay-specific, and their apparent conservation advantage cannot by itself establish complete-knockout lethality. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]

## Continuous fitness is more informative than a binary call

Continuous RB-TnSeq fitness performed better than the binary essentiality fraction for identifying knockout-essential genes. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] Inverted fitness produced an AUC (Area Under the ROC Curve, a threshold-independent classification measure) of 0.700 in rich medium and 0.725 in minimal medium, whereas essentiality fraction produced AUC values of 0.344 and 0.403 in the same media. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]

The discordant groups also differed in their continuous measurements. Knockout-essential/RB-TnSeq-dispensable genes had mean essentiality fraction = 0.0034, standard deviation = 0.010, mean fitness = -0.79, and standard deviation = 0.93. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] Knockout-dispensable/RB-TnSeq-essential genes had mean essentiality fraction = 0.1023, standard deviation = 0.118, mean fitness = -0.34, and standard deviation = 0.66. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] The 18 genes called essential by both assays had mean essentiality fraction = 0.0969, standard deviation = 0.041, mean fitness = -1.18, and standard deviation = 1.49. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]

These results support treating fitness as a graded phenotype rather than converting it immediately into an essential/dis­pensable label. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] They also refine the broader [[concepts/gene-essentiality]] distinction between predicting lethality and predicting the magnitude of growth impairment. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] The conservation analysis **supports** this refinement but also limits its interpretation: essential genes were 82% core, compared with 66% for always-neutral genes, while the overall association between fitness breadth and core status was weak (Spearman rho=0.086, p=8.1e-230). [src: fitness_effects_conservation] Thus, stronger or broader measured fitness effects can align with conservation without making conservation a strong predictor of assay-defined essentiality. The synthesis **further supports** this graded interpretation by finding that core genes were 1.78x more likely than accessory genes to have strong condition-specific phenotypes, rather than being uniformly essential. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

The core-gene trade-off analysis **further supports** retaining continuous and condition-specific measurements rather than treating conserved genes as uniformly essential: trade-off genes were 1.29 times more likely to be core than non-core genes (odds ratio 1.29, p=1.2e-44). [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] It also found that core-gene burden was function-specific, with core genes more burdensome in Protein Metabolism, Motility, and RNA Metabolism, but non-core genes more burdensome in Cell Wall genes; the reported differences were +6.2, +7.8, +12.9, and -14.1 percentage points, respectively. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] These results refine the conservation comparison by showing that core status can coexist with substantial, reversible laboratory costs rather than implying universal essentiality. The synthesis **supports** this refinement: 24.4% of core genes were beneficial when deleted, compared with 19.9% of accessory genes, so conservation did not identify an inert housekeeping genome. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

The new pangenome linkage **further supports** retaining conservation as contextual evidence rather than a binary surrogate. Across the 33-organism analysis, essential-core genes were 41.9% enzymes and 13.0% hypothetical, whereas essential-auxiliary genes were 13.4% enzymes and 38.2% hypothetical; essential-unmapped genes were 44.7% hypothetical. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] This distribution suggests that some RB-TnSeq-defined essential functions are poorly characterized or strain-specific, but it does not resolve whether their insertion phenotype would equal complete-gene deletion. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]

## Why the assays can disagree

The report interprets the pattern as consistent with insertion mutants retaining partial function, whereas complete deletion removes the entire gene. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] Partial or truncated protein production, read-through transcription, retained functional domains, and aggregation across conditions are proposed mechanisms for RB-TnSeq mutants appearing dispensable when complete knockouts are lethal. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] These mechanisms remain hypotheses in this analysis rather than directly resolved demonstrations. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]

The reverse discordance is also informative: some genes classified as RB-TnSeq-essential were knockout-dispensable. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] This pattern suggests that insertion position, polar effects on neighboring genes, or assay-specific fitness costs may make an insertion mutant more impaired than a complete deletion, but these explanations were not directly established by the report. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]

The conservation analysis **refines** this interpretation by showing that core genes had heavier tails in both negative and positive fitness effects and that 24.4% of core genes versus 19.9% of auxiliary genes were ever beneficial when deleted. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] This does not establish a mechanism for individual assay disagreements, but it indicates that large effects in either direction can occur in conserved genes and should not be treated as evidence that accessory genes alone carry condition-dependent costs. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] Consistently, the core-gene trade-off analysis classified 28,017 genes as Costly + Conserved and 5,526 as Costly + Dispensable; it interprets the former as genes maintained despite laboratory cost, while cautioning that this is an inference from laboratory fitness and conservation rather than direct measurement of natural selection. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] The synthesis **supports** this caution while refining the selection interpretation: costly-and-conserved genes are evidence for purifying selection in natural environments, not direct measurements of natural-environment fitness. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

The conservation analysis **supports** the measurement-bias interpretation by noting that its essential-gene definition is an upper bound: missing transposon fitness data can reflect short genes, low-complexity regions, or scaffold-edge placement rather than essentiality, and essential genes were slightly shorter on average. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] It also found that pangenome coverage and clade size affect the core-versus-auxiliary classification, including trivially high core fractions in clades containing only 2 genomes. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] These limitations provide additional reasons not to interpret an RB-TnSeq essentiality call, or its association with core status, as a direct complete-knockout phenotype.

## Relation to FBA and growth assays

The assay disagreement is not equivalent to a general failure of every essentiality predictor. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] In the refined analysis, FBA (flux balance analysis) versus knockout data showed moderate concordance, with rich-medium recall = 60.8%, precision = 64.0%, specificity = 79.7%, F1 = 0.624, and Cohen’s kappa = 0.486 across 724 genes. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] In minimal medium, the comparison covered 833 genes and yielded recall = 65.6%, precision = 69.2%, specificity = 78.9%, F1 = 0.673, and Cohen’s kappa = 0.493. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]

However, among 478 genes that were all TnSeq-dispensable, FBA class did not predict quantitative growth-defect status: chi-squared = 0.93, p = 0.63, 2 df, and the Kruskal-Wallis test on mean growth rates was H = 1.67, p = 0.43. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] Thus, the evidence supports a distinction between FBA’s moderate performance at the lethal-versus-dispensable boundary and its inability in this subset to explain the severity of growth defects among transposon-dispensable genes. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]

## Condition dependence

Assay discordance is partly condition-dependent because mutant growth phenotypes vary across environments. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] Across eight carbon sources, 333 of 478 genes (70%) showed condition-specific growth defects, 10 genes (2%) showed defects across all eight conditions, and 135 genes (28%) showed no defect on any condition. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] Mean pairwise defect correlation was 0.38, with a range of -0.03 to 1.0. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] These findings support [[concepts/condition-specific-fitness]]: an assay comparison made across mismatched or aggregated conditions can conflate perturbation modality with environmental dependence.

The conservation analysis **supports** the importance of retaining this condition dependence: genes with strong `specificphenotype` annotations were 77.3% core versus 70.3% without such annotations, and 4,450 genes (2.7%) were neutral overall but critical in one condition. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] These conditional effects were more common among core genes (3.0%) than auxiliary genes (1.7%) or singleton genes (1.6%), so condition-specific behavior is not restricted to accessory genes. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] The result is consistent with the existing conclusion that aggregating conditions can obscure phenotype differences, while also cautioning that laboratory condition coverage does not represent all ecological niches. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] The core-gene trade-off analysis **reinforces** this point by finding that the burden paradox reverses across functional categories and by interpreting conserved, costly genes as conditionally valuable rather than universally essential. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] The synthesis **supports** this interpretation and identified 1,116 co-regulated fitness modules across 32 organisms; 86% of their genes were core versus an 81.5% baseline, with 59% of modules more than 90% core genes. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] This organization of effects into conserved modules further argues against reducing context-dependent fitness to isolated binary essentiality calls.

The new analysis **supports** condition matching as a priority because its RB-TnSeq essentiality calls came from the growth conditions represented by library construction, while genes essential only under stress were not captured. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] Its proposed extension—testing genes with fitness < -2 under stress conditions—directly addresses whether conditionally important genes show different conservation patterns. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]

## Tensions

The data support two conclusions that must be kept separate: continuous transposon fitness contains useful information about knockout lethality, but thresholded RB-TnSeq essentiality shows poor agreement with complete-knockout calls. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] The first conclusion is supported by AUC values of 0.700 and 0.725 for inverted fitness, while the second is supported by the 0.05-threshold AUC of 0.344 for essentiality fraction and Cohen’s kappa of -0.081. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] The apparent tension is resolved by distinguishing graded fitness prediction from binary assay concordance rather than treating either assay as a universal definition of essentiality.

The conservation evidence **supports** the graded-fitness conclusion but introduces a qualification rather than a contradiction: the conservation gradient was statistically robust yet weak, and singleton neutrality may reflect poor transposon coverage rather than true neutrality. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] This means that conservation cannot independently resolve whether an apparently neutral insertion mutant retains function or simply lacks reliable fitness measurement.

The core-gene trade-off evidence **refines** the interpretation of costly conserved genes but does not directly establish natural selection: its Costly + Conserved category is inferred from laboratory burden and conservation, whereas the assay-discordance evidence shows that laboratory measurements can depend on perturbation modality and condition. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] Accordingly, costly conservation should be treated as a selection hypothesis requiring ecological validation, not as direct evidence that a gene is essential in nature. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] The new synthesis **supports** this tension: it identifies 28,017 genes as both costly and conserved and 5,526 as costly and dispensable, but explicitly treats these categories as selection signatures rather than direct natural-fitness measurements. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

The two conservation analyses should also not be conflated: one reports 82% core for essential genes versus 66% for always-neutral genes across 43 bacteria, whereas the new 33-organism linkage reports 86.1% versus 81.2% for essential versus non-essential genes. [src: fitness_effects_conservation, conservation_vs_fitness] These results are directionally consistent but differ in category definitions and organism sets; neither resolves the poor binary agreement between insertion and complete-knockout assays. [src: fitness_effects_conservation, conservation_vs_fitness]

## Open Directions

- Perform condition-matched RB-TnSeq and complete-knockout experiments, then test whether discordance remains after aligning media and growth conditions. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]
- Analyze insertion positions, gene domains, transcriptional polarity, and truncated-protein potential for the 211 knockout-essential/RB-TnSeq-dispensable genes to test the proposed partial-function mechanisms. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]
- Compare continuous fitness, essentiality fraction, knockout calls, and growth rates with a preregistered threshold-selection and multiple-testing procedure to determine which representation generalizes across conditions. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]
- Fit combined FBA-plus-fitness-plus-proteomics models and evaluate whether multi-omic predictors improve knockout-essentiality classification over any single assay. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]
- Stratify the condition-matched comparison by core, auxiliary, and singleton status, while testing insertion coverage, to determine whether the weak conservation gradient reflects biological buffering or measurement bias. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- Test the 28,017 Costly + Conserved genes in ecologically matched environments and with complete knockouts to distinguish natural selection for conditionally valuable functions from assay-specific burden. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]
- Test whether the 1,116 conserved fitness modules remain coordinated in condition-matched complete-knockout experiments, distinguishing module-level biology from insertion-specific effects. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]
- Extend the 33-organism linkage from binary essentiality to fitness < -2 under stress conditions, and test whether conditionally important genes have distinct core, auxiliary, and unmapped conservation patterns. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]
- Reanalyze the 3,683 essential-auxiliary genes with ortholog data and complete knockouts to determine whether they compensate for missing core functions or reflect insertion-specific measurement artifacts. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]
