# Horizontal gene transfer and mobile elements as a source of novel and dark genes

This page asks whether novel, singleton and unannotated ("dark") genes come from mobile elements and horizontally acquired defense modules. The evidence is proxy-based: COG (clusters of orthologous groups) categories, GC deviation, mobile-element proximity and dark islands. It is suggestive and convergent, but it does not prove that any gene was horizontally transferred. [src: cog_analysis, truly_dark_genes]

## Literature Context

The one candidate paper is a genome-mining study of 16 bacterial isolates from hypersaline, desert and petroleum-contaminated soils in Iran. It identified 229 biosynthetic gene clusters (BGCs), and 56.8% of them showed no significant similarity to known clusters [PMID 41578173](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41578173/). The same work reported six novel, high-quality prophages that are largely uncharacterized in public databases. These prophages carried a putative bacteriocin cluster (UviB), which the authors read as a direct role in host fitness [PMID 41578173](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41578173/). The genomes also held diverse defense systems, including abundant CRISPR-Cas arrays. The prophages encoded anti-CRISPR proteins, and BGCs, prophages and defense systems co-localized into genomic islands [PMID 41578173](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41578173/). The paper is an abstract-level genome-mining survey. It does not test horizontal transfer directly, and it does not examine dark genes or COG categories.

The findings are loosely consistent with the hub's proxy-based reading, and the match is partial. The hub reports that novel and singleton genes are enriched for COG L (mobile elements) and V (defense mechanisms). It also reports that truly dark genes sit near mobile elements, forming "dark islands", and it hypothesizes that some islands are prophage remnants and others recently acquired metabolic cassettes. The candidate study shows that prophages, defense systems and secondary-metabolism clusters can cluster together in islands in extremophile genomes [PMID 41578173](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41578173/). That is an analogy to the hub's "mobile defense islands" idea, which rests on the composite LV assignments (mobile plus defense) with 76% consistency. The paper does not analyze COG composite assignments. It therefore neither supports nor undermines the hub's claim that composite codes are not annotation artifacts.

The paper's finding that many clusters and prophages are uncharacterized is compatible with the hub's concern that mobile-associated content is poorly annotated. The hub notes, for example, that costly+dispensable genes have SEED coverage of 50.8% and KEGG coverage of 20.0%. It is only a hypothesis that such island-borne, poorly characterized content contributes to dark-gene neighbourhoods, because the abstract identifies no dark genes. The paper's prophage-encoded bacteriocin cluster fits the hub's suggestion that mobile-element proximity relates to transferred genes with community-relevant functions such as bacteriocins [PMID 41578173](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41578173/). It does so as a single example from 16 isolates, not a test.

Overall, the candidate extends the hub's context by suggesting that defense-metabolic islands exist in extremophiles. It does not contradict the hub's claims, and it does not resolve the hub's tension between strong L and V enrichment and the small mobile tier (6.5% of dark-gene OGs, 1.9% of dark genes). The hub's own conclusions remain proxy evidence, and none of them proves transfer. See also [[concepts/reannotation-lag-versus-genuinely-unannotatable-dark-genes]].

## Composite and category enrichment in novel genes

In the bacterial pangenome analysis, novel/singleton genes are enriched for COG L (mobile elements) by +10.88%, with 100% consistency across species, which the report calls the strongest signal. [src: cog_analysis, discoveries] They are also enriched for V (defense mechanisms) by +2.83%, with 100% consistency. [src: cog_analysis, discoveries] The report reads the L enrichment as suggesting that most genomic novelty comes from mobile elements. [src: cog_analysis, discoveries] It also states that horizontal gene transfer (HGT), not vertical inheritance, is the primary innovation mechanism. [src: cog_analysis, discoveries] It links this to Koonin & Wolf (2008) and Treangen & Rocha (2011), which it describes as showing HGT as the primary source of prokaryotic gene novelty. [src: cog_analysis] The HGT conclusion is an interpretation of category enrichment, not a direct measurement of transfer. See also [[entities/cog-category-l-replication-recombination-and-repair]].

Composite COG codes give a further hint. "LV" means mobile plus defense, and "EGP" means amino acid plus carbohydrate plus inorganic ion functions. [src: cog_analysis] LV shows +0.34% enrichment and 76% consistency. [src: cog_analysis] The report says composite assignments are not annotation artifacts, should not be filtered as noise, and suggest functional modules such as "mobile defense islands". [src: cog_analysis]

## Costly, dispensable genes

Costly+dispensable genes show a similar pattern. The report concludes they are almost exclusively mobile-element debris rather than degrading metabolic pathways, and suggests HGT-mediated genome expansion as the main source of costly non-conserved genes. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] Singletons make up 24.2% of them, and no costly+conserved genes are singletons. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] This contrast is partly structural, because core genes cannot be singletons by definition. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] These genes are poorly annotated. SEED coverage is 50.8% versus 74.9% in costly+conserved genes, and KEGG coverage is 20.0% versus 42.7%. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] Orphan genes with no ortholog group are 44.5% of costly+dispensable genes versus 13.1% of costly+conserved genes. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] See mobile element origin of costly non conserved genes.

## Dark genes: composition, proximity and islands

Truly dark genes carry several signatures consistent with recent HGT. Their GC deviation from the host genome mean is higher than that of annotation-lag genes (mean |ΔGC| = 0.047 vs 0.038, d = 0.247, p = 1.3e-43). [src: truly_dark_genes, discoveries] Strong GC deviation (|z| > 2) affects 9.2% of truly dark genes versus 4.0% of annotation-lag genes. [src: truly_dark_genes] The report describes these properties as consistent with recent HGT outpacing annotation databases. [src: discoveries]

Among truly dark genes, 12.0% lie within 2 genes of a mobile element such as a transposase, integrase or phage protein. [src: truly_dark_genes, discoveries] In the 4,394 truly dark genes of ICA (independent component analysis) organisms, 12.0% are near a mobile element, and 41% of neighbouring genes are also hypothetical, forming "dark islands". [src: truly_dark_genes, discoveries] The report suggests dark genes concentrate in recently acquired genomic islands rather than being scattered at random. [src: discoveries]

The report hypothesizes that dark islands include recently acquired genomic islands, phage-derived regions supported by integrase and phage-protein proximity, and species-specific neighbourhoods that arose after the last common ancestor shared with well-studied organisms. [src: truly_dark_genes] It proposes that some islands are prophage remnants and others recently acquired metabolic cassettes. [src: truly_dark_genes] It links mobile-element proximity to the tendency of transferred genes to encode community-relevant functions such as antibiotic resistance, bacteriocins and specialized metabolites. [src: truly_dark_genes] It says the GC and proximity signatures agree with Daubin et al. (2003) and others, who showed enrichment of transferred genes among hypothetical proteins. [src: truly_dark_genes] Taken together, the report reads shorter sequences, accessory-genome enrichment, higher GC deviation, mobile-element proximity and narrow taxonomic breadth as convergent evidence for recently acquired, rapidly evolving genes rather than annotation lag. [src: truly_dark_genes, discoveries] Related pages: [[concepts/reannotation-lag-versus-genuinely-unannotatable-dark-genes]], [[concepts/sequence-composition-and-conservation-of-unannotatable-genes]].

### Mobile elements in conservation tiers

Mobile elements, detected by phylogenetic patchiness (presence in distant phyla but few species per phylum), form a small share of dark-gene conservation tiers. [src: functional_dark_matter] Among 11,774 dark-gene root OGs (ortholog groups), the "mobile" tier is 6.5%, while kingdom is 55.9%. [src: functional_dark_matter] At gene level (57,011 dark genes including 19,014 Fitness Browser-only fallback assignments), mobile is 1.9% and kingdom 51.4%. [src: functional_dark_matter] So the mobile-patchiness signal covers only a minority of dark genes. See [[concepts/phylogenetic-breadth-and-pangenome-conservation-of-dark-genes]].

## Tensions

- The strong L and V enrichment among novel genes supports a mobile-element origin of novelty. [src: cog_analysis] Yet only 6.5% of dark-gene OGs, and 1.9% of dark genes, fall in the mobile tier. [src: functional_dark_matter] These measure different gene sets and are not directly comparable.
- GC deviation is used as an HGT signature. [src: truly_dark_genes] The report cautions it is imperfect, since it can reflect gene-specific composition biases such as membrane proteins, or amelioration over time. [src: truly_dark_genes]

## Open Directions

- Test proximity to mobile elements for the novel/singleton genes enriched in L and V, to see whether the category signal and the dark-gene neighbourhood signal pick out the same genes.
- Combine GC deviation, mobile-element proximity and phylogenetic patchiness per gene, controlling for membrane-protein composition, to reduce the proxy ambiguity.
- Characterize dark islands for prophage markers versus metabolic cassettes, and check whether island genes carry composite LV assignments.
- Compare mobile-element proximity in truly dark versus annotation-lag genes to separate novelty from lag.
