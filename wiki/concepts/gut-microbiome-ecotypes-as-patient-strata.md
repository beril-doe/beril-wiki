Community-state typing asks whether gut metagenomes fall into a small number of reproducible compositional states and — the translational question — whether those states are stable enough to serve as *patient strata* for targeted intervention. A K = 4 consensus on 8,489 CMD ([[entities/curatedmetagenomicdata]]) [[entities/metaphlan3]] samples — 5,333 HC (healthy controls) plus 3,156 IBD (inflammatory bowel disease)/other — yields four ecotypes that the digest reports as aligning with published gut-microbiome enterotype literature (Vandeputte 2017, Lloyd-Price 2019) and as cleanly separating disease from healthy [src: discoveries]. How much weight a chosen K can bear is the general problem treated in [[concepts/ecotype-clustering-validity]].

The consensus is not a single clustering. Training ran two independent methods over K ∈ {2..8}: LDA (latent Dirichlet allocation, a topic decomposition) on pseudo-counts and GMM (Gaussian mixture model) on CLR (centred log-ratio) + PCA-20, with their assignments combined. Per-method fit measures (LDA held-out perplexity, GMM BIC) decrease monotonically with K, so the discriminating signal was the cross-method ARI (adjusted Rand index, the agreement between two clusterings) between LDA and GMM, which has a local maximum at K = 4 (ARI = 0.131) and a second peak at K = 7 (0.140); a parsimony rule — smallest K within 0.02 ARI of the maximum — selects K = 4. Per-sample agreement between the two methods at K = 4 is 48.9 % [src: ibd_phage_targeting].

The four states differ in dominant taxa and in which diagnoses they collect. CD is Crohn's disease, UC ulcerative colitis, T1D and T2D type 1 and type 2 diabetes, CDI *Clostridioides difficile* infection, and nonIBD a non-IBD comparison group [src: discoveries].

| Ecotype | Samples | Dominant taxa | Diagnosis distribution | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E0 — Diverse commensal | 3,604 | *F. prausnitzii* / *R. bromii* / *B. uniformis* / *Bifidobacterium* balanced | 66.8 % of HC | [src: discoveries] |
| E1 — Bacteroides2 transitional | 2,601 | *P. vulgatus* / *B. uniformis* / *B. dorei* | 48 % CD, 58 % UC, 100 % T1D, 97 % T2D, 67 % nonIBD | [src: discoveries] |
| E2 — *Prevotella copri* enterotype | 920 | *P. copri* 28 % | 16.9 % of HC, ~0 % disease (non-Western healthy) | [src: discoveries] |
| E3 — Severe Bacteroides-expanded | 1,364 | *P. vulgatus* 14 % + *B. fragilis* 3.6 % | 50 % CD, 40 % UC, 67 % IBD acute flare, 38 % CDI | [src: discoveries] |

The stratification signal is that disease does *not* collapse onto one state: CD and UC patients distribute across E1 and E3 rather than concentrating in any single ecotype, which is what downstream within-ecotype analyses are meant to exploit, while healthy samples concentrate in E0 + E2 (84 % combined); the assignments are saved as the consumable artifact `data/ecotype_assignments.tsv` [src: discoveries]. The co-occurrence of the Bacteroides2 state with metabolic disease (T1D, T2D) *and* IBD is reported as an echo of the broader "low-diversity disease state" hypothesis, and so stands as a hypothesis rather than a demonstrated mechanism [src: discoveries].

The project report behind this digest entry is the same analysis at finer resolution, not an independent replication of it: the same four states and the same sample counts appear with per-taxon abundances — E0 diverse commensal, 3,604 samples, *F. prausnitzii* 6.8 %, *R. bromii* 4.5 %, *B. uniformis* 4.6 %, *P. vulgatus* 4.4 %, 66.8 % of HC; E1 Bacteroides2 transitional, 2,601 samples, *P. vulgatus* 9.8 %, *B. uniformis* 7.2 %, *Phocaeicola dorei* 3.5 %, 48 % CD, 58 % UC, 100 % T1D, 97 % T2D, 67 % nonIBD; E2 *prevotella copri* enterotype, 920 samples, *P. copri* 28 %, *F. prausnitzii* 6 %, 16.9 % HC, ~0 % disease (non-Western healthy); E3 severe Bacteroides-expanded, 1,364 samples, *P. vulgatus* 14.2 %, *B. fragilis* 3.6 %, 50 % CD, 40 % UC, 67 % IBD acute, 38 % CDI, donor 2708 [src: ibd_phage_targeting].

The report treats "≥ 3 reproducible ecotypes" as **directional support** for its hypothesis H1a, and maps E0 / E1 / E2 / E3 recognizably onto the original Bacteroides / Prevotella / Ruminococcus enterotype framework (Arumugam 2011, Costea 2018), with E1 / E3 read as the Bacteroides2 (Bact2) low-cell-count dysbiosis signature documented in CD by Vandeputte et al. 2017 [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. That **refines** the strata from bare statistical clusters into states with a prior literature identity, but it does not establish robust reproducibility: the same report records per-sample cross-method agreement of only 48.9 % at K = 4 and real cross-study variance in the ecotype framework itself, with a leave-one-study-out ARI of 0.113 [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. Resemblance to published enterotypes and a large training sample therefore **weaken** rather than settle the "reproducible" label — a large n does not make a 48.9 %-agreement partition stable [src: ibd_phage_targeting].

Transfer to clinical material was tested on the UC Davis Kuehl_WGS material: all 26 samples (23 unique patients) were projected onto the K = 4 reference via a synonymy layer, with 262 unique [[entities/kaiju]]-classified species normalized to 97 canonical species in the training feature space [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. The cohort percentages below are therefore fractions of 26 *samples*, not of 23 patients, and the sample set includes repeat sequencing of the same individuals [src: ibd_phage_targeting].

The projection also exposes an asymmetry between the two consensus methods that qualifies the transfer. Projecting Kaiju data onto a MetaPhlAn3-trained embedding leaves 54 % of Kuehl feature rows outside the training feature space; LDA on pseudo-counts tolerates this by treating absence as not-detected, whereas GMM on CLR + PCA forces all 26 Kuehl samples into a single Gaussian (E3) at confidence > 0.97 — an artifact, not biology — so LDA supplies the primary projection calls and GMM is advisory [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. Only one of the two methods that defined the strata survives the cross-classifier move [src: ibd_phage_targeting], which is the portability problem examined in [[concepts/cross-cohort-microbiome-portability]] and [[concepts/classifier-database-compatibility-in-taxonomic-quantification]].

- **E0, 7 samples (27 % of UC Davis)**: minimal pathobiont burden — the report judges that it possibly does not need the cocktail [src: ibd_phage_targeting]
- **E1, 11 samples (42 % of UC Davis)**: the dominant CD ecotype, for which the full Tier-1 cocktail ([[entities/escherichia-coli]] AIEC — adherent-invasive *E. coli* — plus Tier-2 [[entities/enterocloster-bolteae]] / [[entities/eggerthella-lenta]] and bile-acid monitoring) is called appropriate [src: ibd_phage_targeting]
- **E3, 8 samples (31 % of UC Davis)**: severe Bacteroides-expanded, with *E. coli* and *M. gnavus* ([[entities/mediterraneibacter-gnavus]]) prominent, so cocktail plus ecological-cost annotation is more critical given the higher pathobiont burden [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

The distribution across the four states is non-random: χ²(3) versus uniform = 10.0, p = 0.019, computed over those 26 samples. UC Davis looks Western — no E2 *Prevotella*-dominant patients — with active disease dominating (73 % E1 or E3), which the report records as **directional support** for H1b, that patients distribute across multiple ecotypes rather than concentrating in one, validating the stratified-targeting premise [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. The per-stratum cost of acting on such a call is treated in [[concepts/ecological-cost-of-microbiome-target-depletion]] and [[concepts/phage-therapy-evidence-translation]].

Repeat samples bear on stability, but most of them test the assay rather than the patient: 1112 → E3 at both timepoints; `p1` / `p1reseq` → E3 both; `p2` → E1 both; 1460 (calprotectin 7,280 μg/g) → E1; and patient 6967 flips E1 ↔ E3 between two samples, described as the first direct observation of intra-patient ecosystem instability and flagged as relevant to dosing-schedule implications [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. The 1112 pair is a technical replicate (Spearman ρ = 1.000 on Tier-A, taken as evidence that Kaiju is reliable across reseq replicates), and 6967 is the only multi-timepoint UC Davis patient with biological-replicate samples [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. The stable repeats are therefore technical reproducibility, not demonstrated biological stability, and the one patient sampled biologically twice changed state — which **weakens** any reading of ecotype as a fixed patient attribute and makes a stratum assignment a time-stamped measurement rather than a baseline label [src: ibd_phage_targeting].

Evidence grade. The four-state partition rests on a large cross-study sample and is reported as a consensus [src: discoveries], but its internal reproducibility is weak on the report's own measures (48.9 % cross-method agreement, leave-one-study-out ARI 0.113) [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. The clinical application rests on 26 samples from 23 patients, one usable biological time course, a single p = 0.019 χ² against uniform, and two hypotheses labelled only *directionally supported*, so community-state typing as a patient-stratification device should be read as a working hypothesis, not an established finding [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. Whether a cheap assay could assign a patient to a stratum at the bedside is the separate question tracked in [[concepts/proxy-assays-for-community-state-assignment]].

## Open Directions

- Settle whether K = 4 states are reproducible at all before using them as strata: re-select K with resampling and report cross-method agreement and leave-one-study-out ARI per candidate K, since the chosen K = 4 rests on a cross-method ARI of 0.131 against a 0.140 peak at K = 7 and a 48.9 % per-sample agreement [src: ibd_phage_targeting].
- Quantify intra-patient state stability with a cohort designed for it: 6967 is the only UC Davis patient with biological multi-timepoint samples, so the single observed E1 ↔ E3 flip cannot bound a per-patient switching rate or a dosing interval [src: ibd_phage_targeting].
- Re-project the clinical cohort with a MetaPhlAn3-based profile of the same samples, which would test whether the E0/E1/E3 split is a property of the communities or of the Kaiju-to-MetaPhlAn3 feature mapping that already collapses the GMM arm onto E3 at confidence > 0.97 [src: ibd_phage_targeting].
- Join ecotype assignments from `data/ecotype_assignments.tsv` to available inflammatory markers to test whether E1 versus E3 membership predicts anything clinical beyond composition — for instance whether the E1 sample with calprotectin 7,280 μg/g is typical of E1 or an outlier [src: discoveries, ibd_phage_targeting].
- Probe the absence of E2 as a portability limit: E2 holds 16.9 % of healthy controls and ~0 % disease in the cross-study consensus, and 0 of the 26 UC Davis samples fall in it, so this cohort cannot test whether a *P. copri*-dominant patient would need a different cocktail at all [src: discoveries, ibd_phage_targeting].
- Check whether the Bacteroides2-with-metabolic-disease pattern (100 % T1D, 97 % T2D in E1) survives per-study stratification, since those diagnosis fractions could reflect single contributing cohorts rather than a cross-population association [src: discoveries].
