# Environmental occurrence does not establish compound catabolism

Detection of an organism, genus, or community in an environment demonstrates occurrence or abundance, but does not establish that the organism degrades a target compound there. The [[summaries/enigma_carbon_census_1__REPORT]] provides a direct example: its environmental atlas measured organismal abundance and occurrence, whereas compound catabolic activity was not measured. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The [[summaries/pseudomonas_carbon_ecology__REPORT]] **supports and refines** this distinction: standardized GapMind predictions across 12,732 *Pseudomonas* genomes identified pathway-completeness patterns and modest environment-associated signal, but did not measure substrate uptake, pathway expression, growth, or carbon flux. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] The [[summaries/essential_metabolome__REPORT]] **supports** the same boundary: GapMind classified 17 of 18 amino-acid biosynthesis pathways as complete in all 7 successfully mapped organisms, but these computational pathway calls did not establish pathway activity, substrate uptake, or growth on defined media. [src: essential_metabolome]

The [[summaries/metabolic_capability_dependency__REPORT]] **supports and sharpens** this distinction by directly comparing complete genomic pathways with experimentally measured gene-fitness dependence across 1,695 pathway-organism pairs from 48 organisms. Among complete pathways, 267 (15.8%) were classified as latent capabilities, with mean absolute fitness t-score below 1.0 and fewer than 5% essential genes, showing that encoded completeness can coexist with fitness neutrality under tested laboratory conditions. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] Carbon-source utilization pathways were especially often latent: 217 of 892 (24.3%), compared with 48 of 735 (6.5%) amino-acid biosynthesis pathways. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] Thus, even a capability prediction paired with fitness data measures condition-specific dependency rather than proving that a detected environmental taxon catabolized a compound in situ. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]

The [[summaries/nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology__REPORT]] **supports** the same evidence boundary while adding a community-scale metabolomics comparison: GapMind pathway completeness was associated with ambient metabolite intensity for some amino-acid pathways, including leucine (r = −0.390, q = 0.022, n = 62) and arginine (r = −0.297, q = 0.049, n = 80), but GapMind measured genomic potential rather than expression or realized catabolic flux. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] Thus, even a pathway–metabolite association is evidence about community potential and metabolite pools, not proof that detected taxa catabolized a particular compound in situ. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

## Evidence from the ENIGMA Carbon Census

The global environmental atlas covered 86 implicated genera using 3825 taxonomy-bearing NMDC metagenomes and 302 Planet Microbe marine runs. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] In NMDC, 83/86 genera were detected in 1719 metagenomes, and 99% of samples were labeled through two independent ontology systems. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The macro-environment counts were soil 2260, freshwater 723, periphyton 472, plant 119, and sediment 42. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

Alongside the global atlas, the project reframed one deliverable as a straight SSO field-occurrence atlas (Zhou Lab 16S, `enigma_coral` bricks) rather than fabricate a confounded result: 62 of the implicated utilizer genera are observed in the SSO field at genus resolution, with top field prevalences ~0.7–0.9 for the aromatic utilizers (e.g. 3-hydroxybenzoic-acid utilizers at 0.90 field prevalence). [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

Periphyton revealed a strong Burkholderiales/Comamonadaceae occurrence reservoir: listed genera reached approximately 96–97% prevalence with mean relative abundance approximately 0.005–0.009. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Label-free abundance outliers included Nocardioides at 0.43 in epipsammon, Hydrogenophaga at 0.28 in epiphyton, and Mycobacterium at 0.22 in soil; outliers occurred for 34 genera in periphyton and 32 in soil. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] These measurements identify environments where candidate organisms occur, but they do not show that those organisms consumed any of the 83 enrichment compounds. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The reservoir is partly a labeling result: a periphyton (freshwater-biofilm) ENVO/GOLD class separated out from bulk freshwater is what surfaced this Comamonadaceae/Burkholderiales reservoir at ~97% prevalence, which bulk-water labels hide. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The report also marks the standing of that claim: the freshwater-biofilm/periphyton enrichment of the clade is reported primarily on the project's own NB07b abundance data (~97% prevalence in periphyton samples) rather than a single comparative-microbiology citation, and should be read as this project's observation pending a targeted literature anchor. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The marine arm similarly provided an occurrence contrast rather than a catabolism assay: all 68/68 listed genera showed positive abundance across 302 Planet Microbe runs, while terrestrial/freshwater genera generally occurred at approximately 1e-3 to 1e-4 abundance in open ocean. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Alteromonas was a genuine marine member, occurring at 0.048 in 240/302 runs with prevalence 0.79; Pseudomonas and Sphingomonas had abundances 0.011 and 0.0063, respectively. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] These values support biome occupancy comparisons, not claims of compound transformation. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The marine arm is additionally small and gene-blind — 302 Planet Microbe runs carrying presence/abundance only — and the project judges it suitable only as a negative biome contrast. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The soil-versus-freshwater abundance contrast, which the report labels exploratory, recovered textbook biogeography: soil-enriched genera were classic soil taxa (Mycobacterium log2 +5.09, Terriglobus +5.29, Nitrobacter +4.79, Afipia, Streptomyces, Nocardioides, Mesorhizobium) and freshwater-enriched genera were aquatic Betaproteobacteria (Cellvibrio log2 −2.19, Curvibacter, Rhodoferax, Comamonas, Acidovorax); recovering known biogeography from occurrence data is not evidence that any of these taxa processed a census compound. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

## Why the evidence types must remain separate

The census connected environmental occurrence with higher-tier evidence, including ENIGMA-isolate utilizer predictions and GTDB strain placement, but those evidence streams did not convert genus-level environmental detection into an activity measurement. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Deliverable (a) contained 569 ENIGMA-isolate utilizer prediction rows across 8 ENIGMA-isolate-callable compounds, while deliverable (b) placed 494 strain records representing 359 distinct strains on GTDB taxonomy. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] These predictions and placements identify genetic or isolate-level utilization potential; the environmental atlas identifies where implicated genera were detected. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The *Pseudomonas* analysis **supports** the same evidence boundary from a larger comparative-genomics perspective. It found significant differences in completeness for 43 of 62 GapMind carbon pathways between *Pseudomonas* s.s. and *Pseudomonas_E*, and a permutation test associated carbon profiles with isolation environment at p = 0.006; however, these were genome-derived capability and association results rather than observations of compound catabolism in environmental samples. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] Its Random Forest environment classifier achieved balanced accuracy of 0.408 +/- 0.169 against a 0.250 chance baseline, **refining** the interpretation: carbon capability profiles can contain ecological information without being sufficient to identify fine-grained environments or establish realized activity. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

The metabolic-capability study **refines** this evidence hierarchy by showing that complete pathways can be latent, intermediate, or active under laboratory fitness assays: 267 (15.8%), 547 (32.3%), and 881 (51.9%) of 1,695 complete pathway-organism pairs fell into those classes. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] The latent fraction ranged from 4.7% to 21.1% across tested thresholds, and the report notes that laboratory media may miss environmental dependencies. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] This strengthens the conclusion that genomic completeness is neither a direct activity measurement nor a universal measure of ecological importance; only 48 organisms were fitness-tested among 293,000 genomes with pathway predictions. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]

The essential-metabolome pilot **refines** this evidence hierarchy by showing that even apparently conserved biosynthetic capacity remains a computational inference. Among 7 organisms mapped from an underlying 45-organism essential-gene collection, 17 of 18 amino-acid pathways were complete in all 7, while *Desulfovibrio vulgaris* lacked a complete serine pathway under the report's GapMind criterion. [src: essential_metabolome] The result supports pathway-capacity comparisons, but the report explicitly treats possible serine auxotrophy as a hypothesis because divergent or unannotated pathways may be missed and no growth test was performed. [src: essential_metabolome]

The NMDC integration **refines** this distinction by connecting community-weighted pathway completeness to measured metabolites across 220 samples and 80 pathways. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] Across 13 testable amino-acid pathways, 11/13 (85%) correlations with metabolite intensity were negative, with a sign-test p = 0.011; however, the predictor was the fraction of taxa with a complete GapMind pathway, and GapMind does not show that the pathway was expressed or that a target compound was consumed. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] The result therefore supports a weak community-scale Black Queen Hypothesis signal while remaining intermediate between occurrence and direct catabolism evidence. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

The distinction is especially important because only 9 of 83 compounds were callable under the project's definition, and the effective carbon-callable set was 8 after xanthine was removed as a carbon-catabolic call. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The remaining 74/83 compounds were organism-dark within the queried the KBase Data Lakehouse and curated resources, meaning that environmental detection of possible hosts cannot by itself resolve their missing catabolic determinants. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] This supports [[concepts/cross-tenant-data-bridging]]: potential inferred from genomes or isolate predictions should not be presented as realized activity in environmental samples. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The metabolic-capability study also **supports** the annotation caveat behind this boundary: its pathway-fitness mapping used SEED subsystem annotations as a proxy for GapMind membership, which could introduce false positives, and it recommends direct GapMind per-step gene assignments for greater precision. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]

The census states the limit of its own inference chain directly: field occurrence does not validate the upstream compound-to-pathway-to-organism inference, and the project cannot claim that any genus degrades a census compound in any sampled environment. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

## Production, pools, and flux are different measurements

The [[summaries/fw300_metabolic_consistency__REPORT]] **sharpens** this boundary inside a single organism by separating production evidence from utilization evidence. FW300-N2E3 increases tryptophan in its exometabolome (Web of Microbes (WoM) action = Increased), has 231 genes with significant fitness when grown on tryptophan in the Fitness Browser, and GapMind predicts a complete tryptophan biosynthesis pathway; yet 0 out of 50 *P. fluorescens* strains in BacDive can utilize tryptophan as a carbon source (n=50, pct_positive=0.0, per-strain consensus). The report calls this the most robust discordance in the dataset — the organism produces tryptophan and grows on it as a sole carbon source, while the species as a whole cannot catabolize it — and reads overflow metabolism serving a cross-feeding or signaling function as an interpretation rather than a measured function. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]

The grading matters for how much weight the discordance carries: tryptophan is recorded as Increased in WoM against BacDive utilization of 0+/50-, which the report grades High confidence (n=50) and labels a robust overflow/cross-feeding signal. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]

The methodological point generalizes: exometabolomic production and growth-assay utilization test fundamentally different capabilities. A bacterium can release a metabolite via overflow metabolism, biosynthetic byproduct release, or active secretion for ecological purposes (signaling, cross-feeding, antimicrobial) without being able to catabolize it for energy, so production without catabolism need not be a direct contradiction. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]

The same report's trehalose case shows the explanatory gap being filled by hypothesis. Trehalose production by FW300-N2E3 (WoM: Increased) coexists with low species-level catabolism (1/6 BacDive strains positive, per-strain consensus), and the proposed explanation — trehalose as an osmoprotectant secreted during growth on R2A medium as a constitutive stress-protection strategy — rests on contextual studies of osmotic-stress upregulation in *Pseudomonas protegens* and ethanol-stimulated production in *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* rather than a direct mechanism test in FW300-N2E3. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]

The cross-project digest [[summaries/discoveries]] states the general rule that keeps occurrence, pools, and activity non-substitutable: a measured metabolite pool reflects the difference between production and consumption rates, plus dietary/host inputs, so pool measurements and flux measurements are not interchangeable and both belong in the analysis when both are available. [src: discoveries]

Sometimes the missing activity measurement is absent by construction. The 2018 Web of Microbes snapshot (see [[summaries/webofmicrobes_explorer__REPORT]]) uses a 4-action encoding in which organism actions are 'E' (Emerged — absent from the control medium and now detected, i.e. de novo biosynthesis) and 'I' (Increased — present in the control and raised), mutually exclusive with 0 overlap across 10,744 observations, while the control carries 'D' (Detected in medium) and 'N' (Not detected); there is no Decreased (consumption) action for any organism and all 742 'D' observations belong to the control, so the database records only production, not consumption. [src: discoveries, webofmicrobes_explorer] The original Kosina et al. (2018) paper describes "decrease" as a valid action, so consumption data may exist in the newer GNPS2 version, but that is unverified here. [src: webofmicrobes_explorer]

[[summaries/pitfalls]] records this gap as a property of the export rather than a query error: the 2018 snapshot has no Decreased/consumption action by design, so consumption cannot be inferred from its action codes. [src: pitfalls]

The consequence is a ceiling on the questions the resource can answer: without consumption observations, whether consumed metabolites predict gene essentiality cannot be tested, and the data are small (37 organisms, 5 projects) and from a single laboratory. [src: webofmicrobes_explorer]

## Pool-level evidence in a human cohort

The [[summaries/ibd_phage_targeting__REPORT]] **supports** the pool-versus-flux separation with a direction reversal inside one project: the `06_polyamine_urea` theme was CD-DOWN at pathway level (OR=0.42) while polyamines as a metabolite class were CD-UP at OR=14.6, the largest theme-level effect in its metabolomics analysis. The report's parenthetical label "biosynthesis flux" for the pathway-level quantity should not be read as a measurement: no flux was measured, and the report elsewhere describes the same pathway-level signal as production capacity. It treats both readouts as correct rather than averaging them, because production, consumption, diet, and host inputs can decouple a metabolite pool from biosynthetic flux; pool and flux measurements are not interchangeable. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

At metabolite level, 5 of 9 polyamines were CD-up — putrescine (cliff=+0.45, FDR=0.08), N1-acetylspermine (+0.56, 0.06), N-acetylputrescine (+0.46, 0.06), anserine (+0.43, 0.08) and diacetylspermine (+0.43, 0.08), where FDR is the false-discovery-rate-adjusted significance — and the report's reading that pool accumulation can follow increased catabolism of dietary protein and mucin plus reduced microbial polyamine clearance, without elevated biosynthesis flux, is a proposed mechanism and not a measured flux result. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

A parallel mismatch appears for lipids: 6 of 15 long-chain polyunsaturated fatty acids were CD-up — adrenate (C22:4, +0.55, FDR=0.027), arachidonate (C20:4, +0.48, 0.05), docosapentaenoate (C22:5, +0.45, 0.06), docosahexaenoate (C22:6, +0.41, 0.08), eicosapentaenoate (C20:5, +0.40, 0.09) — and this metabolite-pool elevation was theme-significant, whereas the related fat-metabolism/glyoxylate theme was sub-threshold and not Fisher-significant at pathway level (OR=0.88). Impaired host fatty-acid uptake or beta-oxidation, increased dietary fat mobilization, and reduced microbial PUFA biohydrogenation are offered as alternative explanations rather than established causes, and the mechanistic connection drawn between the pathway theme and the metabolite pool is an interpretation. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

Bile acids show the inference being pushed a step further. Only 3 of 21 bile-acid-theme metabolites were CD-up — free taurine (cliff=+0.47) and tauro-alpha-muricholate and tauro-beta-muricholate (+0.40) — which the report interprets as consistent with reduced microbial bile-acid 7alpha-dehydroxylation, although free taurine is the conjugating amino acid rather than a conjugated bile acid itself. The report elsewhere states that tauro-muricholate confirms an *F. plautii* 7alpha-dehydroxylation deficit; read against this page's boundary, that is an interpretation of a pool measurement, not a measured enzymatic rate. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

Its most activity-directed evidence is a paired sample-level correlation: 468 paired CSM* HMP2 samples x 8 species x 583 metabolites by Spearman rho, in which *Flavonifractor plautii*, *Eggerthella lenta* and *Enterocloster bolteae* show the predicted substrate-product signature (negative with primary tauro-conjugated bile acids, positive with secondary bile acids) while *Mediterraneibacter gnavus* and *E. coli* show the opposite pattern, and *E. coli* dominates cohort-level correlations (cadaverine +0.45, choline +0.25, tryptophan +0.25). The report calls this the direct sample-level confirmation of the bile-acid 7alpha-dehydroxylation network operating in HMP2 samples; it is correlational evidence rather than an intervention, so it narrows but does not close the gap between occurrence and activity. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

Its ranked pathway associations mix measurement with annotation in the same way: glyoxylate bypass (rho_meta 0.797), PWY-6803 phosphatidylcholine acyl editing (0.725), PWY-7385 1,3-propanediol biosynthesis (0.707), allantoin degradation to glyoxylate (0.685), 2-methylcitrate cycle I (0.683), heme biosynthesis from glycine (0.640) and L-arginine degradation II (AST pathway, 0.617) are correlation coefficients, listed in the source table alongside a separate rank column, whereas the adherent-invasive *E. coli* mechanistic context attached to each row is interpretation. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

*Hungatella hathewayi* shows TMA/choline theme enrichment (OR=9.33, FDR=0.048), mechanistically consistent with the CutC glycyl radical enzyme that breaks down choline to trimethylamine (TMA) in human gut microbes, with the host converting TMA to TMAO. The further description of *H. hathewayi* as functionally a TMA-producer pathobiont in CD samples is inferred from pathway findings rather than from a direct measurement of its TMA production. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

The matching metabolite evidence is weaker than the pathway signal: TMAO was not theme-significant, and the TMA/choline mechanism is recorded as only partially corroborated, via CD-up C16/C18:1 carnitines. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

Urobilin runs the other way and replicates: it was CD-down (cliff=-0.38, FDR=0.09), interpreted as reduced gut-bacterial bilirubin reduction, and CD-down in 3/3 cross-cohort matches with a median Franzosa cliff of −0.747, the largest cross-cohort effect size in the panel and the project's strongest replicated metabolite-level finding. Replication fixes the direction of a pool-level difference; the attributed loss of bilirubin-reducing commensals remains an interpretation. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

## Enrichment response is not catabolic proof

The [[summaries/lignin_community_enrichment__REPORT]] **supports** the boundary from the enrichment side: a compound-amended passage series measures which taxa increase, not which taxa degrade the compound. Pseudomonas was absent in base (<0.1%) and became dominant in all enriched conditions (23–53%), a genus the report associates with the beta-ketoadipate pathway and protocatechuate catabolism. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Comamonas was low in base (0.1%) and enriched 17–51x in Round 2 groups (3.6–5.1%), with aromatic-compound including phenylpropanoid degradation noted. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Aminobacter, noted for aromatic amine metabolism, was absent in base and Round 1 and appeared in the Round 2 L-L (7.6%) and LC-L (6.4%) groups, which does not establish its absence from other Round 2 groups. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Rhodococcus, which the report calls a well-characterized lignin degrader, was present only at low levels across conditions (0.03–0.67%) and was highest in lignin-only (L, 0.67%). [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

Flavobacterium shows how far abundance data get pushed toward function: negligible in base (0.1%), it became a major component under lignin enrichment (14.1% in the L group; CLR — centered log-ratio — effect +11.2 in Base vs L) and fell to 1.4% under labile carbon co-supplementation (LC), which the report reads as suggesting a specialist selected specifically by lignin-derived aromatics rather than a generalist copiotrophic responder — a tentative reading leaning on external PAH-degradation literature (Lyu et al. 2014, PMID: 24957593) rather than a degradation assay here. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

Two of its interpretive anchors are external rather than internal: the dominance of Pseudomonas and Acinetobacter in lignin-enriched communities is related to a separate study of aromatic-degrading enzyme diversity across 26 soil bacterial genomes (Hossain et al. 2024), and the enrichment of Comamonas in later passages is compared with its identification as a major genus in Napier-grass lignocellulose-degrading consortia (Kanokratana et al. 2018, PMID: 29169786). [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

## Activity evidence imported from other studies

The [[summaries/clay_confined_subsurface__REPORT]] imports activity evidence in the same way: it attributes protein-level expression of the toolkit modules to Bagnoud (2016) and does not establish that those modules are expressed in the genomes analyzed there. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]

The [[summaries/harvard_forest_warming__REPORT]] reports pmoA/pmoB upregulation in both horizons, which it calls unexpected for an aerated temperate forest, and supports the interpretation with subtropical forest +4°C warming experiments in which net CH₄ uptake increased 12-61% across soil depths and the relative abundance of high-affinity USCα methanotrophs rose at 0-10 cm. [src: harvard_forest_warming] That precedent (Zhang L et al. 2024, *Sci. Total Environ.*, PMID:38561130) is cited as an external forest-warming methanotroph-activation anchor, not as independent evidence generated here, so the proposed warming-induced moisture decrease raising CH₄ availability remains interpretive rather than a flux measured at the site. [src: harvard_forest_warming]

The [[summaries/phb_granule_ecology__REPORT]] makes the inferential step explicit in its own design: per-sample polyhydroxybutyrate (PHB) capacity was computed as the abundance-weighted sum of genus-level phaC prevalence across 6,365 NMDC metagenomic samples, after a two-tier taxonomy mapping placed 3,014/3,492 (86.3%) NMDC taxon columns onto GTDB genera with known PHB status, with a median 87.2% of taxonomic abundance matched to pangenome genera — inference from taxonomy and gene presence, not a measurement of PHB. [src: phb_granule_ecology] Its abiotic associations are correspondingly modest (|rho| < 0.12), and the report notes that NMDC abiotic measurements are point-in-time snapshots rather than measures of temporal variability, so the depth and temperature readings are inferential. [src: phb_granule_ecology]

The [[summaries/cf_formulation_design__REPORT]] **refines** the boundary for a design application: it nominates sugar alcohols (xylitol, myoinositol) and pentoses (xylose, arabinose, fucose, rhamnose) as strong prebiotic candidates because they are genomically complete in its formulation species, absent from PA14's metabolic repertoire, actively transported by commensals in patient airways, commercially available and FDA-GRAS, and, for xylitol, already used in CF airway products for other indications. [src: cf_formulation_design] These are genomic and expression grounds rather than demonstrated selective growth, and the absence claim is specific to the PA14 strain: the 1% completeness the report tabulates for fucose and for rhamnose concerns the broader *P. aeruginosa* genome collection and does not contradict PA14-specific absence. [src: cf_formulation_design]

## Interpretation and scope

The environmental atlas supports prioritizing locations and taxa for enrichment, particularly periphyton sites associated with the observed Burkholderiales/Comamonadaceae reservoir. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] It does not establish substrate uptake, pathway expression, growth on the compound, transformation-product formation, or flux through a catabolic pathway because no environmental dataset measured the census compounds. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The project therefore reported an SSO field-occurrence atlas rather than a statistical groundwater-versus-necromass source contrast. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] H3 was untestable and confounded because only 2 of the 8 ENIGMA-isolate-callable compounds were necromass-sourced, and both were phthalate-class aromatics with Actinomycetota-heavy utilizers. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] This limitation connects to [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]] and [[concepts/environment-embedding-geography]]: environmental labels can structure where organisms are found, but they do not substitute for compound-resolved activity measurements. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The *Pseudomonas* results **refine** this scope rather than overturning it. The dominant PCA separation was between *Pseudomonas* subgenera, driven by sugar-pathway loss, while lifestyle categories substantially overlapped within *Pseudomonas_E*; the report also identifies phylogenetic confounding because species relatedness was not explicitly controlled. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] Thus, even a statistically detectable association between predicted carbon profiles and environment may reflect lineage structure, incomplete pathway resolution, or metadata limitations rather than in situ use of a particular compound. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

The NMDC result likewise does not overturn the boundary: the 220-sample ecosystem comparison found strong Soil–Freshwater separation in pathway completeness, but all 33 Freshwater samples lacked paired metabolomics, and usable pH, temperature, and total organic carbon measurements were absent. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] The observed pathway–metabolite relationships therefore cannot distinguish realized activity from genomic composition, abiotic gradients, or other study effects without expression and environmental controls. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

The environmental abundance analysis also requires caution in statistical interpretation. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Soil-versus-freshwater enrichment statistics treated each metagenome as independent despite compositional, zero-inflated relative abundances, and all 83 genera reached q<0.05 with many q values near 1e-70. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The project judged direction and rank more trustworthy than the p-values and considered label-free outlier discovery the more defensible signal. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] This reinforces [[concepts/sampling-depth-and-downsampling-effects]] by showing that occurrence-based associations require study-aware designs before being interpreted as ecological or metabolic causality.

## Tensions

A high environmental prevalence can support the practical hypothesis that a taxon is accessible for enrichment, but it cannot establish that the taxon catabolizes a particular compound in situ. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The census found 3-hydroxybenzoic-acid utilizers at 0.90 field prevalence at genus resolution while also explicitly classifying the environmental atlas as an abundance or occurrence proxy rather than evidence of compound degradation or activity. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The apparent tension is resolved by treating occurrence as site-selection evidence and catabolism as a separate claim requiring compound-resolved experiments or activity measurements. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The *Pseudomonas* report does not contradict this boundary: its environment-associated carbon profiles are pathway predictions from genomes, not environmental activity measurements. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] It does, however, show that capability profiles may provide a useful intermediate ecological signal, while the modest classifier performance and strong subgenus separation limit claims about compound-specific environmental catabolism. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

The essential-metabolome result also **supports rather than contradicts** the boundary: near-complete amino-acid pathway predictions across 7 organisms do not establish biosynthetic activity, and the apparent *D. vulgaris* serine auxotrophy remains unresolved without growth testing. [src: essential_metabolome] Its limited 7-organism pilot and missing *E. coli* GapMind coverage further caution against treating computational completeness as a universal or experimentally validated property. [src: essential_metabolome]

The NMDC report **supports** this interpretation rather than contradicting it: negative associations between community pathway completeness and ambient amino-acid intensity are compatible with community metabolic interdependence, but they do not directly demonstrate catabolic uptake, pathway expression, or carbon flux. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

The metabolic-capability study does not contradict the occurrence–activity boundary. Its latent-capability result was obtained from laboratory fitness conditions, and its ecotype associations were observational; all 10 target species formed metabolic clusters, but environment-cluster associations were significant only for *Salmonella enterica* and *Phenylobacterium* sp., with no explicit phylogenetic correction. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] These results **refine** the boundary by showing that genomic capability can carry ecological information while remaining insufficient to establish environmental catabolism. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]

One live disagreement concerns how much weight a correlation can carry. The [[summaries/ibd_phage_targeting__REPORT]] argues that its paired taxon–metabolite correlation is independent of and stronger than within-carrier pathway differential abundance, because pathway differential abundance confirms genomic capability whereas the paired correlation confirms active enzymatic in vivo activity. This page records the disagreement rather than resolving it: a correlation between a taxon's abundance and a metabolite pool is more than a capability call, but it does not establish enzymatic causation, and the report's own pool-versus-flux caveat applies to it. ## Open Directions

- Pair the 86 implicated genera and 83 census compounds with compound-resolved enrichment cultures, substrate depletion measurements, and transformation-product assays to test whether environmental occurrence predicts realized catabolism. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]
- Use metatranscriptomics or metaproteomics on periphyton and soil samples enriched for the observed genera to test whether candidate catabolic pathways are expressed in the presence of specific census compounds; the current atlas measured occurrence but no compound activity. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]
- Apply study-aware mixed models or sample-level permutations to the NMDC data to test whether environmental occurrence differences remain after accounting for sampling structure and compositional, zero-inflated abundances. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]
- Pair the NMDC pathway-completeness and metabolomics matrices with metatranscriptomics, pH, temperature, and total organic carbon to test whether the leucine and arginine associations persist after controlling for expression and abiotic gradients. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]
- Experimentally test the 29 fully orphan compounds in periphyton-sited enrichments to determine whether the observed Burkholderiales/Comamonadaceae reservoir contains unrecognized utilizers. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]
- Combine GTDB strain placement, pathway-completeness checks, and isotope tracing for selected callable compounds to distinguish genetic utilization potential from measured carbon incorporation. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]
- Extend the *Pseudomonas* capability analysis with aromatic-degradation modules and PGLS, then test prioritized predictions using compound-resolved enrichment or isotope tracing; this would separate lineage-associated pathway presence from realized environmental catabolism. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- Inspect lower-confidence serine GapMind predictions in *D. vulgaris*, review experimental literature, and test growth on serine-free minimal medium; this would distinguish a genuine biosynthetic gap from pathway-detection failure. [src: essential_metabolome]
- Map direct GapMind per-step gene assignments to the fitness data and test whether latent carbon pathways remain neutral under environmentally relevant media; this would separate proxy-annotation error from condition-specific metabolic capability. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]
- Inoculate the proposed aromatic-utilizer enrichments with freshwater-biofilm material, as the Comamonadaceae periphyton reservoir suggests, and measure substrate depletion; the reservoir currently motivates an experimental direction rather than demonstrating a catabolic link. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]
- Map FW300-N2E3's tryptophan fitness-important genes to specific GapMind pathway steps to determine whether they are biosynthetic, catabolic, or regulatory; this would distinguish overflow metabolism from active catabolism, which the project leaves unresolved. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]
- Pair metatranscriptomics with metabolomics for a subset of NMDC samples to compare expressed pathway completeness, genomic potential, and metabolite abundance, directly testing whether expression rather than gene presence drives the Black Queen Hypothesis signal. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]
- Predict functional profiles from the lignin-enrichment 16S data (PICRUSt2 or Tax4Fun) to test whether lignin catabolism pathways (beta-ketoadipate, protocatechuate) are enriched; until then the observed genus shifts remain abundance responses. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]
