# Pathway-completeness predictions gain support from matched growth phenotypes

[[concepts/computational-pathway-prediction-validation]] addresses when computational pathway-completeness predictions are supported by experimentally observed growth. In the FW300-N2E3 case, [[entities/gapmind]] pathway predictions were compared with [[entities/kescience-fitnessbrowser]] growth phenotypes and [[entities/web-of-microbes]] metabolite observations rather than treated as stand-alone evidence. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]

## Core Evidence

GapMind, a pathway-prediction resource that evaluates whether an organism has the genes needed for metabolic steps, predicted complete pathways for all 13 matched metabolites: lactate, valine, alanine, arginine, aspartate, glutamic acid, glycine, phenylalanine, proline, trehalose, tryptophan, malate, and lysine. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]

All 13 metabolites also showed growth in Fitness Browser experiments, producing 13/13 agreement between pathway-completeness predictions and experimental growth for FW300-N2E3. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]

This 13/13 match supports the use of pathway-completeness predictions as organism-level evidence for metabolic capability in this dataset. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] The evidence is supportive rather than definitive validation, because the comparison covers only 13 metabolites and the source report does not establish that pathway completeness predicts growth under every medium or environmental condition. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]

The cross-project digest records this same comparison and names the organism: all 13 Web of Microbes (WoM) exometabolomics-produced metabolites that could be mapped to GapMind pathways had “complete” pathway predictions, and all 13 also showed growth in Fitness Browser experiments for [[entities/pseudomonas-fw300-n2e3]], reported there as *Pseudomonas fluorescens* FW300-N2E3. [src: discoveries] The digest attributes the match in part to the matched metabolites being common amino acids and organic acids with well-characterized pathways, and files the entry under the same project, so it is a restatement of that project's result rather than an independent confirmation of it. [src: discoveries]

Within the matched subset, [[entities/malate]] is the case with agreement across all four evidence types: Increased in WoM, growth (92 genes), utilized in BacDive (49/49, 100%), and a complete pathway. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]

- [[entities/arginine]] agrees across all four evidence types: Increased in WoM, growth (270 genes), utilized in BacDive (40/48, 83%), and a complete pathway. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]
- [[entities/valine]] likewise agrees across all four: Emerged in WoM, growth (160 genes), utilized in BacDive (1/1), and a complete pathway — though its utilization call rests on a single strain. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]

These predictions were not recomputed for the comparison: the `gapmind_pathways` table of the `kbase_ke_pangenome` collection supplied the pathway-completeness predictions, so the matched-growth check evaluates a pre-existing pangenome-level resource against organism-level phenotypes. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]

The report sets the 13/13 result beside published assessments rather than deriving it from them: it notes that Price et al. (2022) reported high accuracy for GapMind pathway predictions when validated against fitness data, which is an independent literature claim and not a second measurement of agreement in FW300-N2E3. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]

The report also attributes improved amino-acid biosynthesis annotations to GapMind 2024 (Price et al. 2024) and states that the pangenome-level predictions held in the KBase Data Lakehouse inherit this accuracy. Inherited accuracy is an extrapolation from the tool's version history, not a separate validation result for this corpus. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]

The essential-metabolome pilot **supports and bounds** this interpretation: 17 of 18 amino-acid biosynthesis pathways were complete in all 7 mapped organisms — arg, asn, chorismate, cys, gln, gly, his, ile, leu, lys, met, phe, pro, thr, trp, tyr, val — but *Desulfovibrio vulgaris*, an anaerobic sulfate-reducing bacterium, lacked a complete serine pathway under the report’s computational criterion, an apparent absence predicted computationally rather than a demonstrated auxotrophy. [src: essential_metabolome] This broader result supports pathway completeness as a useful capability-level signal, while its 7-organism sample and computational-only calls **refine** the 13/13 result’s generality: apparent completeness is not evidence of universal coverage or experimental function. [src: essential_metabolome]

The larger metabolic-capability analysis **supports and refines** this interpretation: among 1,695 complete organism–pathway pairs from 48 organisms, 267 (15.8%) were classified as latent capabilities, meaning that complete pathways had low aggregate fitness signal, while 881 (51.9%) were active dependencies and 547 (32.3%) were intermediate. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] Thus, matched growth provides stronger evidence that a predicted pathway can be functional, but completeness alone does not imply measurable dependency under the tested conditions. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] Carbon-source pathways were especially likely to be latent (217 of 892, 24.3%), compared with amino-acid biosynthesis pathways (48 of 735, 6.5%), supporting condition- and pathway-category-aware validation. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]

The pathway-capability analysis **refines** this interpretation by separating genomic capability from experimentally observed dependency: across 161 organism–pathway pairs, complete pathways could be either fitness-important or apparently dispensable under the tested conditions. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] Its four-way classification identified 57 Active Dependency pairs, 66 Latent Capability pairs, 24 Incomplete but Important pairs, and 14 Missing pairs. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] Thus, matched growth supports capability, but pathway completeness alone does not establish condition-independent growth dependence; notably, all 66 aggregate Latent Capability pairs became fitness-important under at least one condition type, especially nitrogen limitation, stress, or carbon limitation. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] This condition-specific reclassification is itself subject to a median-based importance-threshold caveat, because applying a median threshold to each subset can cause pathways to cross it by construction. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

That classification was produced by crossing GapMind pathway completeness predictions with RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing, a pooled mutant-fitness assay) data from the Fitness Browser, which classified 161 organism-pathway combinations across 7 model bacteria into four categories: **Active Dependency** (35.4%), **Latent Capability** (41.0%), **Incomplete but Important** (14.9%), and **Missing** (8.7%). [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

Within those 161 pairs the largest single category is Latent Capability at 41.0% (66/161), ahead of Active Dependency at 35.4% (57/161); the 24/161 (14.9%) Incomplete but Important pairs are pathways GapMind scores as incomplete whose mapped genes are nonetheless fitness-important, which the report reads as annotation gaps or salvage routes, and only 8.7% (14/161) are Missing (neither complete nor important). [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

That classification rests on a narrow overlap: of the 48 organisms in the Fitness Browser, only 7 have matching GapMind genome data, and across these organisms 23 GapMind pathways had sufficient gene-level annotation to classify, yielding the 161 organism–pathway pairs. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

Its two thresholds are explicit and chosen: a pathway counts as complete when GapMind predicts it complete in >50% of conspecific genomes, and as fitness-important when the composite importance score exceeds the data-driven median threshold. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

The report does not defend that median threshold as principled: it states that calibrating against an independent validation set — for example known essentials from `essential_metabolome` — would provide a more defensible threshold. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

The overlap also biases the validation: those 7 organisms have near-complete core genomes, which compresses the conservation validation signal, so agreement with conservation is weakly resolved by construction in this tier. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

![Four-category overview](figures/fig1_four_category_overview.png) [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

![Classification heatmap](figures/tier1_classification_heatmap.png) [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

The cross-project digest reports this same four-category classification in percentage form — across 7 Fitness Browser organisms × 23 GapMind pathways, 35.4% Active Dependencies (complete + fitness-important), 41.0% Latent Capabilities (complete but fitness-neutral in rich media), 14.9% Incomplete but Important, and 8.7% Missing — and files it under the pathway-capability project, so it restates the counts above rather than adding an independent sample. [src: discoveries, pathway_capability_dependency] On those figures the Latent Capability share (41.0%) exceeds the Active Dependency share (35.4%), so a complete prediction on its own is a weak indicator of measured dependency in rich media. [src: discoveries]

The same metabolites were drawn from an integration of WoM exometabolomics, Fitness Browser mutant-fitness data, BacDive utilization phenotypes, and GapMind predictions, allowing computational predictions to be evaluated alongside both metabolite production and growth-associated measurements. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] This cross-database design connects the question to [[concepts/multi-omics-integration]] and [[concepts/cross-tenant-data-bridging]]. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]

## Agreement Between Predictions Is Partial and Scope-Bounded

A gapfilling comparison **refines** rather than extends the support from matched growth: of 104 GapMind-gapfill pathway pairings, GapMind pathway predictions partially corroborated the gapfilling results. [src: annotation_gap_discovery] GapMind identified pathways as incomplete (`not_present` or `steps_missing`) for many of the same carbon sources where [[entities/modelseed]] required gapfilling, but the report does not quantify “many”, so the concordance is directional rather than counted. [src: annotation_gap_discovery] This **refines** [[concepts/metabolic-model-gapfilling]]: two completeness-style predictions agreeing in part is weaker evidence than a prediction matched to a growth phenotype. [src: annotation_gap_discovery]

Both comparisons are bounded by what the predictor covers: GapMind covers ~80 carbon and amino acid pathways, not full metabolism, and many gapfilled reactions fall outside GapMind’s pathway coverage. [src: annotation_gap_discovery] The CF formulation project states the same scope from the other side — GapMind predicts pathway completeness for ~80 carbon and amino acid pathways, many not among that study’s 22 tested substrates — and used it to compare its commensals against [[entities/pseudomonas-aeruginosa]]. [src: cf_formulation_design]

## Where Prediction Accuracy Has Been Measured Directly

A head-to-head benchmark **refines** the validation picture by measuring what pathway completeness buys as a predictor: on 118 testable pairs, GapMind achieved 96.5% recall and 79% precision — it almost never misses a grower but sometimes predicts growth when the pathway is present but unused — while CSP transfer, a model trained on a separate condition set and applied to matched conditions, reached AUC 0.800 (area under the receiver-operating-characteristic curve, a rank-based accuracy measure) on the 23% of conditions that match the CSP training set. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma]

The same report's preliminary model comparison is not a like-for-like ranking, because coverage and evaluation settings differ across rows (GBDT, gradient-boosted decision trees; KO, KEGG Orthology gene families):
- ENIGMA-only GBDT (NB06), generic KO features, n=7: AUC 0.633, coverage 100% [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma]
- GapMind baseline, pathway completeness for amino acid/carbon: AUC 0.646, accuracy 78.8%, coverage 24.3% [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma]
- CSP transfer (matched conditions, CSP-trained KOs): AUC 0.800, accuracy 76.8%, coverage 23% [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma]
- CSP internal (5-fold), described as the achievable ceiling with sufficient data: AUC 0.858 [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma]

The coverage gap bounds all of this: ~76% of ENIGMA conditions (metals, antibiotics, nitrogen, stress) have neither GapMind pathway coverage nor CSP training data, and prediction on these conditions falls to AUC ~0.63 — no better than generic KO features. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma]

The report's reading is that the two approaches are complementary rather than competing: GapMind (78.8% accuracy, 24% coverage, mechanistic) for high-confidence pathway-level predictions, and GBDT (AUC 0.78 on amino acids, broader coverage, data-driven) for broader coverage with lower confidence. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma]

The benchmark's own follow-up is a proposal, not a result: the 50-experiment list (fumaric acid, melibionic acid, nitrate, etc. × [[entities/prescottella]]/[[entities/microbacterium]]) is the deliverable, and its wet-lab execution is an ENIGMA experimental decision rather than an analytical one. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma]

An independent phenotype cross-check **supports** the reliability of the completeness scores themselves at larger scale: [[entities/bacdive]] metabolite utilization cross-validated GapMind predictions at 83.1% consistency, which that report takes as confirming that the pathway completeness scores are reliable. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] This **refines** rather than overturns the FW300-N2E3 tension recorded below, where the one informative component of the four-database comparison returned a null. [src: discoveries]

## What GapMind Scores, and How It Must Be Queried

GapMind has exactly 80 pathways total. [src: pitfalls] The pathway-capability project gives the split and the omission: 18 amino acid biosynthesis plus 62 carbon utilization pathways, with other metabolic domains — cofactor biosynthesis, lipid metabolism, secondary metabolism — not assessed. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

A carbon-focused project states the same boundary from its own side: its analysis covers GapMind's 62 carbon pathways, and it proposes extending beyond them to aromatic compound degradation ([[entities/kegg]] modules for toluene, benzoate, naphthalene), which it expects to carry stronger environmental signal — an expectation marked as likely, not a tested result. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] That report also proposes cross-referencing its carbon pathway predictions with RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing) fitness data from the Fitness Browser to establish experimentally which pathways are functionally important in specific carbon sources; that validation is proposed there, not performed. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

The score field is categorical, not a flag: GapMind score categories are `complete`, `likely_complete`, `steps_missing_low`, `steps_missing_medium`, and `not_present`, not a simple binary 'present' flag. [src: pitfalls]

One analysis failed silently on exactly this point: it counted exactly 80 pathways for every species with 0% present because it queried `score_category = 'present'`, a value that does not exist, and because each genome-pathway pair has multiple rows, one per step or component in the pathway. [src: pitfalls] The corrected approach is to take the best score for each genome-pathway pair before aggregating to species level. [src: pitfalls]

For core-scope queries the field is binary: in `kbase.ke_pangenome.gapmind_pathways` with `sequence_scope = 'core'`, `score_simplified` contains only `0.0` (pathway incomplete) or `1.0` (pathway complete), not a continuous confidence score. [src: pitfalls] The same entry describes that table as genome-level, one row per genome-pathway pair, and advises aggregating to species level with `MAX(score_simplified) GROUP BY clade_name, pathway` before joining to species data. [src: pitfalls]

Threshold choice can erase the signal entirely. Using `gapmind_pathways` with `sequence_scope = 'core'` for broad taxonomic comparisons yields 0% completeness across all compartments, because the core-level scoring threshold is too stringent for genus-level or cross-species comparisons where pathway genes may not be universally present. [src: pitfalls] The plant-microbiome project reports the same null from its own run: the GapMind pathway completeness analysis yielded 0% completeness across all compartments at the core level, suggesting the core-level scoring threshold is too stringent for that broad taxonomic comparison. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The recommended fix is to use max-aggregation at the genus level instead of core-level scoring. [src: pitfalls]

That fix is not free of its own artifacts, but the revised source moves most of the blame away from aggregation. An earlier reading held that the NB06 complementarity analysis used max-aggregated species-to-genus GapMind scores, which inflated Cohen's d (a standardized effect size) to -7.54, with a prevalence-weighted re-test producing Cohen's d = -0.39 (credible magnitude, same direction) and the core-level threshold left as a separate open question. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The revised account states instead that the Phase 1 NB06 result of "Cohen's d = −7.54" was **largely a Cohen's d formula error** — NB06 divided the mean difference by the SD of permutation means, which shrinks toward zero by the central limit theorem, while the correct denominator is the raw pair-level SD; re-computing NB06's max-aggregated complementarity with a consistent formula on the same data gives d ≈ −0.43, and switching the aggregation from species-max to species-prevalence further reduces |d| to 0.39. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] On that decomposition, of the apparent "20× reduction" ~90% was the formula correction and ~10% was the aggregation correction, and the direction is robust under both methods — so the aggregation choice is a real but secondary distortion here, not the principal cause of the inflated effect size. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

Community-scale use requires the same decision. One community analysis computed pathway completeness with the binary `frac_complete` metric (fraction of taxa in the community whose GapMind score ≥ 5, i.e. "complete" with no missing steps), choosing it over `frac_likely_complete` (score ≥ 4) because a score of 5 reflects GapMind's unambiguous "complete" call, with `frac_likely_complete` computed separately for sensitivity comparison. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

Its predictor is methodologically distinct from the earlier work it agrees with: using GapMind pathway completeness as the predictor is pathway-level completeness rather than flux-based or PICRUSt-style modelling, yet it arrives at conclusions comparable to Mallick et al. (2019) and Noecker et al. (2016), both of which demonstrated that community metabolomics is partially predictable from genomic/metagenomic functional profiles. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

The sharpest limit on all completeness-based inference is stated there plainly: GapMind represents genomic potential, not expression — pathway completeness indicates whether the genes are present, not whether they are actively expressed — so transcriptomic data would be needed to determine whether expressed biosynthesis correlates more strongly with metabolite pools. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

## Completeness Predicts Other Genomic Traits, But Not Specifically

Pathway completeness has also been used to predict gene carriage rather than growth, and there its specificity fails. [[entities/tryptophan-biosynthesis]] completeness (GapMind score ≥ 0.9) significantly predicts [[entities/ipdc]] presence: species with a complete trp pathway carry ipdC at 2.5% versus 0.9% in trp-incomplete species ([[entities/fishers-exact-test]] OR = 2.81, p = 6.3e-10; Model 1 logit, trp only: OR = 2.81, 95% CI 1.97–4.01, p = 1.4e-08, n = 11,272; Model 2 adding a soil covariate: OR_trp = 2.87, p = 7.0e-09). [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]

The intended negative control fails: the [[entities/tyrosine]] pathway also predicts ipdC presence at similar effect size (OR = 3.62, p = 2.3e-11), so the association cannot be read as tryptophan-specific. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]

The report offers a regulatory reading rather than an artifact: the ipdC gene in [[entities/enterobacter-cloacae]] and related species is regulated by TyrR, a transcription factor it describes as activated by all three aromatic amino acids — tryptophan, phenylalanine and tyrosine (Ryu & Patten 2008, PMID 18757531). [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]

Stratification reverses the sign: within soil/rhizosphere species (n = 1,039) the association reverses (OR = 0.30, p = 0.02), meaning trp-complete soil species are less likely to carry ipdC, while in non-soil species (n = 10,233) the positive association holds strongly (OR = 3.56, p = 7.7e-13). [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]

None of these associations is phylogenetically adjusted: a phylum + soil logistic model (Model 3) was attempted but failed due to quasi-complete separation caused by ipdC's rarity across phyla (214 species, 1.9%), so the conclusions above rest on Model 1 (trp only) and Model 2 (trp + is_soil). [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]

## Mapping Noise Between Predicted Pathways and Measured Genes

Both capability/dependency analyses depend on a mapping step that is itself approximate. Pathway fitness metrics were computed for 48 organisms from the Fitness Browser, spanning diverse proteobacteria, firmicutes and other phyla, and for each organism SEED subsystem annotations were used as a proxy to map GapMind pathway members to Fitness Browser genes rather than using direct GapMind gene assignments. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]

That proxy adds noise in a direction that matters for the conclusions: mapping GapMind pathway membership via SEED subsystem annotations introduces false positives — genes in related subsystems — that dilute the fitness signal, potentially mis-classifying pathways that would otherwise differ in conservation. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]

The report's own remedy is a precision upgrade it did not run: SEED-proxy pathway mapping introduces noise, and using GapMind's per-step gene assignments instead would improve pathway-to-fitness mapping precision and reduce false positives. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]

The companion project's mapping has the analogous weakness on the annotation side: its mapping from Fitness Browser genes to GapMind pathways relies on KEGG annotations (besthitkegg to EC to KEGG map), so genes without KEGG annotations or with incorrect annotations are missed, which may underestimate the true number of genes per pathway. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

## Completeness as a Design Hypothesis

Pathway completeness is also used prospectively, with no matched growth data at all. In the CF consortium-design project, genomic pathway comparison identifies sugar alcohols ([[entities/xylitol]], [[entities/myoinositol]], [[entities/arabinose]], [[entities/xylose]]) as candidate prebiotics — substrates commensals can metabolize but PA14 cannot. [src: cf_formulation_design] These are candidates proposed from prediction, not interventions shown effective in that report, which ties this validation question to [[concepts/competitive-exclusion-consortium-design]]. [src: cf_formulation_design]

Six GapMind pathways are complete in at least one commensal species but absent in *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* PA14, and each pathway is carried by only 1–2 of the 5 core species — species-specific capabilities, not consortium-wide. [src: cf_formulation_design]

- [[entities/myoinositol]]: PA 0%; *R. dentocariosa* (100%); selectivity **1.00** [src: cf_formulation_design]
- [[entities/xylitol]]: PA 0%; *S. salivarius* (98%), *G. sanguinis* (100%); selectivity **1.00** [src: cf_formulation_design]
- [[entities/xylose]]: PA 0%; *N. mucosa* (100%), *G. sanguinis* (100%); selectivity **1.00** [src: cf_formulation_design]
- [[entities/arabinose]]: PA 0%; *N. mucosa* (100%), *G. sanguinis* (100%); selectivity **1.00** [src: cf_formulation_design]
- [[entities/fucose]]: PA 1%; *N. mucosa* (100%), *G. sanguinis* (100%); selectivity **0.99** [src: cf_formulation_design]
- [[entities/rhamnose]]: PA 1%; *N. mucosa* (100%), *G. sanguinis* (100%); selectivity **0.99** [src: cf_formulation_design]

The amino-acid arm of the same search returned a null: a key surprise was the complete absence of selective amino acid prebiotics — PA14 is simply too metabolically versatile on amino acids — and the genomic extension instead points to sugar alcohols and pentoses that commensals can metabolize but PA14 cannot, with xylitol singled out because it is already FDA-approved for CF airway use. [src: cf_formulation_design]

These remain predictions. The report frames the discovery of sugar alcohol prebiotics through genomic pathway comparison as a qualitatively different prebiotic strategy from amino acid supplementation, but growth selectivity is untested, and its own per-species detail limits the headline: each candidate pathway occurs in only 1–2 core species, xylitol completeness is 98% in *Streptococcus salivarius*, and PA fucose and rhamnose completeness is 1%. [src: cf_formulation_design] The broad “100% commensal pathway completeness vs 0% PA” statement is accordingly a genomic prediction that must be validated experimentally, not an observed growth result. [src: cf_formulation_design]

The validating experiment is specified but not performed: test growth of the 5 core formulation species + PA14 on xylitol, myoinositol, xylose, arabinose, fucose, and rhamnose as sole carbon sources, using the same endpoint optical density (OD) and growth curve assays as the current study. [src: cf_formulation_design]

Strain-level generality was likewise addressed genomically rather than phenotypically: because a formulation based on the carbon utilization profiles of specific PROTECT isolates could fail if metabolic capabilities vary significantly between strains of the same species, GapMind metabolic pathway conservation was examined across 499 pangenome genomes in the 5 core species. [src: cf_formulation_design] This converts strain generality into a further conservation claim, so it inherits the same prediction-only limits rather than resolving them. [src: cf_formulation_design]

## Incomplete Predictions as Hypotheses About Dark Genes

Incomplete predictions generate candidate hypotheses of their own. Across 44 Fitness Browser-linked species, GapMind pathway analysis identified 1,256 organism-pathway pairs with nearly-complete metabolic pathways (score: `steps_missing_low`) where dark genes with strong fitness effects co-occur; the query covered 305M GapMind pathway rows filtered to those 44 species. [src: functional_dark_matter] These are organism-level co-occurrences — each pair represents a species that has a nearly-complete pathway and harbors dark genes, but no direct gene-to-gap enzymatic matching was performed, so the pairs are candidate gap-fillers rather than confirmed enzymes, and they carry less weight than a prediction matched to a measured phenotype. [src: functional_dark_matter]

The co-occurrence suggests dark genes could encode missing steps, but confirming this requires EC (Enzyme Commission) number matching, structure prediction, or experimental validation. [src: functional_dark_matter]

- Fucose utilization (carbon): 32 organisms — [[entities/marinobacter]], *P. stutzeri* RCH2, *D. vulgaris* [src: functional_dark_matter]
- Rhamnose utilization (carbon): 31 — Marinobacter, *P. putida*, Phaeo [src: functional_dark_matter]
- Sorbitol utilization (carbon): 30 — *D. desulfuricans*, *D. vulgaris*, Miyama [src: functional_dark_matter]
- Myoinositol utilization (carbon): 28 — *P. putida*, *P. syringae*, WCS417 [src: functional_dark_matter]
- Gluconate utilization (carbon): 26 — Marinobacter, *D. desulfuricans* [src: functional_dark_matter]
- Asparagine biosynthesis (amino acid): 24 — across diverse phyla [src: functional_dark_matter]

The organisms with the most gapped pathways — Marinobacter (49), *D. desulfuricans* ME-23 (45), *P. stutzeri* (45) — are also those with the largest dark gene complements and most specific fitness phenotypes, which **suggests the hypothesis that** their missing steps are encoded by functionally dark genes rather than being true pathway absences. [src: functional_dark_matter]

Curated domain matching narrows the candidate space without validating it: of 1,256 organism-pathway pairs, pathway-enzyme mappings (EC prefixes, PFam protein-family models, functional keywords) identified 42,239 gene-pathway candidates across 3,186 unique dark genes, with 5,398 high-confidence (EC prefix match), 4,687 medium-confidence (PFam family match), and 32,154 low-confidence (keyword match) hits. [src: functional_dark_matter] Compatibility narrows the search from all dark genes in a gapped organism to those with enzymatically plausible annotations, but does not establish enzymatic function, which **connects** this to [[concepts/evidence-triangulation-for-functional-annotation]] and [[concepts/experimental-prioritization-of-functional-dark-matter]]. [src: functional_dark_matter]

The scope limit recurs here: GapMind covers amino acid biosynthesis and carbon utilization pathways but not all metabolic functions, so dark genes involved in signaling, regulation, or structural roles are not captured, and the analysis identifies organism-level co-occurrence of pathway gaps and dark genes rather than direct gene-to-step enzymatic assignments. [src: functional_dark_matter] NB10 partially addresses that limitation with curated pathway-enzyme domain matching (EC prefix, PFam family, and keyword compatibility), identifying 42,239 domain-compatible gene-pathway candidates including 5,398 high-confidence EC matches; even so, full gene-to-step validation would require AlphaFold structure prediction or experimental enzymology. [src: functional_dark_matter] This **supports** the argument in [[concepts/pathway-versus-reaction-evidence-resolution]] that pathway-level agreement cannot substitute for step-level assignment. [src: functional_dark_matter]

## Interpretation and Limits

The agreement supports the interpretation that FW300-N2E3 possessed the predicted biosynthetic or utilization capacity for the 13 matched metabolites, but it does not by itself identify which pathway steps were responsible for the observed fitness signal. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] The pathway-capability analysis **supports** this limitation: its mapping of fitness evidence to GapMind pathways classified capability and dependency at pathway level, while the report still treated gene-level importance and condition coverage as separate evidence. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] Its larger analysis also found that pathway-level conservation did not distinguish latent capabilities from active dependencies, with Mann–Whitney U p = 0.94 for active > latent, so conservation is not a substitute for matched phenotype evidence. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]

The essential-metabolome pilot **further supports** this validation boundary: its 7 organisms had 18/18 amino-acid pathways in 6 cases and 17/18 in DvH, but the report explicitly states that these computational predictions do not establish viability or biosynthetic essentiality. [src: essential_metabolome] Its underlying essential-gene measurements used RB-TnSeq, or random-barcode transposon sequencing, in rich media, where nutrient supplementation can make biosynthetic genes appear non-essential. [src: essential_metabolome] Thus, pathway completeness and essentiality remain distinct claims requiring defined-medium growth or other direct validation. [src: essential_metabolome]

The report distinguishes metabolite production from utilization: WoM exometabolomics can detect overflow metabolism, biosynthetic byproduct release, or active secretion, whereas growth assays measure whether a substrate supports growth. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] Consequently, the 13/13 agreement between GapMind and Fitness Browser does not imply that every predicted pathway should produce detectable extracellular metabolite accumulation or that every produced metabolite must be a usable carbon or nitrogen source. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]

The broader cross-database comparison included 58 WoM metabolites, but only 21/58 (36%) could be tested against at least one other database, leaving 37 metabolites (64%) without cross-database tests. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] The 13 GapMind matches therefore provide strong agreement within the matched subset while leaving the untested metabolite space unresolved. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]

The source report planned, but deferred, an NB04 analysis that would map fitness-important genes to specific GapMind pathway steps. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] This unresolved gene-to-step mapping limits mechanistic interpretation, particularly for tryptophan, which was increased in WoM, had a complete GapMind pathway, and supported growth involving 231 significant Fitness Browser genes, while 0 out of 50 *P. fluorescens* strains in BacDive utilized it as a carbon source. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]

The tryptophan result therefore supports a distinction between predicted biosynthetic capacity and species-level catabolic utilization rather than contradicting the GapMind prediction. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] The report treats the possibility that tryptophan overflow serves cross-feeding or signaling as a hypothesis, not as an established mechanism. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]

The metabolic-capability study **further refines** the validation boundary by showing that its latent fraction was sensitive to classification thresholds, ranging from 4.7% to 21.1% across 16 tested threshold combinations, and that only 48 fitness-tested organisms were available among 293,000 genomes with pathway predictions. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] Its positive correlation between latent-capability rate and pangenome openness (Spearman ρ = 0.69, p = 0.0004, n = 22 clades) suggests that genome dynamics and ecological context may influence whether encoded capability is expressed as measurable dependency, but this does not establish causality. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]

The essential-metabolome analysis **also refines** the coverage claim: although its underlying essential-gene collection contained 45 organisms, only 7 mapped successfully, and [[entities/escherichia-coli]] K-12 had 0 GapMind predictions in the relevant pangenome collection. [src: essential_metabolome] The missing coverage was attributed to exclusion from [[entities/gtdb]] species-level pangenome construction because too many genomes were available, leaving the remaining 38 organisms unknown. [src: essential_metabolome] This supports treating broad pathway-conservation results as a pilot rather than as pan-bacterial validation. [src: essential_metabolome]

GapMind’s own stated limits bound every result above: the predictions are computational, not experimental; the “complete” and “likely_complete” categories were used and may miss partial pathways; and non-canonical or poorly characterized pathways are not detected. [src: essential_metabolome]

Completeness-style metrics can also be non-comparable across the compounds they score. In the ENIGMA carbon census, “high-certainty” is not comparable across compounds because `sig_completeness` is the fraction of each compound’s own signature reactions a genome carries, so a single-reaction signature ([[entities/terephthalic-acid]], 1 required reaction) trivially scores 1.0 (“high”) for any carrier, while a 3-reaction signature ([[entities/phthalic-acid]]) cannot. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The “terephthalic 34/34 high” headline is therefore the weakest completeness call, not the strongest. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

Those calls also inherit their filter: the 8 `enigma_isolate_call` callables rest on a genome-prevalence-<10% signature-reaction filter that keeps a reaction only if it is catabolic for the compound ([[entities/kegg]] degradation map membership or a 3-reaction curated allowlist), which **connects** this validation question to [[concepts/callability-limited-comparative-inference]]. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

## Tensions

**Is matched growth independent validation, or structurally guaranteed?** The FW300-N2E3 project presents the 13/13 GapMind–Fitness Browser agreement as support for pathway-completeness predictions. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] Against that, the digest entry for the same project decomposes the 94% mean concordance across four databases into Fitness Browser 21/21 = 100% (structural), GapMind 13/13 = 100% (structural), and [[entities/bacdive]] 3/7 = 43% (informative), with 37/41 comparisons concordant (90.2%), and reports that a binomial test comparing BacDive utilization of WoM-produced metabolites (43%) against the species baseline (27.5%) shows no significant difference (p=0.40). [src: discoveries] Its reading is that the high concordance is largely inevitable, because Fitness Browser always shows growth for metabolites used as sole carbon or nitrogen sources and GapMind predicts complete pathways for common amino acids. [src: discoveries] The two sides are not averaged here: the matched-growth agreement stands as a consistency check within a favourable metabolite set, while the one informative component of the comparison returned a null. [src: discoveries, fw300_metabolic_consistency]

**Internal denominator discrepancies in the completeness pilot.** The essential-metabolome pilot’s carbon-source side lists its most widely conserved carbon sources — TCA cycle intermediates (fumarate, succinate), fermentation products (acetate, propionate, L-lactate), all amino acids as catabolizable carbon sources, nucleotide derivatives (deoxyribose, deoxyribonate), and the polyamine putrescine — as “present in all 7 organisms, 87.5%”, and gives ethanol and deoxyinosine as “nearly universal (6 of 7 organisms, 75%)”. [src: essential_metabolome] The printed percentages and the stated organism counts do not correspond, and this page reproduces both as given rather than recomputing or reconciling either. [src: essential_metabolome] The report’s reading that this pattern suggests a shared central catabolic capacity for breaking down common organic compounds is an interpretation of predictions, not a direct metabolic measurement. [src: essential_metabolome]

**How many rows per genome-pathway pair?** The pitfalls digest states in one entry that `gapmind_pathways` is genome-level, with one row per genome-pathway pair, and in another that each genome-pathway pair has MULTIPLE rows, one per step or component in the pathway. [src: pitfalls] Both statements are reproduced above as written and the discrepancy is not resolved here; it matters because the two readings imply different aggregation code — a single `MAX` across genomes versus a best-score reduction within each genome-pathway pair first. [src: pitfalls]

## Relation to Existing Concepts

This evidence **supports** [[concepts/metabolic-model-gapfilling]] by showing that a pathway-completeness prediction agrees with an independent growth phenotype for all 13 matched metabolites. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]

It **refines** [[concepts/metabolic-model-gapfilling]] by indicating that complete pathway predictions can be assessed against organism-specific growth data, while also showing that pathway completeness does not resolve production-versus-utilization differences. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] The pathway-capability analysis further **refines** this concept by showing that completeness and dependency are distinct labels and that 24 Incomplete but Important pairs may reflect annotation gaps, alternative routes, or salvage processes rather than simple absence of functional capacity. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] The essential-metabolome result **supports and qualifies** the same concept by pairing high pathway completeness with an apparent DvH serine gap that requires growth testing and by documenting substantial GapMind coverage limitations. [src: essential_metabolome]

It **connects** [[concepts/pathway-versus-reaction-evidence-resolution]] to a concrete validation gap: the available result is pathway-level agreement, whereas the deferred NB04 analysis would test whether individual fitness-important genes map to the predicted pathway steps. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]

The new comparison also **supports** condition-aware validation: the 13/13 result is a useful matched phenotype check, but the finding that all 66 Latent Capability pairs became important under at least one condition type indicates that validation should span carbon, nitrogen, stress, and other conditions rather than rely on a single aggregate fitness summary. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] The larger analysis **supports** this emphasis by finding category-specific latent rates and substantial threshold sensitivity. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]

See the source-level syntheses in [[summaries/pathway_capability_dependency__REPORT]], [[summaries/metabolic_capability_dependency__REPORT]], and [[summaries/essential_metabolome__REPORT]].

[src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]

- Compare complete GapMind predictions with growth across multiple media and single-substrate conditions, and ask how often pathway completeness remains predictive when environmental context changes. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]
- Integrate WoM metabolite profiles with GapMind predictions and community metabolic modeling to test whether predicted biosynthetic capacity explains tryptophan secretion and potential cross-feeding in the Oak Ridge groundwater community. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]

[src: essential_metabolome]
