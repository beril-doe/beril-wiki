# Functional annotation of AMR genes and mechanism classification

The evidence comes from three projects. Two of them report a sizeable share of genes that the scheme cannot place, so their mechanism fractions depend on the classifier. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas, amr_environmental_resistome]

## Literature Context

The candidate papers show that AMR gene classification depends heavily on the database and annotation scheme used. MEGARes 2.0 organises resistance determinants in an acyclic hierarchical ontology with four compound types, 57 classes, 220 mechanisms and 1,345 gene groups covering 7,868 accessions. It also extends coverage to biocide and metal resistance [PMID 31722416](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31722416/). MEGARes 3.0 expands this to 59 classes, 233 mechanisms, 1448 gene groups and 8733 accessions. It adds SNP and indel location information for 337 ARGs whose resistance-conferring variants could not previously be confirmed in metagenomic reads [PMID 36382407](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36382407/). A comparison of annotation tools in Klebsiella pneumoniae found that database completeness varies. For some antibiotics even the most complete databases remain insufficient for accurate phenotype classification. That work builds "minimal models" from known markers only, to expose where novel marker discovery is needed [PMID 41254078](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41254078/).

Other candidates use CARD-based annotation and report mechanism categories. These include endodontic metatranscriptomes, where efflux and target alteration/protection were the main mechanisms (ten teeth) [PMID 38719087](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38719087/). They also include a study of 16 Streptococcus hyovaginalis isolates, where 31 distinct resistance genes were found by CARD searching [PMID 42375241](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42375241/). Several other genome reports apply COG, CARD or ResFinder annotation to single isolates or small collections. These are an Acinetobacter junii strain with 6 multidrug resistance genes [PMID 42963458](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42963458/), a carbapenem-resistant Klebsiella isolate [PMID 41250062](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41250062/) and 83 Salmonella Enteritidis strains [PMID 38538253](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38538253/). They illustrate the tools in use but say little about classifier limits. PlasGO addresses a related annotation gap by predicting Gene Ontology terms for plasmid proteins, and it assigned high-confidence terms to over 95% of previously unannotated proteins [PMID 39704702](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39704702/).

The corpus findings are consistent with this literature on the main point. The hub reports that keyword classification of [[entities/amrfinderplus]] products leaves 22% of atlas clusters unclassified. It also reports that 18.7% of environmental resistome clusters (15,550) cannot be classified from gene name or product. The Klebsiella comparison likewise shows that a single annotation scheme leaves knowledge gaps [PMID 41254078](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41254078/). The atlas proposal to replace keywords with systematic CARD ARO mapping fits the use of curated hierarchical ontologies such as MEGARes [PMID 31722416](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31722416/). The corpus's inclusion of mercury and arsenic families, which drive the COG P enrichment, parallels MEGARes' inclusion of metal and biocide determinants. The corpus extends the literature by quantifying the unclassified fraction at pan-bacterial scale. It also reports that Tier 1 (N=110) and Tier 2 (N=691) fitness distributions are indistinguishable (KS p = 0.17). None of the candidates tests either point, so these are novel. The candidates do not examine the finding of zero Pfam hits among AMR clusters, or COG V enrichment of 7.05x. Those findings are neither supported nor contradicted here.

## How mechanisms are assigned

The pan-bacterial atlas classifies mechanisms by keyword matching against [[entities/amrfinderplus]] product descriptions (for example "beta-lactamase", "efflux", "acetyltransferase"). It does not use the CARD Antibiotic Resistance Ontology (ARO). [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] The environmental resistome report classifies mechanisms from gene name or product annotation. It splits tetracycline resistance between efflux (tet(A-E,K,L)) and target modification (tet(M,O,Q,W) ribosomal protection proteins). [src: amr_environmental_resistome] See also [[entities/target-modification-resistance]].

The fitness-cost report uses two tiers. Tier 1 is [[entities/bakta-amr]] identification, and Tier 2 is keyword annotation expansion. [src: amr_fitness_cost] Tier 1 (N=110) and Tier 2 (N=691) gene sets show indistinguishable fitness distributions (KS p = 0.17). The report reads this as showing that the keyword expansion does not dilute or bias the signal. [src: amr_fitness_cost] Its class distribution lists beta-lactam (44 T1), mercury (27), arsenic (22) and efflux_rnd (22). [src: amr_fitness_cost]

## COG category enrichment

Among 77K AMR clusters with eggNOG annotations, compared with an 86M baseline, COG V (Defense mechanisms) is 7.05x enriched (14.9% versus 2.1%). The report takes this as confirming that AMR annotations target bona fide defense systems. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] COG P (Inorganic ion transport) is 1.93x enriched (10.7% versus 5.6%), reflecting mercury merA, merP, merC and arsenic arsD, arsC families. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] See [[concepts/metal-resistance-genes-within-amr-catalogs]]. COG J (Translation) is 1.50x enriched, consistent with ribosomal protection proteins erm and helR. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] Replication (COG L, 0.12x), lipid metabolism (COG I, 0.10x) and cell motility (COG N, 0.08x) are strongly depleted. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]

## Annotation depth

The atlas finds that 93.0% of AMR clusters have both Bakta product annotations and eggNOG hits, and the remaining 7.0% have Bakta annotations only. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] Sparsely annotated (1-source) clusters are enriched for singletons (55.4% vs 34.6%). [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] Zero AMR clusters have Pfam domain hits in [[entities/bakta-pfam-domains]]. The report suggests AMRFinderPlus and Pfam HMM scans target non-overlapping sequence space for these families. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] AMRFinderPlus hit detection methods in bakta_amr are HMM (51.5%), BLASTP (22.7%), EXACTP (13.0%), PARTIALP (9.7%) and ALLELEP (3.0%). [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] Related: [[concepts/annotation-source-disagreement-and-coverage]].

## Limits of the classification

- Keyword classification leaves 22% as Other/Unclassified in the atlas. The 22.2% category includes products matching no keyword set, such as ribosomal protection proteins with non-standard names and novel resistance mechanisms. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]
- In the environmental resistome report, 18.7% of AMR clusters (15,550) could not be classified from gene name or product annotation. They are excluded from the mechanism fractions. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] This could bias the H3 analysis if unclassified genes are non-randomly distributed across environments. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] See [[concepts/environment-dependent-resistance-mechanism-composition]].
- AMRFinderPlus focuses on known resistance genes and may underestimate novel resistance in environmental bacteria. This would bias results toward more AMR in clinical species, where resistance genes are well characterized. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]
- In the fitness-cost report, 86% of AMR genes are Tier 2 keyword matches from bakta annotations. These may include non-AMR genes such as general efflux transporters. Consistent Tier 1 sensitivity results mitigate this concern. [src: amr_fitness_cost] See [[concepts/essentiality-and-annotation-caveats-in-transposon-based-resistance-gene-fitness-analysis]].
- The Tier 2 product classifier omits fosfomycin and tellurite resistance annotations. About 25 genes sit in the unknown mechanism category and could be reclassified as enzymatic_inactivation and metal_resistance respectively. [src: amr_fitness_cost]
- The atlas proposes replacing keyword classification with systematic CARD ARO mapping, and mapping bakta_db_xrefs cross-references to ARO terms, to reduce the unclassified fraction. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] It also proposes cross-referencing AMR proteins with AlphaFold/PDB structures to identify novel resistance folds. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]

## Open Directions

- Map bakta_db_xrefs cross-references to CARD ARO terms, replacing the keyword scheme. Then measure how far the 22% Other/Unclassified fraction falls.
- Test whether the 15,550 unclassified clusters in the environmental resistome report are distributed non-randomly across environments. This would show whether the exclusion biases the H3 mechanism fractions.
- Add fosfomycin and tellurite rules to the Tier 2 classifier and rerun the mechanism-level fitness comparison.
- Cross-reference AMR proteins with AlphaFold/PDB structures to identify novel resistance folds among poorly annotated clusters.
