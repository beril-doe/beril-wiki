# Cluster assumptions and weak separation limit inference about bacterial gene-content ecotypes

The [ecotype_functional_differentiation__REPORT](../summaries/ecotype_functional_differentiation__REPORT.md) report identified gene-content ecotypes by applying principal component analysis (PCA) followed by KMeans, but weak cluster separation, model assumptions, incomplete annotation, and absent phylogenetic controls limit how confidently these clusters can be interpreted as biological ecotypes. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] The [ecotype_analysis__REPORT](../summaries/ecotype_analysis__REPORT.md) analysis **supports** this caution by finding that phylogeny generally explained gene-content similarity better than environmental similarity across a broader comparison. [^ecotype_analysis] The [ecotype_env_reanalysis__REPORT](../summaries/ecotype_env_reanalysis__REPORT.md) **refines** the environmental conclusion: a more systematic genome-level environmental classification confirmed the null result rather than showing that clinical sampling bias explained it. [^ecotype_env_reanalysis] Evidence from [prophage_ecology__REPORT](../summaries/prophage_ecology__REPORT.md) **refines** this whole-genome conclusion by showing that a specific, environmentally variable gene-module system can carry environmental structure beyond phylogeny, without demonstrating that such structure forms discrete ecotypes. [^prophage_ecology] The [plant_microbiome_ecotypes__REPORT](../summaries/plant_microbiome_ecotypes__REPORT.md) **supports** the weak-separation interpretation in an independent plant-associated comparison: refined compartment effects explained approximately 6% of functional-marker variance, while the much larger preliminary effect largely disappeared after removing genome-rich species. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

## Evidence for ecotype structure

Valid gene-content ecotypes were detected in 12 of 15 sampled species (80%), covering 1,820 genomes across 12 species from 6 phyla. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] The species averaged 3.7 ecotypes, with a range of 2–6, and the mean silhouette score was 0.215 while the median was 0.174. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] Silhouette scores quantify how much observations are separated from their assigned cluster relative to neighboring clusters, so these values indicate that the inferred partitions were generally overlapping rather than sharply separated. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation]

Separation varied substantially among species: *Erwinia amylovora* had the strongest reported separation, with a silhouette score of 0.468 and 2 ecotypes, whereas *Bacteroides xylanisolvens* had a score of 0.366 and 6 ecotypes. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] The weakest reported signals occurred in *Staphylococcus simulans*, with a score of 0.118, and *Streptococcus pseudopneumoniae*, with a score of 0.131. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] Thus, the analysis supports recurring gene-content structure, but it does not establish that every inferred ecotype is a sharply bounded biological population. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation]

The broader ecotype analysis **refines** this result by comparing 13,381 genomes across 224 species and producing correlation results for 172 species: the median partial correlation was 0.0025 for environment versus 0.0143 for phylogeny, and phylogeny dominated in 60.5% of species versus 39.5% for environment. [^ecotype_analysis] A significant positive environmental effect occurred in 12 species (7.0%), a significant negative effect in 4 species (2.3%), and no significant effect in 156 species (90.7%). [^ecotype_analysis] These results support interpreting cluster-level gene-content structure as potentially real but not automatically environmental, while suggesting that environmental adaptation may affect specific gene subsets rather than whole-genome content. [^ecotype_analysis]

The environmental-versus-human-associated reanalysis **supports** this interpretation within a consistent method: environmental species had median partial correlation 0.051, compared with 0.084 for human-associated species, and the one-sided Mann–Whitney U test for Environmental > Human-associated gave U=1536 and p=0.83. [^ecotype_env_reanalysis] The fraction of environmental genomes per species was also unrelated to partial-correlation strength (Spearman rho=-0.085, p=0.25), while the corresponding human-associated analysis gave rho=0.030 and p=0.69. [^ecotype_env_reanalysis] Thus, the confirmed clinical sampling bias did not account for the weak environment–gene-content relationship. [^ecotype_env_reanalysis]

The plant-associated analysis **supports** the conclusion that detectable context effects need not define strong ecotypes. Using 607 root, rhizosphere, and phyllosphere species, PERMANOVA gave R² = 0.071, while distance-based redundancy analysis (db-RDA), which isolates centroid or location shifts, gave location-only R² = 0.060; this represented 84% of the PERMANOVA R², with 16% attributable to dispersion. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] PERMDISP also detected dispersion heterogeneity, with F = 15.6 and p = 0.001, and the preliminary R² = 0.527 fell to 0.072 after excluding three genome-rich species per compartment. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] This **refines** the interpretation of weak cluster separation by showing that statistically significant compartment structure can be small and sensitive to taxonomic sampling.

The prophage analysis **supports the narrower gene-subset interpretation**: across 1,773 species, genome size, environment, and family-level phylogeny each had p=0.01 in PERMANOVA of prophage-module composition, but genome size was dominant (F=212.99), followed by environment (F=30.04) and phylogeny (F=6.17). [^prophage_ecology] Environmental effects remained significant within every genome-size quartile, with all p < 6.5e-78, and environmental niche breadth correlated with prophage module count after controlling for genome size (partial Spearman rho=0.468, p=8.41e-110). [^prophage_ecology] This is evidence that selected functional modules can encode environmental differentiation beyond phylogeny, but it does not establish discrete ecotypes: individual TerL lineages showed no environment-specific enrichment after FDR correction in 0/500 tests, and the report explicitly describes module-level and lineage-level ecology as decoupled. [^prophage_ecology]

## Cluster-model limitations

KMeans was selected after PCA, with up to 50 principal components, by searching k = 2–6 and choosing the result with the best silhouette score. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] KMeans assumes approximately spherical clusters and requires a chosen number of clusters, so its partitions may reflect the geometry imposed by the method rather than discrete natural ecotypes. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] HDBSCAN was unavailable on the cluster; the report identifies it as a potentially better comparator because it can represent variable-density subpopulations. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation]

The minimum validity rules required at least 2 ecotypes, at least 10 genomes per ecotype, and at least 20 assigned genomes in total. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] These thresholds make the retained clusters analyzable, but they do not independently demonstrate that the groups are ecologically or evolutionarily discrete. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] The prophage results **reinforce** this distinction: environmentally structured modules were detected without independent enrichment of complete TerL-defined lineages, consistent with modular exchange or partial gene-content gradients rather than necessarily discrete populations. [^prophage_ecology]

The plant study **supports** testing cluster validity against alternative representations and definitions. Its refined 17-marker panel produced weak compartment separation despite significant tests, while categorical beneficial/pathogenic assignments remained unstable: 878 of 1,115 plant-associated species (78.7%) were classified as dual-nature, and a curated panel misclassified all four neutral controls. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The report therefore treated continuous pathogenicity ratios as more informative than categorical cohorts, a result that **refines** the broader warning that thresholded gene-content classes should not be equated with biological ecotypes. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

## Functional differentiation does not resolve cluster validity

Across 257 chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests covering 12 species and 23 COG categories, 170 tests (66.1%) remained significant after Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction at q < 0.05. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] All 12 species had at least one significantly differentiated COG category. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] These results support nonrandom functional differences among the KMeans-defined groups, but functional differences alone do not show that the groups are discrete ecotypes rather than ends of continuous gene-content gradients or phylogenetically structured variation. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation]

The report found that adaptive categories had a significance rate of 79.8% (67/84), compared with 68.8% (33/48) for housekeeping categories, with a 1.16x ratio. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] Their mean effect size was 0.0136 versus 0.0064 for housekeeping categories, a 2.13x ratio, and a one-sided Mann–Whitney U test gave p = 2.53 x 10^-6. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] These findings strengthen the case that gene-content partitions capture systematic functional variation, while the small proportional shifts mean that statistical significance should not be treated as evidence of strong ecological separation. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation]

The largest mean effect sizes were 0.0392 for COG category S (unknown function) and 0.0337 for category L (replication, recombination, and repair). [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] The report states that approximately 38% of gene clusters had COG annotations, leaving 62% unannotated, so the functional comparison may miss ecotype-specific genes and may favor better-characterized functions. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] The prominence of category S therefore makes cluster interpretation dependent on unresolved functional annotation rather than providing independent validation of ecotype boundaries. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] The prophage study **supports this annotation caution**: it inferred prophage-associated genes from eggNOG annotations rather than dedicated prophage detection, so its near-universal module prevalence may include domesticated remnants and bacterial homologs, with an uncharacterized false-positive rate. [^prophage_ecology]

The plant analysis **refines** the interpretation of functional differentiation by showing that annotation and marker architecture can produce apparently broad biological categories. Of 5,671 eggNOG ortholog groups, 5,341 (94.2%) were significant in raw plant-versus-non-plant tests, but only 50 retained significance after phylum-level control, and the report treated the raw near-saturation as mainly taxonomic composition rather than plant-specific enrichment. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] This supports controlling genome size and phylogeny before interpreting functional enrichment as ecotype-specific adaptation. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

## Phylogenetic and sampling confounding

The analysis sampled 15 species from 456 species eligible after filtering, and valid clusters were obtained for 12 species. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] The sample was stratified across genome-count bins of 50–100, 100–200, and 200–300 genomes, with 5 species per bin, but it may not capture broader phylogenetic or ecological diversity. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation]

The report did not apply within-species phylogenetic controls such as core-genome trees, so the observed partitions cannot distinguish ecological adaptation from phylogenetic or demographic substructure. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] This limitation connects the result to [phylogenetic-confounding-of-pangenome-associations](phylogenetic-confounding-of-pangenome-associations.md) and means that the apparent ecotype–function relationship remains an association until phylogenetic structure is modeled. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] The broader partial-correlation analysis **supports** this concern: phylogeny dominated the gene-content signal in 60.5% of species, while environmental effects were absent or non-significant in 156 of 172 species (90.7%). [^ecotype_analysis]

The plant analysis **supports** the importance of coverage and identifier quality for within-species ecotype inference. Only 18 of 65 plant-associated species with at least 20 genomes had phylogenetic-tree distance data, and only 5 of 17 testable species passed Bonferroni-corrected Fisher tests for plant-association segregation. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Two species, *Xanthomonas campestris* and *Xanthomonas vasicola*, showed especially strong host-associated subclade results, but the report recovered 1,306/1,306 genomes only after reconciling accession prefixes between phylogenetic and environmental tables. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] This **refines** the existing concern: apparent absence of ecotype structure can reflect missing trees or metadata joins, whereas apparent structure still requires phylogenetic controls and broader coverage.

The prophage analysis **qualifies rather than overturns** this concern. Its environmental signal exceeded its family-level phylogenetic effect for prophage-module composition, but genome size was the strongest predictor and residual confounding could not be excluded despite stratification and partial correlation. [^prophage_ecology] Its AlphaEarth analysis also covered only 28% of genomes, producing a biased subsample toward clinically and environmentally well-sampled lineages. [^prophage_ecology] Thus, a gene subset can show environmental structure after reported controls without validating whole-genome ecotype boundaries.

Two species were lost because of transient Spark S3 read errors, and variable Spark query times ranged from 94s–1642s during heavy cluster usage. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] These data-access failures could affect which sampled species contribute to the comparison, although the report does not quantify the resulting selection bias. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation]

The environmental comparison is itself limited because AlphaEarth embeddings covered only 28.4% of genomes, geographic coordinates were often missing or imprecise, and partial correlations assume linear relationships between distance matrices. [^ecotype_analysis] Host-associated geographic coordinates may represent collection sites rather than organisms’ actual microenvironments; environmental and host-associated bacteria consequently showed no significant difference in environmental effects (p=0.66). [^ecotype_analysis] These limitations **refine** the interpretation of weak environmental separation: a weak measured signal does not by itself exclude habitat-associated gene-content differentiation, especially where the embedding or metadata does not represent the relevant microenvironment. [^ecotype_analysis]

The reanalysis **supports** the null comparison despite addressing one classification limitation: among 224 species selected with at least 20 genomes with AlphaEarth embeddings and at least 30% coverage, 106 (47%) were majority human-associated, 47 (21%) majority environmental, and 71 (32%) Mixed/Other, using majority votes over genome-level isolation-source assignments. [^ecotype_env_reanalysis] Environmental species had a higher NaN partial-correlation rate than human-associated species, 10/47 = 21% versus 7/100 = 7%; this filtering would, if anything, favor detection of a stronger environmental signal, which was not observed. [^ecotype_env_reanalysis] The continuous environmental-fraction analysis likewise found no relationship, reducing—but not eliminating—the limitation of majority-vote categories. [^ecotype_env_reanalysis]

## Tensions

The original ecotype analysis reported a median partial correlation of 0.003 across its analysis and the reanalysis reported 0.081, characterized as a 27x difference. [^ecotype_env_reanalysis] The reanalysis used all genomes with embeddings, including up to 3,505 genomes per species, rather than diversity-maximizing downsampling with a maximum of 250 genomes, and used different genome sets and distance distributions. [^ecotype_env_reanalysis] These absolute values are not comparable across methodologies, although the Environmental versus Human-associated comparison remains valid because it was performed within one consistent method. [^ecotype_env_reanalysis] The original coarse classification yielded p=0.66, whereas the genome-level harmonized reanalysis yielded p=0.83; both support a null environmental-group comparison, but the methodological discrepancy leaves the magnitude of the correlations unresolved. [^ecotype_analysis][^ecotype_env_reanalysis]

The prophage result introduces a related scope tension, not a direct contradiction: whole-genome comparisons found weak or usually nonsignificant environment–gene-content relationships, whereas prophage-module composition showed an environmental effect after genome-size and family-level comparisons. [^ecotype_analysis][^ecotype_env_reanalysis][^prophage_ecology] The evidence cannot determine whether the difference reflects genuine concentration of ecological adaptation in prophage modules, annotation or sampling differences, or residual confounding; it therefore does not justify treating prophage-module structure as validated ecotype structure. [^prophage_ecology]

The plant-compartment result introduces a second scope tension rather than contradicting the weak whole-genome environmental signal. Refined plant-marker data detected significant compartment separation with PERMANOVA pseudo-F = 23.2, p = 0.001, and R² = 0.071, but db-RDA location-only R² = 0.060 and the effect was highly sensitive to genome-rich taxa. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] This **refines** the null environmental interpretation: ecological structure may be detectable in selected functional markers or particular host-associated lineages without producing strong, generalizable whole-genome ecotypes. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

## Interpretation

The evidence supports a cautious conclusion: within-species gene-content variation is often functionally differentiated, but the available clustering results do not establish uniformly discrete or ecologically defined ecotypes. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] The main unresolved issue is whether the observed structure reflects robust biological subpopulations, continuous accessory-genome variation, phylogenetic history, or a mixture of these processes. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] Resolving that issue would refine [ecotype-environment-gene-content](ecotype-environment-gene-content.md) and [pangenome-integration](pangenome-integration.md) rather than simply increasing the number of KMeans partitions. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] The ecotype analysis further suggests the hypothesis that AlphaEarth embeddings do not fully capture ecologically relevant environmental variation, while its predominance of phylogenetic signal argues against treating whole-genome gene-content clusters as environmental ecotypes without targeted validation. [^ecotype_analysis]

The prophage evidence **refines** this interpretation: environmental differentiation may be concentrated in particular accessory modules, such as structural and anti-defense prophage functions, even when complete lineage-level or whole-genome ecotype structure is weak. [^prophage_ecology] This remains an inference because the prophage annotations were not independently validated and the strongest predictor was genome size. [^prophage_ecology]

The reanalysis **refines** this hypothesis rather than establishing a mechanism: environmental embeddings had a 3.4x geographic-signal ratio versus 2.0x for human-associated embeddings, yet environmental species did not have stronger gene-content correlations. [^ecotype_env_reanalysis] Human-associated species may retain geographic structure because regional epidemiological patterns could cause different lineages, such as Klebsiella or Enterococcus, to dominate different regions, but this is a proposed explanation rather than a demonstrated mechanism. [^ecotype_env_reanalysis] Unequal genome counts may also give frequently sampled, often clinical species more power to detect weak correlations, while the Mixed/Other group’s highest median partial correlation, 0.109, may reflect diverse sampling campaigns; both explanations remain hypotheses. [^ecotype_env_reanalysis]

The plant evidence **supports** treating ecotype-like environmental differentiation as scale-dependent and provisional. Plant-associated genomes showed 11.7% Jaccard overlap between pangenome-derived and MGnify rhizosphere genera, and the report attributed the mismatch to different isolation-metadata and metagenomic-detection processes. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Its host/subclade results therefore provide candidate lineages for targeted validation, not evidence that compartment labels define universal ecotypes. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

## Open Directions

- Recluster the 1,820 genome assignments with HDBSCAN and compare cluster membership, silhouette scores, and cluster number with the KMeans results to test whether ecotype structure is robust to cluster geometry and density assumptions. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- Add within-species core-genome trees to the 12 successful species and use phylogenetically controlled association tests to ask whether COG differentiation remains after accounting for clade structure. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- Scale the same PCA/KMeans and HDBSCAN comparison from the 15-species sample to all 456 eligible species to test whether weak separation and functional differentiation generalize across genome-count bins and phylogenetic diversity. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- Reanalyze the 62% of gene clusters lacking COG annotations with AlphaFold or domain analysis to ask whether unannotated genes improve separation or reveal adaptive functions hidden by annotation coverage. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- Join ecotype assignments to habitat metadata and test with phylogenetically controlled models whether the inferred groups predict environments rather than only gene-content or lineage structure. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- For the 172 species with correlation results, replace or supplement AlphaEarth distances with direct environmental metadata and test nonlinear, phylogenetically controlled associations to determine whether the weak environmental signal reflects measurement limitations or genuinely weak habitat structuring. [^ecotype_analysis]
- Compare downsampled and full-genome extraction on the same species, controlling genome count and genome set, to resolve the 27x partial-correlation discrepancy; then repeat the environmental comparison with genome count as a covariate. [^ecotype_env_reanalysis]
- Replace majority-vote labels with structured ENVO terms from env_broad_scale and test functional subsets such as transport and secondary metabolism to determine whether whole-genome distances mask habitat-associated gene-content signals. [^ecotype_env_reanalysis]
- Partition prophage and other accessory modules from whole-genome gene-content distances, then compare KMeans, HDBSCAN, and phylogenetically controlled environmental associations to test whether module-level environmental structure produces reproducible ecotypes or continuous gradients. [^prophage_ecology]
- Validate the prophage-module signal with dedicated prophage detection and genome-level rather than taxonomy-bridged metagenomic measurements to determine whether environmental associations persist after annotation and genus-conservation biases are removed. [^prophage_ecology]
- Apply phylogenetically controlled compartment and host-association models to the plant-marker panel, testing whether the R² = 0.060 location effect persists after balancing dominant taxa and expanding tree coverage. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
- Compare marker-defined, accessory-gene, and whole-genome clusters in the 5 plant-associated species with corrected subclade associations, including *Xanthomonas campestris* and *Xanthomonas vasicola*, to test whether host structure is discrete or continuous. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
