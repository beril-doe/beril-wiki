# Cluster assumptions and weak separation limit inference about bacterial gene-content ecotypes

The [[summaries/ecotype_functional_differentiation__REPORT]] report identified gene-content ecotypes by applying principal component analysis (PCA) followed by KMeans, but weak cluster separation, model assumptions, incomplete annotation, and absent phylogenetic controls limit how confidently these clusters can be interpreted as biological ecotypes. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] The [[summaries/ecotype_analysis__REPORT]] analysis **supports** this caution by finding that phylogeny generally explained gene-content similarity better than environmental similarity across a broader comparison. [src: ecotype_analysis] The [[summaries/ecotype_env_reanalysis__REPORT]] **refines** the environmental conclusion: a more systematic genome-level environmental classification confirmed the null result rather than showing that clinical sampling bias explained it. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] Evidence from [[summaries/prophage_ecology__REPORT]] **refines** this whole-genome conclusion by showing that a specific, environmentally variable gene-module system can carry environmental structure beyond phylogeny, without demonstrating that such structure forms discrete ecotypes. [src: prophage_ecology] The [[summaries/plant_microbiome_ecotypes__REPORT]] **supports** the weak-separation interpretation in an independent plant-associated comparison: refined compartment effects explained approximately 6% of functional-marker variance, while the much larger preliminary effect largely disappeared after removing genome-rich species. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Two further lines of evidence extend the same caution beyond auxiliary gene content: antimicrobial-resistance (AMR) profile clustering in [[summaries/amr_strain_variation__REPORT]] produced well-separated clusters in only a minority of species [src: amr_strain_variation], and the gut community-composition ecotypes of [[summaries/ibd_phage_targeting__REPORT]] were only marginally stable even within one pooled dataset and weakly stable across independent studies: bootstrap ARI (adjusted Rand index, a chance-corrected measure of agreement between two partitions) 0.13–0.17 and mean LOSO (leave-one-study-out) ARI 0.113 both fall below that project's > 0.30 "stable" threshold [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. Within-species metabolic-pathway clustering **refines** rather than overturns this picture: individual species can yield sharply separated pathway partitions, while the number of partitions recovered depends on where the dendrogram is cut. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency, pathway_capability_dependency]

## Evidence for ecotype structure

Valid gene-content ecotypes were detected in 12 of 15 sampled species (80%), covering 1,820 genomes across 12 species from 6 phyla. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] The species averaged 3.7 ecotypes, with a range of 2–6, and the mean silhouette score was 0.215 while the median was 0.174. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] Silhouette scores quantify how much observations are separated from their assigned cluster relative to neighboring clusters, so these values indicate that the inferred partitions were generally overlapping rather than sharply separated. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]

Separation varied substantially among species: *Erwinia amylovora* had the strongest reported separation, with a silhouette score of 0.468 and 2 ecotypes, whereas *Bacteroides xylanisolvens* had a score of 0.366 and 6 ecotypes. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] The weakest reported signals occurred in *Staphylococcus simulans*, with a score of 0.118, and *Streptococcus pseudopneumoniae*, with a score of 0.131. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] Thus, the analysis supports recurring gene-content structure, but it does not establish that every inferred ecotype is a sharply bounded biological population. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]

The per-species results for the 12 species with valid ecotypes were reported as follows, with *Enterobacter cloacae* and *Enterobacter cloacae* M listed as separate species. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]

- *Staphylococcus simulans*: 78 genomes, 3 ecotypes, silhouette 0.118, 63 assigned genomes. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- *Enterococcus D gallinarum*: 95 genomes, 3 ecotypes, silhouette 0.245, 86 assigned genomes. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- *Pectobacterium carotovorum*: 57 genomes, 2 ecotypes, silhouette 0.196, 57 assigned genomes. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- *Streptococcus pseudopneumoniae*: 127 genomes, 4 ecotypes, silhouette 0.131, 118 assigned genomes. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- *Bacillus safensis*: 120 genomes, 3 ecotypes, silhouette 0.141, 117 assigned genomes. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- *Limosilactobacillus fermentum*: 118 genomes, 4 ecotypes, silhouette 0.143, 107 assigned genomes. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- *Enterobacter cloacae* M: 136 genomes, 4 ecotypes, silhouette 0.178, 123 assigned genomes. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- *Enterobacter kobei*: 252 genomes, 2 ecotypes, silhouette 0.159, 252 assigned genomes. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- *Enterobacter cloacae*: 216 genomes, 5 ecotypes, silhouette 0.170, 210 assigned genomes. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- *Erwinia amylovora*: 231 genomes, 2 ecotypes, silhouette 0.468, 231 assigned genomes. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- *Bacteroides xylanisolvens*: 207 genomes, 6 ecotypes, silhouette 0.366, 207 assigned genomes. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- *Mycobacterium avium*: 249 genomes, 6 ecotypes, silhouette 0.263, 249 assigned genomes. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]

Clustering on a different feature space changes both how often clusters are found and how well separated they are. Among 974 species with sufficient genomes (>=15) for clustering, UMAP (uniform manifold approximation and projection, a nonlinear embedding) followed by DBSCAN (density-based clustering) on AMR Jaccard distances identified at least 2 distinct AMR ecotypes in 190 species (19.5%), with a median silhouette score of 0.620, described in that report as good cluster quality. [src: amr_strain_variation] This **refines** rather than contradicts the weak-separation result: sharper partitions were obtained on a narrow resistance-gene feature set with a density-based algorithm, but only in a minority of species, which suggests the hypothesis that breadth of detection and sharpness of separation depend on feature choice and clustering method. [src: amr_strain_variation] Because the two analyses used unmatched species sets, features, and algorithms, they establish no such trade-off and their silhouette values are not directly comparable. [src: amr_strain_variation, ecotype_functional_differentiation]

Metabolic-pathway presence/absence features produced well-separated within-species partitions in the species where they were tested. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] All 10 target species with at least 50 genomes and at least 15 variable pathways showed meaningful metabolic clustering, with all silhouette scores above 0.2 and scores ranging from 0.35 (*PALSA-747* sp.) to 0.89 (*Salmonella enterica*), which the report reads as near-perfect metabolic separation in some species and softer ecotype boundaries in others. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] *Salmonella enterica* itself contributed 11,396 genomes, 6 metabolic clusters, and a silhouette of 0.894. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] A wider pass over 225 species with sufficient genome diversity (50+ genomes and 3+ variable pathways), using hierarchical clustering of binary pathway profiles under Jaccard distance, identified a median of 4 metabolic ecotypes per species, with a maximum of 8. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] This **refines** the weak-separation result rather than contradicting it: separation is high where clusters are defined by a small set of pathway presence/absence calls, and because the species sets, features, and algorithms differ from those of the gene-content analysis, the silhouette values are not directly comparable. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency, ecotype_functional_differentiation]

Valid partitions can also be highly unbalanced. In lung *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, two metabolic subpopulations were identified: a major cluster of 1,743 genomes (97%) with full metabolic capacity and a minor cluster of 53 genomes (3%) lacking TCA (tricarboxylic acid) cycle intermediates, with the minor cluster enriched for cystic fibrosis isolates (25% CF vs 16%). [src: cf_formulation_design] The further suggestion that this minor cluster is chronically adapted and even more dependent on external metabolites, and therefore potentially more vulnerable to competitive exclusion, is speculative rather than measured. [src: cf_formulation_design] No separation statistic is reported for this 3% subpopulation, so on the available evidence it cannot be distinguished from the tail of a continuous metabolic gradient. [src: cf_formulation_design]

The broader ecotype analysis **refines** this result by comparing 13,381 genomes across 224 species and producing correlation results for 172 species: the median partial correlation was 0.0025 for environment versus 0.0143 for phylogeny, and phylogeny dominated in 60.5% of species versus 39.5% for environment. [src: ecotype_analysis] A significant positive environmental effect occurred in 12 species (7.0%), a significant negative effect in 4 species (2.3%), and no significant effect in 156 species (90.7%). [src: ecotype_analysis] These results support interpreting cluster-level gene-content structure as potentially real but not automatically environmental, while suggesting that environmental adaptation may affect specific gene subsets rather than whole-genome content. [src: ecotype_analysis]

The environmental-versus-human-associated reanalysis **supports** this interpretation within a consistent method: environmental species had median partial correlation 0.051, compared with 0.084 for human-associated species, and the one-sided Mann–Whitney U test for Environmental > Human-associated gave U=1536 and p=0.83. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] The fraction of environmental genomes per species was also unrelated to partial-correlation strength (Spearman rho=-0.085, p=0.25), while the corresponding human-associated analysis gave rho=0.030 and p=0.69. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] Thus, the confirmed clinical sampling bias did not account for the weak environment–gene-content relationship. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]

The plant-associated analysis **supports** the conclusion that detectable context effects need not define strong ecotypes. Using 607 root, rhizosphere, and phyllosphere species, PERMANOVA gave R² = 0.071, while distance-based redundancy analysis (db-RDA), which isolates centroid or location shifts, gave location-only R² = 0.060; this represented 84% of the PERMANOVA R², with 16% attributable to dispersion. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] PERMDISP also detected dispersion heterogeneity, with F = 15.6 and p = 0.001, and the preliminary R² = 0.527 fell to 0.072 after excluding three genome-rich species per compartment. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] This **refines** the interpretation of weak cluster separation by showing that statistically significant compartment structure can be small and sensitive to taxonomic sampling. An earlier PERMDISP test in the same project reported significant dispersion heterogeneity between cohorts (H=33.12, p=3.0e-7), which the report treats as making its PERMANOVA R²=0.53 an upper bound that may partly reflect variance differences rather than pure location shifts, while the compartment effect remained supported by Fisher enrichment tests (69/96 significant). [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The prophage analysis **supports the narrower gene-subset interpretation**: across 1,773 species, genome size, environment, and family-level phylogeny each had p=0.01 in PERMANOVA of prophage-module composition, but genome size was dominant (F=212.99), followed by environment (F=30.04) and phylogeny (F=6.17). [src: prophage_ecology] Environmental effects remained significant within every genome-size quartile, with all p < 6.5e-78, and environmental niche breadth correlated with prophage module count after controlling for genome size (partial Spearman rho=0.468, p=8.41e-110). [src: prophage_ecology] This is evidence that selected functional modules can encode environmental differentiation beyond phylogeny, but it does not establish discrete ecotypes: individual TerL lineages showed no environment-specific enrichment after FDR correction in 0/500 tests, and the report explicitly describes module-level and lineage-level ecology as decoupled. [src: prophage_ecology]

## Cluster-model limitations

KMeans was selected after PCA, with up to 50 principal components, by searching k = 2–6 and choosing the result with the best silhouette score. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] KMeans assumes approximately spherical clusters and requires a chosen number of clusters, so its partitions may reflect the geometry imposed by the method rather than discrete natural ecotypes. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] HDBSCAN was unavailable on the cluster; the report identifies it as a potentially better comparator because it can represent variable-density subpopulations. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]

For that clustering procedure — PCA with up to 50 components followed by KMeans with k=2-6 selected by silhouette — the minimum validity rules required at least 2 ecotypes, at least 10 genomes per ecotype, and at least 20 assigned genomes in total. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] These thresholds make the retained clusters analyzable, but they do not independently demonstrate that the groups are ecologically or evolutionarily discrete. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] The prophage results **reinforce** this distinction: environmentally structured modules were detected without independent enrichment of complete TerL-defined lineages, consistent with modular exchange or partial gene-content gradients rather than necessarily discrete populations. [src: prophage_ecology]

Threshold choice is a comparable degree of freedom in the pathway analyses. The 225-species metabolic ecotype counts came from binary pathway profiles (complete/incomplete across 80 pathways) clustered hierarchically with Jaccard distance, with the number of ecotypes per species determined by cutting the dendrogram at 50% of maximum distance. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] The report calls that fixed 50% threshold a simplification, states that different distance thresholds would yield different ecotype counts, and says the median of 4 ecotypes per species should be interpreted as an order-of-magnitude estimate. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] This **supports** the page's central caution from a different direction: as with the KMeans k search, the reported number of ecotypes is partly a property of the cutoff rule rather than of the organisms. [src: pathway_capability_dependency, ecotype_functional_differentiation]

The plant study **supports** testing cluster validity against alternative representations and definitions. Its refined 17-marker panel produced weak compartment separation despite significant tests, while categorical beneficial/pathogenic assignments remained unstable: 878 of 1,115 plant-associated species (78.7%) were classified as dual-nature, and a curated panel misclassified all four neutral controls. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The report therefore treated continuous pathogenicity ratios as more informative than categorical cohorts, a result that **refines** the broader warning that thresholded gene-content classes should not be equated with biological ecotypes. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

## Model selection and cross-study stability of community-composition ecotypes

Choosing the number of clusters cannot rely on per-method fit alone. On one gut-microbiome dataset of roughly 8.5K samples, both LDA (latent Dirichlet allocation, a topic model) training perplexity and GMM (Gaussian mixture model) BIC (Bayesian information criterion) computed on CLR (centered log-ratio) transformed data with 20 principal components were monotone with K, always preferring the largest K available, and held-out 5-fold perplexity gave the same monotone signal, so it did not distinguish overfitting from genuine structure. [src: discoveries] The discriminating signal was the cross-method ARI (adjusted Rand index, a chance-corrected measure of agreement between two partitions) between LDA and GMM at each K, which peaked at K=7 (0.140) and K=4 (0.131); a parsimony rule taking the smallest K within 0.02 of the peak selected K=4, the value the report judged most interpretable (E0 healthy, E1 Bacteroides2 disease, E2 Prevotella, E3 severe Bacteroides). [src: discoveries] The same project proposes cross-method ARI as a general K-selection criterion when per-method fit is monotonically decreasing in K. [src: ibd_phage_targeting] This **refines** the KMeans search described above, which chose k by the best silhouette score within a single method and therefore carries no cross-method agreement check. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation, discoveries]

The project's own report states the same selection with its method details: LDA on pseudo-counts and GMM on CLR + PCA-20 were trained as two independent methods on 8,489 MetaPhlAn3 samples (`fact_taxon_abundance`, CMD_HEALTHY + CMD_IBD cohorts) across K ∈ {2..8}; per-method fit measures (LDA held-out perplexity, GMM BIC) monotonically decreased with K, the cross-method ARI between LDA and GMM had a local maximum at K = 4 (ARI = 0.131) and a second peak at K = 7 (0.140), and a parsimony rule — smallest K within 0.02 ARI of the maximum — selected K = 4, where per-sample method agreement was 48.9 %. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

The resulting framework is described in that report as four reproducible IBD ecotypes — a framing the report itself says must be qualified, since its bootstrap ARI of 0.13–0.17 and mean LOSO ARI of 0.113 fail its own > 0.30 "stable" threshold — trained on 8,489 curatedMetagenomicData MetaPhlAn3 samples (5,333 HC, healthy controls, plus 3,156 IBD/other), with consensus K = 4 selected by cross-method ARI between LDA and GMM: diverse-commensal (E0, 66.8 % of HC), Bacteroides2-transitional (E1, dominant in CD/UC/T1D/T2D), *Prevotella copri* enterotype (E2, non-Western healthy), and severe Bacteroides-expanded (E3, IBD flare / CDI / donor 2708). [src: ibd_phage_targeting] That consensus K=4 assignment was applied to 8,489 cMD samples, 1,627 HMP2 samples, and 23 UC Davis patients, with 80% HMP2 projection confidence and disease stratification at χ²(2)=15.6, p=0.016, and it resolved E1-transitional from E3-severe within Bacteroides-dominant samples, giving a disease-stratifying signal on the 8.5 K-sample set used in [[concepts/gut-microbiome-ecotypes-as-patient-strata]]. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

The ecotype labels are anchored in named taxa, so their interpretation inherits the taxonomy. The Clostridiales-expansion pathobionts at ranks 6–10 — *Enterocloster asparagiformis*, *Intestinibacter bartlettii*, *Hungatella symbiosa*, *Erysipelatoclostridium ramosum*, together with the related clostridial reclassifications (*E. bolteae*, *E. clostridioformis*, *E. citroniae*) in the lower-ranked list — are described as species in the Lachnospiraceae / Erysipelotrichaceae expansion characteristic of the Bacteroides-2 dysbiosis subtype that defines E1. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

Placement of the UC Davis cohort onto the four-ecotype embedding was non-random: all 26 Kuehl_WGS samples, covering 23 unique patients, were projected onto the K = 4 reference via the synonymy layer, distributing as E0 diverse commensal 7 samples (27 %), E1 Bacteroides2 transitional 11 samples (42 %), E2 *Prevotella copri* enterotype 0 samples, and E3 severe Bacteroides-expanded 8 samples (31 %), with χ²(3) vs uniform = 10.0, p = 0.019, which the report reads as consistent with an active-disease Western cohort, with active disease dominating (73 % E1 or E3). [src: ibd_phage_targeting] Those percentages are reported at sample level over the 26 samples rather than per patient, so sample-level and patient-level fractions should not be conflated. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

Internal resampling and cross-study transfer were both weak for these taxonomic clusters, and the two measures are not interchangeable. Bootstrap 80 %-subsample ARI was 0.13–0.17 on this data, whereas LOSO (leave-one-study-out) ARI across 8 independent sub-studies ranged 0.00–0.28 with mean 0.113, revealing per-substudy variation that the bootstrap masks. [src: ibd_phage_targeting] The report says the bootstrap ARI of 0.13–0.17 understated the instability, records the leave-one-substudy-out result across the top 8 cMD sub-studies as mean ARI 0.113 with range [0.000, 0.282] and mean per-sample agreement 55.0 % (range 16.9 %–85.5 %), notes that this fails the > 0.30 "stable" threshold, and files a formal LOSO verdict of "study-dependent" at mean ARI 0.113. [src: ibd_phage_targeting] The report states that the ecotype framework itself has real cross-study variance at LOSO ARI 0.113 while maintaining that its operational Tier-A targets are externally validated. [src: ibd_phage_targeting] This **supports** the page's central caution with an independent data type and connects it to [[concepts/cross-cohort-microbiome-portability]]: a partition can be put to operational use while neither resampling nor between-study agreement shows it to be a sharply reproducible division of samples. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

The instability was concentrated in particular studies rather than spread evenly. LifeLinesDeep (LOSO ARI 0.21, per-sample agreement 85.5 %) and HansenLBS (65 %) aligned well with the consensus framework, whereas AsnicarF (38 %) and VilaAV (17 %) did not. [src: ibd_phage_targeting] Part of that gap is a baseline rather than study heterogeneity: the consensus itself was an LDA+GMM consensus with only 48.9 % cross-method agreement, so LDA-versus-consensus disagreement contributes to every LOSO comparison, and the report states that the substudy-to-substudy variance is real and the "four reproducible ecotypes" framing must be qualified. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

The project's own stopping rules record which of these checks passed. Against a feature-leakage bound requiring held-out-species Jaccard > 0.5, the observed values were E1 = 0.230 and E3 = 0.064, both failing; ecotype-specific Tier-A counts passed a threshold of ≥ 3, at 51 for E1 and 40 for E3 (the latter single-study); the engraftment-pathobiont criterion passed at 5/5 tested under a confound-free cohort-level contrast against a target of ≥ 3 of 6; and ecotype-framework internal stability failed, with bootstrap ARI 0.16 against a > 0.30 threshold. [src: ibd_phage_targeting] The report reads that failure as documenting a framework that is internally marginally stable — ARI 0.13–0.17 across 5 × 80 % subsamples — usable for downstream stratification because the projection is deterministic once fit, but not externally replicated at that stage and required to be flagged. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

Framework stability and operational-claim replication are treated as distinct properties that need separate tests. [src: ibd_phage_targeting] The leave-one-substudy-out and pathway-refit analyses show real cross-study and cross-feature-basis ecotype variance, while the external HMP2 projection recovers non-random disease stratification (χ² p=0.016) with high projection confidence (80 % of samples have max posterior > 0.70) and the operational E1 Tier-A findings replicate at 88.2 % sign concordance. [src: ibd_phage_targeting] On this evidence the report holds the framework to be usable even though its boundaries are not bit-reproducible across studies, which **refines** rather than removes the caution above: a weakly reproducible partition can still carry claims that replicate externally, and the two properties must be measured separately. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

Changing the feature basis while holding the samples fixed also moved the partition. A K=4 LDA refit on `fact_pathway_abundance` — 3,145 CMD_IBD samples, 2,000 top-variance HUMAnN3 pathways after prevalence and informative filtering — recovered the taxon-based consensus only partly: ARI = 0.113 with per-sample agreement 50.6 %, and per-ecotype agreement of E1 = 65.3 % (n=1,839), E2 = 47.4 % (n=19), E3 = 30.7 % (n=1,244), and E0 = 0 % (n=43), E0 being the healthy-dominant ecotype and rare in the CMD_IBD pathway cohort. [src: ibd_phage_targeting] A scope limitation bears directly on that E0 figure: `fact_pathway_abundance` is CMD_IBD only, with no healthy-control pathway coverage, so the refit is within-disease only. [src: ibd_phage_targeting] The recorded pathway-ecotype versus taxon-ecotype verdict is PARTIAL, and the report reads the pattern as indicating that the ecotype structure is mixed ecological and taxonomic, not purely one or the other. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

Assignment also depends on the modelling pipeline. Positioning LDA against Dirichlet multinomial mixtures, the report states that LDA on pseudo-counts yields equivalent assignments at this sample scale, citing prior literature and a cross-method ARI check against an independent CLR-based GMM. [src: ibd_phage_targeting] The reported K = 4 between-method agreement of 48.9 % does not itself establish such equivalence, and the report reads that value as consistent with the microbiome continuum being real and the clusters being discretized for operational purposes — the same continuum caveat raised for gene-content ecotypes. [src: ibd_phage_targeting, ecotype_functional_differentiation] Projection between classifiers was additionally asymmetric: across Kaiju and MetaPhlAn3 outputs, LDA was robust while CLR+GMM was fragile, so cluster membership can depend on the taxonomic classifier as well as on the clustering model. [src: ibd_phage_targeting] Projecting the Kaiju-based Kuehl samples onto the MetaPhlAn3-trained embedding left 54 % of Kuehl feature rows outside the training feature space, which LDA on pseudo-counts handled by treating absence as not-detected, whereas the same sparsity forced all 26 Kuehl samples into a single Gaussian (E3) at confidence > 0.97 — recorded as an artifact, not biology. [src: ibd_phage_targeting] The UC Davis Kuehl projection onto K = 4 therefore used LDA as the primary call, with GMM advisory. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

The report is explicit about what the labels are: ecotype calls are hard cluster assignments of an underlying continuum, with soft probabilities preserved in `data/ecotype_assignments.tsv` for downstream use and hard calls to be treated as operational labels, not biology. [src: ibd_phage_targeting] That file carries 8,489 rows, one per CMD sample, each with the K = 4 consensus ecotype plus the LDA and GMM calls and a `methods_agree` flag. [src: ibd_phage_targeting] The framing is referred to prior literature on Dirichlet multinomial mixtures as generative models for microbial metagenomics and on enterotypes in the landscape of gut microbial community composition. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

Metadata cannot substitute for the measurement that defines the clusters. A classifier trained on HC/IBD status, sex, and age reached macro AUC 0.80 (area under the receiver-operating curve) on pooled cross-validation but agreed with the metagenomic projection for only 41 % of UC Davis patients, collapsing to "IBD → E1" because `is_ibd` was the dominant feature and became constant on the test cohort; metagenomics remained required for patient-level assignment in an all-CD cohort. [src: ibd_phage_targeting] This null result **supports** treating ecotype labels as measurement-dependent rather than as clinical categories. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

Statistics built on cluster labels need their own null models. A permutation null for the Jaccard overlap of the top-30 targets of E1 and E3 under randomized ecotype labels gave mean 0.785 ± 0.054, against an observed Jaccard of 0.104 with empirical p = 0.000 over 200 permutations, so the two ecotypes' target sets genuinely diverge rather than reflecting a random-overlap artifact. [src: ibd_phage_targeting] The report retracts its earlier framing that a Jaccard of 0.14 supported the same hypothesis, noting that the value sat near the random-overlap baseline and had no attached null distribution: the conclusion survived, the statistic did not. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

A related failure mode is reusing the clustering features for the within-cluster test. In that project's original NB04 analysis — since superseded — K=4 LDA ecotypes were trained on MetaPhlAn3 species abundances and within-ecotype CD-vs-HC CLR-Mann-Whitney tests were then run on the same species matrix, producing a 33-species within-ecotype Tier-A list with effect sizes of +0.5 to +3.0 CLR-Δ. [src: pitfalls] The central pitfalls digest names the consequence selection-on-outcome confounding: within-cluster effect sizes are mechanically inflated for cluster-defining taxa, and the within-cluster top-N list is substantially the cluster definition itself. [src: pitfalls] That original list is invalidated rather than merely caveated, and must not be read as a rigor-controlled result: held-out-species sensitivity gave Jaccard E1 = 0.230 and E3 = 0.064, below the < 0.3 "leakage dominates" bound, and an independent-design confound-free within-IBD-substudy contrast found that most of the NB04 Tier-A E1 candidates had negative within-study effects, making them ecotype-markers rather than CD-drivers. [src: pitfalls] This **refines** the null-model point above — see [[concepts/selection-on-outcome-leakage]] — because the dependence arises from the shared feature matrix rather than from the label assignment that a permutation null randomizes. [src: pitfalls]

A direct test of whether a different feature space yields a more portable partition came out negative. The H3d-clust hypothesis asked whether metabolite-derived clustering achieves higher cross-cohort stability than the taxonomic-derived ecotype framework, which if true would make refitted ecotypes a more cross-cohort-portable basis for clinical translation; it used an m/z-bridge metabolite feature panel with pooled HMP2 and Franzosa metabolomics and no raw reads. [src: ibd_phage_targeting] The method pooled 326 subjects (106 HMP2 × 220 Franzosa) on the 111 unique m/z-bridge metabolite features, standardized per metabolite, reduced to 15 PCA components, and fit K-means with K=4, comparing against a taxonomic LOSO ARI baseline of 0.113 and a bootstrap baseline of 0.16. [src: ibd_phage_targeting] The four resulting clusters were cohort-pure despite mixed diagnoses:

- Cluster 0: 86 subjects, Franzosa 100 %, 46 Control + 30 UC + 10 CD. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]
- Cluster 1: 58 subjects, HMP2 100 %, 29 CD + 23 UC + 6 nonIBD. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]
- Cluster 2: 134 subjects, Franzosa 100 %, 78 CD + 46 UC + 10 Control. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]
- Cluster 3: 48 subjects, HMP2 100 %, 21 CD + 7 UC + 20 nonIBD. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

Cross-cohort LOSO ARI was 0.000 with HMP2 held out and 0.000 with Franzosa held out, both substantially lower than the taxonomic 0.113 baseline, while the within-pooled bootstrap (80 % × 30) ARI was 0.937 against the taxonomic 0.16 baseline. [src: ibd_phage_targeting] The report treats that 0.937 as misleading as a stability metric: it measures how reproducibly the pooled K-means recovers cohort-batch structure under subsampling, not whether the clustering is biologically informative, since the cohort batch effect is visible in any subsample — the batch mechanism discussed in [[concepts/study-batch-confounding-of-environmental-associations]]. [src: ibd_phage_targeting] The report generalizes that within-pooled bootstrap stability metrics are misleading in this regime because they measure batch reproducibility, not biological reproducibility, and notes that the cluster structure was cohort-driven rather than diagnosis-driven, with PC1 at 79 % variance separating the cohorts cleanly. [src: ibd_phage_targeting] The stated verdict is that H3d-clust is NOT SUPPORTED, because cross-cohort LOSO ARI = 0.000 falls far below the 0.113 taxonomic baseline, so the metabolite-feature framework on this bridge panel does not achieve higher cross-cohort stability than the taxonomic framework. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

The consequences are bounded by what was tested. The taxonomic ecotype framework (consensus K=4) remains that project's primary ecotype basis for its Pillar 4–5 cocktail design. [src: ibd_phage_targeting] The H3d-clust failure is recorded as documenting that taxonomic-feature ecotypes remain the primary clinical-translation basis, while metabolite-feature ecotypes would require external batch correction. [src: ibd_phage_targeting] The negative verdict applies specifically to raw m/z-bridge clustering without batch correction, and the report expects ComBat, SVA, or RUV to improve cross-cohort LOSO ARI substantially. [src: ibd_phage_targeting] K=4 was also plan-imposed to match the taxonomic framework, and the natural cluster count on this feature space might instead be K=2, separating HMP2 from Franzosa. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

Within-patient instability is documented, but only in a single case. Patient 6967 — the only multi-timepoint UC Davis CD patient with documented E1↔E3 ecotype drift across 2 visits, and the only one with biological-replicate samples — shifted from E1 (confidence 0.64) to E3 (confidence 0.41). [src: ibd_phage_targeting] *M. gnavus* expanded 14.0× (0.53 → 7.45 reads) as the dominant signature, *H. hathewayi* changed 1.3× (0.27 → 0.36), and cocktail Jaccard between visits was 0.60, with 3 shared members (H. hathewayi + M. gnavus + E. lenta, the universal Tier-1 trio) and 2 visit-1-only members (E. bolteae + F. plautii, E1-specific). [src: ibd_phage_targeting] Both confidences were moderate, so the label shift could partly reflect classifier uncertainty, whereas the 14× *M. gnavus* and 3× *E. lenta* expansions are quantitative biological observations independent of the ecotype label. [src: ibd_phage_targeting] Resting on one patient, this suggests the hypothesis that ecotype membership is temporally labile rather than establishing it. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

Ecotype structure is also entangled with the disease axis in multi-omic models: E1-Bacteroides2-transitional and E3-Bacteroides-expanded species both load positive on the first canonical component CC1 while E0-commensal species load negative, so ecotype is implicit in the species-loading structure and the diagnosis-associated axis may partly encode it; regressing factor scores on ecotype plus diagnosis is listed as a future extension rather than performed. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

## Functional differentiation does not resolve cluster validity

Across 257 chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests covering 12 species and 23 COG categories, 170 tests (66.1%) remained significant after Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction at q < 0.05. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] All 12 species had at least one significantly differentiated COG category. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] These results support nonrandom functional differences among the KMeans-defined groups, but functional differences alone do not show that the groups are discrete ecotypes rather than ends of continuous gene-content gradients or phylogenetically structured variation. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]

The report found that adaptive categories had a significance rate of 79.8% (67/84), compared with 68.8% (33/48) for housekeeping categories, with a 1.16x ratio. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] Their mean effect size was 0.0136 versus 0.0064 for housekeeping categories, a 2.13x ratio, and a one-sided Mann–Whitney U test gave p = 2.53 x 10^-6. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] These findings strengthen the case that gene-content partitions capture systematic functional variation, while the small proportional shifts mean that statistical significance should not be treated as evidence of strong ecological separation. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]

The largest mean effect sizes were 0.0392 for COG category S (unknown function) and 0.0337 for category L (replication, recombination, and repair). [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] The report states that approximately 38% of gene clusters had COG annotations, leaving 62% unannotated, so the functional comparison may miss ecotype-specific genes and may favor better-characterized functions. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] The prominence of category S therefore makes cluster interpretation dependent on unresolved functional annotation rather than providing independent validation of ecotype boundaries. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] The prophage study **supports this annotation caution**: it inferred prophage-associated genes from eggNOG annotations rather than dedicated prophage detection, so its near-universal module prevalence may include domesticated remnants and bacterial homologs, with an uncharacterized false-positive rate. [src: prophage_ecology]

The plant analysis **refines** the interpretation of functional differentiation by showing that annotation and marker architecture can produce apparently broad biological categories. Of 5,671 eggNOG ortholog groups, 5,341 (94.2%) were significant in raw plant-versus-non-plant tests, but only 50 retained significance after phylum-level control, and the report treated the raw near-saturation as mainly taxonomic composition rather than plant-specific enrichment. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] This supports controlling genome size and phylogeny before interpreting functional enrichment as ecotype-specific adaptation. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

## Phylogenetic and sampling confounding

The analysis sampled 15 species from 456 species eligible after filtering, and valid clusters were obtained for 12 species. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] The sample was stratified across genome-count bins of 50–100, 100–200, and 200–300 genomes, with 5 species per bin, but it may not capture broader phylogenetic or ecological diversity. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]

The report did not apply within-species phylogenetic controls such as core-genome trees, so the observed partitions cannot distinguish ecological adaptation from phylogenetic or demographic substructure. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] This limitation connects the result to [[concepts/phylogenetic-confounding-of-pangenome-associations]] and means that the apparent ecotype–function relationship remains an association until phylogenetic structure is modeled. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] The broader partial-correlation analysis **supports** this concern: phylogeny dominated the gene-content signal in 60.5% of species, while environmental effects were absent or non-significant in 156 of 172 species (90.7%). [src: ecotype_analysis]

The plant analysis **supports** the importance of coverage and identifier quality for within-species ecotype inference. Only 18 of 65 plant-associated species with at least 20 genomes had phylogenetic-tree distance data, and only 5 of 17 testable species passed Bonferroni-corrected Fisher tests for plant-association segregation. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Two species, *Xanthomonas campestris* and *Xanthomonas vasicola*, showed especially strong host-associated subclade results, but the report recovered 1,306/1,306 genomes only after reconciling accession prefixes between phylogenetic and environmental tables. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] This **refines** the existing concern: apparent absence of ecotype structure can reflect missing trees or metadata joins, whereas apparent structure still requires phylogenetic controls and broader coverage.

The prophage analysis **qualifies rather than overturns** this concern. Its environmental signal exceeded its family-level phylogenetic effect for prophage-module composition, but genome size was the strongest predictor and residual confounding could not be excluded despite stratification and partial correlation. [src: prophage_ecology] Its AlphaEarth analysis also covered only 28% of genomes, producing a biased subsample toward clinically and environmentally well-sampled lineages. [src: prophage_ecology] Thus, a gene subset can show environmental structure after reported controls without validating whole-genome ecotype boundaries.

Two species experienced transient Spark S3 read errors, one further species, *Limisoma* sp., had insufficient structure for valid clustering, and variable Spark query times ranged from 94s–1642s during heavy cluster usage. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] These data-access failures could affect which sampled species contribute to the comparison, although the report does not quantify the resulting selection bias. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]

The environmental comparison is itself limited because AlphaEarth embeddings covered only 28.4% of genomes, geographic coordinates were often missing or imprecise, and partial correlations assume linear relationships between distance matrices. [src: ecotype_analysis] Host-associated geographic coordinates may represent collection sites rather than organisms’ actual microenvironments; environmental and host-associated bacteria consequently showed no significant difference in environmental effects (p=0.66). [src: ecotype_analysis] These limitations **refine** the interpretation of weak environmental separation: a weak measured signal does not by itself exclude habitat-associated gene-content differentiation, especially where the embedding or metadata does not represent the relevant microenvironment. [src: ecotype_analysis]

The reanalysis **supports** the null comparison despite addressing one classification limitation: among 224 species selected with at least 20 genomes with AlphaEarth embeddings and at least 30% coverage, 106 (47%) were majority human-associated, 47 (21%) majority environmental, and 71 (32%) Mixed/Other, using majority votes over genome-level isolation-source assignments. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] Environmental species had a higher NaN partial-correlation rate than human-associated species, 10/47 = 21% versus 7/100 = 7%; this filtering would, if anything, favor detection of a stronger environmental signal, which was not observed. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] The continuous environmental-fraction analysis likewise found no relationship, reducing—but not eliminating—the limitation of majority-vote categories. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]

## Tensions

The original ecotype analysis reported a median partial correlation of 0.003 across its analysis and the reanalysis reported 0.081, characterized as a 27x difference. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] The reanalysis used all genomes with embeddings, including up to 3,505 genomes per species, rather than diversity-maximizing downsampling with a maximum of 250 genomes, and used different genome sets and distance distributions. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] These absolute values are not comparable across methodologies, although the Environmental versus Human-associated comparison remains valid because it was performed within one consistent method. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] The original coarse classification yielded p=0.66, whereas the genome-level harmonized reanalysis yielded p=0.83; both support a null environmental-group comparison, but the methodological discrepancy leaves the magnitude of the correlations unresolved. [src: ecotype_analysis, ecotype_env_reanalysis]

The prophage result introduces a related scope tension, not a direct contradiction: whole-genome comparisons found weak or usually nonsignificant environment–gene-content relationships, whereas prophage-module composition showed an environmental effect after genome-size and family-level comparisons. [src: ecotype_analysis, ecotype_env_reanalysis, prophage_ecology] The evidence cannot determine whether the difference reflects genuine concentration of ecological adaptation in prophage modules, annotation or sampling differences, or residual confounding; it therefore does not justify treating prophage-module structure as validated ecotype structure. [src: prophage_ecology]

The plant-compartment result introduces a second scope tension rather than contradicting the weak whole-genome environmental signal. Refined plant-marker data detected significant compartment separation with PERMANOVA pseudo-F = 23.2, p = 0.001, and R² = 0.071, but db-RDA location-only R² = 0.060 and the effect was highly sensitive to genome-rich taxa. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] This **refines** the null environmental interpretation: ecological structure may be detectable in selected functional markers or particular host-associated lineages without producing strong, generalizable whole-genome ecotypes. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

A third tension concerns which statistic should certify a cluster. Density-based clustering of AMR profiles reported a median silhouette of 0.620 in the species where clusters were found, while auxiliary gene-content clustering reported a mean silhouette of 0.215. [src: amr_strain_variation, ecotype_functional_differentiation] Gut community-composition clustering instead reported resampling statistics: on taxonomic features, bootstrap ARI 0.13–0.17 and mean LOSO ARI 0.113 across 8 sub-studies, both low; on metabolite features, a within-pooled bootstrap ARI of 0.937 that the report attributes to reproducible cohort-batch structure rather than biology, alongside cross-cohort LOSO ARI 0.000 for either held-out cohort. [src: ibd_phage_targeting] In the comparisons cited here, these statistics answer different questions — separation within one dataset versus reproducibility across datasets — and are reported for different feature spaces, so the apparent disagreement about whether microbial ecotypes are well defined cannot be settled by comparing the reported values. [src: amr_strain_variation, ecotype_functional_differentiation, ibd_phage_targeting] Pathway-presence clustering adds a further feature space to the same comparison, with all 10 target species above silhouette 0.2 and scores reaching 0.89, values obtained on a species set that was never matched to the gene-content analysis. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency, ecotype_functional_differentiation]

## Interpretation

The evidence supports a cautious conclusion: within-species gene-content variation is often functionally differentiated, but the available clustering results do not establish uniformly discrete or ecologically defined ecotypes. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] The main unresolved issue is whether the observed structure reflects robust biological subpopulations, continuous accessory-genome variation, phylogenetic history, or a mixture of these processes. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] Resolving that issue would refine [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]] and [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] rather than simply increasing the number of KMeans partitions. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] The ecotype analysis further suggests the hypothesis that AlphaEarth embeddings do not fully capture ecologically relevant environmental variation, while its predominance of phylogenetic signal argues against treating whole-genome gene-content clusters as environmental ecotypes without targeted validation. [src: ecotype_analysis]

The prophage evidence **refines** this interpretation: environmental differentiation may be concentrated in particular accessory modules, such as structural and anti-defense prophage functions, even when complete lineage-level or whole-genome ecotype structure is weak. [src: prophage_ecology] This remains an inference because the prophage annotations were not independently validated and the strongest predictor was genome size. [src: prophage_ecology]

The reanalysis **refines** this hypothesis rather than establishing a mechanism: environmental embeddings had a 3.4x geographic-signal ratio versus 2.0x for human-associated embeddings, yet environmental species did not have stronger gene-content correlations. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] Human-associated species may retain geographic structure because regional epidemiological patterns could cause different lineages, such as Klebsiella or Enterococcus, to dominate different regions, but this is a proposed explanation rather than a demonstrated mechanism. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] Unequal genome counts may also give frequently sampled, often clinical species more power to detect weak correlations, while the Mixed/Other group’s highest median partial correlation, 0.109, may reflect diverse sampling campaigns; both explanations remain hypotheses. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]

The plant evidence **supports** treating ecotype-like environmental differentiation as scale-dependent and provisional. Plant-associated genomes showed 11.7% Jaccard overlap between pangenome-derived and MGnify rhizosphere genera, and the report attributed the mismatch to different isolation-metadata and metagenomic-detection processes. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Its host/subclade results therefore provide candidate lineages for targeted validation, not evidence that compartment labels define universal ecotypes. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

## Open Directions

- Recluster the 1,820 genome assignments with HDBSCAN and compare cluster membership, silhouette scores, and cluster number with the KMeans results to test whether ecotype structure is robust to cluster geometry and density assumptions. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- Add within-species core-genome trees to the 12 successful species and use phylogenetically controlled association tests to ask whether COG differentiation remains after accounting for clade structure. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- Scale the same PCA/KMeans and HDBSCAN comparison from the 15-species sample to all 456 eligible species to test whether weak separation and functional differentiation generalize across genome-count bins and phylogenetic diversity. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- Reanalyze the 62% of gene clusters lacking COG annotations with AlphaFold or domain analysis to ask whether unannotated genes improve separation or reveal adaptive functions hidden by annotation coverage. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- Join ecotype assignments to habitat metadata and test with phylogenetically controlled models whether the inferred groups predict environments rather than only gene-content or lineage structure. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- For the 172 species with correlation results, replace or supplement AlphaEarth distances with direct environmental metadata and test nonlinear, phylogenetically controlled associations to determine whether the weak environmental signal reflects measurement limitations or genuinely weak habitat structuring. [src: ecotype_analysis]
- Compare downsampled and full-genome extraction on the same species, controlling genome count and genome set, to resolve the 27x partial-correlation discrepancy; then repeat the environmental comparison with genome count as a covariate. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]
- Replace majority-vote labels with structured ENVO terms from env_broad_scale and test functional subsets such as transport and secondary metabolism to determine whether whole-genome distances mask habitat-associated gene-content signals. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]
- Partition prophage and other accessory modules from whole-genome gene-content distances, then compare KMeans, HDBSCAN, and phylogenetically controlled environmental associations to test whether module-level environmental structure produces reproducible ecotypes or continuous gradients. [src: prophage_ecology]
- Validate the prophage-module signal with dedicated prophage detection and genome-level rather than taxonomy-bridged metagenomic measurements to determine whether environmental associations persist after annotation and genus-conservation biases are removed. [src: prophage_ecology]
- Apply phylogenetically controlled compartment and host-association models to the plant-marker panel, testing whether the R² = 0.060 location effect persists after balancing dominant taxa and expanding tree coverage. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
- Compare marker-defined, accessory-gene, and whole-genome clusters in the 5 plant-associated species with corrected subclade associations, including *Xanthomonas campestris* and *Xanthomonas vasicola*, to test whether host structure is discrete or continuous. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
- Recluster the 12 gene-content species with a second model family, such as a Gaussian mixture on CLR-transformed data, and select k by cross-method adjusted Rand index rather than silhouette alone, to test whether the reported partitions survive a cross-method agreement criterion. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation, discoveries]
- Apply leave-one-study-out and bootstrap resampling to the same gene-content clusters, as was done for gut community ecotypes, to measure whether the structure is reproducible across independent genome collections or only within one pooled set. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation, ibd_phage_targeting]
- Repeat the m/z-bridge metabolite clustering after ComBat, SVA, or RUV batch correction and ask whether cross-cohort LOSO ARI rises above the taxonomic 0.113 baseline, which would separate a batch artifact from a genuine absence of metabolite-defined ecotypes. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]
- Regress multi-omic factor scores on ecotype plus diagnosis to test whether residual factor variance encodes ecotype-specific biology rather than disease status alone. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]
- Cluster AMR profiles and auxiliary gene content on one matched species set using the same embedding and algorithm, to determine whether the difference between the reported silhouette values of 0.620 and 0.215 reflects the feature space or the clustering method. [src: amr_strain_variation, ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- Test whether the 53-genome minor metabolic subpopulation of lung *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* is recovered under resampling and alternative clustering, and whether its missing TCA-cycle intermediates predict measured metabolite dependence rather than inferred competitive vulnerability. [src: cf_formulation_design]
- Define plant-associated subclades from accessory-gene presence/absence profiles instead of the current core-phylogeny method, and compare gene-content trees against species trees to identify HGT-driven incongruences; both remain outstanding because they require larger gene-cluster-by-genome data pulls than that project executed. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
- Analyze within-species pathway variation in *Pseudomonas fluorescens* and *Pseudomonas putida*, which are isolated from multiple environments, to test whether metabolic ecotypes — subpopulations adapted to different niches — exist within those species; the analysis is proposed rather than performed. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- Recut the 225-species pathway dendrograms at several distance thresholds and compare the resulting ecotype counts with silhouette-selected and density-based partitions, to establish how much of the reported median of 4 metabolic ecotypes is set by the 50%-of-maximum-distance cut rule.
