# Taxonomic Resolution Controls Detectable Functional Associations

Taxonomic resolution controls which genotype-to-function relationships can be detected because aggregation determines both how community taxa are bridged to pangenome clades and how much abundance is retained for functional scoring. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] In the ENIGMA analysis, genus-level mapping produced different coverage, ambiguity, and statistical behavior across relaxed, strict, and unique-clade species-proxy modes, so an apparent functional association cannot be interpreted independently of taxonomic representation. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

The project workflow linked [[entities/enigma-coral]] geochemistry and community composition to [[entities/kbase-ke-pangenome]] clades and [[entities/eggnog]]-derived functional proxies across 108 samples. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] The complete analysis is summarized in [[summaries/enigma_contamination_functional_potential__REPORT]]. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

## Resolution Modes and Coverage

The taxonomy bridge parsed 27,690 GTDB species rows, mapped 530 genera, left 862 genera unmapped, and produced 530 strict clades, 7,380 relaxed clades, and 150 species-proxy clades restricted to genera mapping to exactly one clade. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] The bridge contained 8,242 genus-to-clade rows because genus-to-clade relationships expanded many-to-many. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] Ambiguity was substantial: 380 mapped genera were multi-clade genera, and the maximum was 433 clades per genus. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] The most ambiguous genera were pseudomonas with 433 clades, streptomyces with 378, prevotella with 358, streptococcus with 214, and mycobacterium with 186. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

Strict and relaxed modes retained a mean mapped abundance fraction of 0.343, whereas the species-proxy mode retained a mean mapped abundance fraction of 0.031. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] Across functional-feature rows, strict and relaxed modes had a mapped-abundance range of 0.031 to 0.854, while the species-proxy mode had a range of 0.0002 to 0.543. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] Thus, the more restrictive unique-clade mode reduced taxonomic ambiguity but also discarded much of the abundance available for functional inference. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

The ENIGMA taxonomy table provided labels through Genus but no species or strain labels, preventing direct species-level bridge testing in this dataset slice. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] This makes genus-level aggregation a necessary but potentially masking representation: strain-level adaptation may be hidden when distinct functional states are combined within a genus. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] The [[summaries/ecotype_functional_differentiation__REPORT]] **supports** this masking concern from a complementary within-species analysis: valid gene-content ecotypes were detected in 12 of 15 sampled species (80%), and 170 of 257 COG-category tests (66.1%) were significant after BH-FDR correction across those species. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] The result therefore strengthens the case that finer within-species structure can carry detectable functional differences, while not by itself showing that those differences are ecological adaptations. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]

The cross-species [[summaries/cog_analysis__REPORT]] **refines** this resolution argument by showing that even broad COG, or Clusters of Orthologous Groups, categories retain a systematic core-versus-novel functional structure: across 32 species spanning 9 phyla and 357,623 genes, novel or singleton genes were enriched in mobile-element COG L by +10.88%, defense COG V by +2.83%, and unknown-function COG S by +1.64%, while core genes were depleted in translation COG J by -4.65% and several metabolic categories. [src: cog_analysis] This supports treating functional resolution as an inference choice: genus-level or broad-category aggregation can conceal structured differences in gene novelty and ecological function even when the signal is consistent across taxa. [src: cog_analysis]

## Evidence from Contamination–Defense Associations

The predeclared genus-level Spearman tests of site defense score against contamination were null in both broad mapping modes. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] In the relaxed-all-clades mode, rho was 0.0587, the 95% bootstrap confidence interval was [-0.128, 0.250], Spearman p was 0.546, and FDR q was 0.862. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] In the strict-single-clade mode, rho was 0.0682, the 95% bootstrap confidence interval was [-0.111, 0.253], Spearman p was 0.483, and FDR q was 0.849. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] FDR, or false-discovery-rate correction, controls the expected proportion of false discoveries among results called significant. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

The confirmatory null was unchanged across four contamination definitions: the composite all-metals index, uranium-only, the top-3-variance-metals index, and the first principal component of metal z-scores. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] All 8 confirmatory variant tests remained non-significant after FDR, with q = 0.546 across the tests, including uranium-only. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

Coverage-aware modeling altered the detectable signal but did not make it resolution-independent. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] In adjusted ordinary least-squares models, the relaxed-all-clades defense-score beta was 0.000751 with 95% bootstrap confidence interval [0.000224, 0.001779], p = 0.000398, and FDR q = 0.0462. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] The corresponding strict-single-clade estimate was beta = 0.000640 with 95% bootstrap confidence interval [0.000169, 0.001538], p = 0.00354, and FDR q = 0.130. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] These results support a coverage-sensitive defense association in the relaxed mode, but they do not establish a robust association across both mapping modes after global FDR. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

Fraction-aware adjusted models showed the same directional pattern without resolving the mode dependence. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] The relaxed-all-clades beta was 0.000607 with 95% bootstrap confidence interval [0.000213, 0.001221], p = 0.00144, and FDR q = 0.0838, while the strict-single-clade beta was 0.000566 with 95% bootstrap confidence interval [0.000214, 0.001113], p = 0.00548, and FDR q = 0.130. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

The species-proxy mode further weakened detectable association: its defense trend was positive but non-significant, with rho = 0.169 and Spearman p = 0.081. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] No high-coverage species-proxy test was feasible at mapped-abundance fraction >= 0.25 because of low retained coverage, and the corresponding high-coverage analysis had n = 1. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] The species-proxy result is therefore best treated as sensitivity evidence that increased clade specificity can reduce power when it sharply lowers coverage, rather than as evidence against contamination-linked functional differentiation. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

## Robustness and Interpretation

Within-fraction tests did not reproduce the stronger aggregate defense pattern. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] Fraction-aware analyses used 212 sample-fraction rows, consisting of 106 rows for the 0.2_micron_filter fraction and 106 rows for the 10_micron_filter fraction. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] Within-fraction defense tests were non-significant in relaxed mode, with p = 0.767 for the 0.2-micron fraction and p = 0.898 for the 10-micron fraction, and in strict mode, with p = 0.780 and p = 0.793, respectively. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] This supports the interpretation that the aggregate monotonic defense signal is not robustly reproducible within individual fraction strata. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

High-coverage subsets produced nominal defense p-values of 0.0207 for relaxed mode and 0.00980 for strict mode, but their global FDR q-values were 0.301 and 0.189, respectively. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] Most non-defense outcomes remained non-significant, although site stress score in the strict high-coverage subset had rho = 0.2489 and p = 0.0407. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] These results refine the interpretation of taxonomic-resolution effects: changing the mapping mode changes both the functional score's coverage and the evidential threshold reached by downstream models. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

The broad conclusion is not that contamination has no ecological or functional effect. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] Rather, this ENIGMA subset did not show a robust community-level shift in inferred stress-related functional potential when coarse COG-category proxies were aggregated at genus resolution. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] COG provides broad functional categories rather than curated metal-resistance pathways. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] The COG analysis **refines** this limitation rather than contradicting the ENIGMA null: COG annotations covered approximately 70% of genes, and its 32-species comparison found structured enrichment of mobile, defense, and unknown functions among novel or singleton genes alongside conserved metabolic and housekeeping functions among core genes. [src: cog_analysis] Thus, broad COG scores can contain real, taxonomically widespread functional structure while still being incomplete and too coarse to establish a contamination association. [src: cog_analysis] The result is compatible with contamination-driven taxonomic turnover that is functionally redundant or operates at species, strain, or pathway resolution finer than the current mapping. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] The ecotype analysis **refines** this interpretation by showing that functional differentiation can be widespread within species, but its use of gene-content clusters and COG profiles also retained important uncertainties: only approximately 38% of gene clusters had COG annotations, and without within-species phylogenetic controls it could not distinguish ecological adaptation from phylogenetic or demographic substructure. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]

Composite COG assignments containing multiple functional letters were treated as biologically meaningful rather than annotation artifacts in the cross-species analysis; the LV composite showed +0.34% enrichment with 76% consistency, and composite categories were counted once per gene rather than split across component letters. [src: cog_analysis] This **supports** retaining biologically meaningful composite categories in future resolution comparisons, while testing whether their interpretation changes with taxonomic aggregation. [src: cog_analysis]

This finding connects to [[concepts/taxonomic-resolution-dependent-functional-inference]] by showing that taxonomic granularity is part of the inference model, not merely a reporting choice. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] It also supports [[concepts/pangenome-integration]], because bridge ambiguity and unmapped genera directly determine the fraction of community abundance contributing to inferred functions. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] The coverage and bridge limitations are related to [[concepts/callability-limited-comparative-inference]] and [[concepts/environmental-resistome]], since missing taxonomic detail can limit both comparability and detection of within-species functional differences. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

## Tensions

The relaxed coverage-adjusted model yielded FDR q = 0.0462, whereas the strict coverage-adjusted model yielded FDR q = 0.130, and both confirmatory genus-level modes were null after predeclared testing. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] This is a methodological tension between a coverage-sensitive exploratory association and a resolution- and specification-robust association, not evidence that the conflicting estimates should be averaged. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] The difference could reflect retained abundance, bridge ambiguity, covariate sensitivity, taxonomic aggregation, or a combination of these factors. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

The within-species ecotype evidence supports the premise that finer functional structure exists, but it does not resolve whether such structure is ecological or phylogenetic because the ecotype analysis lacked within-species phylogenetic controls. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] This tension limits direct extrapolation from gene-content ecotypes to contamination-associated community functions: the ENIGMA analysis is null or mode-sensitive at genus resolution, whereas the ecotype analysis detects functional differentiation within species. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]

The COG comparison found consistent core-versus-novel functional partitioning across 32 species, whereas the ENIGMA analysis did not find a robust contamination-associated shift in coarse COG proxies at genus resolution. [src: cog_analysis] [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] This is not a direct contradiction because the studies test different signals: broad evolutionary partitioning by gene novelty versus a site-level ecological association after taxonomic bridging. [src: cog_analysis] [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

## Open Directions

- Obtain species- or strain-level ENIGMA or metagenomic data and repeat the strict, relaxed, and unique-clade bridge analyses to test whether contamination-linked functional associations emerge below Genus resolution. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]
- Replace broad COG-fraction proxies with curated metal-stress gene sets and pathway-level summaries, then test whether higher-resolution functional definitions distinguish taxonomic turnover from functional redundancy. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]
- Quantify the contribution of the 862 unmapped genera and expand the genus-to-clade bridge, then evaluate whether missing abundance explains the difference between mapping modes. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]
- Fit mixed-effects or hierarchical models including depth, location cluster, sampling date, and compositional controls to test whether the relaxed-mode defense association persists under richer site structure. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]
- Compare aggregate, within-fraction, and high-coverage analyses using the same resolution-specific samples and bootstrap procedure to determine whether coverage or taxonomic ambiguity is the dominant source of sensitivity. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]
- Overlay core-genome phylogenetic trees on the 12-species ecotype assignments and repeat COG enrichment tests to separate within-species ecological differentiation from phylogenetic structure. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- Extend the species- and strain-resolution bridge with the ecotype assignments, then test whether the functional differences detected in gene-content ecotypes predict contamination-associated community scores after coverage adjustment. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- Repeat the taxonomic-resolution comparison with COG L, COG V, and composite LV features separately, testing whether mobile-element and defense signals remain detectable as clade specificity increases and mapped abundance decreases. [src: cog_analysis]
