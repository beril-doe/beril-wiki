# Taxonomic Resolution Controls Detectable Functional Associations

Taxonomic resolution controls which genotype-to-function relationships can be detected because aggregation determines both how community taxa are bridged to pangenome clades and how much abundance is retained for functional scoring. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] Mapped coverage—the fraction of community abundance linked to the functional-feature construction—can therefore make community-level associations reflect representation rather than biological shifts. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] Functional redundancy, in which different taxa provide overlapping functions, is a compatible hypothesis: environmental selection may change community membership while aggregated functional summaries remain stable, but this dataset does not directly demonstrate redundancy. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

The [functional_dark_matter__REPORT](../summaries/functional_dark_matter__REPORT.md) **supports** this coverage-and-resolution interpretation from a complementary genome-centered analysis. Across 48 Fitness Browser organisms, 57,011 of 228,709 genes (24.9%) were classified as dark, but dark-gene fractions ranged from more than 35% to less than 15%, a pattern interpreted as annotation-depth variation rather than established differences in functional content. [^functional_dark_matter] Full GTDB r214 pangenome propagation increased dark-gene conservation coverage from 32,791 (57.5%) to 37,997 (66.6%) by recovering 5,206 additional genes, showing that detectable functional inference changes substantially with database and taxonomic coverage. [^functional_dark_matter] The report therefore **refines** the existing claim: taxonomic resolution is not only an aggregation choice, but also a function of annotation depth, ortholog-group breadth, and which organisms are represented in the reference collection. [^functional_dark_matter]

The ENIGMA workflow linked [enigma-coral](../entities/enigma-coral.md) geochemistry, community composition, [kbase-ke-pangenome](../entities/kbase-ke-pangenome.md) clades, and [eggnog](../entities/eggnog.md)-derived functional proxies across 108 samples. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] The geochemistry matrix had shape `(108, 49)`, the community data contained 41,711 taxon rows, and the analysis covered 212 distinct communities and 1,392 distinct genera. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] The analysis is summarized in [enigma_contamination_functional_potential__REPORT](../summaries/enigma_contamination_functional_potential__REPORT.md), while the related plant-association analysis is summarized in [plant_microbiome_ecotypes__REPORT](../summaries/plant_microbiome_ecotypes__REPORT.md). [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

## Resolution, Coverage, and Bridge Constraints

The taxonomy bridge parsed 27,690 GTDB species rows, mapped 530 genera, left 862 genera unmapped, and produced 530 strict clades, 7,380 relaxed clades, and 150 species-proxy clades restricted to genera mapping to exactly one clade. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] It generated 8,242 genus-to-clade rows through many-to-many expansion. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] Ambiguity was substantial: 380 mapped genera were multi-clade genera, and the maximum was 433 clades per genus. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] The most ambiguous genera were `pseudomonas` with 433 clades, `streptomyces` with 378, `prevotella` with 358, `streptococcus` with 214, and `mycobacterium` with 186. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

Strict and relaxed modes retained a mean mapped abundance fraction of 0.343, whereas the species-proxy mode retained a mean of 0.031. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] Across functional-feature rows, strict and relaxed modes had a mapped-abundance range of 0.031 to 0.854, while the species-proxy mode had a range of 0.0002 to 0.543. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] The species-proxy mode retained 150 unique-clade genera compared with 530 mapped genera overall, and the analysis generated 3,630 functional feature rows across mapping modes. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] Thus, the more restrictive unique-clade mode reduced taxonomic ambiguity but also discarded much of the abundance available for functional inference. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

The functional-dark-matter analysis **supports** this trade-off between specificity and coverage. Its environmental analyses tested 151 accessory dark-gene clusters across 31 species, but only 10 of 137 clusters with environmental-category tests were significant at FDR < 0.05 and only 1 of 67 showed significant AlphaEarth embedding divergence. [^functional_dark_matter] A pre-registered lab–field mapping found 29 of 47 testable clusters concordant (61.7%), while NMDC, the National Microbiome Data Collaborative dataset, independently matched 5 of 6 carrier genera and confirmed 4 of 4 testable abiotic predictions. [^functional_dark_matter] These results show that finer gene- and carrier-level resolution can recover environmentally structured signals, but sparse metadata and incomplete mapping constrain how broadly they can be detected. [^functional_dark_matter]

The source taxonomy table provided labels through Genus but no species or strain labels, preventing direct species-level bridge testing in this dataset slice. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] Genus-level aggregation is therefore necessary but potentially masking: strain-level adaptation may be hidden when distinct functional states are combined within a genus. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] The bridge ambiguities can redistribute functional features across clades and weaken genus-level resolution of species- or strain-specific environmental responses. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

The plant-association analysis **supports** this masking concern but also **refines** its expected magnitude. Across 607 root, rhizosphere, and phyllosphere species, the refined marker panel detected compartment separation with PERMANOVA R² = 0.071, while location-only db-RDA R² = 0.060; 84% of the PERMANOVA signal was attributable to location and 16% to dispersion. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The earlier R² = 0.527 fell to 0.072 after excluding three genome-rich species per compartment, an 86% loss, indicating that apparent broad functional separation can be dominated by taxonomic sampling. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] This supports the existing concern that aggregation can conceal or manufacture associations, while showing that even refined compartment effects may be small rather than universally strong. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The [ecotype_functional_differentiation__REPORT](../summaries/ecotype_functional_differentiation__REPORT.md) **supports** this masking concern from a complementary within-species analysis: valid gene-content ecotypes were detected in 12 of 15 sampled species (80%), and 170 of 257 COG-category tests (66.1%) were significant after BH-FDR correction across those species. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] This strengthens the case that finer within-species structure can carry detectable functional differences, while not showing that those differences are ecological adaptations. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation]

The plant analysis further **refines** the inference by finding that marker associations were often genus-scale: after genus-cluster-robust modeling, 8/14 markers passed the stated significance and effect-size thresholds, but within-genus label shuffling retained only nitrogen fixation, ACC deaminase, and T3SS. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Its phylogenetic-tree coverage was also limited: only 18 of 65 species with at least 20 genomes had tree distance data, and only 5 of 17 testable species passed Bonferroni-corrected Fisher tests. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Thus, finer resolution can reveal structure, but the detectable signal and its ecological interpretation remain constrained by within-genus controls and coverage. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The cross-species [cog_analysis__REPORT](../summaries/cog_analysis__REPORT.md) **refines** the resolution argument. Across 32 species spanning 9 phyla and 357,623 genes, novel or singleton genes were enriched in mobile-element COG L by +10.88%, defense COG V by +2.83%, and unknown-function COG S by +1.64%, while core genes were depleted in translation COG J by -4.65% and several metabolic categories. [^cog_analysis] COG, or Clusters of Orthologous Groups, categories therefore retain systematic core-versus-novel functional structure, but genus-level or broad-category aggregation can conceal structured differences in gene novelty and ecological function even when the signal is consistent across taxa. [^cog_analysis]

Functional-dark-matter conservation analysis **supports** the importance of choosing an informative taxonomic scale but **refines** the meaning of conservation. An initial eggNOG breadth classification assigned 30,721 of 30,756 dark-gene clusters (99.9%) to universal breadth and was poorly discriminative; species counts ranged from 1 to 33, with median 1 and mean 2.2, and the species-count variant had Spearman ρ = 0.982 with the original ranking but only 62% top-50 overlap and 58% top-100 overlap. [^functional_dark_matter] After GTDB r214 propagation, root ortholog groups were distributed across kingdom-, class-, family-, genus-, mobile-, phylum-, order-, and species-level tiers, with 55.9% classified as kingdom-level and 0.5% as species-level. [^functional_dark_matter] A broad taxonomic label can therefore indicate conservation without identifying the resolution at which ecological function is expressed. [^functional_dark_matter]

## Coverage-Sensitive Contamination–Defense Associations

The contamination index combined arsenic, cadmium, chromium, copper, lead, nickel, uranium, and zinc using per-metal `log1p` z-scoring followed by a row-wise mean. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] Across the 108 samples, it ranged from -0.448 to 3.836, with median -0.271 and IQR [-0.363, 0.053]. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

Predeclared genus-level Spearman tests of site defense score against contamination were null in both broad mapping modes. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] In the relaxed-all-clades mode, rho was 0.0587, the 95% bootstrap confidence interval was [-0.128, 0.250], Spearman p was 0.546, and FDR q was 0.862. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] In the strict-single-clade mode, rho was 0.0682, the 95% bootstrap confidence interval was [-0.111, 0.253], Spearman p was 0.483, and FDR q was 0.849. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] FDR, or false-discovery-rate correction, controls the expected proportion of false discoveries among results called significant. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

The confirmatory null was unchanged across four contamination definitions: the composite all-metals index, uranium-only, the top-3-variance-metals index, and the first principal component of metal z-scores. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] All 8 confirmatory variant tests remained non-significant after FDR, with q = 0.546 across the tests, including uranium-only. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

Coverage-aware modeling **refines** the confirmatory null by showing that detectable signal depends on both mapped abundance and mapping mode. In adjusted ordinary least-squares models, adjusting for contamination, depth, latitude, longitude, and mapped abundance fraction, the relaxed-all-clades defense-score beta was 0.000751 with 95% bootstrap confidence interval [0.000224, 0.001779], p = 0.000398, and FDR q = 0.0462. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] The corresponding strict-single-clade estimate was beta = 0.000640 with 95% bootstrap confidence interval [0.000169, 0.001538], p = 0.00354, and FDR q = 0.130. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] These results support a coverage-sensitive defense association in the relaxed mode, but do not establish a robust association across both mapping modes after global FDR. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

The functional-dark-matter report **supports** treating coverage as an inferential constraint rather than as a neutral preprocessing detail. Its Fitness Browser collection contained 37/48 Pseudomonadota organisms, and the top 500 candidates included 417 Gammaproteobacteria, 71 Alphaproteobacteria, 17 Deltaproteobacteria, 12 Bacteroidetes, 10 Betaproteobacteria, 5 Firmicutes, 2 Cyanobacteria, and 0 Archaea, Actinobacteria, or Epsilonproteobacteria. [^functional_dark_matter] The report explicitly notes that uneven condition coverage and taxonomic composition can make organisms with deeper profiles appear more informative. [^functional_dark_matter] This **refines** interpretation of the ENIGMA mode difference: apparent resolution effects can combine retained abundance, taxonomic representation, annotation depth, and experimental coverage.

Fraction-aware adjusted models showed the same directional pattern without resolving mode dependence. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] The relaxed-all-clades beta was 0.000607 with 95% bootstrap confidence interval [0.000213, 0.001221], p = 0.00144, and FDR q = 0.0838, while the strict-single-clade beta was 0.000566 with 95% bootstrap confidence interval [0.000214, 0.001113], p = 0.00548, and FDR q = 0.130. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

The species-proxy mode further weakened detectable association: its defense trend was positive but non-significant, with rho = 0.169 and Spearman p = 0.081. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] No high-coverage test was feasible at mapped-abundance fraction >= 0.25 because of low retained coverage, and the corresponding high-coverage analysis had n = 1. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] This is sensitivity evidence that increased clade specificity can reduce power when it sharply lowers coverage, rather than evidence against contamination-linked functional differentiation. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

## Robustness and Functional-Redundancy Interpretation

Fraction-aware analyses used 212 sample-fraction rows, consisting of 106 rows for the 0.2_micron_filter fraction and 106 rows for the 10_micron_filter fraction. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] Within-fraction defense tests were non-significant in relaxed mode, with p = 0.767 for the 0.2-micron fraction and p = 0.898 for the 10_micron fraction, and in strict mode, with p = 0.780 and p = 0.793, respectively. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] This supports the interpretation that the aggregate monotonic defense signal is not robustly reproducible within individual fraction strata. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

High-coverage subsets produced nominal defense p-values of 0.0207 for relaxed mode and 0.00980 for strict mode, but their global FDR q-values were 0.301 and 0.189, respectively. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] Most non-defense outcomes remained non-significant, although site stress score in the strict high-coverage subset had rho = 0.2489 and p = 0.0407. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] These results refine the interpretation of taxonomic-resolution effects: changing the mapping mode changes both the functional score's coverage and the evidential threshold reached by downstream models. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

The plant analysis **supports** the hypothesis that aggregation can conceal environmental differentiation: its 11.7% Jaccard overlap between 487 pangenome-derived plant genera and 438 MGnify rhizosphere genera reflects different isolation-metadata and metagenomic-detection processes, while compartment labels were available for only 7,995 of 293,059 genomes (2.7%). [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Its corrected complementarity analysis found co-occurring genus pairs slightly less complementary than random pairs, with Cohen’s d approximately -0.4 and permutation p < 0.001, rather than demonstrating strong functional complementarity. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] This refines functional-redundancy interpretations: stable aggregate functions may reflect taxonomic or measurement coverage, but the plant result does not establish redundancy as the mechanism. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

Together, the null confirmatory results, coverage-sensitive exploratory effects, and weak within-fraction reproducibility support the hypothesis that coarse functional summaries can conceal environmental selection or confound it with represented abundance. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] Environmental differentiation may instead be detectable in taxonomic composition or gene content before it is visible in aggregated categories, connecting this result to [ecotype-environment-gene-content](ecotype-environment-gene-content.md). [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] The broad conclusion is not that contamination has no ecological or functional effect. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] Rather, this ENIGMA subset did not show a robust community-level shift in inferred stress-related functional potential when coarse COG-category proxies were aggregated at genus resolution. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] The result is compatible with contamination-driven taxonomic turnover that is functionally redundant or operates at species, strain, or pathway resolution finer than the current mapping. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

COG provides broad functional categories rather than curated metal-resistance pathways. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] The COG analysis **refines** this limitation rather than contradicting the ENIGMA null: COG annotations covered approximately 70% of genes, and its 32-species comparison found structured enrichment of mobile, defense, and unknown functions among novel or singleton genes alongside conserved metabolic and housekeeping functions among core genes. [^cog_analysis] Thus, broad COG scores can contain real, taxonomically widespread functional structure while remaining incomplete and too coarse to establish a contamination association. [^cog_analysis]

The ecotype analysis **refines** this interpretation by showing that functional differentiation can be widespread within species, but its use of gene-content clusters and COG profiles also retained important uncertainties: only approximately 38% of gene clusters had COG annotations, and without within-species phylogenetic controls it could not distinguish ecological adaptation from phylogenetic or demographic substructure. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation]

Functional-dark-matter prioritization further **supports** pathway- and gene-level follow-up as a way to test whether broad categories conceal signal. GapMind identified 1,256 organism–pathway pairs across 44 species in which nearly complete pathways co-occurred with strongly fitness-active dark genes, but these were organism-level co-occurrences rather than direct gene-to-enzyme assignments. [^functional_dark_matter] Supplementary domain matching produced 42,239 gene–pathway candidates across 3,186 dark genes, including 5,398 high-confidence EC-prefix matches, 4,687 medium-confidence Pfam-family matches, and 32,154 low-confidence keyword matches. [^functional_dark_matter] This **refines** the redundancy hypothesis: pathway-level and gene-level resolution may recover ecologically relevant differences, but computational assignments require experimental validation.

Composite COG assignments containing multiple functional letters were treated as biologically meaningful rather than annotation artifacts in the cross-species analysis; the LV composite showed +0.34% enrichment with 76% consistency, and composite categories were counted once per gene rather than split across component letters. [^cog_analysis] This **supports** retaining biologically meaningful composite categories in future resolution comparisons while testing whether their interpretation changes with taxonomic aggregation. [^cog_analysis]

This finding supports [pangenome-integration](pangenome-integration.md), because bridge ambiguity and unmapped genera directly determine the fraction of community abundance contributing to inferred functions. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] It also connects to [callability-limited-comparative-inference](callability-limited-comparative-inference.md) and [environmental-resistome](environmental-resistome.md), since missing taxonomic detail can limit comparability and detection of within-species functional differences. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] It supports [multi-omics-integration](multi-omics-integration.md) by demonstrating that integrating geochemistry, community composition, taxonomy, pangenome clades, and functional annotations does not eliminate coverage, mapping, or resolution constraints. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

## Tensions

The relaxed coverage-adjusted model yielded FDR q = 0.0462, whereas the strict coverage-adjusted model yielded FDR q = 0.130, and both confirmatory genus-level modes were null after predeclared testing. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] This is a methodological tension between a coverage-sensitive exploratory association and a resolution- and specification-robust association, not evidence that the estimates should be averaged. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] The difference could reflect retained abundance, bridge ambiguity, covariate sensitivity, taxonomic aggregation, or a combination of these factors. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

The functional-dark-matter report adds a related coverage tension: 29 of 47 testable lab–field clusters were concordant (61.7%), but the one-sided binomial test gave p = 0.072, whereas Fisher’s combined probability across 47 individual tests gave p = 0.031. [^functional_dark_matter] NMDC confirmed all 4 testable pre-registered abiotic predictions, but the report cautions that 441 of 449 exploratory trait tests reached FDR < 0.05, largely because of compositional coupling. [^functional_dark_matter] These results **refine** rather than resolve the ENIGMA tension: finer-resolution validation can produce coherent directional evidence, while broad or exploratory associations remain sensitive to coverage and compositional structure.

The within-species ecotype evidence supports the premise that finer functional structure exists, but it does not resolve whether that structure is ecological or phylogenetic because the ecotype analysis lacked within-species phylogenetic controls. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] This limits direct extrapolation from gene-content ecotypes to contamination-associated community functions: the ENIGMA analysis is null or mode-sensitive at genus resolution, whereas the ecotype analysis detects functional differentiation within species. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] [^ecotype_functional_differentiation]

The plant-association evidence **refines** rather than resolves this tension: compartment effects remained statistically significant but explained only 0.060 of location-only variance, and the earlier large effect was reduced to R² = 0.072 after removal of genome-rich species. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The same report found 78.7% of 1,115 plant-associated species classified as dual-nature under a refined marker scheme, but categorical assignments misclassified all four neutral controls in a curated 18-organism panel. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] These results show that finer or richer marker resolution can produce extensive apparent functional structure while still being sensitive to taxonomic composition, genome size, and classifier definitions. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The COG comparison found consistent core-versus-novel functional partitioning across 32 species, whereas the ENIGMA analysis did not find a robust contamination-associated shift in coarse COG proxies at genus resolution. [^cog_analysis] [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential] This is not a direct contradiction because the studies test different signals: broad evolutionary partitioning by gene novelty versus a site-level ecological association after taxonomic bridging. [^cog_analysis] [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

## Open Directions

- Obtain species- or strain-level ENIGMA or metagenomic data and repeat strict, relaxed, and unique-clade bridge analyses to test whether contamination-linked functional associations emerge below Genus resolution. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential]
- Replace broad COG-fraction proxies with curated metal-stress gene sets and pathway-level summaries, then test whether higher-resolution functional definitions distinguish taxonomic turnover from functional redundancy and whether associations persist after mapped-coverage adjustment. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential]
- Quantify the contribution of the 862 unmapped genera and expand the genus-to-clade bridge, evaluating whether missing abundance explains differences between mapping modes, weak broad functional signals, and score instability. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential]
- Fit mixed-effects or hierarchical models including depth, location cluster, sampling date, and compositional controls to test whether the relaxed-mode defense association persists under richer site structure. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential]
- Compare aggregate, within-fraction, and high-coverage analyses using the same resolution-specific samples and bootstrap procedure to determine whether coverage or taxonomic ambiguity is the dominant source of sensitivity. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential]
- Overlay core-genome phylogenetic trees on the 12-species ecotype assignments and repeat COG enrichment tests to separate within-species ecological differentiation from phylogenetic structure. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- Extend the species- and strain-resolution bridge with the ecotype assignments, then test whether functional differences detected in gene-content ecotypes predict contamination-associated community scores after coverage adjustment. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- Repeat the taxonomic-resolution comparison with COG L, COG V, and composite LV features separately, testing whether mobile-element and defense signals remain detectable as clade specificity increases and mapped abundance decreases. [^cog_analysis]
- Add plant-compartment and host metadata to species- and strain-level bridge analyses, then test whether the small compartment signal and the 11.7% pangenome–MGnify genus overlap are explained by taxonomic sampling, detection process, or genuine functional differentiation. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
- Expand BERDL phylogenetic-tree coverage and apply within-species phylogenetic controls to the plant-associated ecotypes, especially the 47 of 65 candidate species lacking tree data, before interpreting subclade functional associations. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
- Use the functional-dark-matter covering sets and top candidate organisms to design cross-taxonomic validation experiments, testing whether gene-level fitness phenotypes and pathway predictions remain concordant when reference taxa, condition coverage, and conservation breadth are varied. [^functional_dark_matter]
- Compare detailed results with [enigma_contamination_functional_potential__REPORT](../summaries/enigma_contamination_functional_potential__REPORT.md) and integrate the workflow with [multi-omics-integration](multi-omics-integration.md) to quantify how geochemistry, community composition, pangenome mapping, and functional annotation jointly constrain inference. [^enigma_contamination_functional_potential]
