# Intrinsic versus acquired resistance gene conservation

This page covers intrinsic versus acquired resistance gene conservation. Across several projects, antimicrobial resistance (AMR) genes differ in core versus accessory status by mechanism: mechanism is associated with conservation [src: amr_fitness_cost], and AMR genes overall are less often core than the pangenome average. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] Phylogenetic-signal results for metal AMR in bacteria fit the same picture: constitutive core resistance looks mainly vertically inherited, while accessory accumulation looks shaped by horizontal transfer. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] Much of the intrinsic/acquired reading is interpretation of conservation patterns, not direct measurement of mobility. The page stops at gene-level mobility mechanisms.

## Literature Context

The candidate papers are genome-level studies of single taxa. They bear on the hub's central idea that resistance genes split into a conserved intrinsic set and a mobile accessory set. None of them tests the split across bacteria as a whole. In Achromobacter xylosoxidans, isolates carry intrinsic resistance to several antibiotics and can also acquire resistance determinants. The resistome is described as limited, and the non-core resistance genes were primarily associated with the mobilome, which points to horizontal gene transfer as a shaping force [PMID 42371691](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42371691/). Resistance-associated mutations in the AxyZ efflux pump regulator, together with bla AXC-1, were found only in clinical genomes [PMID 42371691](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42371691/). In Acinetobacter baumannii, which is notable for intrinsic multidrug resistance and genomic plasticity, a comparison of 20 carbapenem-resistant clinical strains found an open pangenome. Resistance and virulence genes made up 0.61% to 2.23% of that pangenome. Oxacillinase genes and efflux pumps mainly mediated carbapenem resistance. Plasmids were highly diverse, and the dissemination of resistance genes was linked to insertion sequences [PMID 41051204](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41051204/). In Salmonella enterica, 28 isolates from animals in Brazil had only one-third of genes classified as core. The authors saw a central role for mobile genetic elements in shaping the accessory genome. They detected 58 resistance-associated genes, both intrinsic and acquired, and noted that the study was limited by sample size and geographic scope [PMID 42103022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42103022/). In Pseudomonas aeruginosa clone C, the core-encoded PaftsH1 supports growth and intrinsic antibiotic resistance. The horizontally acquired xenolog PaftsH2 cannot functionally replace it. The authors trace this to the membrane-cytoplasmic linker, and the work is a mechanistic study, not a pangenome survey [PMID 38176647](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38176647/).

The corpus finding that AMR genes are depleted from the core genome, at 30.3% core against a 46.8% baseline, is consistent with these taxon-level reports. In each, accessory or non-core resistance genes are tied to mobile elements and intrinsic determinants sit in the chromosome. The Achromobacter, Salmonella and Acinetobacter papers support the corpus reading that acquired resistance is accessory and mobility-associated [PMID 42371691](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42371691/) [PMID 42103022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42103022/) [PMID 41051204](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41051204/). The Pseudomonas result agrees with the hub's intrinsic-core versus acquired-accessory contrast for one gene family. In that case the acquired copy is also functionally distinct [PMID 38176647](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38176647/).

The corpus extends this work in scale and in mechanism-level resolution. It gives per-mechanism core fractions, reports the OR=0.49 depletion, and compares soil with clinical species. The candidate papers do not provide these. The Achromobacter finding that clinical genomes carry specific resistance alleles fits the corpus claim of clinical accessory enrichment, but it comes from one species and does not test that claim directly. The corpus's pan-bacterial dichotomy therefore remains novel relative to this evidence. Its intrinsic and acquired labels are still inferred from conservation patterns, and the candidate papers do not add direct measurements of gene location for the atlas clusters. They contain no result in tension with the hub.

The hub's core-versus-accessory inference stays an interpretation of conservation patterns. The Achromobacter, Acinetobacter and Salmonella papers link accessory resistance to mobile elements within single taxa. They offer supporting but indirect evidence for the hub's open direction on testing mobile-element association.

## Overall depletion of AMR from the core genome

AMR genes are less conserved than the pangenome average. In the bacterial pangenome atlas, 30.3% of AMR genes are core versus a 46.8% baseline (OR=0.49, chi-squared=23,117, p≈0). [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] (OR = odds ratio.) The auxiliary genome is 2.2x enriched for AMR, at 33.6% versus 15.3%. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] In a separate pangenome inventory (the prophage_amr_comobilization report), AMR gene clusters are 69.7% accessory and 36.1% singleton. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization] In a paired test of 4,252 species with ≥5 AMR clusters each, 63.7% had AMR less core than their species baseline (Wilcoxon p=1.1e-130, mean difference -0.102). [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] The atlas report says its analysis across 14,723 species shows the OR=0.49 depletion is a universal bacterial pattern, not specific to individual pathogens. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] It also says the accessory enrichment agrees with studies of Neisseria gonorrhoeae, Salmonella and Corynebacterium striatum, and describes the confirmed universal pattern as spanning 27K species. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] The report gives 14,723 species in one place and 27K in the other, and the claims do not reconcile the two scopes. An example at the extreme is Klebsiella pneumoniae, with 1,115 AMR clusters, only 7 core, across 13,637 genomes. [src: discoveries]

## Mechanism and gene family

Mechanism strongly predicts conservation status in amr_fitness_cost (χ² = 69.3, p = 1.4×10⁻¹³). [src: amr_fitness_cost] Metal resistance genes are 44% accessory, efflux 13% and enzymatic inactivation 16%. [src: amr_fitness_cost]

The atlas gives per-mechanism hit counts, share of hits and core fraction:

- Beta-lactamase: 12,824 hits (15.4%), 54.9% core. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]
- Cell wall modification: 4,964 hits (6.0%), 45.2% core. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]
- Efflux: 14,338 hits (17.3%), 30.9% core. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]
- Enzymatic inactivation: 15,755 hits (19.0%), 28.2% core. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]
- Other/Unclassified: 18,448 hits (22.2%), 24.0% core. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]
- Target modification: 6,955 hits (8.4%), 19.6% core. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]
- Oxidoreductase: 6,852 hits (8.3%), 15.4% core. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]
- Regulatory: 2,848 hits (3.4%), 6.5% core. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]

Beta-lactamases are enriched for core status against baseline (p=7.7e-74), which the report describes as intrinsic resistance in the chromosomal backbone. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] Efflux splits: intrinsic pumps such as emhABC are >95% core, while acquired efflux genes are accessory. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] Regulatory genes are only 6.5% core, and known mobile elements blaTEM, tet(C) and ant(2'')-Ia are 0% core. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]

The atlas report draws the contrast explicitly. Intrinsic genes (ampC, emhABC, rox) are vertically inherited and present in >95% of genomes within a species. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] Acquired genes (blaTEM, tet cassettes, ant(2'')-Ia) are fully accessory, often 0% core, and appear as singletons or in a minority of genomes. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] The report describes the acquired genes as horizontally transferred and spreading under antibiotic selection. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] It claims novelty for showing this mechanism-specific dichotomy at pan-bacterial scale. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]

The pattern also appears in one organism. In Desulfovibrio vulgaris Hildenborough (DvH), lab-antibiotic genes (73.4%) and heavy-metal-resistance genes (71.2%) are the least conserved, below the 76.3% baseline. The report suggests these are recently acquired adaptive traits on mobile genetic elements. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]

## Phylogenetic signal of core and accessory metal AMR

In the microbeatlas_metal_ecology report, bacterial core AMR fraction has Pagel's λ of 0.441 (p = 1.8×10⁻⁸ by likelihood ratio test, LRT) across 606 genera. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] The report orders bacterial metal-AMR signal as core fraction above type diversity above cluster count. It reads this as mainly vertical inheritance of constitutive (core) resistance, with horizontal transfer and local metal exposure shaping accessory accumulation and type expansion. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] It considers core fraction λ exceeding total cluster count λ consistent with Gillieatt & Coleman (2024), who found core and accessory metal AMR differ in association with mobile genetic elements (MGE). [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] This reading is interpretation; it **supports** the mechanism-level picture above but does not measure mobility directly.

## Ecology of the split

Soil AMR is predominantly core, while clinical AMR is accessory. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] Clinical species have 68% accessory AMR versus 43% in soil species. [src: discoveries] The environmental report reads this as ancient chromosomal resistance in soil and mobile resistance in the clinic. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] It also reads soil species' fewer, more intrinsic AMR genes as an ancient baseline resistome, and clinical enrichment above it as anthropogenic selection. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] It presents this as confirming the core-intrinsic and accessory-acquired pattern that Jiang et al. (2024) saw in river metagenomes. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]

The report also finds soil and aquatic metal-resistance genes more often core, which it takes to suggest ancient adaptation. Clinical and gut efflux and target-modification genes are more often accessory, which it takes to reflect recent HGT-driven accumulation (HGT = horizontal gene transfer). [src: amr_environmental_resistome] It links this to the mechanism result. Efflux genes are core because they serve host-associated organisms across conditions. Metal resistance is accessory because it is needed only in metal-rich environments. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] This **refines** the mechanism result with an ecological explanation, which is the report's interpretation.

## Acquisition history, not cost

Conservation does not track fitness cost in the amr_fitness_cost report: mechanism does not predict cost [src: amr_cofitness_networks], and core and accessory genes show no cost difference. [src: amr_fitness_cost] The report concludes that conservation reflects how and when genes were acquired, not what they cost. [src: amr_fitness_cost, discoveries] The amr_cofitness_networks report restates this. Cost depends on the organism's gene content and growth context, and conservation on the mechanism's acquisition history. [src: amr_cofitness_networks] The amr_fitness_cost report suggests that retention forces, such as horizontal transfer frequency and environment-specific advantage, differ from the forces governing cost. [src: amr_fitness_cost] For the equal costs, it proposes that horizontal transfer may capture genes already cost-optimized in donor lineages, or that recipients compensate rapidly. [src: amr_fitness_cost] It also proposes that efflux is often chromosomally encoded as part of the general stress response and stays core, while metal-resistance operons are frequently on mobile elements and stay accessory, at the same modest cost once established. [src: amr_fitness_cost]

## Tensions

- Acquired genes might be expected to track lineage less than intrinsic genes. Yet non-core (putatively acquired) AMR genes show stronger phylogenetic signal (median r = 0.222) than core genes (median r = 0.117; paired t-test t = -8.35, p = 7.0e-16, n = 489). [src: amr_strain_variation] The report notes that near-universal core genes have near-zero Jaccard distances and little variance. This suppresses Mantel r and partly explains the lower core signal independent of biology. [src: amr_strain_variation]
- Core is not fully fixed within species. Atlas core AMR genes are 77.3% fixed and 22.7% variable. [src: amr_strain_variation] Auxiliary genes are 57.3% variable and 42.7% rare. [src: amr_strain_variation] Singletons are 21.3% variable and 78.7% rare. [src: amr_strain_variation]
- The atlas report claims universality. It also cautions that some singleton AMR clusters may be annotation artifacts. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] The per-gene and per-phylum tests are exploratory and hypothesis-generating. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] No Bonferroni or FDR (false discovery rate) correction was applied, because primary p-values were extreme. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]

## Open Directions

- Test whether "core" intrinsic genes sit in chromosomal backbone context and accessory ones near mobile elements. Use gene-neighborhood data on the same atlas clusters. This would turn the intrinsic/acquired labels from inference into measurement.
- Repeat the soil versus clinical core fraction comparison after excluding singleton clusters. This would show how much annotation artifacts drive the accessory excess.
- Fit mechanism, environment and phylum jointly in one model of core status. This would separate the ecological explanation from the mechanism effect.
- Compare costs of recently transferred and long-resident accessory genes. This would test the donor cost-optimization hypothesis.
- Re-run the phylogenetic-signal comparison with a metric not suppressed by near-universal genes. This would settle whether acquired genes truly carry stronger signal.
- Test the vertical-inheritance reading of core metal AMR by comparing MGE association of core and accessory metal AMR clusters in the 606 genera with signal. This would close the gap between λ ordering and direct mobility evidence.
