# Evolutionary loss of costly, non-conserved genes

Costly, non-conserved genes are a distinct pangenomic class whose laboratory burden and limited distribution suggest ongoing evolutionary instability rather than persistent core metabolism. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] The analysis examines 5,526 genes in this class among 142,190 genes from 43 bacteria. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] The underlying evidence and methods are summarized in [[summaries/costly_dispensable_genes__REPORT]]. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

## Central claim

The report supports the interpretation that many costly+dispensable genes are horizontally acquired genomic material—including insertion sequences, prophage remnants, transposases, and defense systems—that imposes a fitness burden and is subsequently vulnerable to gene loss. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] This interpretation **refines** [[concepts/gene-essentiality]] by separating laboratory-measured burden from long-term pangenome conservation: a gene can reduce laboratory fitness without being evolutionarily retained across strains. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

The evidence is strongest for mobile and recently acquired DNA, but the report does not establish that every costly+dispensable gene is nonfunctional or destined for deletion. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] Instead, the findings suggest the hypothesis that selection removes costly accessory genes unless environmental or community conditions periodically reward their functions. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] New evidence that trade-off genes are 1.29 times more likely to be core than non-core (odds ratio 1.29, p=1.2e-44) **refines** this interpretation: costly, condition-dependent genes are not uniformly unstable, and some are retained broadly because their costs and benefits vary across conditions. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]

The cross-project synthesis **supports** the costly-versus-dispensable selection signature: it identifies the 5,526 costly-and-dispensable genes as candidates for ongoing gene loss, while treating 28,017 genes that are both costly in the laboratory and conserved as stronger evidence for retention under purifying selection in environments not represented by the assay. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] This distinction is evidence for, rather than a direct measurement of, natural selection and therefore **supports** the cautious interpretation above. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

The broader fitness–conservation analysis **refines** this class-based interpretation: across approximately 194,000 genes from 43 bacteria, conservation increased with fitness importance, but the relationship was weak, with essential genes 82% core versus always-neutral genes 66% core and breadth of fitness effects showing Spearman rho=0.086, p=8.1e-230. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] In a separate integration of Fitness Browser genes with pangenome clusters, essential genes were 86.1% core versus 81.2% for non-essential genes, with a median odds ratio of 1.56 across 33 organisms; 18 of 33 organisms showed significant enrichment after Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate correction. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] This **supports** the direction of the fitness–conservation association while **refining** its interpretation: essentiality is associated with only modestly greater core conservation, so costly+dispensable genes remain a specific candidate class for loss rather than evidence that laboratory fitness alone predicts evolutionary fate. [src: fitness_effects_conservation, conservation_vs_fitness]

## Evidence for mobile-element-associated burden

Costly+dispensable genes are 7.45 times more likely than costly+conserved genes to contain mobile-element keywords such as transposase, integrase, phage, IS element, recombinase, or prophage (OR=7.45, p=4.6e-71). [src: costly_dispensable_genes] The SEED category “Phages, Prophages, Transposable elements, Plasmids” is enriched 11.7-fold in the costly+dispensable group (FDR=1.3e-17), where FDR means false discovery rate. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] “Virulence” is enriched 26.7-fold (FDR=5.6e-14), although that result is based on small counts of 21 versus 4 genes. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

These enrichments **support** [[concepts/mobile-element-associated-fitness-burden]] and **refine** [[concepts/horizontal-gene-transfer-driven-innovation]] by indicating that horizontal acquisition can produce genes that are both potentially useful in particular settings and burdensome in laboratory conditions. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

## Evidence for recent acquisition and weak retention

Only 50.8% of costly+dispensable genes have SEED annotations, compared with 74.9% of costly+conserved genes. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] KEGG annotation rates are 20.0% and 42.7%, respectively. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] The costly+dispensable group contains 44.5% orphan genes with no ortholog group, compared with 13.1% among costly+conserved genes. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

The median ortholog breadth is 15 organisms for costly+dispensable genes and 31 organisms for costly+conserved genes (Mann–Whitney p=4.0e-99, rank-biserial r=0.233). [src: costly_dispensable_genes] Costly+dispensable genes also have a 24.2% singleton fraction, whereas no costly+conserved genes are singletons; this comparison is partly structural because core genes cannot be singletons by definition. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] Within the dispensable category, costly genes are only slightly more likely than neutral genes to be singletons (OR=1.09, p=0.02). [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

The costly+dispensable genes have a median length of 615 bp, compared with 765 bp for costly+conserved genes (p=4.2e-75, rank-biserial r=0.170). [src: costly_dispensable_genes] Their shorter length is consistent with the presence of IS elements and gene fragments, but does not by itself prove that individual genes are degraded remnants. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] Together, poor annotation, orphan status, narrow ortholog breadth, singleton frequency, and short length **support** [[concepts/gene-function-acquisition-depth]] and [[concepts/functional-dark-matter]] as relevant frameworks for distinguishing recent acquisition from deeply conserved cellular function. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

The fitness–conservation analysis **refines** this interpretation by showing that novel singleton genes had near-zero mean fitness in the tested laboratory assays, while noting that poor transposon coverage may also produce this apparent neutrality. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] This supports treating weak retention and measured neutrality as distinct observations rather than assuming that singleton genes are intrinsically unimportant. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] The new pangenome linkage analysis further **supports** the functional-dark-matter interpretation for a different conservation class: 44.7% of essential-unmapped genes and 38.2% of essential-auxiliary genes were hypothetical, compared with 13.0% of essential-core genes. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] Thus, poor annotation is not restricted to genes that are costly and dispensable; it can also mark essential genes whose sequence relationships or pangenome placement are unresolved. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]

## Depletion from core metabolism

Fourteen SEED top-level categories are significantly depleted in costly+dispensable genes at FDR < 0.05, including Protein Metabolism, Respiration, Carbohydrates, Amino Acids, Cofactors/Vitamins, Motility, Stress Response, and RNA Metabolism. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] By contrast, the 28,017 costly+conserved genes are maintained in categories that include core cellular functions such as Protein Metabolism, Respiration, and Motility. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

This contrast **supports** [[concepts/core-genome-burden-paradox]]: laboratory cost does not imply evolutionary loss when natural selection can offset that cost in environments not represented by the laboratory assay. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] The synthesis provides an aligned quantitative comparison: 24.4% of core genes show positive fitness when deleted, compared with 19.9% of accessory genes, and core genes are 1.78x more likely to have strong condition-specific phenotypes. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] Thus, the lower retention of costly+dispensable genes does not mean that accessory genes are generally more burdensome; it **supports** the existing interpretation that burden and conservation interact differently for core and accessory material. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

The fitness–conservation analysis **supports** this paradox and extends it beyond the costly+conserved comparison: core genes had heavier fitness-effect tails in both negative and positive directions, with 24.4% ever beneficial when deleted versus 19.9% of auxiliary genes. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] Core genes can therefore combine broad retention with condition-dependent costs and benefits, rather than representing uniformly low-burden functions. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

The core-gene trade-off analysis further **supports** this paradox, finding 25,271 trade-off genes overall (17.8% of genes examined) and showing that core genes are more likely than non-core genes to fall in this condition-dependent class. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] However, the effect is function-specific: core genes are more burdensome in Protein Metabolism, Motility, and RNA Metabolism, whereas non-core genes are more burdensome in Cell Wall functions, with differences of +6.2, +7.8, +12.9, and -14.1 percentage points, respectively. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] This **refines** the interpretation by showing that conservation does not impose one universal burden pattern across functional categories. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]

The comparison also **supports** [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] by showing that laboratory fitness measurements and pangenome conservation provide complementary evidence about gene persistence. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] The 28,017 Costly + Conserved genes are interpreted as genes retained despite laboratory cost, whereas the 5,526 Costly + Dispensable genes remain the more specific class associated with weak retention and possible loss. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] The new integration **supports** this complementarity by linking 177,863 Fitness Browser gene-to-cluster pairs, while also showing that pangenome coverage and gene-identifier compatibility can limit inference: 44 of 48 organisms were mapped, 33 entered downstream analysis, and ten were excluded for <90% DIAMOND coverage. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]

## Conditional persistence

The costly+dispensable class is not phenotypically inert: 14.1% of its genes have condition-specific phenotypes, compared with 16.7% of costly+conserved genes and 2.7% of neutral+dispensable genes. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] This result **supports** [[concepts/condition-specific-fitness]] and suggests the hypothesis that episodic benefits can delay the loss of genes that are costly under tested laboratory conditions. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

The condition-specific result does not demonstrate that these genes are maintained by selection in nature because the phenotype data are biased toward conditions that can be tested in the laboratory. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] A gene may therefore appear costly and dispensable in the available data while retaining value in unmeasured environments, hosts, or microbial communities. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] The stronger core enrichment among trade-off genes **supports** this caution and indicates that condition dependence can characterize conserved genes as well as accessory genes. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] The synthesis likewise states that laboratory measurements do not directly establish natural-environment fitness, **reinforcing** the need to treat evolutionary loss as a hypothesis rather than an observed fate. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

The new analysis **supports** this caution: strong condition-specific phenotypes occurred in 77.3% core genes versus 70.3% of genes without such annotations, and 4,450 genes (2.7%) were neutral overall but critical in one condition; these ephemeral-niche genes were more common among core genes (3.0%) than auxiliary genes (1.7%) or singleton genes (1.6%). [src: fitness_effects_conservation] This **refines** the proposed persistence mechanism by showing that conditional effects are not concentrated only in accessory genes. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] Essentiality evidence also **refines** the condition argument: the new essentiality calls were inferred from RB-TnSeq, a random-barcode transposon sequencing approach, under library-construction and growth conditions represented in the Fitness Browser, so they do not capture genes essential only under untested stresses. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]

## Organism-level heterogeneity

*Pseudomonas stutzeri* RCH2 contributes 21.5% of its genes as costly+dispensable, compared with 14.0% for the next organism, *Bacteroides thetaiotaomicron*. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] The report identifies a recent mobile-element invasion or strain-specific genomic expansion as possible explanations, but leaves the cause unresolved. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] This outlier **qualifies** the cross-organism interpretation and links the concept to [[concepts/subsurface-bacillota-specialization]]. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

## Interpretation and limits

The report interprets the costly+dispensable class as genomic debris or unstable accessory material produced by horizontal gene transfer, but the evidence is partly dependent on annotation, orthology, and laboratory-fitness definitions. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] “Costly” is defined as max_fit > 1 in any experiment, so classification can be driven by a single experiment and is sensitive to fitness-data noise. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] The pangenome classification is binary, so it does not capture the fraction of genomes carrying each gene. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

SEED and KEGG annotations cover only 56-79% of genes, and the unannotated fraction may have different functional profiles. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] The 90% identity DIAMOND threshold used to link Fitness Browser measurements with pangenome genes may miss recently acquired genes with low sequence similarity. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] Ortholog groups were assigned using bidirectional best hits across 48 organisms, so genes with orthologs outside that set may be misclassified as orphans. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

The 21.5% value for *Pseudomonas stutzeri* RCH2 may reflect strain-specific genomic features rather than a general pattern. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] These limitations **support** treating evolutionary loss as a testable interpretation rather than an established fate for all costly, non-conserved genes. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] The core-gene analysis adds a related limitation: its interpretation of costly-but-conserved genes as targets of purifying selection is based on laboratory fitness and conservation patterns, not direct measurement of natural selection. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]

The new comparison adds that fitness measurements were biased toward rich media and standard stresses, covered 43 bacteria primarily from Proteobacteria, and used single-gene knockouts that did not capture epistatic interactions. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] Its 16-percentage-point conservation gradient was statistically robust but fitness importance remained only a weak predictor of conservation, and singleton neutrality may reflect poor transposon coverage. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] These limitations **refine** the evolutionary-loss interpretation: laboratory burden and conservation are informative complementary signals, not a direct test of natural gene loss. The new linkage analysis adds that the main Escherichia coli clade was absent because it contained too many genomes, Keio mapped to a small clade at only 26.1% coverage, and Dyella79 was excluded because a locus-tag mismatch produced a 0% join rate. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] These coverage and identifier limitations further **support** caution when interpreting apparent conservation differences.

## Open Directions

- Reanalyze gene presence as a continuous fraction of pangenome genomes, rather than a binary core/accessory label, and test whether increasing laboratory burden predicts decreasing prevalence after controlling for organism and gene length. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]
- Re-link Fitness Browser genes to pangenomes with sequence-similarity-sensitive homology methods and manual synteny checks to determine how many apparent costly+dispensable genes were missed or misclassified by the 90% identity DIAMOND threshold. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]
- Longitudinally test whether costly+dispensable genes are lost, retained, or pseudogenized along closely related strain phylogenies, while comparing them with the more prevalent Costly + Conserved trade-off class. [src: costly_dispensable_genes, core_gene_tradeoffs]
- Test *Pseudomonas stutzeri* RCH2 against related strains using mobile-element annotation, genome architecture, and phylogenetic reconstruction to distinguish recent invasion from strain-specific genomic expansion. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]
- Measure the 14.1% condition-specific subset across community, host-associated, and environmental conditions using targeted competition assays to ask whether context-specific benefits explain persistence of costly+dispensable genes. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]
- Stratify the cost–conservation relationship by functional category and condition, especially Cell Wall versus Protein, Motility, and RNA Metabolism genes, to test whether the observed burden reversal replicates beyond the analyzed dataset. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]
- Characterize the 48 accessory fitness modules containing co-regulated functions exclusively in the flexible genome, testing whether their prevalence and laboratory burden predict persistence or loss across related strains. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]
- Test whether the weak fitness–conservation gradient persists after restricting analyses to well-covered genes and adding epistasis-aware, non-rich-media assays, particularly for singleton and condition-specific genes. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- Reanalyze the 3,683 essential-auxiliary genes and 1,259 essential-unmapped genes using continuous pangenome prevalence, identifier-resolved orthology, and condition-specific fitness assays to test whether poorly annotated essential genes are retained, replaced, or lost through distinct evolutionary routes. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]
- Extend the essentiality comparison beyond binary calls by correlating mean fitness effects with pangenome prevalence and testing whether genes with fitness < -2 under stress conditions show a different cost–conservation relationship. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]
