# Genomic under-representation limits inference about microbial functional potential

Public genome references do not represent all soil microbiomes evenly, so functional-potential inference can be limited by where genomes have been sampled and how completely they have been reconstructed. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] This concept connects the report to [[concepts/provenance-aware-resource-discovery]], [[concepts/environment-embedding-geography]], and [[concepts/genomic-under-representation]]. The [[summaries/truly_dark_genes__REPORT]] adds a complementary annotation-level estimate: persistent hypothetical genes remain after modern reannotation, even among genes with database links. [src: truly_dark_genes] The new [[summaries/functional_dark_matter__REPORT]] **supports** this sampling-and-annotation interpretation by identifying 57,011 dark genes among 228,709 genes across 48 organisms, while cautioning that dark-gene fractions ranging from more than 35% to less than 15% may reflect annotation-depth variation rather than established differences in functional content. [src: functional_dark_matter]

## Evidence from the Genomic Discovery Index

The report introduces the Genomic Discovery Index (GDI), defined as OTU Richness / (Mean Genome Completeness + 1), and calculates it in 1° spatial bins. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] The index is intended to characterize spatial gaps in genomic representation rather than directly measure biological novelty. [src: soil_frontier_genomics]

Forest had GDI = 902.36 and cropland had GDI = 890.82, while grassland had GDI = 503.42 and wetland had GDI = 525.13. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] Forest and cropland were therefore identified as jointly the highest-GDI biomes, while grassland and wetland were relatively well-mapped. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] The 1.3% difference between Forest GDI = 902.36 and Cropland GDI = 890.82 is not meaningfully distinguishable without bootstrap confidence intervals, so the evidence supports a joint highest-GDI interpretation rather than a ranked ordering. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] Bootstrap 95% confidence intervals for biome-level GDI rankings are still pending because they require a raw-data re-run. [src: soil_frontier_genomics]

Frontier areas with GDI > 1000 had mean pH = 6.74, compared with mean pH = 5.94 in mapped areas, a +0.8 pH unit gap. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] This pattern suggests a hypothesis of systematic under-sampling of alkaline soil microbiomes in public genomic databases, but it does not by itself establish that alkaline organisms are harder to assemble or annotate. [src: soil_frontier_genomics]

## Implications for functional inference

The GDI results indicate that forest and cropland soils can be diverse while remaining poorly represented at the genomic level, creating a potential limitation for functional inferences based on genome reference databases in these biomes. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] This **supports** the broader [[concepts/genomic-under-representation]] claim that uncharacterized or underrepresented genomic diversity can constrain interpretation of microbial functional potential. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] The functional-dark-matter census **refines** that claim by distinguishing annotation status from experimentally observed phenotype: 7,787 dark genes had strong fitness effects and 9,557 were essential, giving 17,344 dark genes with measurable phenotypes, although neither phenotype class supplies a direct molecular function. [src: functional_dark_matter]

The Fitness Browser–pangenome integration **refines** this distinction between biological importance and annotation or sampling visibility: among 33 organisms, 27,693 putative essential genes were identified, but 1,259 essential-unmapped genes were 44.7% hypothetical and 3,683 essential-auxiliary genes were 38.2% hypothetical. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] Thus, genes inferred to be required for viability can remain poorly characterized or absent from a usable pangenome linkage, so functional inference is constrained even when fitness evidence is available; the result is an extrapolation across the sampled organisms and growth conditions, not evidence that all hypothetical genes are essential. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]

The truly dark gene census **refines** this claim by separating database or annotation lag from genes that remain hypothetical after reannotation. Of 57,011 dark genes, 33,105 were reclassified by Bakta, 6,427 remained hypothetical in both pipelines, and 17,479 lacked pangenome links for assessment. [src: truly_dark_genes] Among the linked genes, the persistent set was shorter, less conserved, and more taxonomically restricted than annotation-lag genes; however, these properties are measured in the Fitness Browser corpus and do not by themselves establish biological novelty or horizontal transfer. [src: truly_dark_genes] The new analysis **supports** the same boundary: Bakta reclassified 33,105 of 39,532 pangenome-linked dark genes, but 6,427 remained hypothetical in both Fitness Browser and Bakta; 79.4% of that latter group had UniRef50 links, showing that sequence linkage is not equivalent to functional interpretation. [src: functional_dark_matter]

Essential genes were 86.1% core compared with 81.2% for non-essential genes, with a median odds ratio of 1.56; this **supports** the expectation that viability-required genes are generally conserved within species, while also showing that conservation is not a substitute for functional annotation. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] Eighteen of 33 organisms showed statistically significant enrichment by Fisher's exact test after Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate correction (BH-FDR q < 0.05). [src: conservation_vs_fitness]

The functional-dark-matter analysis **refines** conservation-based inference by showing that its initial eggNOG breadth classification assigned 30,721 of 30,756 dark-gene clusters (99.9%) to universal breadth and was therefore poorly discriminative; a full GTDB r214 analysis increased conservation coverage from 32,791 (57.5%) to 37,997 (66.6%) dark genes. [src: functional_dark_matter] These results support treating database breadth as an evidence layer requiring calibration, not as a direct measure of biological ubiquity or functional understanding. [src: functional_dark_matter]

GDI is a novel index without published precedent, and it has an important interpretive limitation: because GDI = Richness / (Mean_Completeness + 1), it can equal 902 even when there are zero genomes, because completeness = 0 makes the denominator 1. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] GDI also conflates sampling gap and OTU richness, allowing the richness term to dominate; separate reporting of richness and completeness with a two-dimensional scatterplot would be more interpretable. [src: soil_frontier_genomics]

The alkaline-soil pattern may reflect a sampling gap rather than a genome-recovery or annotation gap, because alkaline soils may have fewer samples sequenced in the first place. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] This **refines** [[concepts/provenance-aware-resource-discovery]] by showing that database completeness must be interpreted alongside the sampling effort that generated the underlying [[entities/16s-amplicon-sequencing]] observations and genome-completeness data. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] The truly dark gene results likewise show that sequence identifiers are not equivalent to functional interpretation: 79.4% of truly dark genes had UniRef50 links, but only 4.0% had Pfam hits and 4.6% had KEGG KOs. [src: truly_dark_genes]

## Relation to spatial prediction

The report also tested whether measured environmental and industrial variables could predict functional gene counts across 5,441 soil samples. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] All predictive model families had negative out-of-sample R²: Soil & Climate, R² = −0.205 ± 0.197; Geochemical, R² = −0.331 ± 0.071; and Industrial, R² = −0.221 ± 0.042. [src: soil_frontier_genomics]

The clay-shield test produced low-clay cross-validation R² = −0.268 and high-clay cross-validation R² = −0.292, with a difference of 0.024 and 95% CI: −0.423, 0.161. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] Because the confidence interval includes zero, high-clay soils were not significantly more predictable than low-clay soils. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] These results **support** [[concepts/environment-embedding-geography]] in emphasizing that apparent global unpredictability must be separated from spatial autocorrelation, train/test distributional shift, batch effects, unmeasured confounders, and high-leverage outliers. [src: soil_frontier_genomics]

The negative out-of-sample R² values do not alone establish a biological null: the current analyses do not distinguish modelling failure caused by distributional shift or outlier leverage from genuine unpredictability of functional gene counts from the measured predictors. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] Only the third explanation would support a strong biological null interpretation. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] This **supports** a cautious interpretation of the truly dark gene findings as well: short genes are harder to annotate and measure for fitness, and polar effects may confound some phenotypes, so annotation resistance cannot alone be treated as evidence of a novel function. [src: truly_dark_genes]

The functional-dark-matter environmental analyses **refine** this caution: 29 of 47 testable accessory-dark-gene clusters were lab–field concordant, but the one-sided binomial test gave p = 0.072, and NMDC trait-feature analysis found 441 of 449 exploratory tests significant at FDR < 0.05, a pattern attributed largely to compositional coupling. [src: functional_dark_matter] Thus, environmental association can prioritize hypotheses, but broad trait correlations do not by themselves validate a gene function or establish causal ecological adaptation. [src: functional_dark_matter]

## Annotation coverage favors well-studied lineages

The AlphaFold–MSA annotation analysis included only gene clusters with a non-UPI UniProt accession, giving 29.3% bridge coverage. Well-characterised organisms with established reference proteomes (e.g., *E. coli*, *Pseudomonas*, *Bacillus*) are over-represented in UniProt, so the analysis is biased toward the better-studied portion of bacterial diversity. [src: alphafold_msa_annotation] The report states that the annotation gap for the remaining 70.7% is likely larger, but this is a hypothesis rather than a measurement. Its limitations section instead describes 71% without AlphaFold entries, skewed toward poorly characterised organisms, so the two sections give the excluded share differently. [src: alphafold_msa_annotation] This **supports** the page's claim that reference coverage, not only biology, shapes which genes can be functionally interpreted. [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]

In the annotation-gap discovery analysis, resolution rates ranged from 20% (*Bacteroides thetaiotaomicron*) to 71% (*Klebsiella michiganensis*), and [[entities/bacteroides-thetaiotaomicron]] (Btheta) had 3 of 15 gaps resolved (20.0%). [src: annotation_gap_discovery] The report links higher resolution to better-annotated reference genomes and stronger Fitness Browser coverage, and links the lowest resolution to phylogenetic distance from the proteobacterial majority. These are interpretations, not controlled tests. [src: annotation_gap_discovery] Because 12 of 14 organisms were Proteobacteria and *B. thetaiotaomicron* was the sole Bacteroidetes, the suggestion that the approach transfers less well to phylogenetically distant clades with divergent metabolism rests on single-organism evidence and should be treated as a hypothesis. [src: annotation_gap_discovery]

In the ecotype functional-differentiation analysis, COG (Clusters of Orthologous Groups) functional annotation covered only ~38% of gene clusters. The 62% without annotations may include many ecotype-specific adaptive genes in poorly characterized categories, which could bias comparisons toward well-annotated housekeeping functions. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] This **supports** treating annotation depth as a confounder of functional comparisons rather than a neutral background. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]

Several projects show that annotation-based absence is not biological absence. The lanthanide methylotrophy atlas would not detect genuinely novel rare-earth-element-handling enzymes that lack KEGG/RefSeq homologs. [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas] In the IBD phage-targeting analysis, the other 5 Tier-A core species sit in MIBiG dark matter. They carry substantial biosynthetic gene cluster (BGC) content (*E. lenta* 41, *M. gnavus* 58, *E. bolteae* 18) but no MIBiG-annotated iron or genotoxin clusters, so missing annotation should not be equated with missing BGCs. [src: ibd_phage_targeting] For fungi, the NCBI ITS_RefSeq_Fungi reference database (19,375 sequences) may underrepresent environmental taxa. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

Annotation density can also be tested directly as a bias. The gene-function ecological agora analysis had a preregistered adjustment for per-genome annotated fraction as a nuisance covariate, but it was deferred across phases. When it was finally run, OLS regressions of per-clade scores on four z-scaled clade-level covariates (clade size, mean annotated fraction, mean GC%, mean genome size) gave producer_z R² = 0, so producer_z was uncorrelated with all four tested covariates. The report calls producer_z bias-immune on that basis, but the claim covers only these covariates. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] The consumer_z model had R² = 0.053. That value describes the joint four-covariate fit, not annotation density alone. Its z-scaled coefficients were −1.27 for mean annotated fraction (higher-annotated clades giving more negative consumer_z), +1.13 for clade size, −0.49 for mean genome size, and −0.05 for mean GC. The report describes this small consumer-side bias term (R² = 0.05) as not changing any verdict. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] This **refines** the page's concern. In this analysis, annotation-related covariates carry a measurable but small and verdict-neutral bias for consumer scores and none detectable for producer scores. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

## Organism-dark compounds as a map of resource coverage

The ENIGMA carbon census applies the under-representation problem to substrates rather than genes (see [[concepts/organism-dark-compounds]]). Of 83 compounds (59 SSO-groundwater + 24 necromass), all 83 resolved to structures (InChIKey via PubChem) and 54 linked to KEGG, but only 9 were callable. A compound counted as callable if it had an ENIGMA-isolate utilizer call or a Tier-1 measured random-barcode transposon sequencing (RB-TnSeq) carbon-source fitness experiment. Eight were callable on an ENIGMA-isolate-call basis. A ninth, lauric acid, was callable on a measured-fitness basis, but its Fitness Browser organism is a reference bacterium, not an ENIGMA isolate. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The report also flags that xanthine is mis-scored as carbon-catabolic: its allowlisted reaction (xanthine→urate, xanthine oxidase) belongs to the purine pathway, which is nitrogen acquisition, not carbon catabolism. The report therefore says the carbon-callable set is effectively 8, not 9, while 9 remains its reported callable count. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The remaining 74/83 (89%) compounds were organism-dark, and the report summarizes the census as ~89% organism-dark. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The dark set is dominated by alkaloids, shikimates/phenylpropanoids, terpenoids, and fatty acids, and the callable compounds are mostly pollutant-adjacent aromatics. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Dark fractions were nearly identical across sources (groundwater 53/59 = 90%, necromass 21/24 = 88%), which the report interprets as darkness being a chemical-class property rather than a sampling-source artifact. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] However, H1, the hypothesis of a coverage gradient by chemical class, was not formally supported because the analysis was underpowered, and the pre-registered null of uniform coverage across classes was not rejected. The class pattern is therefore directional only. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Descriptively, dark fractions are similar between groundwater and necromass. This census does not establish that chemical class matters more than sampling source. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The 74 organism-dark compounds comprise 33 KEGG-linked with no reaction in genomes, 29 fully orphan (no KEGG), 6 biosynthesis-known/catabolism-unknown, and 6 with only generic reactions. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The 29 fully orphan compounds skew necromass-heavy (13/24 necromass vs 16/59 groundwater). [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The dark set is therefore not uniformly orphan. Tyramine, guanidineacetic acid, cinnamic acid, caffeic acid, palmitic acid, and farnesol have annotated biosynthetic-direction signatures but no catabolic call. The report flags them for MIBiG triage as an external consult because MIBiG was not queried in the KBase Data Lakehouse. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

The report treats this as resource-darkness, not proof of absence from science. Class-level catabolic literature exists for several of the compounds (e.g. monoterpenes, nicotine), and the literature-rescue channel returned zero only because it used a shallow PubMed-title screen, which is a method floor rather than evidence of absence. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Callable compounds had median Complexity 133 vs 207 for dark compounds (Mann–Whitney p=0.034, uncorrected; n=9 callable). The report treats this as directional only and as at least as much an annotation-coverage ceiling as a biological-bioavailability one, because simple pollutant-adjacent aromatics are the molecules that curated catabolism resources cover. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] This **supports** the page's distinction between annotation visibility and functional reality. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

## Taxonomic and genome-set coverage limits

Pangenome construction excludes species at both ends of genome sampling. In the Fitness Browser–pangenome integration, Cola, Kang, Magneto, and SB2B were unmatched because their species had too few genomes in GTDB for pangenome construction. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] At the other end, the essential-metabolome report attributes the absence of *E. coli* to its exclusion from GTDB pangenome construction for having too many genomes for species-level analysis. [src: essential_metabolome]

GapMind coverage restricted the essential-metabolome analysis to a pilot study with 7 organisms rather than the intended 45 Fitness Browser organisms with essential-gene data. [src: essential_metabolome] Because its phylogenetic diversity was limited (mostly Proteobacteria + 1 Deltaproteobacterium), the pilot cannot generalize to pan-bacterial scale. Coverage is unknown for the remaining 38 Fitness Browser organisms, *E. coli* is absent, and organisms with divergent metabolic genes may be missed. [src: essential_metabolome]

The Bacillota_B pangenome universe contained 334 genomes, much smaller than the plan's 6,700 estimate, so the realized clade sample was far below what the study design assumed. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

Archaea show how under-representation becomes a power problem. Only 48 archaeal genera had both MicrobeAtlas representation (≥ 3 OTUs) and AMR data in the pangenome. The archaeal PGLS (phylogenetic generalized least squares, a regression that accounts for shared ancestry) gave a non-significant positive metal-type coefficient (β = +0.0145, p = 0.467) with standard errors >1× the coefficient, which the report reads as severe underpowering rather than a true null. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] The 48 genera are biased toward cultured lineages (methanogens, halophiles, thermoacidophiles) and miss environmentally dominant MicrobeAtlas archaea (Thaumarchaeota, Woesearchaeota). The report calls for new archaeal pangenome sequencing and annotation, including metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs, genomes reconstructed from community sequencing), particularly Thaumarchaeota. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] This **supports** [[concepts/cultivation-collection-bias-in-ecological-genomics]] as a mechanism of genomic under-representation. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

The truly dark gene census **supports** an archaeal annotation gap at the gene level. Methanococcus strains S2 and JJ account for 55% of all truly dark genes, and across organisms 4–96% of dark genes resist Bakta annotation, with archaeal organisms at the high end. [src: truly_dark_genes] The report attributes this to the underrepresentation of archaea in annotation databases, an interpretation drawn from few archaeal organisms. [src: truly_dark_genes]

Candidate phyla and rare lineages are also missing. In the clay-confined subsurface analysis, CPR/DPANN episymbionts and rock-attached *Geobacter*/*Geothrix* lineages are essentially absent. The report states that MAG-augmented work is needed to test whether the genuine clay-confined community matches literature predictions. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] The gene-function ecological agora project likewise treats CPR/DPANN under-representation as a substrate constraint rather than an analysis failure, and its effective-N reporting flags it per phase. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

Mixing genome types adds a detection caveat: the PHB granule analysis includes both complete genomes and MAGs, which may have variable gene detection rates. [src: phb_granule_ecology]

The scale of the gap is framed by cited literature, not by BERIL measurement. The functional-dark-matter report cites Pavlopoulos et al. (2023) as identifying 106,198 novel protein families from 26,931 metagenomes, doubling the number of known protein families. [src: functional_dark_matter] The IBD phage-targeting report cites a study that reconstructed the genome of a novel butyrate-producing clostridial scavenger from metagenomic sequence data. [src: ibd_phage_targeting]

## Tensions

The GDI pattern is consistent with under-representation of alkaline-soil microbiomes, but reverse causality remains unresolved because lower genome representation could result from fewer 16S samples rather than from assembly or annotation difficulty. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] The report lists one analysis as still needed: controlling for the number of 16S samples per pH bin before computing GDI, which would distinguish true assembly or annotation gaps from sampling gaps. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] The report therefore does not establish whether the observed gap is primarily a sampling gap, a genome-recovery gap, or an annotation gap. [src: soil_frontier_genomics]

The GDI formulation can identify locations combining high OTU richness with low mean completeness, but its value is not a pure measure of discovery deficit because richness and completeness are combined in one ratio. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] This tension requires reporting the component measurements separately before GDI differences are interpreted as differences in genomic under-representation.

The truly dark gene analysis **refines rather than resolves** this tension: among linked dark genes, 6,427 remained hypothetical after Bakta, but 17,479 unlinked genes could not be assessed, and Bakta can produce false-negative functional calls. [src: truly_dark_genes] Thus, the measured residual annotation gap is not interchangeable with the total biological novelty in public genomes. [src: truly_dark_genes]

The conservation analysis adds a related boundary rather than resolving the gap: its essentiality calls come from RB-TnSeq, a random-barcode transposon sequencing approach, under represented library-construction and growth conditions, and its pangenome coverage varies among clades. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] Essential-unmapped genes may therefore reflect missing linkage or divergent core functions, but the analysis cannot distinguish these explanations universally. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]

The functional-dark-matter analysis introduces a further sampling tension: 37 of 48 Fitness Browser organisms were Pseudomonadota, and none of the top 500 candidates came from Archaea, Actinobacteria, or Epsilonproteobacteria. [src: functional_dark_matter] This **supports** concern that apparent functional priorities may reflect condition and taxonomic coverage rather than the distribution of dark biology across microbes; the extended 50-organism covering set reached 98.7% of ortholog groups across 6 phyla but lacked Fitness Browser condition profiles for its non-Fitness-Browser organisms. [src: functional_dark_matter]

The functional-dark-matter taxon-coverage table makes this skew explicit (organisms; top-500 genes; gap severity): Gammaproteobacteria 21 organisms, 417, Saturated; Betaproteobacteria 8 organisms, 10, Moderate; Alphaproteobacteria 5 organisms, 71, Low; Deltaproteobacteria 5 organisms, 17, Low; Bacteroidetes 5 organisms, 12, Low; Firmicutes 1 organism, 5, Critical; Cyanobacteria 1 organism, 2, Moderate; Archaea 2 organisms, 0, Severe; Actinobacteria 0 organisms, no top-500 count reported, Critical; Epsilonproteobacteria 0 organisms, no top-500 count reported, High. [src: functional_dark_matter] The absence of Actinobacteria and Epsilonproteobacteria is a coverage null, not evidence that these clades lack dark genes of interest. [src: functional_dark_matter]

The microbeatlas metal-ecology report gives two different archaeal sample-size thresholds for two different purposes. It estimates that ≥200 archaeal genera with AMR data are needed for adequate power, which requires new archaeal pangenome sequencing and annotation. Separately, its future-work section proposes repeating the PGLS once ≥ 100 archaeal genera with AMR data are available, after coverage expands to environmental MAGs, particularly Thaumarchaeota. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] The report does not say whether a rerun at the ≥ 100-genus threshold would itself be adequately powered. A rerun at that size could therefore still be inconclusive for the current non-significant archaeal result. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

## Open Directions

- Re-run the BERIL Observatory 16S tables and [[entities/kbase-ke-pangenome]] completeness data with rarefaction or uniform 16S sequencing-depth correction, then ask whether forest and cropland remain the highest-GDI biomes after sampling effort is equalized. [src: soil_frontier_genomics]
- Compute bootstrap 95% confidence intervals for biome-level GDI values and explicitly compare Forest GDI = 902.36 with Cropland GDI = 890.82, asking whether their apparent difference is distinguishable from resampling uncertainty. [src: soil_frontier_genomics]
- Control GDI for the number of 16S samples in each pH bin, asking whether the +0.8 pH unit gap between frontier and mapped areas persists after sampling intensity is accounted for. [src: soil_frontier_genomics]
- Use spatial blocking and decomposition of test-fold error to separate distributional shift, high-leverage outliers, and genuine unpredictability in the negative out-of-sample R² results, asking which mechanism explains the prediction failure. [src: soil_frontier_genomics]
- Report OTU richness and mean genome completeness as separate dimensions alongside GDI, asking whether the same locations are identified as underrepresented when the ratio-based index is not used. [src: soil_frontier_genomics]
- Extend pangenome linkage to the 17,479 unlinked dark genes and compare their Bakta, Pfam, KEGG, eggNOG, and orthology coverage with the 6,427 linked truly dark genes, asking how much of the residual functional gap is a linkage artifact. [src: truly_dark_genes]
- Link the 3,683 essential-auxiliary and 1,259 essential-unmapped genes to broader pangenomes and condition-specific Fitness Browser measurements, asking whether poor annotation reflects strain-specific compensation, divergent core functions, or growth-condition dependence. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]
- Reweight the functional-dark-matter prioritization across taxonomic strata and condition depth, then test whether the top candidates and covering set change when Pseudomonadota and heavily assayed organisms no longer dominate. [src: functional_dark_matter]
- Apply sample-label permutations and annotated-accessory-gene controls to the NMDC and lab–field analyses, asking whether the observed environmental concordance survives compositional coupling and baseline annotation controls. [src: functional_dark_matter]
- Query MIBiG for the 6 biosynthesis-known/catabolism-unknown compounds and replace the PubMed-title screen with a deeper literature search, asking how many organism-dark compounds are resource-dark rather than unstudied. Expand the callable set enough to give the chemical-class hypothesis a test with adequate power. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]
- Add environmental archaeal MAGs, particularly Thaumarchaeota, to [[entities/kbase-ke-pangenome]] and rerun the archaeal PGLS. Ask whether the positive metal-type direction persists at ≥ 100 genera and whether reaching the ≥200-genus adequate-power estimate changes the conclusion. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]
- Apply the annotation-gap resolution pipeline to non-Proteobacterial organisms beyond *B. thetaiotaomicron*, asking whether low resolution is a clade-level property or a single-organism result. [src: annotation_gap_discovery]
- Annotate a sample of clusters lacking a non-UPI UniProt accession with sequence-only methods, asking whether the excluded fraction really carries a larger annotation gap than the analysed subset. [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]
