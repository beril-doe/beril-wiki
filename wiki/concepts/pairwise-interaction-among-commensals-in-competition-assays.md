# Pairwise interaction among commensals in competition assays

Commensal pairs tested against Pseudomonas aeruginosa PA14 are on average mildly antagonistic. Neisseria mucosa pairs are near-additive. The evidence is thin: it comes from one report, [[summaries/cf_formulation_design__REPORT]], and rests on a small number of comparisons, so additive scoring of formulations stays provisional. [src: cf_formulation_design]

## Literature Context

The candidate papers show that pairwise outcomes are common currency in microbiome work, but that they are an uncertain guide to larger communities. In larval zebrafish, two-species competition outcomes failed to predict species abundances in communities of up to five species. Interbacterial interactions became weaker when more species were present, which the authors read as higher-order interactions that stabilize complex communities [PMID 33051365](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33051365/). In the Hydra system, frequency-dependent, nonlinear growth rates of two commensals went beyond simple pairwise competition. This agreed with the synergistic anti-fungal activity seen in vivo [PMID 26236827](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26236827/).

Pairwise co-culture screens of other commensal collections give a mixed picture. In a gut co-culture study, most validated taxon pairs were neutralism (51.67%), then commensalism (21.67%), amensalism (18.33%), competition (5%) and exploitation (3.33%). The authors conclude that positive relationships tend to be overestimated [PMID 38600293](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38600293/). In upper respiratory isolates from 3 healthy adults and 3 chronic rhinosinusitis patients, antagonism among healthy-donor isolates was limited to corynebacterial inhibition of staphylococci. Coaggregation was rare (2/153 pairwise interactions) [PMID 28857734](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28857734/). Nasal species also modulated S. aureus growth in a strain- and community-specific way, with nutrient-dependent interactions as key drivers [PMID 42920146](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42920146/). Among Neisseria, pairwise biofilms of meningococcal strains, and of meningococcal and Neisseria lactamica strains, showed coexistence. No competition was observed even under nutrient limitation. Some combinations showed commensalism or cooperative behavior [PMID 28382026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28382026/). In Coreoidea stinkbugs, pairwise co-culture infections revealed host-specific selectivity among Caballeronia strains, even though each strain colonized alone [PMID 41230826](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41230826/). Methodological papers add that interaction parameters can be estimated from kinetic data, though only in a simulated and a limited experimental setting [PMID 38083503](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38083503/). Metabolic models can also predict pairwise interaction classes in anaerobic digestion [PMID 32905861](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32905861/). None of these studies used the PA14 competition assay.

The corpus finding of a mildly antagonistic mean synergy of −5.8% across commensal pairs against PA14 is consistent with the literature in one respect. Pairwise outcomes vary by pair and context, and neutral or near-additive results are common [PMID 38600293](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38600293/). The near-additive Neisseria mucosa pairs (+5.3% and −2.2%) fit the coexistence seen in Neisseria biofilms [PMID 28382026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28382026/). That comparison is loose, because the biofilm work measured coexistence and not inhibition of a pathogen. The corpus's rests on 8 comparisons across 5 unique pairs, so it is too thin to confirm or contradict these studies.

The zebrafish and Hydra results bear on the report's main caveat. They suggest the hypothesis that additive scoring of 5-species formulations could fail through higher-order effects, even after all 10 pairs are measured [PMID 33051365](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33051365/) [PMID 26236827](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26236827/). Those are other host systems, so this is an extrapolation. The corpus's proposed 10-pair matrix would close only the pairwise gap. The PA14 competition data themselves are novel within this candidate set, as none of these papers tests commensal pairs against P. aeruginosa.

## What the competition assay showed

The report describes its competition assay as testing 3 commensal pairs against PA14 at multiple inoculation densities. [src: cf_formulation_design] The results table and the caveats give other counts. The table lists 5 pairs, the caveats cite 8 comparisons across 5 unique pairs, and the report says current data cover 5 of the 10 possible pairs in the 5-species formulation. [src: cf_formulation_design] The claims do not reconcile the "3 pairs" description with these counts. The "3 pairs" figure may refer to a narrower part of the assay, but the report does not say. Read all of these counts with that gap in mind.

Pairwise testing found near-additive inhibition for N. mucosa combinations and modest antagonism in some pairs. This informs which combinations go on to mouse models. [src: cf_formulation_design] Overall mean synergy was −5.8%, which the report reads as mildly antagonistic on average. [src: cf_formulation_design] The N. mucosa pairs were near-additive (+5.3% and −2.2%). The report takes this as support for N. mucosa as a formulation anchor that does not interfere with partners. [src: cf_formulation_design] See also [[entities/neisseria-mucosa]].

Per-pair values against PA14:

| Pair | Mean pair inhibition | Mean single best inhibition | Synergy |
|---|---|---|---|
| N. mucosa + ASMA-2260 | 47% | 42% | +5.3% [src: cf_formulation_design] |
| ASMA-3913 + ASMA-2260 | 52% | 51% | +1.4% [src: cf_formulation_design] |
| N. mucosa + ASMA-2464 | 40% | 42% | −2.2% [src: cf_formulation_design] |
| ASMA-3913 + ASMA-2464 | 37% | 51% | −14.2% [src: cf_formulation_design] |
| ASMA-1478 + ASMA-1197 | −3% | 17% | −19.8% [src: cf_formulation_design] |

The strongest antagonism was up to −19.8% synergy. [src: cf_formulation_design]

## Data limits that make additive scoring provisional

Formulation scoring assumes additive inhibition, so a combination's effect equals the mean of its members. Synergy would make this an underestimate. Antagonism would make it an overestimate. [src: cf_formulation_design] The conclusions rest on only 8 comparisons across 5 unique pairs, which is an underpowered sample. [src: cf_formulation_design] Only 3 A × 3 B isolate combinations were tested. This limits prediction of interactions for the full formulation. [src: cf_formulation_design] The current data cover only 5 of the 10 possible pairs in the 5-species formulation. [src: cf_formulation_design] A single antagonistic interaction between untested core members could invalidate the formulation design. The additive scoring behind all NB05 and NB05b formulation rankings is provisional until the full matrix is measured. [src: cf_formulation_design] See [[concepts/multi-criterion-design-of-protective-microbiome-formulations]].

### The duplicated table

NB08 found that fact_pairwise_interaction is identical to fact_carbon_utilization (correlation = 1.0, mean difference = 0.0). [src: cf_formulation_design] The cross-project digest [[summaries/pitfalls]] records the same finding for the PROTECT Gold tables. [src: pitfalls] As a result, endpoint OD (optical density) data do not capture co-culture metabolic interactions. Per-substrate analysis is not possible with current data. [src: cf_formulation_design, pitfalls] NB08 therefore pivoted to the RFU-based competition assay alone. [src: pitfalls, cf_formulation_design] That assay does capture pairwise effects. [src: cf_formulation_design]

## Proposed validation

The report proposes measuring all 10 pairwise combinations of the 5 core species in the PA14 competition assay at multiple inoculation densities, as its highest-priority validation experiment. [src: cf_formulation_design] It expects a complete 10-pair matrix to validate or correct additive formulation scores, identify combinations to avoid, and guide selection of 3- and 4-member subsets for mouse models. [src: cf_formulation_design]

## Open Directions

- Measure all 10 pairwise combinations of the 5 core species in the PA14 competition assay. This would close the gap of 5 untested pairs.
- Use the complete matrix to validate or correct the additive formulation scores, flag combinations to avoid, and choose the 3- and 4-member subsets for mouse models.
- Clarify from the raw assay records how the "3 pairs", "5 unique pairs" and "8 comparisons" counts relate. This would settle the scope of the current data.
- Replicate the existing pairs to test whether the −5.8% mean and the N. mucosa near-additivity hold beyond 8 comparisons. This addresses the underpowered sample.
- Obtain a distinct co-culture dataset, or repair the duplicated table, so per-substrate interactions can be assessed.
