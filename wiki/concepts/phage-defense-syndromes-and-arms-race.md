# Defense-system syndromes and prophage-associated anti-phage arms races

This concept examines whether bacterial anti-phage defense systems form recurring combinations and whether broader defense repertoires are associated with prophage burden. The evidence comes from the [[summaries/phage_defense_arsenal__REPORT]], which analyzed seven defense-system families across the KBase Data Lakehouse pangenome. The [[summaries/prophage_amr_comobilization__REPORT]] extends this question to associations between prophage markers and antimicrobial-resistance (AMR) repertoire breadth, while [[summaries/prophage_ecology__REPORT]] tests prophage-module composition across bacterial phylogeny, environmental gradients, and NMDC metagenomes. The [[summaries/snipe_defense_system__REPORT]] adds the SNIPE defense system, its proposed ManYZ-dependent phage-entry mechanism, and evidence for a widespread, predominantly accessory defense family. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

## Pan-bacterial defense repertoire

Markers for at least one defense system were detected in 27,626 of 27,690 species-level pangenomes (99.8%), yielding 930,573 marker hits across 825,476 unique gene clusters. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] The quality-filtered analysis included 7,323 species with at least five genomes. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

Species-level prevalence ranged from 7.2% for [[entities/cbass]] to 96.1% for [[entities/crispr-cas]]; the other systems were BREX at 80.1%, restriction-modification (R-M) Type I at 76.8%, DISARM at 58.4% under the strict call, Retron at 54.7%, R-M Type II at 38.9%, and Gabija at 22.8%. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] R-M Type I exceeded 80% in Pseudomonadota and Bacillota, CBASS remained below 20% in all phyla except Bacteroidota at approximately 10–15%, and Gabija reached 30–40% in Bacillota versus below 10% in Actinomycetota. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

The apparent CRISPR-Cas prevalence depends strongly on detection method: EggNOG description matching produced 96% species prevalence, whereas the specific Cas1 Pfam marker PF01867 produced approximately 55% prevalence on the same pangenome. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] The report therefore treats 96% as an upper bound, and syndrome comparisons that use the combined EggNOG-or-Pfam presence set should be interpreted as recall-oriented rather than maximally specific. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

SNIPE provides a complementary example of broad but annotation-sensitive defense distribution. Its DUF4041/PF13250 marker occurred in 4,572 gene clusters across 1,696 species and 33 bacterial and archaeal phyla; 86.7% of those clusters were accessory or singleton (13.3% core, 30.7% accessory, and 56.1% singleton). [src: snipe_defense_system] This **supports** the broader interpretation that defense markers span diverse taxa while often residing outside stable core inheritance, but it also **refines** prevalence estimates because only 54 of the 4,572 DUF4041 clusters carried the Mug113 description associated with the PF13455 nuclease domain. [src: snipe_defense_system]

A related annotation-specific caveat applies to prophage burden. In a separate analysis, prophage-associated annotations occurred in all 27,702 analyzed bacterial species, but the calls were based on eggNOG functional annotations rather than dedicated prophage detection; near-universal prevalence may therefore include domesticated remnants and bacterial homologs of phage genes. [src: prophage_ecology] This **refines** interpretation of the defense–prophage associations below: the burden signal is robust as a marker-defined association, but true intact-prophage prevalence remains uncertain. [src: prophage_ecology]

## Defense-system syndromes

A syndrome is a recurring combination of defense systems across species. Using 1,000 phylum-stratified column-permutation null permutations and Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate (BH-FDR) correction, 27 of 28 defense-system pairs showed significant positive co-occurrence at q < 0.05. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] This broad positive structure supports [[concepts/cofitness-network-architecture]] by showing that defense systems can form coordinated network-level architectures rather than appearing as independent traits. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

The strongest pair was R-M Type II × Gabija, with 2,429 observed co-occurrences versus a null mean of 1,555, z = 46.1, and odds ratio (OR) = 24.0. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] The report presents this pairing as a novel pan-bacterial syndrome at this scale: R-M Type II uses sequence-specific cleavage, whereas Gabija uses a nuclease/helicase pair triggered by nucleotide-pool depletion, and both systems act on double-stranded DNA. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

Other strong positive pairings included BREX × DISARM with 4,968 observed co-occurrences, null mean = 4,629, z = 31.1, and OR = 8.2; BREX × Retron with 5,021, 4,681, z = 29.7, and OR = 8.6; DISARM × Retron with 4,280, 3,844, z = 28.4, and OR = 5.2; R-M Type I × DISARM with 4,970, 4,673, z = 27.5, and OR = 8.4; R-M Type I × BREX with 5,963, 5,732, z = 26.9, and OR = 8.7; R-M Type II × DISARM with 3,424, 2,981, z = 24.4, and OR = 4.1; R-M Type II × BREX with 3,929, 3,637, z = 23.0, and OR = 6.4; and R-M Type I × R-M Type II with 3,948, 3,665, z = 22.5, and OR = 7.6. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

SNIPE **refines** this syndrome framework by linking defense to a host entry route rather than simply adding another co-occurrence marker. The proposed system constitutively localizes to the inner membrane and cleaves phage DNA as it passes through the ManYZ mannose-transporter pore, potentially retaining mannose transport while blocking phage infection. [src: snipe_defense_system] In *E. coli* K-12, direct RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing) data from 168 experiments per ManXYZ gene show the largest defects on D-glucosamine and D-mannose, while cofitness correlations of 0.851 for *manX*↔*manZ*, 0.705 for *manX*↔*manY*, and 0.725 for *manY*↔*manZ* support their operation as a single operon. [src: snipe_defense_system] This **supports** a defense–metabolic trade-off hypothesis, but the transporter mechanism is established directly only for the SNIPE/ManYZ model and should not be generalized to the other syndromes. [src: snipe_defense_system]

The SNIPE domain correction also **refines** marker-based syndrome interpretation: PF13250 is the corrected Pfam assignment for DUF4041, whereas PF13455/Mug113 is the SNIPE nuclease family and is distinct from canonical GIY-YIG PF01541; zero surveyed clusters contained both DUF4041/PF13250 and PF01541. [src: snipe_defense_system] The recommended SNIPE search therefore combines PF13250, DUF4041, or T5orf172 with PF13455 rather than treating PF01541 as the nuclease marker. [src: snipe_defense_system]

CRISPR-Cas × CBASS was the only pair that did not reach significance, with z = 0.21 and empirical p = 0.98. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] These systems also had the largest prevalence gap, at 96% versus 7%. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] The null-model results establish co-occurrence, but they do not by themselves establish biochemical cooperation, shared genomic localization, or direct protection against the same phages. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

## Prophage-associated defense expansion

Species-level defense-system count correlated positively with prophage-cluster burden, with Spearman ρ = 0.609, p ≈ 0, and n = 7,323. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] After residualizing on log10 median genome size and phylum, the partial correlation remained ρ = 0.301 with p = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁵³. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] A negative-binomial generalized linear model found positive contributions from prophage-cluster count, β = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴, p < 0.001, and log10 genome size, β = 0.755, p < 0.001. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] Each 10-fold genome-size increase was associated with e^0.755 ≈ 2.1× more defense systems. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

The prophage ecology analysis **supports** the environmental relevance of this burden association while qualifying its interpretation. In a 1,773-species subsample, PERMANOVA (permutation-based multivariate analysis of variance) found significant effects of genome-size quartile, environment, and family-level phylogeny on prophage-module composition, each with p=0.01; genome size was dominant (F=212.99), followed by environment (F=30.04) and phylogeny (F=6.17). [src: prophage_ecology] Environmental effects remained significant within every genome-size quartile, with all p < 6.5e-78, and environmental niche breadth correlated with prophage module count after controlling for genome size (partial Spearman rho=0.468, p=8.41e-110). [src: prophage_ecology] Thus, the existing defense–prophage relationship is consistent with ecological structure beyond genome size and host phylogeny, although the new analysis also reports genome size as the dominant predictor and cannot exclude residual confounding. [src: prophage_ecology]

The AMR co-mobilization analysis **supports** the direction of this prophage-burden pattern: across 4,770 species, prophage marker density was positively associated with AMR repertoire breadth (Spearman rho=0.572, p<10^-300, n=4,770), with a partial correlation of rho=0.464 after controlling for genome count. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization] The association was significant across Pseudomonadota (rho=0.54), Bacillota_A (rho=0.55), Bacillota (rho=0.40), Bacteroidota (rho=0.59), and Actinomycetota (rho=0.29). [src: prophage_amr_comobilization] This is a related species-level association, not evidence that defense systems themselves cause AMR accumulation or that prophages mediate the transfer.

SNIPE **supports** the interpretation that mobile defense systems can contribute to this broader burden pattern: 80.4% of DUF4041-containing clusters (3,675/4,572) were represented by T5orf172 or DUF4041 descriptions, and the accessory-plus-singleton fraction was 86.7%. [src: snipe_defense_system] However, the environmental association was modest rather than habitat-diagnostic: among AlphaEarth-embedded species, SNIPE-bearing species numbered n=1,069 and non-SNIPE species n=13,977; 22 of 64 dimensions differed after Bonferroni correction, with the largest effect in A19 at Cohen’s d = 0.26 and p = 5.0e-14. [src: snipe_defense_system]

All nine major phyla showed positive, significant partial correlations between defense count and prophage-cluster burden: Campylobacterota, n = 102, ρ = 0.530, p = 9.8×10⁻⁹; Bacillota, n = 735, ρ = 0.481, p = 8.4×10⁻⁴⁴; Bacillota_A, n = 1,134, ρ = 0.419, p = 2.0×10⁻⁴⁹; Verrucomicrobiota, n = 129, ρ = 0.369, p = 1.6×10⁻⁵; Patescibacteria, n = 191, ρ = 0.343, p = 1.2×10⁻⁶; Pseudomonadota, n = 2,172, ρ = 0.342, p = 9.6×10⁻⁶¹; Cyanobacteriota, n = 124, ρ = 0.309, p = 4.9×10⁻⁴; Bacteroidota, n = 925, ρ = 0.261, p = 6.3×10⁻¹⁶; and Actinomycetota, n = 803, ρ = 0.185, p = 1.3×10⁻⁷. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

Together, these associations are consistent with a prophage-associated anti-phage arms-race hypothesis, but they do not formally establish a phylogenetically independent arms race. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] The analysis controlled for phylum categorically rather than using a phylogenetically corrected regression, and the negative-binomial model fixed dispersion at alpha = 1.0 rather than estimating it. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] The prophage classifier also included broad module candidates such as integrase, holin, endolysin, CI-like repressor, and tail proteins that can match non-phage bacterial genes; consequently, `n_prophage_modules` saturated at 7 for 35% of species, while the unbounded `n_prophage_clusters` count was used as the primary arms-race predictor. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

The new analysis **refines** the arms-race interpretation by separating near-universal core-like modules from environmentally variable functions. Packaging and lysogenic-regulation modules occurred in 100.0% of species and lysis in 99.9%, whereas anti-defense occurred in 64.3%, head morphogenesis in 56.1%, and tail in 55.6%; only 34.9% carried all 7 modules. [src: prophage_ecology] In constrained tests, human-associated environments were enriched for tail (log2(OR)=2.21), head morphogenesis (log2(OR)=1.98), and anti-defense (log2(OR)=1.70), while anti-defense was depleted in freshwater (log2(OR)=-0.74) and animal-associated environments (log2(OR)=-0.24). [src: prophage_ecology] These associations are consistent with stronger phage-mediated selection in some human-associated settings, but they are not a direct mechanistic demonstration of coevolution. [src: prophage_ecology]

The new evidence also **refines** the phylogenetic interpretation: among 824 TerL lineages with at least 5 species, 325 were classified as specialists and 499 as generalists, yet no individual lineage showed significant environment-specific enrichment in 0/500 tests after FDR correction. [src: prophage_ecology] Module-level environmental ecology and lineage-level ecology therefore appear decoupled in this analysis, consistent with modular exchange without independent whole-lineage adaptation. [src: prophage_ecology]

## Accessory acquisition and interpretation limits

Six of seven systems were significantly enriched in the auxiliary and singleton pangenome relative to a background of 46.8% core and 37.9% singleton gene clusters, with χ² p ≈ 0 for all systems. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] Retron had 8.2% core and 65.8% singleton fractions; Gabija had 9.1% and 68.8%; R-M Type II had 9.4% and 68.2%; R-M Type I had 9.8% and 65.3%; CBASS had 11.8% and 61.1%; BREX had 20.7% and 55.8%; CRISPR-Cas had 27.4% and 47.2%; and DISARM had 40.6% and 42.3%. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

For the accessory-enriched systems, singleton fractions were 1.5–1.8× above the 37.9% background baseline, which is consistent with horizontal transfer of defense loci through mobile genetic elements. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] This supports [[concepts/gene-function-acquisition-depth]] by linking defense-system presence to accessory and singleton gene-cluster distribution, while the inference remains limited by marker specificity. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

SNIPE **supports** this mobile-defense interpretation: among its 4,572 DUF4041-containing clusters, 13.3% were core, 30.7% accessory, and 56.1% singleton, and the marker spanned 1,696 species and 33 phyla. [src: snipe_defense_system] The distribution is consistent with frequent gain or loss and mobile defense-island carriage, but DUF4041 can occur in non-SNIPE proteins and eggNOG annotations may miss divergent homologues. [src: snipe_defense_system]

The prophage ecology analysis **refines** the mobile-module model: packaging, lysis, and lysogenic regulation were more consistently co-localized, whereas integration and anti-defense were more dispersed across contigs, with mean co-localization values of 0.986, 0.901, and 1.000 for the three core-like modules versus 0.420 and 0.281 for integration and anti-defense. [src: prophage_ecology] This pattern is consistent with a physically linked prophage backbone plus accessory defense islands, but it was tested in only 15 species and does not establish transfer between elements. [src: prophage_ecology]

The AMR analysis **qualifies** a simple immediate-neighborhood interpretation of mobile-element association. Among 36,041 AMR gene instances, 12,026 (33.4%) were within 50 genes of a strict prophage marker, but only 3,731 (10.4%) were within 10 genes and 1,991 (5.5%) within 5 genes; the median distance among co-localized genes was 34 genes. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization] AMR genes within 10 genes were 67.6% accessory versus 65.5% for distal genes, with OR = 1.10, one-sided p=0.005, and bootstrap 95% confidence interval [1.024, 1.185], a modest 2.1 percentage point difference. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization] The threshold-dependent odds ratios—0.78 at 3 genes, 0.92 at 5, 1.10 at 10, 1.19 at 15, and 1.28 at 50—together with a median species-level OR of 0.85 indicate heterogeneous proximity effects rather than uniform co-mobilization. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]

The DISARM accessory-enrichment result is unreliable because the DrmB SNF2 helicase Pfam anchor PF00176 is a widespread housekeeping-helicase domain. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] Retron detection likewise used the broad RVT_1 Pfam PF00078, and the stringent call reduced candidates from 15,109 to 15,098 by dropping 11 species; these candidates should therefore be interpreted as reverse-transcriptase candidates in defense-syndrome context rather than characterized retron systems. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

The analysis set contained 7,323 of 27,690 pangenome species, or 26%, because of the at-least-five-genome filter. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] This sampling restriction favors well-sampled, culturable, high-priority organisms, so extrapolation to the full 293K-genome tree, including many environmental MAGs, remains untested. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] The AMR analysis likewise sampled 20 genomes per species for its 100-species neighborhood analysis rather than all 293K genomes, so its proximity estimates may change under exhaustive sampling. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]

The prophage ecology analysis adds that only 28% of genomes had AlphaEarth environmental embeddings, producing a biased subsample toward clinically and environmentally well-sampled lineages; NCBI isolation_source metadata were also sparse and inconsistently labeled. [src: prophage_ecology] Its NMDC validation was indirect, relying on genus-level conservation of prophage content, although taxonomy-based burden inference across 6,365 samples achieved 87.2% median matching coverage. [src: prophage_ecology] SNIPE’s AlphaEarth comparison was similarly limited to 28.4% of genomes and was biased toward environmental isolates with latitude/longitude metadata; the dimensions, including A19, have no assigned biological interpretation. [src: snipe_defense_system]

## Tensions

The observed breadth of the defense arsenal is partly in tension with marker-level specificity: EggNOG description matching gives 96% CRISPR-Cas prevalence, while the Cas1 PF01867 marker gives approximately 55%, and the DISARM anchor can identify non-DISARM SNF2 helicases. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] SNIPE adds a related tension: 4,572 DUF4041 clusters were detected, but only 54 carried the Mug113 co-annotation, and DUF4041 may occur outside complete SNIPE architectures. [src: snipe_defense_system] The co-occurrence and arms-race patterns are therefore strongest as system-presence associations, while their mechanistic interpretation requires context-aware multi-marker validation. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

The modest and threshold-dependent AMR–prophage proximity effect is also in tension with a strong direct-mobilization reading of the species-level prophage-density/AMR-breadth association: the latter has rho=0.572 across 4,770 species, whereas only 10.4% of AMR instances were within 10 genes of a strict prophage marker. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization] These results need not conflict, because species-level co-acquisition and transfer need not produce immediate gene adjacency; dedicated prophage calls and sequence-resolved analyses are required to distinguish those explanations. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]

The new prophage ecology results create a further qualification rather than a resolved contradiction: environmental effects on prophage-module composition remained after genome-size stratification and exceeded the reported family-level phylogenetic effect, but genome size was still dominant (F=212.99), and the prophage calls may include bacterial homologs and domesticated remnants. [src: prophage_ecology] This is compatible with an ecological arms-race signal, but does not by itself establish that intact prophages caused the environmental or defense associations. [src: prophage_ecology]

SNIPE’s low Lambdavirus infection rate in PhageFoundry—1 of 188 *E. coli* strains, or 0.5%—is consistent with widespread ManYZ variation or loss, but this does not establish that SNIPE caused the pattern; no *Klebsiella* SNIPE or ManYZ fitness data were available. [src: snipe_defense_system] The report also detected DUF4041 in *Klebsiella* but not in *Acinetobacter*, *P. aeruginosa*, or *P. viridiflava* in the four-species comparison, while co-occurrence with mannose-transporter annotations was only consistent with, rather than proof of, defense at the phage-entry route. [src: snipe_defense_system]

## Open Directions

- Apply PADLOC MacSyFinder-style multi-PFam and gene-order rules to Retron, DISARM, and Gabija, and test whether the R-M Type II × Gabija association remains at 2,429 observed co-occurrences versus a null mean of 1,555 after marker refinement. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]
- Expand the panel beyond seven systems to include Zorya, Thoeris, Wadjet, Druantia, pAgo, PARIS, ThsA-ThsB, and defense-associated antitoxin cassettes, then ask whether the 27-of-28 positive-pair pattern persists. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]
- Fit a GTDB-tree phylogenetic mixed-effects arms-race model using defense count, `n_prophage_clusters`, genome size, and phylum, and test whether the residual association remains after explicit phylogenetic correction. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]
- Apply geNomad or PHASTER to the KBase Data Lakehouse genomes and use scaffold sequences for base-pair-resolution distances, then test whether the species-level AMR breadth association persists after separating phage, plasmid, and ICE mobilization. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]
- Re-call prophages with dedicated tools and test whether the environmental enrichment of tail, head morphogenesis, and anti-defense remains after excluding bacterial homologs and domesticated remnants. [src: prophage_ecology]
- Use sequence-resolved genomes and the 6,365-sample NMDC bridge to test whether pH-associated prophage burden reflects intact prophage abundance, genus-level inference, or pH-linked community composition. [src: prophage_ecology]
- Perform mechanistic phage-challenge experiments comparing R-M Type II-only species with R-M Type II-plus-Gabija species, and ask whether the predicted syndrome provides additional protection against the same phage challenges. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]
- Cross-reference defense syndromes and prophage burden with NCBI isolation-source metadata or AlphaEarth embeddings to test whether habitat-specific defense architectures explain variation not captured by phylum. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]
- Revisit prophage-proximal AMR fitness costs as fitness-browser coverage expands, including all available genomes from Klebsiella pneumoniae, Acinetobacter baumannii, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, and Escherichia coli. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]
- Use PF13250/DUF4041 plus PF13455 gene-order and sequence-profile rules to distinguish complete SNIPE architectures from divergent or non-SNIPE DUF4041 proteins, then test whether SNIPE prevalence and accessory status remain at 4,572 clusters and 86.7% accessory-or-singleton after refinement. [src: snipe_defense_system]
- Generate ManYZ and SNIPE mutant fitness and phage-challenge measurements in *Klebsiella* and other phage-therapy targets, testing whether transporter preservation produces the predicted resistance-versus-metabolic-cost trade-off. [src: snipe_defense_system]
