# Most Plant-Associated Species Carry Both Beneficial and Pathogenic Genomic Signatures

The plant microbiome ecotypes project assigned bacterial species to genome-based cohorts (dual-nature, pathogenic, beneficial, neutral) and found that the dual-nature cohort is the largest one, both across all species analysed and, more strongly, among plant-associated species [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]. Detailed results are in [[summaries/plant_microbiome_ecotypes__REPORT]].

## Cohort Distribution Across Species

Across the refined cohort distribution of 25,660 species, dual-nature species numbered 12,463 (48.6%), pathogenic 6,272 (24.4%), beneficial 3,394 (13.2%) and neutral 3,531 (13.8%) [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes].

Among the 1,115 plant-associated species, dual-nature species numbered 878 (78.7%), pathogenic 108 (9.7%), beneficial 94 (8.4%) and neutral 35 (3.1%) [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]. This **refines** the all-species result: the dual-nature share is higher in the plant-associated subset than in the full species set, while the pathogenic, beneficial and neutral shares are each lower [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes].

## Interpretation and Evidence Grade

The cohort counts are direct tallies from one project's genome-based classification [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]. They suggest the hypothesis that, at the gene-content resolution used for cohort assignment, a strict beneficial-versus-pathogen dichotomy is not recoverable for most plant-associated species; this interpretation rests on a single project and on how the cohort rules define a "dual-nature" signature, so it is not yet an established finding [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes].

## Open Directions

- Within the dual-nature cohort of [[summaries/plant_microbiome_ecotypes__REPORT]], test whether the beneficial and pathogenic marker sets co-occur on the same mobile elements or in the core genome, to ask whether dual-nature status reflects accessory-gene mixing or shared core functions [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes].
- Compare cohort assignments against curated host-interaction phenotypes (where available) for plant-associated species, to measure how often a dual-nature genomic call corresponds to an observed beneficial or pathogenic lifestyle [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes].
- Re-run cohort assignment under stricter or weighted marker thresholds to test whether the dual-nature majority among plant-associated species is robust to marker-definition choices [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes].
