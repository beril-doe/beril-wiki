# Evidence-weighted experimental prioritization of unknown bacterial genes

[[summaries/functional_dark_matter__REPORT]] describes an evidence-weighted framework for converting unknown bacterial genes into experimentally testable hypotheses by combining fitness, essentiality, conservation, pathway, environmental, genomic-context, and annotation evidence. [src: functional_dark_matter]

## Scope and evidence base

Across 48 organisms and 228,709 genes, the analysis identified 57,011 dark genes—hypothetical, DUF, or otherwise uncharacterized genes—representing 24.9% of the catalog. [src: functional_dark_matter] The proportion of dark genes ranged from more than 35% to less than 15% among organisms, which the report interprets as annotation-depth variation rather than established variation in functional content. [src: functional_dark_matter]

Fitness and essentiality supplied the main experimental evidence streams: 7,787 dark genes had strong fitness effects, defined as absolute fitness of at least 2 in at least one condition, and 9,557 were classified as essential because no viable transposon mutants were recovered. [src: functional_dark_matter] Together, 17,344 dark genes had experimentally measurable phenotypes under the available assays. [src: functional_dark_matter] This supports [[concepts/condition-specific-fitness]] and [[concepts/gene-essentiality]] by showing that unknown sequence annotation does not imply an absence of experimentally accessible phenotype. [src: functional_dark_matter]

The evidence base was uneven across gene classes: 39,532 of the 57,011 dark genes had pangenome links, 12,686 were accessory, 511 were both accessory and strongly fitness-active, and 6,142 belonged to independent-component-analysis (ICA) fitness modules, which group genes with coordinated fitness profiles. [src: functional_dark_matter] Stress conditions, including metals, oxidative stress, and osmotic stress, produced the largest concentration of strong dark-gene phenotypes, followed by carbon-source and nitrogen-source conditions. [src: functional_dark_matter]

## Evidence-weighted prioritization

The six-axis prioritization score combined fitness importance, cross-organism conservation, inference quality, pangenome distribution, biogeographic signal, and experimental tractability. [src: functional_dark_matter] It ranked 17,344 phenotype-bearing dark genes, and the top 100 candidates spanned 22 organisms. [src: functional_dark_matter] Shewanella MR-1 contributed 25 of the top 100 candidates, *Pseudomonas putida* N2C3 contributed 18, and Marinobacter contributed 9. [src: functional_dark_matter] Of these candidates, 82/100 had high-confidence functional hypotheses supported by at least 3 evidence types, and 85/100 had module-based predictions. [src: functional_dark_matter]

The score is therefore best interpreted as a decision aid rather than a direct probability that a gene has a particular function. [src: functional_dark_matter] Rank stability was high for overall configurations, with rank correlations remaining ρ > 0.93 across six alternative configurations, but only 64% of the original fitness-active top 50 remained under conservation-dominant or drop-tractability settings. [src: functional_dark_matter] Essential-gene rankings were more sensitive: essential-gene top-50 retention was 36% when tractability was dropped and 48% when neighbor context was dropped. [src: functional_dark_matter] These sensitivity results support [[concepts/experimental-prioritization-of-functional-dark-matter]] as a framework for transparent trade-offs rather than a fixed universal ranking. [src: functional_dark_matter]

## From candidate genes to experiments

A greedy set-cover analysis found that 10 organism–condition experiments would address 242 of the top 500 dark genes, or 45.3%. [src: functional_dark_matter] The first three proposed experiments—Shewanella MR-1 under stress, nitrogen-source, and carbon-source conditions—would address 111 candidates, or 20.8%. [src: functional_dark_matter] The complete ten-experiment sequence also included stress or carbon-source experiments in *P. putida* N2C3, *Sinorhizobium meliloti*, *P. stutzeri* RCH2, Marinobacter, *P. fluorescens* GW456-L13, and *P. putida*. [src: functional_dark_matter]

This experiment-level optimization extends [[concepts/fitness-experiment-depth-and-module-reliability]] by treating condition selection and organism selection as coverage problems rather than evaluating genes independently. [src: functional_dark_matter] The approach also exposes a depth bias: Fitness Browser condition coverage is uneven, and MR-1 has 121 historical conditions, so organisms with deeper condition coverage can produce more specific phenotypes and receive higher prioritization scores. [src: functional_dark_matter]

For non-essential genes, RB-TnSeq—randomly barcoded transposon sequencing—provides condition-specific fitness measurements when viable insertion mutants are available. [src: functional_dark_matter] Essential dark genes require a separate strategy because standard RB-TnSeq yields no viable knockout fitness profiles for them. [src: functional_dark_matter] The report recommends CRISPRi, or CRISPR interference for transcriptional knockdown, including Mobile-CRISPRi for less-established organisms, followed by growth measurements under standard and stress conditions. [src: functional_dark_matter]

## Functional inference before validation

GapMind identified 1,256 organism–pathway pairs across 44 Fitness Browser-linked species in which nearly complete pathways with low `steps_missing_low` scores co-occurred with strongly fitness-active dark genes. [src: functional_dark_matter] The most frequent gaps were fucose utilization in 32 organisms, rhamnose utilization in 31, sorbitol utilization in 30, myoinositol utilization in 28, gluconate utilization in 26, and asparagine biosynthesis in 24. [src: functional_dark_matter]

These pathway results generate organism-level hypotheses rather than direct gene-to-enzyme assignments. [src: functional_dark_matter] Domain matching produced 42,239 gene–pathway candidates across 3,186 unique dark genes, including 5,398 high-confidence EC-prefix matches, 4,687 medium-confidence Pfam-family matches, and 32,154 low-confidence keyword matches. [src: functional_dark_matter] Individual assignments therefore require EC matching, structure prediction, enzymology, or other experimental validation. [src: functional_dark_matter] This evidence boundary refines [[concepts/metabolic-model-gapfilling]] and [[concepts/evidence-triangulation-for-functional-annotation]] by separating pathway plausibility from gene-level functional proof. [src: functional_dark_matter]

Cross-organism and genomic-context evidence increased prioritization confidence without constituting direct functional validation. [src: functional_dark_matter] Fitness concordance identified 65 ortholog groups present in at least three organisms whose dark-gene orthologs showed measurable effects under the same condition classes, with several groups reaching concordance of 1.00. [src: functional_dark_matter] Cross-species synteny conserved 17,058 of 21,011 tested dark-gene–operon-partner pairs in at least one other organism and 10,150 in at least 3 organisms. [src: functional_dark_matter] Independent co-fitness analysis found evidence for 2,899 of 32,075 non-essential operon pairs, including 1,129 mutual top-5 pairs, and 998 pairs were double-validated by conserved synteny and strong co-fitness. [src: functional_dark_matter] These results support [[concepts/cofitness-network-architecture]] while retaining the distinction between association and experimentally demonstrated function. [src: functional_dark_matter]

## Conservation and campaign design

Full GTDB r214 pangenome analysis expanded conservation coverage from 32,791 dark genes, or 57.5%, to 37,997, or 66.6%, by querying 93.5M gene-cluster annotations and recovering 5,206 additional dark genes through ortholog-group propagation. [src: functional_dark_matter] A conservation-weighted covering set selected 42 organisms covering 95.6% of importance-weighted priority across 28,584 high-priority dark genes. [src: functional_dark_matter] A separate darkness-spectrum analysis classified 57,011 dark genes into T1 Void (4,273), T2 Twilight (12,282), T3 Dusk (16,103), T4 Penumbra (22,500), and T5 Dawn (1,853). [src: functional_dark_matter]

The conservation-by-ignorance classification assigned 6.0% of dark genes to strong testable hypotheses, 52.5% to weak leads, and 41.5% to true knowledge gaps. [src: functional_dark_matter] The report’s highest-ranked knowledge gaps included COG0468 with 27,427 species across 142 phyla, COG0443 with 27,279 species, and COG0491 with 27,393 species. [src: functional_dark_matter] These results support [[concepts/functional-dark-matter]] and [[concepts/pangenome-integration]], but broad conservation should be treated as a prioritization signal rather than evidence that a gene has the same function in every lineage. [src: functional_dark_matter]

## Environmental validation and its limits

Within-species carrier-versus-non-carrier analyses tested 151 accessory dark-gene clusters across 31 species, and 10 of 137 clusters with environmental-category tests showed significant enrichment at FDR < 0.05. [src: functional_dark_matter] One of 67 clusters showed significant AlphaEarth embedding divergence. [src: functional_dark_matter] A pre-registered lab–field mapping found 29 of 47 testable clusters concordant, or 61.7%, with pH-associated genes showing 100% concordance across 4 tests and nitrogen-source genes showing 78% concordance across 9 tests. [src: functional_dark_matter]

NMDC, the National Microbiome Data Collaborative dataset, independently mapped 5 of 6 carrier genera to 47 taxon columns across 6,365 metagenomic samples and confirmed all 4 testable pre-registered abiotic predictions. [src: functional_dark_matter] Nitrogen-source carriers correlated with total nitrogen at ρ = +0.109 across n = 1,231 samples with FDR = 2.3e-4 and with ammonium nitrogen at ρ = +0.231 across n = 1,230 samples with FDR = 8.0e-16. [src: functional_dark_matter] pH carriers correlated with pH at ρ = +0.157 across n = 4,366 samples with FDR = 7.4e-25, and anaerobic carriers correlated negatively with dissolved oxygen at ρ = -0.298 across n = 272 samples with FDR = 1.5e-6. [src: functional_dark_matter]

The environmental evidence is supportive but not conclusive. [src: functional_dark_matter] The one-sided binomial test for 29/47 concordant clusters gave p = 0.072 with Wilson score 95% CI [0.474, 0.742], whereas Fisher’s combined probability across 47 individual tests gave p = 0.031. [src: functional_dark_matter] NMDC trait-feature analysis confirmed all 7 pre-registered trait-condition predictions at FDR < 10⁻²¹, but 441 of 449 exploratory tests also reached FDR < 0.05, which the report attributes largely to compositional coupling because abundant genera contribute to both carrier abundance and community trait scores. [src: functional_dark_matter] This limitation connects the framework to [[concepts/confirmatory-exploratory-ecological-association-discordance]] and [[concepts/coverage-confounding-of-community-functional-scores]]. [src: functional_dark_matter]

## Reannotation and coverage bias

Bakta v1.12.0 with DB v6.0 reclassified 33,105 of 39,532 pangenome-linked dark genes, or 83.7%, as not hypothetical, representing 58.1% of all dark genes. [src: functional_dark_matter] All 100 top candidates received Bakta product descriptions, and 5 dark genes received AMRFinderPlus annotations. [src: functional_dark_matter] A further 6,427 genes remained hypothetical in both Fitness Browser and Bakta but gained UniRef50 links for 79.4%, UniParc/UniRef100 links for 69.1%, and RefSeq links for 62.4%. [src: functional_dark_matter]

These results weaken any interpretation of the 57,011-gene total as a definitive count of true functional darkness because database release and annotation coverage affect whether a gene is labeled unknown. [src: functional_dark_matter] The report also found strong taxonomic imbalance: 37/48 organisms were Pseudomonadota, and the top 500 candidates included 417 from Gammaproteobacteria, 71 from Alphaproteobacteria, 17 from Deltaproteobacteria, 12 from Bacteroidetes, 10 from Betaproteobacteria, 5 from Firmicutes, 2 from Cyanobacteria, and 0 from Archaea, Actinobacteria, or Epsilonproteobacteria. [src: functional_dark_matter] The framework therefore supports [[concepts/bioinformatic-representation-coverage-bias]] and [[concepts/genetic-perturbation-coverage-bias]] by making candidate rankings conditional on both annotation resources and perturbation availability. [src: functional_dark_matter]

## Tensions

The framework simultaneously prioritizes genes with strong measured phenotypes and genes that are conserved or environmentally structured, but these evidence types do not guarantee the same ranking. [src: functional_dark_matter] Conservation-dominant or drop-tractability settings retained only 64% of the original fitness-active top 50, while essential-gene rankings changed substantially when tractability or neighbor context was removed. [src: functional_dark_matter] Environmental associations also support hypotheses while remaining vulnerable to sparse metadata, genus-level matching, common-genus effects, and compositional coupling. [src: functional_dark_matter]

A second tension concerns module and neighborhood evidence: 6,142 dark genes belonged to ICA fitness modules, 30,190 dark genes shared a predicted operon with an annotated gene, and 97.2% had at least one annotated neighbor within a five-gene window, but module and neighborhood predictions are guilt-by-association inferences rather than direct experimental validation. [src: functional_dark_matter] The report further cautions that the 97.2% neighbor rate is expected given a 75% genome-wide annotation rate. [src: functional_dark_matter]

## Open Directions

- Re-run the NMDC carrier–trait analysis with full sample-label permutation tests to determine how much of the 441/449 exploratory-test signal remains after controlling for compositional coupling. [src: functional_dark_matter]
- Process equivalent annotated-accessory-gene controls through the complete biogeographic pipeline to test whether dark-gene environmental enrichments exceed the baseline produced by annotation and sampling structure. [src: functional_dark_matter]
- Use CRISPRi in the highest-ranked essential candidates and measure growth under standard, stress, nitrogen-source, and carbon-source conditions to test whether neighborhood- and domain-based predictions produce reproducible phenotypes. [src: functional_dark_matter]
- Combine EC matching, structure prediction, purified-protein enzymology, and pathway growth assays for the GapMind candidates to resolve organism-level pathway co-occurrence into direct gene-to-reaction assignments. [src: functional_dark_matter]
- Repeat the six-axis prioritization with expanded Fitness Browser condition coverage and more evenly distributed taxa to quantify how much candidate rank is driven by assay depth and Pseudomonadota representation. [src: functional_dark_matter]
- Compare conserved-synteny, co-fitness, and CRISPRi perturbation results for the 998 double-validated operon pairs to test whether network evidence predicts causal functional coupling. [src: functional_dark_matter]
