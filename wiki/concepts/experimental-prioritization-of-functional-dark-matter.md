# Evidence-weighted experimental prioritization of unknown bacterial genes

[functional_dark_matter__REPORT](../summaries/functional_dark_matter__REPORT.md) describes an evidence-weighted framework for converting unknown bacterial genes into experimentally testable hypotheses by combining fitness, essentiality, conservation, pathway, environmental, genomic-context, and annotation evidence. [truly_dark_genes__REPORT](../summaries/truly_dark_genes__REPORT.md) refines this framework by separating annotation-lag genes from genes that remain hypothetical after modern reannotation and by adding a clue-based prioritization of the residual unknown set. [conservation_vs_fitness__REPORT](../summaries/conservation_vs_fitness__REPORT.md) further refines the conservation axis by directly linking Fitness Browser genes to KBase pangenome clusters and testing essentiality against core, auxiliary, and unmapped conservation categories. [discoveries](../summaries/discoveries.md) **supports** the framework's evidence-triangulation premise: across 47 testable dark-gene clusters, 29 (61.7%) showed directional concordance between laboratory fitness conditions and carrier-genome environments, with Fisher's combined p=0.031, while independent NMDC validation confirmed all 4 pre-registered abiotic predictions and all 7 pre-registered trait predictions. [^discoveries]

## Scope and evidence base

Across 48 organisms and 228,709 genes, the analysis identified 57,011 dark genes—hypothetical, DUF, or otherwise uncharacterized genes—representing 24.9% of the catalog. [^functional_dark_matter] The proportion of dark genes ranged from more than 35% to less than 15% among organisms, which the report interprets as annotation-depth variation rather than established variation in functional content. [^functional_dark_matter]

Fitness and essentiality supplied the main experimental evidence streams: 7,787 dark genes had strong fitness effects, defined as absolute fitness of at least 2 in at least one condition, and 9,557 were classified as essential because no viable transposon mutants were recovered. [^functional_dark_matter] Together, 17,344 dark genes had experimentally measurable phenotypes under the available assays. [^functional_dark_matter] This supports [condition-specific-fitness](condition-specific-fitness.md) and [gene-essentiality](gene-essentiality.md) by showing that unknown sequence annotation does not imply an absence of experimentally accessible phenotype. **Discoveries further supports this interpretation** at broader scale: across 25,271 true trade-off genes, 17.8% were important in some conditions and burdensome in others, showing why condition-specific measurements should remain separate evidence axes rather than being collapsed into a single essentiality label. [^discoveries]

The evidence base was uneven across gene classes: 39,532 of the 57,011 dark genes had pangenome links, 12,686 were accessory, 511 were both accessory and strongly fitness-active, and 6,142 belonged to independent-component-analysis (ICA) fitness modules, which group genes with coordinated fitness profiles. [^functional_dark_matter] Stress conditions, including metals, oxidative stress, and osmotic stress, produced the largest concentration of strong dark-gene phenotypes, followed by carbon-source and nitrogen-source conditions. [^functional_dark_matter] The residual truly dark subset is more selective: among genes with strong phenotypes, stress conditions were reported less often for truly dark genes than for annotation-lag genes, while mixed-community, iron, nutrient time-series, and rich-media signals were reported as enriched. [^truly_dark_genes]

The new pangenome linkage supports using conservation as an evidence axis but also calibrates its interpretation: 44 of 48 Fitness Browser organisms mapped to pangenome species clades, producing 177,863 gene-to-cluster links with 100.0% median protein identity and 94.2% median gene coverage; 33 organisms were retained for downstream analysis after coverage and identifier exclusions. [^conservation_vs_fitness] Among 148,826 protein-coding genes in those organisms, 27,693 were putative essential genes, or 18.6% overall, with per-organism values ranging from 12.9-28.9%. [^conservation_vs_fitness] Essentiality was inferred from RB-TnSeq, a random-barcode transposon sequencing approach, under the library construction and growth conditions represented in the Fitness Browser. [^conservation_vs_fitness]

Discoveries **refines** the evidence base by showing that unknown-gene status is often a property of annotation coverage rather than absence of homologous information. Across 27,690 species, 55.9% of dark-gene ortholog groups were kingdom-level, with species counts ranging from 1 to 27,482 and median 135; in a 57,011-gene darkness spectrum, only 4,273 genes (7.5%) were T1 Void, whereas 22,500 (39.5%) were T4 Penumbra with 3–4 converging evidence lines. [^discoveries]

## Evidence-weighted prioritization

The six-axis prioritization score combined fitness importance, cross-organism conservation, inference quality, pangenome distribution, biogeographic signal, and experimental tractability. [^functional_dark_matter] It ranked 17,344 phenotype-bearing dark genes, and the top 100 candidates spanned 22 organisms. [^functional_dark_matter] Shewanella MR-1 contributed 25 of the top 100 candidates, *Pseudomonas putida* N2C3 contributed 18, and Marinobacter contributed 9. [^functional_dark_matter] Of these candidates, 82/100 had high-confidence functional hypotheses supported by at least 3 evidence types, and 85/100 had module-based predictions. [^functional_dark_matter]

The score is therefore best interpreted as a decision aid rather than a direct probability that a gene has a particular function. [^functional_dark_matter] Rank stability was high for overall configurations, with rank correlations remaining ρ > 0.93 across six alternative configurations, but only 64% of the original fitness-active top 50 remained under conservation-dominant or drop-tractability settings. [^functional_dark_matter] Essential-gene rankings were more sensitive: essential-gene top-50 retention was 36% when tractability was dropped and 48% when neighbor context was dropped. [^functional_dark_matter] These sensitivity results support [experimental-prioritization-of-functional-dark-matter](experimental-prioritization-of-functional-dark-matter.md) as a framework for transparent trade-offs rather than a fixed universal ranking. [^functional_dark_matter]

The conservation-versus-fitness analysis supports the use of essentiality as a prioritization signal while showing that it is not equivalent to conservation: essential genes were 86.1% core versus 81.2% for non-essential genes, with a median odds ratio of 1.56; 18 of 33 organisms showed significant enrichment after Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate (BH-FDR) correction at q < 0.05. [^conservation_vs_fitness] Thus, viability-required genes are generally more conserved within species, but the enrichment is modest and most genes in well-characterized bacteria are core regardless of essentiality. [^conservation_vs_fitness] Discoveries **supports this caution** across 48 bacteria: 859 of 17,222 ortholog families (5.0%) were universally essential, 4,799 (27.9%) variably essential, and 11,564 (67.1%) never essential; universally essential families were 91.7% core versus 80.7% for non-essential genes, while orphan essential genes were only 49.5% core. [^discoveries]

The truly dark analysis supports this evidence-weighting principle but narrows the target population. Of 57,011 dark genes, 33,105 were reclassified by Bakta and 6,427 remained hypothetical in both pipelines among 39,532 pangenome-linked genes; 17,479 lacked pangenome links and could not be assessed. [^truly_dark_genes] The persistent set was ranked using fitness importance, annotation clues, ortholog breadth, genomic context, and experimental tractability, producing 100 candidates across 19 organisms; 34 were essential, 53 were in operons, and 30 were in ICA modules. [^truly_dark_genes] This refines the earlier ranking by showing that an unknown-gene campaign should distinguish residual annotation resistance from database lag and should explicitly represent linkage uncertainty.

A 12-dimensional clue matrix further supports prioritizing genes with partial evidence rather than treating all dark genes as equally unknown: 246 genes (3.8%) had zero clues, whereas 2,314 genes (Tier 3 plus Tier 4) had partial functional or phenotype evidence suitable for narrowing experimental hypotheses. [^truly_dark_genes] The result complements, rather than replaces, the earlier finding that 82/100 candidates had hypotheses supported by at least 3 evidence types. [^functional_dark_matter] [^truly_dark_genes] Discoveries **supports and broadens this distinction**: Bakta v1.12.0 reclassified 33,105 of 39,532 dark genes with pangenome links (83.7%), leaving 6,427 truly dark genes; only 246/6,427 (3.8%) had zero annotation clues. [^discoveries]

Annotation breadth itself should be treated as a composite evidence problem. Discoveries found that Bakta and eggNOG were complementary across 132.5M gene clusters: their union increased any-functional-annotation coverage to 77.3%, and Bakta rescued 11.2M clusters among 39.2M missed by eggNOG. [^discoveries] This **supports** using multiple annotation pipelines before assigning experimental priority, while the fact that only 33.3% of Bakta's 17.6M distinct UniRef50 IDs existed in the BERDL UniProt identifier table **refines** the interpretation by making database provenance and identifier coverage part of the ranking uncertainty. [^discoveries]

## From candidate genes to experiments

A greedy set-cover analysis found that 10 organism–condition experiments would address 242 of the top 500 dark genes, or 45.3%. [^functional_dark_matter] The first three proposed experiments—Shewanella MR-1 under stress, nitrogen-source, and carbon-source conditions—would address 111 candidates, or 20.8%. [^functional_dark_matter] The complete ten-experiment sequence also included stress or carbon-source experiments in *P. putida* N2C3, *Sinorhizobium meliloti*, *P. stutzeri* RCH2, Marinobacter, *P. fluorescens* GW456-L13, and *P. putida*. [^functional_dark_matter]

This experiment-level optimization extends [fitness-module-detection-sensitivity](fitness-module-detection-sensitivity.md) by treating condition selection and organism selection as coverage problems rather than evaluating genes independently. [^functional_dark_matter] The approach also exposes a depth bias: Fitness Browser condition coverage is uneven, and MR-1 has 121 historical conditions, so organisms with deeper condition coverage can produce more specific phenotypes and receive higher prioritization scores. [^functional_dark_matter]

For non-essential genes, RB-TnSeq—randomly barcoded transposon sequencing—provides condition-specific fitness measurements when viable insertion mutants are available. [^functional_dark_matter] Essential dark genes require a separate strategy because standard RB-TnSeq yields no viable knockout fitness profiles for them. [^functional_dark_matter] The report recommends CRISPRi, or CRISPR interference for transcriptional knockdown, including Mobile-CRISPRi for less-established organisms, followed by growth measurements under standard and stress conditions. [^functional_dark_matter] The truly dark analysis refines this recommendation by identifying short length, sparse orthology, and limited insertion opportunities as confounders: truly dark genes had a median length of 121 amino acids versus 194 for annotation-lag genes, and short genes can be harder both to annotate and to measure with transposon insertions. [^truly_dark_genes]

Discoveries **supports** the need for orthogonal perturbation and condition coverage: fitness modules derived by robust independent component analysis (ICA) and DBSCAN were stable at 17–52 modules per organism across 32 bacteria, 94.2% had significantly elevated within-module cofitness, and absolute module-membership thresholding increased within-module mean |r| from 0.047 to 0.34. [^discoveries] However, ortholog scope changed the number of cross-organism module families from 27 to 156, and Module-ICA had <1% KO-level precision despite capturing process-level co-regulation; this **refines** module-based prioritization by separating gene-level transfer from process-level hypotheses. [^discoveries]

The conservation-versus-fitness results refine experimental interpretation of essential dark genes. Essential-core genes numbered 22,751 and were 41.9% enzymes and 13.0% hypothetical, whereas essential-auxiliary genes numbered 3,683 and were 13.4% enzymes and 38.2% hypothetical; essential-unmapped genes were 44.7% hypothetical. [^conservation_vs_fitness] Essential-core genes were the best annotated category, with 87% having known function, while essential-auxiliary and essential-unmapped genes provide a more targeted pool for CRISPRi validation because their essentiality is paired with weaker conservation or annotation evidence. [^conservation_vs_fitness] The essential-unmapped category included divergent ribosomal proteins, translation factors, transposases, and DNA-binding proteins, but these assignments remain hypotheses about recently acquired or highly divergent variants of core functions rather than direct functional proof. [^conservation_vs_fitness]

## Functional inference before validation

GapMind identified 1,256 organism–pathway pairs across 44 Fitness Browser-linked species in which nearly complete pathways with low `steps_missing_low` scores co-occurred with strongly fitness-active dark genes. [^functional_dark_matter] The most frequent gaps were fucose utilization in 32 organisms, rhamnose utilization in 31, sorbitol utilization in 30, myoinositol utilization in 28, gluconate utilization in 26, and asparagine biosynthesis in 24. [^functional_dark_matter]

These pathway results generate organism-level hypotheses rather than direct gene-to-enzyme assignments. [^functional_dark_matter] Domain matching produced 42,239 gene–pathway candidates across 3,186 unique dark genes, including 5,398 high-confidence EC-prefix matches, 4,687 medium-confidence Pfam-family matches, and 32,154 low-confidence keyword matches. [^functional_dark_matter] Individual assignments therefore require EC matching, structure prediction, enzymology, or other experimental validation. [^functional_dark_matter] This evidence boundary refines [metabolic-model-gapfilling](metabolic-model-gapfilling.md) and [evidence-triangulation-for-functional-annotation](evidence-triangulation-for-functional-annotation.md) by separating pathway plausibility from gene-level functional proof.

Discoveries **supports this boundary** through a complementary annotation benchmark: eggNOG had higher COG, KEGG, and Pfam coverage, whereas Bakta had higher GO coverage, product descriptions, and unique UniRef50 links; their union reached 77.3% any-functional-annotation coverage but did not make every product assignment experimentally established. [^discoveries] The finding **refines** pathway-based prioritization by favoring candidates where independent annotation, fitness, and biochemical evidence converge rather than treating any single database label as proof.

Cross-organism and genomic-context evidence increased prioritization confidence without constituting direct functional validation. [^functional_dark_matter] Fitness concordance identified 65 ortholog groups present in at least three organisms whose dark-gene orthologs showed measurable effects under the same condition classes, with several groups reaching concordance of 1.00. [^functional_dark_matter] Cross-species synteny conserved 17,058 of 21,011 tested dark-gene–operon-partner pairs in at least one other organism and 10,150 in at least 3 organisms. [^functional_dark_matter] Independent co-fitness analysis found evidence for 2,899 of 32,075 non-essential operon pairs, including 1,129 mutual top-5 pairs, and 998 pairs were double-validated by conserved synteny and strong co-fitness. [^functional_dark_matter] These results support [cofitness-network-architecture](cofitness-network-architecture.md) while retaining the distinction between association and experimentally demonstrated function.

The truly dark results support using context as a prioritization signal: among 4,394 such genes with integrated condition analysis, 25.9% showed operon-like cofitness with adjacent genes at |r| ≥ 0.3, and ICA modules linked candidates to functions including phage integrases, metal transporters, chemotaxis systems, and iron regulation. [^truly_dark_genes] They also reinforce the need for validation because some strong fitness phenotypes may reflect polar effects on downstream genes. [^truly_dark_genes] Discoveries **supports the network evidence while calibrating transferability**: Pfam domains increased module annotation from 92/1,116 (8.2%) to 890/1,116 (79.7%), and ortholog transfer achieved 95.8% precision and 91.2% coverage for gene-level KO prediction, whereas Module-ICA remained a process-level rather than gene-level predictor. [^discoveries]

## Conservation and campaign design

Full GTDB r214 pangenome analysis expanded conservation coverage from 32,791 dark genes, or 57.5%, to 37,997, or 66.6%, by querying 93.5M gene-cluster annotations and recovering 5,206 additional dark genes through ortholog-group propagation. [^functional_dark_matter] A conservation-weighted covering set selected 42 organisms covering 95.6% of importance-weighted priority across 28,584 high-priority dark genes. [^functional_dark_matter] A separate darkness-spectrum analysis classified 57,011 dark genes into T1 Void (4,273), T2 Twilight (12,282), T3 Dusk (16,103), T4 Penumbra (22,500), and T5 Dawn (1,853). [^functional_dark_matter]

The conservation-by-ignorance classification assigned 6.0% of dark genes to strong testable hypotheses, 52.5% to weak leads, and 41.5% to true knowledge gaps. [^functional_dark_matter] The report’s highest-ranked knowledge gaps included COG0468 with 27,427 species across 142 phyla, COG0443 with 27,279 species, and COG0491 with 27,393 species. [^functional_dark_matter] These results support [functional-dark-matter](functional-dark-matter.md) and [pangenome-integration](pangenome-integration.md), but broad conservation should be treated as a prioritization signal rather than evidence that a gene has the same function in every lineage. [^functional_dark_matter]

The Fitness Browser–pangenome analysis supports this calibration while adding a within-species essentiality test: 145,821 linked genes (82.0%) were in core clusters and 32,042 (18.0%) were auxiliary, with 7,574 singleton genes included in the auxiliary category. [^conservation_vs_fitness] Essential genes were more often core than non-essential genes, but the median odds ratio was only 1.56, so essentiality does not identify a uniformly conserved class. [^conservation_vs_fitness] Clade-size and lifestyle stratification indicated that essential-core enrichment was robust across the genomic contexts examined, although clades containing only 2 genomes can have trivially high core fractions and therefore reduced discriminative power. [^conservation_vs_fitness]

The truly dark comparison refines this interpretation: persistent hypothetical genes had a core-genome fraction of 43.1% versus 72.7% for annotation-lag genes, ortholog presence of 29.3% versus 63.7%, and median ortholog breadth of 1 versus 4 organisms, with all reported differences strongly significant. [^truly_dark_genes] Their shorter length and narrower taxonomic distribution are consistent with biological novelty, but the report notes that these measurements do not by themselves establish a function or recent horizontal gene transfer. [^truly_dark_genes]

Discoveries **supports the need for clade-aware calibration**: pangenome openness showed no significant relationship with environment or phylogenetic effects in one analysis (rho=-0.05, p=0.54 and rho=0.03, p=0.73), while variable metabolic pathways strongly predicted openness in another (rho=0.327, p=7.2e-71, and partial rho=0.530, p=2.83e-203 after controlling for genome count). [^discoveries] These results do not replace the existing conservation evidence; they **refine** campaign design by indicating that inferred novelty and conservation can depend on pathway composition, genome sampling, and the analysis scale.

## Environmental validation and its limits

Within-species carrier-versus-non-carrier analyses tested 151 accessory dark-gene clusters across 31 species, and 10 of 137 clusters with environmental-category tests showed significant enrichment at FDR < 0.05. [^functional_dark_matter] One of 67 clusters showed significant AlphaEarth embedding divergence. [^functional_dark_matter] A pre-registered lab–field mapping found 29 of 47 testable clusters concordant, or 61.7%, with pH-associated genes showing 100% concordance across 4 tests and nitrogen-source genes showing 78% concordance across 9 tests. [^functional_dark_matter]

NMDC, the National Microbiome Data Collaborative dataset, independently mapped 5 of 6 carrier genera to 47 taxon columns across 6,365 metagenomic samples and confirmed all 4 testable pre-registered abiotic predictions. [^functional_dark_matter] Nitrogen-source carriers correlated with total nitrogen at ρ = +0.109 across n = 1,231 samples with FDR = 2.3e-4 and with ammonium nitrogen at ρ = +0.231 across n = 1,230 samples with FDR = 8.0e-16. [^functional_dark_matter] pH carriers correlated with pH at ρ = +0.157 across n = 4,366 samples with FDR = 7.4e-25, and anaerobic carriers correlated negatively with dissolved oxygen at ρ = -0.298 across n = 272 samples with FDR = 1.5e-6. [^functional_dark_matter]

The environmental evidence is supportive but not conclusive. [^functional_dark_matter] The one-sided binomial test for 29/47 concordant clusters gave p = 0.072 with Wilson score 95% CI [0.474, 0.742], whereas Fisher’s combined probability across 47 individual tests gave p = 0.031. [^functional_dark_matter] NMDC trait-feature analysis confirmed all 7 pre-registered trait-condition predictions at FDR < 10⁻²¹, but 441 of 449 exploratory tests also reached FDR < 0.05, which the report attributes largely to compositional coupling because abundant genera contribute to both carrier abundance and community trait scores. [^functional_dark_matter] This limitation connects the framework to [confirmatory-exploratory-ecological-association-discordance](confirmatory-exploratory-ecological-association-discordance.md) and [taxonomic-resolution-dependent-functional-inference](taxonomic-resolution-dependent-functional-inference.md).

Discoveries **supports the pre-registered portion but reinforces the caution about exploratory associations**: all 4 pre-registered abiotic predictions and all 7 pre-registered trait predictions were confirmed, while the NMDC metabolomics analysis was dominated by one study, with 125/131 samples (95%) from `nmdc:sty-11-r2h77870`; only approximately 2% of compounds had KEGG IDs, and three amino-acid pathways—cysteine, histidine, and lysine—were untestable because compounds were absent. [^discoveries]

## Reannotation and coverage bias

Bakta v1.12.0 with DB v6.0 reclassified 33,105 of 39,532 pangenome-linked dark genes, or 83.7%, as not hypothetical, representing 58.1% of all dark genes. [^functional_dark_matter] All 100 top candidates received Bakta product descriptions, and 5 dark genes received AMRFinderPlus annotations. [^functional_dark_matter] A further 6,427 genes remained hypothetical in both Fitness Browser and Bakta but gained UniRef50 links for 79.4%, UniParc/UniRef100 links for 69.1%, and RefSeq links for 62.4%. [^functional_dark_matter]

The truly dark analysis supports the interpretation that most dark-gene labels reflect annotation lag while identifying a residual set: 33,105 genes were reclassified, whereas 6,427 remained hypothetical in both pipelines; only 4.0% of the latter had Pfam hits and 4.6% had KEGG KOs, although 79.4% had UniRef50 links. [^truly_dark_genes] These results weaken any interpretation of the 57,011-gene total as a definitive count of true functional darkness because database release and annotation coverage affect whether a gene is labeled unknown. [^functional_dark_matter] The residual estimate is itself incomplete: 17,479 dark genes lacked pangenome links, and the report estimates approximately 2,841 additional truly dark genes among them. [^truly_dark_genes]

Discoveries **supports** the reannotation result and adds a stronger operational distinction: among 39,532 dark genes with pangenome links, 33,105 (83.7%) were reclassified and 6,427 remained truly dark; truly dark genes were shorter (121 versus 194 aa), less conserved (43% versus 73% core), had fewer orthologs (29% versus 64%), had higher GC deviation (d=0.247), and had 41% of neighbors also hypothetical. [^discoveries] Only 246/6,427 (3.8%) had zero annotation clues, so experimental campaigns should prioritize residual genes with interpretable partial evidence before treating the entire dark-gene set as equally inaccessible.

The report also found strong taxonomic imbalance: 37/48 organisms were Pseudomonadota, and the top 500 candidates included 417 from Gammaproteobacteria, 71 from Alphaproteobacteria, 17 from Deltaproteobacteria, 12 from Bacteroidetes, 10 from Betaproteobacteria, 5 from Firmicutes, 2 from Cyanobacteria, and 0 from Archaea, Actinobacteria, or Epsilonproteobacteria. [^functional_dark_matter] The framework therefore supports [structural-annotation-gap](structural-annotation-gap.md) and [genetic-perturbation-coverage-bias](genetic-perturbation-coverage-bias.md) by making candidate rankings conditional on both annotation resources and perturbation availability. [^functional_dark_matter]

Pangenome linkage introduces additional coverage constraints: four Fitness Browser organisms—Cola, Kang, Magneto, and SB2B—were unmatched because their species had too few genomes for GTDB pangenome construction; Dyella79 was excluded because a locus-tag mismatch produced a 0% join rate, and ten organisms were excluded from Phase 2 because they had <90% DIAMOND coverage. [^conservation_vs_fitness] The main *Escherichia coli* clade was absent because it contained too many genomes, while Keio mapped to a small *s__Escherichia_coli_E* clade at only 26.1% coverage. [^conservation_vs_fitness] These limitations refine the interpretation of conservation-weighted rankings: missing or weakly sampled clades can affect both conservation labels and the apparent experimental priority of unknown genes.

Discoveries **further refines** this coverage warning: only 6.8% of species (1,872/27,690) had sufficient AlphaEarth coverage for niche-breadth analyses, and 3,838/83,287 genomes had NaN dimensions; 36.6% of genomes clustered at coordinates with more than 50 genomes of more than 10 species. [^discoveries] Environmental and geographic scores should therefore be reported with coverage filters and treated as conditional evidence, not as universally available ranking dimensions.

## Tensions

The framework simultaneously prioritizes genes with strong measured phenotypes and genes that are conserved or environmentally structured, but these evidence types do not guarantee the same ranking. [^functional_dark_matter] Conservation-dominant or drop-tractability settings retained only 64% of the original fitness-active top 50, while essential-gene rankings changed substantially when tractability or neighbor context was removed. [^functional_dark_matter] Environmental associations also support hypotheses while remaining vulnerable to sparse metadata, genus-level matching, common-genus effects, and compositional coupling. [^functional_dark_matter]

The conservation-versus-fitness evidence adds a related tension rather than resolving it: essential genes were enriched in the core genome, but the median odds ratio was 1.56 and essentiality was measured under a single growth condition represented by RB-TnSeq library construction. [^conservation_vs_fitness] Thus, the modest core enrichment does not establish that genes important under stress or other unmeasured conditions will be conserved in the same way. [^conservation_vs_fitness] The essential-gene definition is also an upper bound because missing insertions can reflect short genes, low-complexity regions, or scaffold edges; essential genes were slightly shorter on average, consistent with insertion bias. [^conservation_vs_fitness]

A second tension concerns module and neighborhood evidence: 6,142 dark genes belonged to ICA fitness modules, 30,190 dark genes shared a predicted operon with an annotated gene, and 97.2% had at least one annotated neighbor within a five-gene window, but module and neighborhood predictions are guilt-by-association inferences rather than direct experimental validation. [^functional_dark_matter] The report further cautions that the 97.2% neighbor rate is expected given a 75% genome-wide annotation rate. [^functional_dark_matter] Discoveries adds a methodological tension: ortholog transfer achieved 95.8% precision and 91.2% coverage for gene-level KO prediction, but Module-ICA had <1% KO-level precision while capturing process-level co-regulation. [^discoveries] These should remain distinct evidence products rather than being combined into one confidence score without calibration.

A third, subset-specific tension concerns stress interpretation. The overall dark-gene analysis reports stress conditions as producing the largest concentration of strong dark-gene phenotypes. [^functional_dark_matter] In contrast, the truly dark analysis reports stress proportions of 28.7% versus 43.2% for annotation-lag genes among strong-phenotype genes, with OR = 0.53 and p < 0.001, while separately reporting stress at 43.3% versus 54.7% in another comparison. [^truly_dark_genes] The results should therefore not be collapsed: stress may be prominent among dark genes overall while being less characteristic of the residual genes that resist reannotation.

A fourth tension concerns pangenome openness and environmental inference. One discoveries analysis found no significant relationship between openness and environment or phylogenetic effects (rho=-0.05, p=0.54 and rho=0.03, p=0.73), whereas another found strong associations between variable metabolic pathways and openness (rho=0.327, p=7.2e-71, and partial rho=0.530, p=2.83e-203 after controlling for genome count). [^discoveries] These results **contradict one another at the level of the tested predictors**, not necessarily at the level of the prioritization framework; the discrepancy should be retained until feature definitions, species coverage, genome-count control, and analysis cohorts are reconciled.

## Open Directions

- Re-run the NMDC carrier–trait analysis with full sample-label permutation tests to determine how much of the 441/449 exploratory-test signal remains after controlling for compositional coupling. [^functional_dark_matter]
- Process equivalent annotated-accessory-gene controls through the complete biogeographic pipeline to test whether dark-gene environmental enrichments exceed the baseline produced by annotation and sampling structure. [^functional_dark_matter]
- Use CRISPRi in the highest-ranked essential candidates and measure growth under standard, stress, nitrogen-source, and carbon-source conditions to test whether neighborhood- and domain-based predictions produce reproducible phenotypes. [^functional_dark_matter]
- Compare essential-core, essential-auxiliary, and essential-unmapped genes across matched RB-TnSeq conditions and pangenome clade sizes to test whether conservation predicts condition-specific fitness beyond binary essentiality. [^conservation_vs_fitness]
- Combine EC matching, structure prediction, purified-protein enzymology, and pathway growth assays for the GapMind candidates to resolve organism-level pathway co-occurrence into direct gene-to-reaction assignments. [^functional_dark_matter]
- Repeat the six-axis prioritization with expanded Fitness Browser condition coverage and more evenly distributed taxa to quantify how much candidate rank is driven by assay depth and Pseudomonadota representation. [^functional_dark_matter]
- Compare conserved-synteny, co-fitness, and CRISPRi perturbation results for the 998 double-validated operon pairs to test whether network evidence predicts causal functional coupling. [^functional_dark_matter]
- Extend pangenome linkage to the 17,479 unlinked dark genes and test whether the estimated approximately 2,841 additional truly dark genes show the same clue, essentiality, and genomic-context distributions as the linked set. [^truly_dark_genes]
- Test whether the 3,683 essential-auxiliary genes compensate for missing core functions by combining ortholog-family analysis, pathway reconstruction, and targeted CRISPRi under the library condition and stress conditions. [^conservation_vs_fitness]
- Apply structure prediction and Foldseek searches to the 100 persistent truly dark candidates, then test the highest-ranked motility, nitrogen-source, stress, and transporter-associated hypotheses experimentally. [^truly_dark_genes]
- Reconcile the conflicting openness results by rerunning both predictor sets on the same species cohort with genome-count adjustment, explicit pathway definitions, and matched environment and phylogeny covariates. [^discoveries]
- Benchmark candidate rankings with Bakta–eggNOG union annotations, Pfam evidence, ortholog transfer, and independent CRISPRi outcomes to quantify whether multi-pipeline annotation improves experimental yield rather than merely reducing the dark-gene label. [^discoveries]
- Expand AlphaEarth-linked environmental validation beyond the 6.8% of species with sufficient coverage and test whether carrier–environment concordance persists after excluding dense coordinate clusters and correcting for study and sampling structure. [^discoveries]
