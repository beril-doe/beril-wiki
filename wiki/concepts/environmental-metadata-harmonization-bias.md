# Environmental Metadata Harmonization Can Reshape Ecological Genomic Inference

Environmental metadata harmonization converts heterogeneous sample descriptions into comparable ecological categories, but the conversion can change which patterns appear biologically meaningful. The [[summaries/env_embedding_explorer__REPORT]] shows that metadata coverage, coordinate quality, category definitions, and sampling composition influence analyses linking environment, geography, and gene content. [src: env_embedding_explorer] The [[summaries/ecotype_env_reanalysis__REPORT]] **refines** this interpretation: although clinical sampling bias is confirmed, a genome-level environmental-versus-human-associated reanalysis found no stronger environment–gene-content relationship in environmental species. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]

## Evidence from the AlphaEarth genome subset

The study analyzed 83,287 genomes with 64-dimensional AlphaEarth satellite-derived embeddings, representing 28.4% of 293,059 genomes in the KBase pangenome database. [src: env_embedding_explorer] Of these records, 79,449 had valid values across all 64 embedding dimensions and 3,838 contained at least one NaN. [src: env_embedding_explorer]

Environmental metadata were unevenly available: cleaned latitude and longitude were available for 83,286 genomes, geo_loc_name for 83,270, isolation_source for 76,295 (91.6%), host for 53,091 (63.7%), env_broad_scale for 34,800 (41.8%), env_local_scale for 31,541 (37.9%), and env_medium for 31,483 (37.8%). [src: env_embedding_explorer] The NCBI ncbi_env table contained 334 distinct harmonized attribute names across 4.1M rows, with collection_date covering 273K genomes, geo_loc_name covering 272K, and isolation_source covering 245K. [src: env_embedding_explorer]

A keyword-matching workflow mapped 5,774 unique isolation_source free-text values into 12 broad categories and captured 71% of genomes with an isolation_source label. [src: env_embedding_explorer] The resulting classification left 17% of genomes in Other and 12.5% in Unknown because isolation_source was missing or null. [src: env_embedding_explorer] The structured env_broad_scale field was cleaner but covered only 42% of genomes, and some records contained bare ENVO identifiers such as ENVO:00000428 or generic values such as not applicable and missing. [src: env_embedding_explorer]

These coverage differences **support** the [[concepts/environmental-resistome]] concern that apparent ecological distributions depend partly on which metadata field is available and how its values are interpreted. [src: env_embedding_explorer] Keyword matching can miss site-specific labels and non-English terms, so these categories are an operational harmonization rather than a complete ecological ontology. [src: env_embedding_explorer] Category assignment can consequently alter the balance between human-associated, environmental, and unresolved samples. [src: env_embedding_explorer] The reanalysis **refines** this concern by using a harmonized env_category mapping and majority vote over genome-level isolation_source assignments: among 224 species selected with at least 20 genomes with AlphaEarth embeddings and at least 30% coverage, 106 (47%) were majority human-associated, 47 (21%) majority environmental, and 71 (32%) Mixed/Other. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]

The [[summaries/ecotype_analysis__REPORT]] **supports** the coverage limitation but tests it in a narrower comparative set: it extracted 13,381 genomes across 224 species and produced correlation results for 172 species. [src: ecotype_analysis] In that analysis, the median partial correlation for environment was 0.0025 versus 0.0143 for phylogeny, and phylogeny dominated the gene-content signal in 60.5% of species versus 39.5% for environment. [src: ecotype_analysis] A significant positive environmental effect occurred in 12 species (7.0%), a significant negative effect in 4 species (2.3%), and no significant effect in 156 species (90.7%). [src: ecotype_analysis] This **refines** the interpretation of the low pooled environmental signal: it is consistent with genome-wide vertical inheritance dominating, while environmental adaptation may be restricted to specific gene subsets rather than whole-genome content. [src: ecotype_analysis]

## Sampling composition as an inferential confounder

The AlphaEarth subset was strongly human-associated: 38% of embedded genomes were human-associated, including 16,390 Human clinical genomes (20%), 13,466 Human gut genomes (16%), and 1,669 Human other genomes (2%). [src: env_embedding_explorer] Environmental categories were smaller: Soil contained 6,073 genomes (7%), Marine 5,850 (7%), and Freshwater 5,840 (7%). [src: env_embedding_explorer] Other contained 13,944 genomes (17%) and included site-specific labels such as Aspo HRL and Olkiluoto, generic labels such as water and bodily fluid, and clinical sites not captured by the keyword rules. [src: env_embedding_explorer]

This composition **supports** the [[concepts/clinical-sampling-bias-and-ecological-inference]] concern that a metadata-defined dataset may not represent the ecological population from which broader conclusions are drawn. [src: env_embedding_explorer] It also **refines** [[concepts/environment-embedding-geography]] analyses because human-associated and environmental samples showed different geographic embedding gradients. Environmental samples—Soil, Marine, Freshwater, Extreme, and Plant—showed mean cosine distance increasing from 0.27 for genomes collected within <100 km to 0.90 at intercontinental distances >10,000 km, a 3.4x gradient. [src: env_embedding_explorer] Human-associated samples increased from 0.37 nearby to 0.75 far away, a flatter 2.0x gradient. [src: env_embedding_explorer]

A pooled analysis can therefore make an environmental signal appear weaker when a large human-associated subset occupies relatively interchangeable urban environments. [src: env_embedding_explorer] The [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]] analysis found that AlphaEarth-based environment similarity had a median partial correlation of 0.0025 with gene content; the 38% human-associated composition may have diluted that relationship. [src: env_embedding_explorer] This is a confounding hypothesis requiring environmental-only reanalysis, not evidence that environmental similarity is generally unrelated to gene content. [src: env_embedding_explorer] The subsequent reanalysis **contradicts** the prediction that removing or distinguishing this composition alone would reveal a stronger environmental signal: environmental species had median partial correlation 0.051 versus 0.084 for human-associated species, with Environmental > Human-associated U=1536 and p=0.83. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] The continuous fraction-based analyses likewise found no relationship for environmental genomes (rho=-0.085, p=0.25) or human-associated genomes (rho=0.030, p=0.69). [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] The independent ecotype analysis **supports** this weak-signal interpretation while showing that phylogeny generally explains more of the observed gene-content similarity than environment. [src: ecotype_analysis]

## Geographic and embedding structure

Across 50,000 sampled genome pairs with good-quality coordinates, mean embedding cosine distance increased monotonically with geographic distance and plateaued at intercontinental scales. [src: env_embedding_explorer] The reported means were 0.41 for <100 km across 231 pairs; 0.51 for 100–500 km across 1,058 pairs; 0.56 for 500–1K km across 2,016 pairs; 0.66 for 1K–2K km across 3,779 pairs; 0.78 for 2K–5K km across 5,824 pairs; 0.80 for 5K–10K km across 20,935 pairs; and 0.82 for 10K–20K km across 16,107 pairs. [src: env_embedding_explorer] The relationship was strongest below 2,000 km and plateaued above 5,000 km, so it is not a simple raw-distance effect. [src: env_embedding_explorer]

The pattern is consistent with spatially autocorrelated environmental context, including likely climate, vegetation, land use, and urban/rural character, but its strength in a pooled dataset also depends on the relative representation of environmental, human-associated, and heterogeneous residual categories. [src: env_embedding_explorer] The reanalysis **refines** this interpretation: environmental embeddings had a 3.4x geographic-signal ratio versus 2.0x for human-associated embeddings, yet the greater geographic signal did not produce stronger species-level gene-content correlations. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] The report offers, but does not establish, the hypothesis that human-associated geographic structure may reflect regional epidemiological lineage patterns rather than ecological differences. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]

Coordinates are metadata, not ground truth. Among 83,286 genomes with latitude and longitude, 50,109 (60.2%) were classified as Good, 30,469 (36.6%) as Suspicious cluster, and 2,708 (3.3%) as Low precision because they used integer degrees. [src: env_embedding_explorer] The suspicious-cluster heuristic flagged locations shared by >50 genomes and >10 species as possible institutional addresses, but it also flagged legitimate field sites. [src: env_embedding_explorer] Examples included Rifle, Colorado (39.54, -107.78; 1,883 genomes; DOE IFRC groundwater research site), Saanich Inlet, British Columbia (48.36, -123.30; 1,529 genomes; oceanographic O2-minimum zone), and Siberian soda lakes (52.11, 79.17; 812 genomes; extremophile sampling campaigns). [src: env_embedding_explorer] Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania (40.44, -79.97; 630 genomes) was likely institutional and included diverse clinical sampling, while Lima, Peru (-12.0, -77.0; 1,641 genomes) had integer coordinates that were likely approximate. [src: env_embedding_explorer]

This **supports** the [[concepts/collection-site-versus-microenvironment-mismatch]] concern: a shared coordinate can represent a genuine field site, an institution, or an approximate geographic placeholder rather than a common microbial microenvironment. [src: env_embedding_explorer] Removing all clustered coordinates could preferentially remove well-sampled environmental campaigns, while retaining them could preserve institutional or clinical sampling concentrations. [src: env_embedding_explorer] Because AlphaEarth embedding cosine distance increased with geographic distance, coordinate errors or institutional clustering can propagate into apparent environmental structure. [src: env_embedding_explorer]

## Category definitions can create or hide ecological structure

UMAP, a nonlinear dimensional-reduction method, projected the embeddings into two dimensions, and DBSCAN, a density-based clustering method, identified 320 clusters. [src: env_embedding_explorer] Many clusters were dominated by a single harmonized environment category; rare categories such as Air, Extreme, and Plant concentrated in a few clusters, whereas Human gut and Human clinical genomes were distributed across many clusters, potentially reflecting geographic substructure. [src: env_embedding_explorer] Taxonomic coloring also showed structure, but that structure was partly confounded with environment; Campylobacterota, for example, were predominantly gut-associated. [src: env_embedding_explorer]

These results **support** stratifying or adjusting analyses by sample environment and taxonomy before interpreting embedding or gene-content gradients as environmental effects. [src: env_embedding_explorer] They also **refine** [[concepts/environmental-embedding-ecological-validity]] and [[concepts/ontology-and-category-schema-sensitivity]]: UMAP cluster structure depends on parameters including n_neighbors and min_dist, and DBSCAN with eps=0.5 produced 320 clusters that may be too fine-grained to match environment categories. [src: env_embedding_explorer]

The reanalysis **supports** retaining continuous or genome-level classifications alongside majority-vote categories: the species-level comparison used 37 Environmental, 93 Human-associated, and 53 Mixed/Other species, while the fraction-based Spearman tests also found no association. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] Environmental species had a higher NaN partial-correlation rate than human-associated species, 10/47 = 21% versus 7/100 = 7%; this filtering would, if anything, favor detection of a stronger environmental signal, which was not observed. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] The ecotype analysis **refines** this recommendation by proposing tests of specific COG functional categories, including V-Defense and L-Mobile, because whole-genome gene content may obscure environmentally selected subsets. [src: ecotype_analysis]

## Tensions

The structured env_broad_scale field is cleaner than free-text isolation_source but covers only 42% of the AlphaEarth genomes, whereas keyword harmonization covers 71% of genomes with an isolation_source label but leaves 17% in Other and 12.5% in Unknown. [src: env_embedding_explorer] This creates a tension between broader coverage and cleaner semantic interpretation: free-text classification may increase inclusion while increasing ambiguity, whereas env_broad_scale may improve consistency while reducing analyzable coverage. [src: env_embedding_explorer]

The embeddings show a clear pooled distance–geography relationship, but the report also identifies strong human-associated sampling bias and a flatter human-associated gradient. [src: env_embedding_explorer] These observations are not contradictory: the pooled gradient may combine genuine environmental distance-decay with differences in sample composition, while the relative contribution of each component remains unresolved. [src: env_embedding_explorer] The geographic signal is likewise compatible with spatially autocorrelated environmental context and with contributions from coordinate clustering or metadata errors. [src: env_embedding_explorer]

The original interpretation proposed that the 38% human-associated composition may have diluted the median partial correlation of 0.0025 and called for environmental-only reanalysis. [src: env_embedding_explorer] The reanalysis instead found no stronger Environmental correlation than Human-associated correlation (U=1536, p=0.83), confirming the null under its own consistent methodology. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] Absolute values cannot settle the discrepancy because the reanalysis median across 183 species was 0.081 versus 0.003 in the original analysis, characterized as a 27x difference; it used all embedded genomes, including up to 3,505 genomes per species, rather than diversity-maximizing downsampling with a maximum of 250 genomes and also used different genome sets. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] The ecotype analysis independently reports an environmental median of 0.0025 and phylogenetic median of 0.0143, but its 13,381-genome, 172-species result set is not directly comparable to either prior median. [src: ecotype_analysis] This **supports** the direction of phylogenetic dominance while preserving a methodological tension over sampling frame, genome set, and correlation estimates. [src: ecotype_analysis]

## Open Directions

- Use isolation_source homogeneity, species composition, and coordinate precision to reclassify the 30,469 Suspicious cluster and 2,708 Low precision coordinates, then test whether the embedding–geographic-distance relationship changes. [src: env_embedding_explorer]
- For the 34,800 genomes with env_broad_scale and the 76,295 with isolation_source, compare keyword categories with structured classifications using confusion matrices and category-specific embedding distances, asking which schema preserves ecological separation with fewer ambiguous assignments. [src: env_embedding_explorer]
- Repeat the [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]] analysis on environmental-only samples using phylogeny-controlled partial correlations, testing whether the median association increases above 0.0025 and whether similar AlphaEarth embeddings predict greater accessory-gene sharing after controlling for taxonomic relatedness. [src: env_embedding_explorer]
- Reweight or downsample Human clinical, Human gut, Human other, Soil, Marine, Freshwater, Extreme, and Plant categories, then repeat the geographic-distance analysis to test whether the reported 0.41-to-0.82 distance pattern changes with composition. [src: env_embedding_explorer]
- Add clinical body-site terms, underground-laboratory terms, generic water-source terms, and env_broad_scale fallback values to the harmonization workflow, then measure how many of the 13,944 Other genomes move into interpretable categories. [src: env_embedding_explorer]
- Re-run UMAP and DBSCAN across alternative n_neighbors, min_dist, and eps values, then test whether environment-dominated clusters persist in the original 64-dimensional space rather than only in the projection. [src: env_embedding_explorer]
- Correlate embedding dimensions A00–A63 with latitude, temperature, precipitation, NDVI, and land-cover classifications to identify which environmental features drive the observed distance-decay pattern. [src: env_embedding_explorer]
- Compare keyword-based isolation_source categories with env_broad_scale for the 42% of genomes covered by the structured field, and test whether environment–gene-content associations are stable across classification schemes. [src: env_embedding_explorer]
- Directly compare full-genome extraction with the original diversity-maximized downsampling, including genome count as a covariate, to resolve the 27x partial-correlation discrepancy and test whether unequal sampling depth inflates weak associations. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]
- Test transport and secondary-metabolism gene subsets, rather than whole-genome Jaccard distances, and repeat the analysis with structured ENVO terms from env_broad_scale to determine whether functional specificity or ontology precision reveals environmental effects masked in the aggregate. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]
- Test V-Defense and L-Mobile COG categories, alternative embedding distances, direct environmental metadata, and ecotype-cluster gene content in the 172-species analysis to determine whether functional or cluster-specific signals exceed the weak whole-genome association. [src: ecotype_analysis]
