# Metabolic guild co-occurrence and redox-driven segregation

The other pairwise coefficients are reported without a stated well count in their claims. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

## Guild assignment

The SSO analysis assigned 65 annotated genera to 11 metabolic guilds, and this revealed distinct functional assemblages at each well. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

## Positive couplings

### Nitrifier and iron oxidizer

Nitrifiers and iron oxidizers co-occurred strongly across the 9 wells (ρ = +0.95), with both concentrated at U3, the inferred plume entry. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The central digest reports the same result for the archaeal ammonia oxidizer Candidatus Nitrosotalea and the iron oxidizer [[entities/sideroxydans|Sideroxydans]] (ρ=+0.95, concentrated at U3). [src: discoveries] The reports call both chemolithotrophs exploiting reduced compounds (NH₄⁺, Fe²⁺) arriving in the plume. [src: discoveries, enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The digest suggests a shared niche at the oxic plume fringe. [src: discoveries] This is an interpretation; the correlation does not test it. The report places the coupling in a functional network overlaying the spatial redox gradient. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

### Syntroph and fermenter

Syntrophs and fermenters co-occurred (ρ = +0.55), read as classic anaerobic food web coupling. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The report calls this consistent with established syntrophic theory. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Fermenters are described as producing organic acids and H₂ that syntrophs consume in obligate partnerships with sulfate reducers or methanogens. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Syntrophic fatty acid degradation is said to need H₂, acetate and formate kept at low concentrations, which creates obligate partnerships. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

### Fermenter and predator

Fermenters and Bdellovibrio predators co-occurred (ρ = +0.85). The report reads this as predators tracking bacterial prey biomass, which is highest where fermentation supports dense populations. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

## Negative couplings and redox exclusion

Denitrifiers and syntrophs showed ρ = −0.67, read as mutual exclusion across the redox gradient. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Denitrifiers are described as thriving where nitrate is available under oxidizing conditions, and syntrophs as requiring strictly anaerobic conditions. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The report maps this onto canonical contaminated-aquifer redox zonation, where electron acceptor availability segregates guilds spatially. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Sulfate reducers and aerobic heterotrophs showed ρ = −0.75, called textbook redox separation at opposite ends of the electron acceptor hierarchy. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

## Limits

The nitrifier–iron oxidizer correlation is stated across 9 wells and is concentrated at one well, U3. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The syntrophy, predation and redox interpretations are the reports' readings, not tested mechanisms. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

## Open Directions

- Test whether the nitrifier–iron oxidizer coupling survives after removing U3, to see whether one well drives ρ = +0.95.
- Measure O₂, nitrate, NH₄⁺ and Fe²⁺ in the same wells and model guild abundance against them. This would separate redox exclusion from other spatial effects.
- Look for the same guild correlations in other well sets or time points, to check that they replicate.
- Check whether syntroph and fermenter genera carry the genes for H₂ or formate exchange, a test of the syntrophy interpretation of ρ = +0.55.
