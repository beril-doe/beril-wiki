# Subsurface Bacillota_B specialization and genome expansion

Deep-clay Bacillota_B differ from soil-baseline Bacillota_B in accessory gene content, genome size, and selected respiratory markers, but the interpretation depends on both phylogeny and whether genomes represent porewater-associated or rock-attached communities. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] [src: clay_confined_subsurface] The cultured deep-clay cohort matched the Bagnoud Mont Terri porewater paradigm rather than demonstrating representation of the rock-attached community. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]

The global soil analysis **refines** the scale at which this specialization should be interpreted: across 5,441 soil samples, measured stressors did not predict functional gene counts out of sample, and the clay-shield test found low-clay cross-validation R² = −0.268 versus high-clay R² = −0.292, difference 0.024, 95% CI: −0.423, 0.161. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] Because that confidence interval includes zero, the global result does not support higher predictability in high-clay soils. This **does not contradict** the deep-clay Bacillota_B genome comparison: the global analysis concerns community-level functional counts and measured stressors, whereas the Bacillota_B studies compare selected genomes and respiratory modules within a lineage and sampled compartment. It does, however, **refine** the specialization claim by showing that local genome-content differentiation should not be generalized to a globally predictable clay effect. [src: soil_frontier_genomics]

The SSO subsurface community analysis **supports** an environmental context for this specialization: hydrogeological zone explained 27.5% of sediment-community variance (F = 4.05, p = 0.0001), and Firmicutes were enriched with depth (Spearman ρ = +0.76). However, this evidence comes from 16S community composition and taxonomy-to-trait inference, not direct Bacillota_B genome measurements; it therefore **refines** rather than independently confirms the accessory-genome mechanism. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The genome-content comparison used 10 deep-clay and 62 soil-baseline Bacillota_B genomes, with eggNOG orthologous-group (OG) enrichment, genome-content statistics, and corrected multi-heme cytochrome detection. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] The deep-clay cohort came from Mont Terri Opalinus borehole and rock-porewater samples plus two Russian Beyelii Yar borehole genomes. The soil-baseline cohort included phylum-matched soil or sediment genomes across Syntrophomonadales, Desulfitobacteriales, Moorellales, Thermacetogeniales, Ammonifexales, Carboxydocellales, Desulfotomaculales, Heliobacteriales, and Thermincolales. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

The compartment-focused comparison analyzed 9 deep-confined genomes, 30 shallow-clay genomes, and a phylum-stratified soil baseline of 150 genomes, with 137 remaining after the reported quality filter. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] The deep cohort contained 8 Mont Terri Opalinus borehole genomes and 1 bentonite-formation genome; the shallow cohort contained 8 Coalvale silty-clay, 1 Cerrado clay, and 21 agricultural-clay genomes. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] Compartment annotations were inferred from isolation-source keywords, so bentonite or “rock” entries could represent porewater or rock-attached material. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]

## Accessory functions and anaerobic specialization

The 10-versus-62 comparison tested 14,109 Firmicutes-level eggNOG OGs with per-OG Fisher’s exact tests, Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate (FDR) correction, a fold-difference threshold of ≥3, and presence in at least 3 anchor genomes. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] It found 547 significantly enriched OGs at q<0.05, exceeding the preregistered H1 prediction of ≥10 and providing strong evidence for broad accessory enrichment. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

Keyword scanning classified 42 enriched OGs as anaerobic-respiration functions, 24 as sporulation-revival functions, 12 as mineral-attachment or exopolysaccharide functions, 4 as anaerobic regulators, and 3 as osmoadaptation functions; 462 were “other or unannotated.” [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] Examples included hydrogenase, cytochrome, sulfite, sulfate, nitrate, and fumarate reductases; NADH dehydrogenase, oxidoreductase, menaquinone, spore and germination, biofilm and capsule, sigma-factor, two-component-system, betaine, ectoine, osmoprotectant, and K⁺-uptake functions. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] Manual inspection **refines** this classification: the 462-OG category undercounted anaerobic-respiration and electron-transfer functions, with the true anaerobic-respiration-related total estimated at 80–100 of 547 enriched OGs. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

Selected examples were COG1977, associated with molybdopterin cofactor metabolism, in 10/10 anchors versus 11/62 baselines; OG 1UIFM, a DsrE/DsrF/DsrH-like family, in 7/10 versus 0/62; OG 1VFGN, a 4Fe–4S dicluster associated with 2-oxoglutarate:ferredoxin oxidoreductase, in 8/10 versus 4/62; and OG 1V0WU, a Major Facilitator Superfamily transporter, in 9/10 versus 5/62. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

These results support the hypothesis that sampled deep-clay Bacillota_B possess a broad accessory repertoire for anaerobic persistence, redox flexibility, mineral association, dormancy or revival, regulation, and osmotic stress. Because the anchor cohort is small and phylogenetically clustered, some OGs may be lineage markers rather than general subsurface-specialization features. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

The compartment analysis **supports** an anaerobic-persistence interpretation but **refines** its phylogenetic and ecological basis. Its anaerobic toolkit combined Wood–Ljungdahl (WL), group 1 [NiFe]-hydrogenase (NiFe), and dissimilatory sulfate-reduction (SR) modules. Mean toolkit scores were 1.889 for anchor_deep (n = 9), 0.033 for anchor_shallow (n = 30), and 0.393 for soil_baseline (n = 140); the proportions containing all three modules were 0.556, 0.000, and 0.021. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]

Against soil baseline, Fisher tests with BH-FDR gave WL OR = 10.4, p_BH = 0.004; NiFe OR = 10.5, p_BH = 0.004; SR OR = 33.8, p_BH = 2.5×10⁻⁴; iron reduction (IR) OR = 0.46, p_BH = 0.69; and nitrogenase OR = 5.4, p_BH = 0.035. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] Within Bacillota_B, WL occurred in 5/5 deep versus 15/19 baseline genomes, p = 0.54 and p_BH = 0.54 (1.0 as reported in the within-phylum table); NiFe occurred in 5/5 versus 14/19, also p = 0.54 and p_BH = 0.54 (1.0 as reported); and SR occurred in 5/5 versus 4/19, OR = ∞, p = 0.003, p_BH = 0.044. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] Thus WL and NiFe primarily tracked Bacillota_B lineage, whereas SR remained associated with the deep cohort after within-phylum control. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]

The strongest compartment-specific result was SR: 5/9 deep genomes carried sulfate-reduction markers, versus 1/9 iron-reduction-marker-positive genomes under the original analysis. Relative to the Mitzscherling (2023) rock-attached null, 5 observed positives versus 0.018 expected yielded binomial p = 4.0×10⁻¹²; the corresponding iron-reduction comparison was 1 observed versus 0.63 expected, p = 0.87. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] This **supports** a porewater-associated sulfate-reduction signature in the cultured deep cohort, not a complete census of rock-attached metabolism. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]

The 8 Opalinus genomes traced to BRC-3 or BIC-A1 borehole sources and included Desulfosporosinus, BRH-c8a, BRH-c4a, Lutibacter, BRH-c54, Roseovarius, and Stenotrophomonas lineages. Rock-attached Geobacter and Geothrix lineages and CPR/DPANN episymbionts were essentially absent, limiting inference about the complete rock-attached community. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]

The SSO data **refine** the environmental interpretation without testing these genomic claims. Inferred denitrification, iron oxidation, nitrification, iron reduction, sulfur oxidation, methanotrophy, and fermentation varied spatially, but genus-level functional annotation covered only 21% of reads and represented literature-linked taxonomic inference rather than direct genomic evidence. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Its inferred redox ladder and plume model are therefore hypotheses awaiting geochemical and metagenomic validation, not evidence that Bacillota_B OGs are expressed or selected in situ. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

## Genome expansion and biosynthetic interpretation

Deep-clay anchor genomes had mean genome sizes of 4,110,038 bp versus 3,046,124 bp in soil baseline, Cohen’s d=+1.39, Mann–Whitney p=0.025. CheckM-rescaled sizes were 4,323,230 bp versus 3,233,715 bp, d=+1.37, p=0.013. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] Mean eggNOG OG counts were 2,630 versus 2,106, d=+1.30, p=0.022; CheckM-rescaled counts were 2,771 versus 2,233, d=+1.32, p=0.009. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] Mean GC content was 48.84% versus 47.76%, d=+0.21, p=0.44. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] Mean CheckM completeness was 94.7% versus 94.3%, d=+0.08, p=0.93. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

Because genome quality was similar while raw and rescaled genome size and OG counts favored anchors, the report concludes that the larger genomes and higher OG counts cannot be explained by a quality artifact. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] All four size and OG-count metrics indicated significantly larger genomes, with p≤0.025 and d≥+1.30, rejecting H2’s prediction of smaller deep-clay genomes. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] This supports a self-sufficiency model for sampled cultivable subsurface Bacillota_B and contrasts with streamlining reported for Patescibacteria, which the report treats as a distinct ecological and phylogenetic group. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

The PHB comparison **refines** this genome-expansion interpretation by showing that genome size can be a major confounder when accessory metabolic capacity is related to ecological breadth. Across 27,690 GTDB species, PHB-positive species had median genome size of 4.34 Mbp versus 2.44 Mbp for PHB-negative species, while the raw PHB–niche-breadth association changed from rho = 0.106 to partial rho = −0.047 after controlling for genome size. [src: phb_granule_ecology] PHB enrichment nevertheless persisted within all four genome-size quartiles, at 1.4–4.6x enrichment with all p < 1e-11. [src: phb_granule_ecology] This **supports** treating genome expansion as biologically relevant in the sampled Bacillota_B cohort, but **refines** any claim that larger genomes alone demonstrate self-sufficiency or broad environmental specialization: genome size may carry both general accessory capacity and lineage- or pathway-specific functions. [src: phb_granule_ecology]

The global soil analysis **refines** this self-sufficiency interpretation. Its negative out-of-sample R² values—Soil & Climate, R² = −0.205 ± 0.197; Geochemical, R² = −0.331 ± 0.071; and Industrial, R² = −0.221 ± 0.042—do not distinguish true biological unpredictability from spatial distributional shift, outlier leverage, or other modelling failure. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] Thus they provide no independent evidence that clay-associated communities generally have expanded, predictable functional repertoires; the genome-expansion result remains a direct, cohort-specific genome comparison. This **supports** retaining the self-sufficiency claim for the sampled Bacillota_B cohort while limiting its extrapolation to global clay soils. [src: soil_frontier_genomics]

The compartment study **does not strengthen** generalized self-sufficiency. GapMind amino-acid pathway completeness across 18 pathways was 16.22/18 versus 16.66/18 before filtering (p = 0.153, d = −0.17), and 15.50/18 versus 17.14/18 after filtering (p = 0.009, d = −0.84). [src: clay_confined_subsurface] This is compatible with accessory expansion beyond the measured amino-acid pathways, and GapMind saturates near 18 for many cultured bacteria. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]

The environmental depth pattern **supports** the possibility that subsurface structure sorts lineages but cannot distinguish genome expansion from taxonomic turnover: same-well/different-depth samples had median Bray–Curtis dissimilarity = 0.977, whereas same-depth/different-well samples had 0.835. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] It therefore strengthens the case for matched depth, geochemistry, and phylogeny when testing accessory functions.

The GDI, defined as OTU Richness / (Mean Genome Completeness + 1), **refines** the representation context for these comparisons. At 1° spatial bins, forest had GDI = 902.36 and cropland had GDI = 890.82, while grassland had GDI = 503.42 and wetland had GDI = 525.13; forest and cropland were therefore jointly highest-GDI biomes. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] Frontier areas with GDI > 1000 had mean pH = 6.74, compared with mean pH = 5.94 in mapped areas, a +0.8 pH unit gap. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] Because GDI can equal 902 even when there are zero genomes and conflates richness with completeness, these results indicate a possible database-representation gap rather than direct evidence of genome expansion or subsurface specialization. [src: soil_frontier_genomics]

## Respiratory-marker correction

The original iron-reduction analysis used K07811, K17324, and K17323, but these are TMAO reductase, glycerol ABC ATP-binding, and glycerol ABC permease genes rather than canonical iron-reduction markers. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] [src: clay_confined_subsurface] The corrected detector used PFAM PF02085, PFAM PF22678, and a CXXCH heme-binding motif count of ≥4 in gene-cluster protein sequences, calling a genome positive when any signal was present. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

Corrected multi-heme cytochrome rates were 5/9 anchor_deep, or 55.6%, versus 1/9, or 11.1%, under the original markers; 12/30 anchor_shallow, or 40.0%, versus 15/30, or 50.0%, originally; and 61/149 soil_baseline, or 40.9%, versus 30/149, or 20.1%, originally. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] All corrected pairwise comparisons were nonsignificant: deep versus shallow OR 1.88, p=0.46; deep versus baseline OR 1.80, p=0.49; shallow versus baseline OR 0.96, p=1.0. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] This **contradicts** the original shallow-enrichment narrative and removes statistical support for a shallow-versus-deep iron-reduction contrast, while retaining the sulfate-reduction signal. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] [src: clay_confined_subsurface]

## Tensions and limits

The 547 enriched OGs provide strong cohort-level differentiation, but the anchor cohort spans four orders while clustering among 3 BRH-c8a, 2 BRH-c4a, 2 Desulfosporosinus, Desulforudis, Ch130, and 1 other genome. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] The compartment study likewise had only 9 deep genomes before filtering; within Bacillota_B, only SR remained significant, while WL and NiFe tracked lineage. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] These studies differ in cohort composition and analytical question, so neither establishes that every anaerobic function is independently selected by depth or compartment. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]

The genome-expansion result supports self-sufficiency, whereas GapMind found no elevated completeness and found 15.50/18 versus 17.14/18 after filtering. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] [src: clay_confined_subsurface] This tension should not be averaged away: OG expansion may capture functions beyond the 18 measured amino-acid pathways, and the cultured cohort may omit extreme self-sufficient lineages represented by MAGs or single-cell genomes. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]

The PHB evidence adds a related **tension** rather than resolving this issue: PHB-positive species are larger and remain environmentally enriched after genome-size stratification, but the PHB–niche-breadth effect reverses after genome-size control. [src: phb_granule_ecology] Thus broad genome expansion, pathway-specific accessory enrichment, and ecological breadth are not interchangeable outcomes; the Bacillota_B cohort-specific genome comparison and the cross-clade PHB analysis address different traits and sampling frames. [src: phb_granule_ecology]

The global soil study adds a scale-related tension: its clay-shield test found no significant high-clay predictability advantage, with difference 0.024 and 95% CI: −0.423, 0.161, whereas the Bacillota_B comparison found large genome-size and OG-count differences in sampled deep-clay anchors. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] The two results address different response variables and sampling frames, so the global null cannot negate the genome comparison; neither can the cohort result establish a general clay effect. The negative global R² values also remain diagnostically incomplete because spatial autocorrelation, train/test distributional shift, high-leverage outliers, and genuine unpredictability were not separated. [src: soil_frontier_genomics]

The compartment inference is limited by keyword-derived annotations, missing rock-attached lineages, and quality changes from 9 to 6 deep genomes, 30 to 30 shallow genomes, and 150 to 137 baseline genomes after filtering. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] With n = 9 before filtering, only large effects, described as Cohen’s d > 0.7 for unfiltered comparisons, are reliably detectable. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] The study applies to cultivable porewater-cultured deep-clay isolates, not the full Mont Terri or bentonite communities. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]

The SSO plume, M5 mixing zone, M6 anaerobic zone, and redox ladder are inferred from 16S composition and trait scores, with direct SSO geochemistry unavailable; they cannot establish that the genomic functions are caused by those gradients. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The sulfate-reduction result was robust to two stricter compartment definitions, but the cohort-level evidence remains a sampled-community result requiring MAG-augmented validation. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]

The Bacillota_B universe contained 334 genomes in the BERDL pangenome, versus the v1.1 plan’s estimate of 6,700. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] The anchor cohort was borehole- and porewater-dominated by construction and cannot test whether rock-attached Bacillota_B differ in gene content. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

The GDI adds a representation limitation: forest and cropland were jointly highest-GDI biomes, but forest GDI = 902.36 versus cropland GDI = 890.82 is a 1.3% difference without bootstrap confidence intervals, and the index can be driven by OTU richness or sampling effort rather than genome recovery. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] The +0.8 pH unit gap likewise may reflect fewer sequenced samples from alkaline pH ranges rather than an assembly or annotation barrier. [src: soil_frontier_genomics]

The PHB cross-clade inference has a parallel limitation relevant to this page: its HGT calls used phylogenetic discordance and core/accessory status as proxies, with 311 potential acquisitions and 278 potential losses, and 60.1% accessory status among discordant PHB-positive species versus 32.3% among all phaC-carrying species. [src: phb_granule_ecology] This **supports** using accessory enrichment as a hypothesis-generating signal for acquisition, but **refines** interpretation of the Bacillota_B accessory OGs because neither analysis directly reconstructs gene trees or separates transfer from lineage structure. [src: phb_granule_ecology]

## Relation to other corpus concepts

This finding extends [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] by showing that within-lineage comparisons reveal accessory OG enrichment and genome expansion while exposing cohort size, phylogenetic structure, and marker-availability effects. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] The PHB analysis **supports** this pangenome-integration perspective: it links pathway prevalence, genome size, environmental category, and core/accessory status across 27,690 GTDB species, while showing that apparent ecological breadth can be genome-size confounded. [src: phb_granule_ecology] The corrected workflow refines [[concepts/multi-heme-cytochrome-detection]] by replacing mismatched KO markers with PFAM and CXXCH signals. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] The SR-rich porewater signature and lineage-dependent WL/NiFe signal connect this concept to [[concepts/phenotype-database-coverage-bias]] and [[concepts/metabolic-model-gapfilling]]. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]

The SSO analysis supports linking this concept to environment-associated community structure but refines the evidentiary boundary: depth-associated Firmicutes and inferred redox gradients motivate genome-resolved tests, not direct claims about Bacillota_B accessory genes. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The global soil analysis further **refines** that boundary by showing that clay-associated functional predictability is not established at global scale and that spatial structure, sampling effort, and database completeness must be separated from biological specialization. [src: soil_frontier_genomics] Evidence is documented in [[summaries/bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory__REPORT]], [[summaries/clay_confined_subsurface__REPORT]], [[summaries/enigma_sso_asv_ecology__REPORT]], [[summaries/soil_frontier_genomics__REPORT]], and [[summaries/phb_granule_ecology__REPORT]]. ## Open Directions

- Reanalyze the 547 enriched OGs with an LLM-based functional scan and manual curation to resolve whether the estimated 80–100 anaerobic-respiration-related OGs form reproducible categories. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]
- Decompose enrichment by genus or lineage using the 10-anchor and 62-baseline cohorts; partition functions into respiratory, persistence, mineral-attachment, regulatory, and osmoadaptation modules. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]
- Apply the framework to additional subsurface comparisons and test whether larger genomes and the accessory profile recur beyond the borehole- and porewater-dominated cohort. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]
- Apply the corrected multi-heme cytochrome detector to the clay-project branch and compare it with the retained sulfite-reduction signal. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]
- Test the genome-expansion/self-sufficiency tension with finer-grained amino-acid EC-number completeness from eggNOG and MAG-augmented Mont Terri, Olkiluoto, MX-80 bentonite, and Oak Ridge cohorts. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]
- Add deep-subsurface MAGs from Mont Terri, Olkiluoto, MX-80 bentonite, and Oak Ridge and repeat sulfate-reduction and corrected iron-reduction detection with rock-attached and uncultivated lineages represented. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]
- Compare BRC-3 and BIC-A1 directly, resolve Bacillota_B differences at genus level, and link genome presence to Bagnoud’s metaproteomic evidence to distinguish porewater cultivation bias from broader specialization. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]
- Link genome-predicted sulfate-reduction markers to Bagnoud’s metaproteomic evidence and apply the diagnostic to other subsurface settings. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]
- Load the 221 SSO geochemistry samples into CORAL and test whether nitrate, pH, and metal concentrations form the predicted northeast-to-southwest gradient; pair them with metagenomics and genome-resolved Bacillota_B analysis. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]
- Extract pump-test ASVs from Brick 460-462 and compare them with the 16S spatial pattern, especially predicted groundwater enrichment of Rhodanobacter at M5. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]
- Re-run the soil-frontier NB05 validation with spatial blocking to separate distributional shift, outlier leverage, and genuine unpredictability in the negative R² results before using global clay predictability as a null for local Bacillota_B specialization. [src: soil_frontier_genomics]
- Recalculate GDI after uniform 16S sequencing-depth correction, report richness and completeness separately, estimate bootstrap 95% CIs for biome rankings, and control the +0.8 pH unit gap for the number of 16S samples per pH bin. [src: soil_frontier_genomics]
- Reanalyze Bacillota_B genome expansion alongside pathway-specific accessory traits, including PHB, with phylogenetic models and genome-size adjustment to test whether subsurface enrichment reflects broad genome capacity or repeated selection for particular functions. [src: phb_granule_ecology]
- Reconstruct gene trees for PHB and Bacillota_B respiratory markers, then compare transfer, loss, core/accessory status, and compartment representation to distinguish horizontal acquisition from lineage-associated retention. [src: phb_granule_ecology]
