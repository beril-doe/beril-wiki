# Subsurface Bacillota_B specialization and genome expansion

This concept describes how deep-clay Bacillota_B lineages differ from soil-baseline Bacillota_B in accessory gene content, genome size, and respiratory-marker profiles. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] The new clay-confined analysis **refines** this picture by showing that an anaerobic-toolkit signal is strongly enriched at cohort level but is largely Bacillota_B-driven, with dissimilatory sulfate reduction remaining the component that survives within-phylum control. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]

The SSO subsurface community analysis **supports** an environmental context for this specialization: hydrogeological zone explained 27.5% of sediment-community variance (F = 4.05, p = 0.0001), and Firmicutes were enriched with depth (Spearman ρ = +0.76). However, this result is based on 16S community composition and taxonomy-to-trait inference, not direct Bacillota_B genome measurements; it therefore **refines** rather than independently confirms the accessory-genome mechanism described here. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The comparison used 10 deep-clay Bacillota_B genomes and 62 soil-baseline Bacillota_B genomes, combining eggNOG orthologous-group (OG) enrichment, genome-content statistics, and corrected multi-heme cytochrome detection. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] The deep-clay cohort was drawn from Mont Terri Opalinus borehole and rock-porewater samples plus two Russian Beyelii Yar borehole genomes, whereas the soil-baseline cohort included phylum-matched soil or sediment genomes across Syntrophomonadales, Desulfitobacteriales, Moorellales, Thermacetogeniales, Ammonifexales, Carboxydocellales, Desulfotomaculales, Heliobacteriales, and Thermincolales. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

## Accessory-function enrichment

The analysis tested 14,109 Firmicutes-level eggNOG OGs with per-OG Fisher’s exact tests, Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate (FDR) correction, a fold-difference threshold of ≥3, and presence in at least 3 anchor genomes. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] It identified 547 significantly enriched OGs at q<0.05, exceeding the preregistered H1 prediction of ≥10 and providing strong support for broad accessory enrichment in the deep-clay cohort. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

Keyword scanning classified 42 enriched OGs as anaerobic-respiration functions, 24 as sporulation-revival functions, 12 as mineral-attachment or exopolysaccharide functions, 4 as anaerobic regulators, and 3 as osmoadaptation functions, while 462 remained in an “other or unannotated” category. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] The annotated examples included hydrogenase, cytochrome, sulfite, sulfate, nitrate, and fumarate reductase functions; NADH dehydrogenase, oxidoreductase, menaquinone, spore and germination, biofilm and capsule, sigma-factor, two-component-system, betaine, ectoine, osmoprotectant, and K⁺-uptake functions. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

Manual inspection refines rather than overturns this result: the 462-OG category undercounted anaerobic-respiration and electron-transfer functions, and the report estimates that the true anaerobic-respiration-related total was closer to 80–100 of the 547 enriched OGs. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] COG1977, associated with molybdopterin cofactor metabolism, occurred in 10/10 anchors versus 11/62 baselines; OG 1UIFM, a DsrE/DsrF/DsrH-like family, occurred in 7/10 anchors versus 0/62 baselines; OG 1VFGN, a 4Fe–4S dicluster associated with 2-oxoglutarate:ferredoxin oxidoreductase, occurred in 8/10 anchors versus 4/62 baselines; and OG 1V0WU, a Major Facilitator Superfamily transporter, occurred in 9/10 anchors versus 5/62 baselines. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

These results support the hypothesis that the sampled deep-clay Bacillota_B possess a broad accessory repertoire relevant to anaerobic persistence, redox flexibility, mineral association, dormancy or revival, regulation, and osmotic stress, although the small and phylogenetically clustered anchor cohort means that some enriched OGs may be lineage markers rather than general subsurface-specialization features. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

The new analysis **supports** the anaerobic-persistence interpretation but **refines** its phylogenetic basis: its combined Wood–Ljungdahl (WL), group 1 [NiFe]-hydrogenase (NiFe), and dissimilatory sulfate-reduction (SR) toolkit was enriched in deep genomes, yet WL and NiFe did not remain significant within Bacillota_B, whereas SR was present in 5/5 deep genomes versus 4/19 baseline genomes (OR = ∞, p = 0.003, p_BH = 0.044). [src: clay_confined_subsurface] Thus, the existing accessory-OG result is best treated as cohort-level specialization with a particularly robust sulfate-reduction component, not as evidence that every anaerobic function is independently deep-clay-selected. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]

The SSO data **refine** the environmental interpretation without testing these genomic claims: inferred denitrification, iron oxidation, nitrification, iron reduction, sulfur oxidation, methanotrophy, and fermentation varied spatially across the site, but genus-level functional annotation covered only 21% of total reads and the assignments were literature-linked taxonomic inferences rather than direct genomic evidence. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The SSO report therefore treats its inferred redox ladder and plume model as hypotheses awaiting geochemical and metagenomic validation, rather than as evidence that the enriched Bacillota_B OGs are expressed or selected in situ. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

## Genome expansion rather than streamlining

Deep-clay anchor genomes had a mean genome size of 4,110,038 bp versus 3,046,124 bp in soil-baseline genomes, with Cohen’s d=+1.39 and Mann–Whitney p=0.025. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] CheckM-rescaled genome sizes were 4,323,230 bp versus 3,233,715 bp, with Cohen’s d=+1.37 and p=0.013. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

Mean eggNOG OG counts were 2,630 in anchors versus 2,106 in baselines, with Cohen’s d=+1.30 and p=0.022. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] CheckM-rescaled OG counts were 2,771 versus 2,233, with Cohen’s d=+1.32 and p=0.009. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] Mean GC content differed modestly, at 48.84% in anchors versus 47.76% in baselines, with Cohen’s d=+0.21 and p=0.44. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

Mean CheckM completeness was effectively identical between cohorts, at 94.7% for anchors and 94.3% for baselines, with Cohen’s d=+0.08 and p=0.93. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] Because genome quality was similar while both raw and CheckM-rescaled genome size and OG-count comparisons favored anchors, the report concludes that the larger deep-clay genomes and higher OG counts cannot be explained by a genome-quality artifact. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

The observed direction rejects the H2 prediction that deep-clay genomes would be smaller: all four size and OG-count metrics indicated significantly larger anchor genomes, with p≤0.025 and Cohen’s d≥+1.30. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] The result supports a self-sufficiency model for the sampled cultivable subsurface Bacillota_B and contrasts with streamlining reported for Patescibacteria, while the report treats these as distinct ecological and phylogenetic groups. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

The clay-confined comparison **does not strengthen** a generalized self-sufficiency interpretation: GapMind amino-acid pathway completeness across 18 pathways was not elevated in its deep cohort, with mean 16.22/18 versus 16.66/18 in the baseline (p = 0.153, Cohen’s d = −0.17) before filtering and 15.50/18 versus 17.14/18 after filtering (p = 0.009, d = −0.84). [src: clay_confined_subsurface] This result is compatible with accessory genome expansion without elevated completeness of the measured amino-acid pathways, and the report notes that the metric saturates near 18 for many cultured bacteria. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]

The SSO depth pattern **supports** the broader possibility that subsurface environmental structure sorts lineages, but it cannot distinguish genome expansion from taxonomic turnover: samples from the same well but different depths had median Bray–Curtis dissimilarity = 0.977, while samples from the same depth zone in different wells had median Bray–Curtis dissimilarity = 0.835. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] This strengthens the rationale for testing Bacillota_B accessory functions against matched depth, geochemistry, and phylogeny rather than treating depth enrichment alone as evidence of larger genomes. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

## Respiratory-marker correction and interpretation

The original clay-project iron-reduction analysis used K07811, K17324, and K17323 as markers, but the report identifies these as TMAO reductase, glycerol ABC ATP-binding, and glycerol ABC permease genes rather than canonical iron-reduction markers. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] The corrected detector combined PFAM PF02085, PFAM PF22678, and a CXXCH heme-binding motif count of ≥4 in gene-cluster protein sequences, calling a genome positive when any signal was present. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

The corrected multi-heme cytochrome comparison found 5/9 positive genomes in anchor_deep, or 55.6%, compared with 1/9, or 11.1%, under the original marker set; 12/30, or 40.0%, in anchor_shallow, compared with 15/30, or 50.0%, originally; and 61/149, or 40.9%, in soil_baseline, compared with 30/149, or 20.1%, originally. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] Corrected pairwise Fisher tests found no significant cohort differences: deep versus shallow had odds ratio 1.88 and p=0.46; deep versus baseline had odds ratio 1.80 and p=0.49; and shallow versus baseline had odds ratio 0.96 and p=1.0. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

This correction weakens the original shallow-enrichment narrative and indicates that corrected multi-heme cytochrome content was similar across the clay cohorts and soil baseline. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] The sulfite-reduction-side finding remains supported, with 5/9 deep-cohort genomes positive versus a Mitzscherling rock-attached null rate of 0.2% and binomial p=4×10⁻¹². [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

The new report **supports** retaining the sulfate-reduction signal while **refining** the iron-reduction interpretation: its corrected detector used PFAM PF02085, PFAM PF22678, and a threshold of ≥4 CXXCH motifs, and yielded 55.6% in anchor_deep, 40.0% in anchor_shallow, and 40.9% in soil_baseline, with all corrected cohort comparisons at Fisher p ≥ 0.46. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] The original shallow-greater-than-deep iron-reduction narrative is therefore withdrawn in the new analysis, while the sulfate-reduction enrichment remained 5 observed positives versus 0.018 expected under the rock-attached null (binomial p = 4.0×10⁻¹²). [src: clay_confined_subsurface]

## Tensions

The accessory-enrichment result supports broad deep-clay specialization, but its interpretation is limited by phylogenetic confounding: the anchor cohort spans four orders yet is clumped among 3 BRH-c8a genomes, 2 BRH-c4a genomes, 2 Desulfosporosinus genomes, Desulforudis, Ch130, and 1 other genome. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] Thus, the 547 enriched OGs provide strong evidence for cohort-level differentiation but do not by themselves establish that every enriched function is a recurrent subsurface adaptation. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

The new analysis **supports** this caution: its deep cohort contained 9 genomes before quality filtering, and within Bacillota_B only SR remained significant after phylogenetic control; WL and NiFe primarily tracked the Bacillota_B lineage. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] The two studies therefore differ in cohort composition and analytical question, rather than providing a basis for treating all anaerobic-toolkit enrichment as independent evidence of specialization. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]

The original iron-reduction contrast is contradicted by the corrected detector: the original marker set suggested shallow enrichment, whereas corrected multi-heme cytochrome calls found no significant cohort differences. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] The sulfite-reduction interpretation is not similarly undermined, because its comparison remained strongly supported by binomial p=4×10⁻¹². [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

The pangenome context also constrains generalization: the Bacillota_B universe contained 334 genomes in the BERDL pangenome, substantially fewer than the v1.1 plan’s estimate of 6,700. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] The anchor cohort was borehole- and porewater-dominated by construction, so this comparison cannot test whether rock-attached Bacillota_B differ in gene content. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

The genome-expansion result and the new self-sufficiency result create a further interpretive tension: the former supports a self-sufficiency model for sampled cultivable subsurface Bacillota_B, whereas the latter found no elevated 18-pathway GapMind completeness in deep genomes. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] [src: clay_confined_subsurface] This should not be resolved by averaging the metrics: genome size and OG count may capture accessory functions beyond the measured amino-acid pathways, while the GapMind result does not reject self-sufficiency as a broader subsurface adaptation because the cultured cohort may omit extreme self-sufficient lineages represented by MAGs or single-cell genomes. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]

The SSO environmental interpretation introduces a related limitation rather than a contradiction: its proposed northeast-to-southwest contamination plume, M5 mixing zone, M6 anaerobic zone, and redox ladder are inferred from 16S composition and trait scores, while direct SSO geochemistry is unavailable. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] These hypotheses cannot currently establish that the genomic accessory functions reported here are caused by the same environmental gradients. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

## Relation to other corpus concepts

This finding extends [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] by showing that within-lineage cohort comparisons can reveal both accessory OG enrichment and genome expansion, while also exposing the effects of cohort size, phylogenetic structure, and marker availability. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

The corrected respiratory-marker workflow directly refines [[concepts/multi-heme-cytochrome-detection]] by replacing mismatched KO markers with PFAM and CXXCH-based signals and by removing statistical support for the original shallow-versus-deep iron-reduction contrast. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

The new cohort-level result also **refines** the interpretation of this concept: deep cultured clay genomes show a sulfate-reduction-rich porewater signature, whereas broader anaerobic-toolkit enrichment is largely explained by Bacillota_B phylogeny. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]

The SSO analysis **supports** linking this concept to environment-associated community structure, but **refines** the evidentiary boundary: depth-associated Firmicutes and inferred redox gradients motivate genome-resolved tests, yet the SSO functions are not direct measurements of Bacillota_B accessory genes. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The full evidence base is documented in [[summaries/bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory__REPORT]], the clay-confined subsurface report, and [[summaries/enigma_sso_asv_ecology__REPORT]]. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] [src: clay_confined_subsurface] [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

## Open Directions

- Reanalyze the 547 enriched OGs with an LLM-based functional scan and manual curation to determine whether the estimated 80–100 anaerobic-respiration-related OGs can be resolved into reproducible functional categories. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]
- Decompose enrichment by genus or lineage, using the existing 10-anchor and 62-baseline cohorts, to test which OGs persist after reducing phylogenetic confounding. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]
- Localize the accessory functions responsible for the genome-size difference by partitioning anchor and baseline OGs into respiratory, persistence, mineral-attachment, regulatory, and osmoadaptation modules. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]
- Apply the same phylum-matched enrichment framework to additional subsurface comparisons to test whether larger genomes and the identified accessory-function profile recur beyond this borehole- and porewater-dominated cohort. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]
- Apply the corrected multi-heme cytochrome detector to the clay-project branch and compare its results with the retained sulfite-reduction signal to determine which parts of the porewater-bias interpretation remain supported. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]
- Test the genome-expansion/self-sufficiency tension with finer-grained amino-acid EC-number completeness from eggNOG and with MAG-augmented Mont Terri, Olkiluoto, MX-80 bentonite, and Oak Ridge cohorts; determine whether accessory expansion and extreme biosynthetic independence co-occur outside the cultured cohort. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]
- Compare BRC-3 and BIC-A1 directly and resolve Bacillota_B differences at genus level, linking genome presence to Bagnoud’s metaproteomic evidence to distinguish porewater cultivation bias from broader community specialization. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]
- Load the 221 SSO geochemistry samples into CORAL and test whether nitrate, pH, and metal concentrations form the predicted northeast-to-southwest gradient; pair these measurements with metagenomics and genome-resolved Bacillota_B analysis to determine whether the inferred environmental gradients explain the accessory functions. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]
- Extract the available pump-test ASVs from Brick 460-462 and compare them with the 16S spatial pattern, especially the predicted groundwater enrichment of Rhodanobacter at M5; this would test whether the SSO community signal reaches the critical hotspot wells excluded from the current groundwater dataset. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]
