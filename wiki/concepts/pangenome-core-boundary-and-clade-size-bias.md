# Pangenome Core Boundaries and Clade-Size Bias

Pangenome core-versus-auxiliary classifications depend not only on gene presence, but also on which genomes are sampled, how many genomes represent a clade, and how completely genes can be linked across datasets. The [[summaries/conservation_vs_fitness__REPORT]] shows that these design choices can make the core boundary more or less discriminative when Fitness Browser fitness data are integrated with KBase pangenome clusters. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] The [[summaries/fitness_effects_conservation__REPORT]] **refines** this result by showing that the conservation gradient persists across approximately 194,000 genes from 43 bacteria, but is statistically robust and only weakly predictive of fitness importance. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

## Sampling depth changes the meaning of “core”

In the integrated analysis, 44 of 48 Fitness Browser organisms were mapped to pangenome species clades, yielding 177,863 gene-to-cluster links with 100.0% median protein identity and 94.2% median gene coverage. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] Thirty-four organisms had at least 90% coverage, while 33 organisms were retained for downstream analysis after Dyella79 was excluded because of a locus-tag mismatch. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]

The classification assigned 145,821 linked genes, or 82.0%, to core clusters and 32,042, or 18.0%, to auxiliary clusters; the 7,574 singleton genes were a subset of the auxiliary category. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] These proportions describe conservation within the sampled clades, not an unconditional boundary between biologically “core” and “accessory” functions. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] This **supports** treating core status as sampling-conditioned: in the separate 43-bacterium analysis, core fractions ranged from 66% among always-neutral genes to 82% among essential genes, rather than defining a sharp fitness boundary. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

The module-level analysis **supports** this sampling-conditioned interpretation while showing that conservation can also be assessed for co-regulated fitness units: module genes were 86.0% core versus 81.5% for all genes, an enrichment of 4.5 percentage points (OR=1.46, p=1.6e-87). [src: module_conservation] The modest absolute difference despite strong statistical significance reinforces that a high baseline core rate limits the discriminative value of the boundary. [src: module_conservation] Among 974 modules with at least 3 mapped genes, 577 (59%) were >90% core, 349 (36%) were mixed, and 48 (5%) were <50% core, extending the conservation question from individual genes to functional units without establishing that accessory modules are horizontally transferred or niche-specific. [src: module_conservation]

The core_gene_tradeoffs analysis **supports** this qualification by identifying 25,271 condition-dependent trade-off genes, or 17.8% of the genes examined, and finding that they were 1.29 times more likely to be core than non-core genes (odds ratio 1.29, p=1.2e-44). [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] Because trade-off status depends on whether fitness is below -1 in some conditions and above 1 in others, this result indicates that a core boundary can capture genes with variable laboratory effects rather than uniformly indispensable genes. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] The detailed evidence is summarized in [[summaries/core_gene_tradeoffs__REPORT]].

The openness analysis **refines** this interpretation by finding no significant relationship between a species’ open/closed pangenome summary and either environment effect (Spearman rho = -0.05, p-value = 0.54) or phylogeny effect (Spearman rho = 0.03, p-value = 0.73). [src: pangenome_openness] Thus, core/accessory boundaries and whole-pangenome openness should not be treated as interchangeable measures of ecological or phylogenetic structuring; the result is a null association among species with both pangenome and ecotype results, not evidence that individual core or accessory genes lack ecological relevance. [src: pangenome_openness]

## Small clades can inflate core fractions

Clades containing only 2 genomes can have trivially high core fractions because a gene present in both genomes equals 100% core, reducing the discriminative power of the core-versus-auxiliary classification. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] Thus, a high core fraction in a small clade may reflect limited sampling rather than broad conservation across the species. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]

The module analysis **supports** retaining this caveat: its pangenome-linked comparison covered a 29/32 organism subset because Cola, Kang, and SB2B lacked pangenome links, with too few genomes in GTDB for pangenome construction. [src: module_conservation] Within the analyzed modules, family breadth did not predict conservation (Spearman rho=-0.01, p=0.914), redirecting interpretation toward gene-level conservation rather than assuming that module families spanning more organisms are more conserved. [src: module_conservation]

This sampling effect is important when interpreting associations between conservation and phenotype. Essential genes were 86.1% core compared with 81.2% for non-essential genes, with a median odds ratio of 1.56, and 18 of 33 organisms showed statistically significant enrichment by Fisher’s exact test after Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate correction at BH-FDR q < 0.05. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] The enrichment supports a relationship between essentiality and within-clade conservation, but the clade-size limitation means that the magnitude and discriminative value of the core category can vary with genome sampling. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] The broader analysis **refines** that interpretation: fitness breadth was positively but weakly associated with core status (Spearman rho=0.086, p=8.1e-230), so large sample sizes do not make conservation a strong standalone predictor of fitness importance. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

## Coverage failure creates a second boundary problem

Coverage determines whether a gene can be assigned to a pangenome cluster at all, so incomplete joins can move genes out of the core-versus-auxiliary comparison or make apparent absence ambiguous. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] Ten organisms were excluded from Phase 2 because they had less than 90% DIAMOND coverage, reducing taxonomic breadth. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]

Four organisms—Cola, Kang, Magneto, and SB2B—were unmatched because their species had too few genomes in GTDB for pangenome construction. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] The main Escherichia coli clade was absent because it contained too many genomes, while Keio, an Escherichia coli BW25113 strain, mapped to the small s__Escherichia_coli_E clade at only 26.1% coverage. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] These exclusions and uneven mappings make the observed core fraction partly a property of which clade was available and successfully joined, not solely a property of gene biology. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]

Dyella79 illustrates a distinct failure mode: it was excluded from Phase 2 because the Fitness Browser gene table used the locus-tag format N515DRAFT_* whereas the protein sequences used ABZR86_RS*, producing a 0% join rate. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] This case shows that identifier incompatibility can alter the analyzed population before biological conservation is assessed. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] The newer analysis **supports** retaining this caveat because singleton genes showed near-zero mean fitness under tested laboratory conditions, while explicitly noting that poor transposon coverage could produce the apparent neutrality. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

The module analysis **further refines** the coverage boundary: its module memberships came from ICA, or independent component analysis, using a |Pearson r| >= 0.3 threshold and a maximum of 50 genes per module, and essential genes were absent because essential loci lack usable transposon-insertion fitness data. [src: module_conservation] Consequently, its 86.0% module-gene core fraction describes the measurable, non-essential portion of the genome and cannot be used to characterize essential-gene modules. [src: module_conservation]

## Implications for integrated fitness analyses

The conservation-versus-fitness result should therefore be interpreted as a clade- and coverage-conditioned association. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] Essential genes were generally more likely to be classified as core, but the analysis also found that most genes in well-characterized bacteria were core regardless of essentiality, making the enrichment modest. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] The broader comparison **supports** this qualification: essential genes were 82% core versus 66% for always-neutral genes across approximately 194,000 genes, a 16-percentage-point gradient that remained weak as a predictor. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

The report’s stratification by clade size and lifestyle indicated that essential-core enrichment was robust across the genomic contexts examined, but the report also cautioned that small clades reduce the discriminative power of core-versus-auxiliary status. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] This combination supports using core status as evidence of conservation while treating it as a sampling-dependent measurement rather than a fixed universal label. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] Condition-specific fitness provides a further refinement: strong condition-specific effects were 77.3% core versus 70.3% for genes without specific phenotypes, indicating that conditionally important genes are not necessarily accessory, although the laboratory condition space remains limited. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

The core_gene_tradeoffs result **refines** this condition-specific interpretation: core-gene burden was function-specific, with core genes more burdensome in Protein Metabolism, Motility, and RNA Metabolism but with the pattern reversed for Cell Wall genes. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] Its selection-signature matrix included 28,017 Costly + Conserved genes, defined as genes conserved despite laboratory-measured cost, but this is evidence from laboratory fitness and conservation patterns rather than direct measurement of natural selection. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] Thus, core status can mark conditionally costly functions without establishing universal essentiality or a uniform burden boundary. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]

The openness null result **refines** the integration boundary: a single whole-pangenome openness metric did not predict whether environment or phylogeny dominated gene-content variation, and the report identifies its limited representation of pangenome structure as a hypothesis-generating caveat rather than a demonstrated mechanism. [src: pangenome_openness] The module-family null result points to a related limitation: module-family breadth was not associated with core fraction, so cross-organism module scope should not be substituted for direct gene-level conservation. [src: module_conservation] This concept therefore refines [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] by identifying clade composition, join coverage, metric choice, and observability of fitness data as interpretive constraints on cross-dataset integration. [src: conservation_vs_fitness; pangenome_openness; module_conservation] It also qualifies the essentiality relationship discussed in [[concepts/gene-essentiality]], because the observed 1.56 median odds ratio depends on the sampled clades and the resulting conservation categories. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] The proposed extension to condition-specific fitness connects this issue to [[concepts/condition-specific-fitness]], since stress-dependent fitness effects may be compared against conservation only after sampling and coverage are controlled. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]

## Tensions

The analysis supports essential genes being more conserved within sampled clades, with 86.1% of essential genes classified as core versus 81.2% of non-essential genes and a median odds ratio of 1.56. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] At the same time, clades containing only 2 genomes can produce trivially high core fractions, and the main Escherichia coli clade was unavailable because it contained too many genomes. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] The tension is therefore between interpreting core status as biological conservation and recognizing that the estimate is shaped by both undersampling and exclusion of overlarge or poorly joinable clades. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]

The 86.1%-versus-81.2% enrichment also differs from the 82%-versus-66% essential-to-always-neutral gradient in the broader analysis. [src: conservation_vs_fitness; fitness_effects_conservation] These values should not be reconciled silently: they arise from different organism sets, phenotype categories, and integration procedures, so the difference is evidence that the estimated conservation boundary is analysis-dependent. [src: conservation_vs_fitness; fitness_effects_conservation]

The module analysis introduces a related observability tension rather than resolving the essentiality comparison: module genes were 86.0% core versus 81.5% of all genes, but zero essential genes appeared in modules because essential genes lack usable transposon-insertion fitness data. [src: module_conservation] Thus, the module-level enrichment cannot be compared directly with essential-versus-non-essential core fractions as if they represented the same observable gene universe. [src: module_conservation]

The core_gene_tradeoffs finding adds a related tension rather than resolving it: core genes were more burdensome in several functional categories, whereas non-core Cell Wall genes were more burdensome. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] This functional reversal means that a core-enrichment or costly-conserved signal cannot be interpreted as a single genome-wide burden rule. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]

The openness analysis does not contradict these within-clade conservation findings, but it **refines** their scope: its null correlations concern species-level environment and phylogeny effect sizes, whereas the conservation analyses compare gene categories within sampled clades. [src: pangenome_openness; conservation_vs_fitness; fitness_effects_conservation] Whether openness fails to predict ecological dominance because the metric is too coarse, because relevant adaptation is concentrated in particular functional categories, or because of limited power remains unresolved. [src: pangenome_openness]

## Open Directions

- Recompute core fractions across controlled subsamples of each clade, using repeated genome down-sampling to ask how the essential-versus-non-essential odds ratio changes with clade size. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]
- Reanalyze the 10 organisms excluded for less than 90% DIAMOND coverage with identifier harmonization and orthology-based recovery, asking whether their exclusion changes the 82.0% core and 18.0% auxiliary proportions. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]
- Compare clades with different genome counts using confidence intervals or hierarchical models, asking whether essential-core enrichment remains after explicitly modeling clade size and coverage. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]
- Extend the comparison from binary essentiality to condition-specific fitness, especially genes with fitness < -2 under stress conditions, asking whether conditionally important genes show the same sampling-sensitive conservation pattern. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]
- Reconcile the 86.1%-versus-81.2% and 82%-versus-66% gradients by rerunning both phenotype classifications on the same clades and coverage-filtered gene set, testing whether category definitions or sampling explain the discrepancy. [src: conservation_vs_fitness; fitness_effects_conservation]
- Test whether the 86.0%-versus-81.5% module-gene enrichment persists after matching module and non-module genes by organism, callability, module size, and pangenome clade size, and separately quantify the essential genes that remain unobservable to ICA. [src: module_conservation]
- Stratify pangenome openness by functional category and lifestyle, and compare openness, auxiliary fraction, Heap’s law alpha, and pangenome fluidity against environment and phylogeny effects to test whether the null result is specific to the single summary metric. [src: pangenome_openness]
- Reclassify the core-gene trade-off and costly-conserved signals within matched clades and functional categories, testing whether the observed burden reversals persist after clade size, coverage, and condition space are controlled. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs; conservation_vs_fitness]
