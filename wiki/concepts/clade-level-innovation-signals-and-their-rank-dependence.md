# Clade-level innovation signals and their rank dependence

Pre-registered clade-by-function tests in the gene_function_ecological_agora project gave an Innovator-Isolated signal for Mycobacteriaceae mycolic acid and an Innovator-Exchange signal for Cyanobacteria photosystem II (PSII) at class rank only. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] Bacteroidota polysaccharide utilization loci (PUL) got a qualified pass. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] The pre-registered Bacteroidota PUL CAZyme (carbohydrate-active enzyme) Innovator-Exchange hypothesis was falsified at all 4 deep ranks (0/4 supported) under the absolute criterion. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] Verdicts depend on taxonomic rank and on within-clade heterogeneity. [src: discoveries, gene_function_ecological_agora] The report acknowledges that sample-size effect inflation, especially PSII n=21, remains a limit. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

## Literature Context

The candidate papers do not test clade-by-function innovation signals directly, so they give only indirect context. Several of them describe gene families in which duplication and horizontal gene transfer (HGT) are both inferred. In the cutinase family, orthologues occur in only three Actinobacteria genera and in oomycetes and fungi. The authors infer transfer between distantly related plant-degrading microbes, plus recent duplication within Phytophthora, where tandem copies number three and ten in two species [PMID 18024004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18024004/). In the oligopeptide transporter family, HGT is described as frequent among prokaryotes, rare among eukaryotes and largely absent between prokaryotes and eukaryotes [PMID 21347612](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21347612/). Plant subtilases are inferred to descend from a single bacterial HGT event followed by duplication [PMID 31462749](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31462749/). Rickettsia effectors show duplication and recombination, together with lateral transfer on plasmids and conjugative transposons [PMID 38564675](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38564675/). These are selected examples, and they do not show that the two processes usually go together across the field.

Two further papers bear on Cyanobacteria and on how much HGT contributes. Studies of MutS-family evolution in plants report cyanobacterial-like MutS1 and MutS2 genes gained by plastid endosymbiotic gene transfer, with MutS2 later duplicated in Viridiplantae [PMID 39071318](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39071318/), [PMID 39692564](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39692564/). This shows cyanobacterial genes acting as donors at deep evolutionary scales. It does not address photosystem II. In Dietzia, the pan-genome is open, and gene duplication and loss contributed to it while HGT was limited. Metabolic capacity was gained mainly through more ancient duplications [PMID 32715552](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32715552/). That is a single-genus result. Nitrogenase is described as having a history of duplication, recruitment, fusion and HGT, and the authors note that sequence divergence, paralogy and HGT confound its phylogeny [PMID 14694078](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14694078/).

The corpus's Cyanobacteria PSII result is consistent with this background in a qualified way. The class-rank Innovator-Exchange signal, with producer d=+1.50 and consumer d=+0.70 across n=21 PSII KOs, and the 7× lower ancient acquisition than atlas-wide PSII, fit a picture of cyanobacterial lineages as donors. The candidate papers support the donor role only for MutS genes and only indirectly [PMID 39071318](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39071318/). The corpus's mycolic-acid finding is Innovator-Isolated, with 79.9% recent and 0% ancient acquisition, which means expansion contained within the clade. It resembles the Dietzia observation that duplication rather than HGT shapes a genus's gene repertoire [PMID 32715552](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32715552/). That comparison is loose, since the Dietzia study covers 26 strains of one genus and no mycolic-acid pathway.

The corpus's central claims are largely not addressed by these papers. These claims include rank dependence of verdicts, the PSII class-rank-only signal, the falsified Bacteroidota PUL hypothesis, and the use of leaf consistency to expose sub-clade heterogeneity. They are novel relative to this candidate set. Nothing retrieved concerns Mycobacteriaceae, Bacteroidota PUL or Cohen's d on Sankoff-derived scores. The vibrio diversity abstract describes genomic events in speciation but gives no comparable test [PMID 15353563](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15353563/). The corpus's own limits remain: the n=21 PSII sample, and the order-rank mycolic d=0.288, which is marginal.

## Convergent evidence for the three findings

For each pre-registered atlas finding (mycolic, PSII, Bacteroidota PUL), three measurement substrates agreed: atlas Cohen's d on Sankoff-derived scores, clade-by-biome Fisher's enrichment with p<10⁻¹¹, and BacDive metabolic phenotypes where coverage existed. [src: discoveries] The report argues that concordance across substrates makes a verdict robust against single-substrate failure. [src: discoveries] See [[concepts/ecological-and-phenotypic-grounding-of-gene-flow-signatures]].

Hypothesis outcomes:
- The phase 2 hypothesis, stated for Mycobacteriota, that mycolic acid is Innovator-Isolated was supported, with NB12 producer d=+0.31 and consumer d=−0.19. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] NB12 supports it at family and order ranks, with producer d=0.31, consumer d=−0.19, p<10⁻¹⁵ and 79.9% recent / 0% ancient acquisition. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]
- Cyanobacteria PSII Innovator-Exchange was supported at class rank, with NB16 producer d=+1.50 and consumer d=+0.70. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] NB16 supports the donor-undistinguished joint Broker-OR-Open Innovator-Exchange hypothesis at class rank (p<10⁻³), with 7× lower ancient acquisition than atlas-wide PSII, interpreted as a donor-origin signature. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]
- Bacteroidota PUL Innovator-Exchange got a qualified pass: deep-rank absolute-zero was falsified, but the Sankoff diagnostic recovered, with atlas d=0.15 in NB08c. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]
- The report's status header counts 2 of 4 pre-registered hypotheses as confirmed. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

## Cyanobacteria PSII: a class-rank signal

The signal appears only at class rank, and pooling washes it out at finer ranks. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] The genus-rank hypothesis could not be evaluated because data were too sparse. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] The report adds that the signal is specifically a class-rank phenomenon, not a generic Cyanobacteria-wide HGT (horizontal gene transfer) signature at every resolution. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

### Rank-by-rank verdicts

PSII is STABLE at genus, family and order ranks, INNOVATOR-EXCHANGE at class rank, and INNOVATOR-ISOLATED at phylum rank. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] The rows below give producer and consumer Cohen's d, with Mann–Whitney p-values for each.

| Rank | n KOs | Producer d | Consumer d | Producer p / consumer p | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| genus (137 Cyano genera pooled) | 2,350 | +0.08 | −0.53 | 1.00 / 1.00 [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] | STABLE |
| family (42 Cyano families) | 705 | +0.20 | −1.28 | 0.97 / 1.00 [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] | STABLE |
| order (19 Cyano orders) | 301 | +0.19 | −1.42 | 0.99 / 1.00 [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] | STABLE |
| class (c__Cyanobacteriia) | 21 | +1.50 | +0.70 | 2×10⁻⁵ / 2×10⁻⁴ [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] | INNOVATOR-EXCHANGE |
| phylum (p__Cyanobacteriota) | 21 | +1.60 | NaN (no consumer null) | 7×10⁻⁶ / n/a [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] | INNOVATOR-ISOLATED |

At class rank both pre-registered criteria pass at α/4 = 0.0125: producer above the atlas non-housekeeping median (d=1.50) and consumer above median (d=0.70). [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] That means PSII KOs are less phylogenetically clumped than typical non-housekeeping KOs at class rank. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

The report explains the dilution this way: the genus, family and order ranks pool 137, 42 and 19 individual Cyano clades, and most genera lack the full PSII paralog repertoire. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] It reads the large class-rank signal and its loss at finer ranks as consistent with PSII paralog expansion being a whole-Cyanobacteriia-class property. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] It also says these analyses are different statistical objects: many clades with variable content versus one clade with pooled content. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] It reads the STABLE verdicts as biologically expected, since PSII is near-universal with deep ancestry within Cyanobacteria. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] It calls class rank the appropriate resolution. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] It frames PSII as a class-defining innovation following Cardona 2018, so the genus null is expected. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] The report regards PSII LC=0.88 (4.4× atlas) as canonical class-defining innovation that validates the Cardona 2018 framing. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

Leaf consistency (LC, the per-clade prevalence of a KO across species; see [[entities/leaf-consistency]]) supports this. Cyanobacteriia × PSII has focal n of 1,005 and median LC of 0.88 against an atlas reference of 0.20, which is 4.4× higher. The report reads this as PSII KOs in 88% of Cyanobacteriia species, so the NB16 d=1.50 sits on a near-uniform population. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

The phylum-rank INNOVATOR-ISOLATED reversal is attributed solely to insufficient reference consumer data (NaN) at that rank, not to a biological contradiction. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

### Acquisition depth

Cyanobacteria PSII gains (n=1,026) had acquisition-depth percentages of recent 32.26, older_recent 36.26, mid 12.96, older 16.47 and ancient 2.05. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] All atlas-wide PSII gains (n=11K) had recent 27.37, older_recent 30.58, mid 12.85, older 14.31 and ancient 14.90. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] The report states that Cyanobacteria has 7× fewer ancient PSII gains than the atlas-wide PSII population. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] It interprets this as a donor-origin signature: PSII originated in or near the Cyanobacteria lineage (per Cardona et al. 2018), so Cyanobacteria does not receive ancient cross-phylum PSII transfers. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] It attributes the atlas-wide ancient fraction (14.9%) to gains in non-Cyanobacteria phyla, including Chloroflexota and certain Proteobacteria, that received PSII via documented HGT. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

### Sample-size concerns

The class-rank result has producer α=2×10⁻⁵ and consumer α=2×10⁻⁴. Both are below the Bonferroni threshold α/4=0.0125, and the effect is d=1.50. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] The report accepts that the n=21 sample size and rank-dependent contradictions are real concerns. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] It also lists sample-size effect inflation, especially PSII n=21, as an acknowledged remaining limit. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] It concedes that Cohen's d has higher variance at small N. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] It argues that a stable d=1.50 with a bootstrap-CI-positive lower bound and α=2×10⁻⁵ is a real effect rather than chance inflation. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] It adds that larger d at smaller N partly reflects the larger effects small-N tests need to clear significance, not necessarily bias. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

The n=21 counts PSII KOs (KEGG orthologs, K02703–K02727 minus a few absent from the atlas), each treated as one observation against 554,280 class-rank KO × clade tuples. They are not 21 independent species. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] The report says the n_neg ≥ 20 minimum of plan M23 does not apply directly, because the small sample is the target and the reference has n_ref ≫ 1000. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

Two literature points bear on this. Benzerara et al. (2026) show recent Cyanobacteria-to-Gammaproteobacteria transfer of ccyA, which the report cites as supporting Cyanobacteria as recent-rank HGT donors. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] Gisriel et al. 2023 report within-Cyanobacteria HGT of FaRLiP photosystem I variants. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] The report says this qualifies but does not contradict the Cardona 2018 donor-origin framing, which applies to PSII core machinery. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

## Mycobacteriaceae mycolic acid: heterogeneity within the clade

The family-rank signal is real but averages over a mixture. At family rank the mycolic producer d was 0.31, supporting Innovator-Isolated. [src: discoveries] Median LC for mycolic KOs was 0.15, below the atlas reference of 0.20. [src: discoveries, gene_function_ecological_agora] The summary table gives focal n of 33,643 and 0.75× the atlas value. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] The report reads this as gains landing in clades where only about 15% of family members carry the KO panel. [src: discoveries] It describes mycolic-positive species as a sub-clade rather than a uniform family-wide trait. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] Leaf consistency exposed heterogeneity that deep-rank Producer × Participation aggregation had obscured. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

The report's reading is that the NB12 family-rank d=0.31 averages mycolic-positive sub-clades (M. tuberculosis, M. leprae and M. avium complexes) with mycolic-negative or atypical members. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] It notes this fits established biology: mycolic-acid biosynthesis is not uniform, and only a subset of species makes mycolates of the chain length characteristic of M. tuberculosis. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] See [[entities/mycobacteriaceae]].

### Family- and order-rank tests

Both pre-registered criteria pass at family and order ranks after Bonferroni correction: producer above the atlas median and consumer below it. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] At family rank, f__Mycobacteriaceae has 582 mycolic KOs, producer d +0.309 (Mann–Whitney p 2×10⁻⁶) and consumer d −0.193 (p <10⁻¹⁵), an Innovator-Isolated verdict. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] At order rank, o__Mycobacteriales has 614 mycolic KOs, producer d +0.288 (p 5×10⁻⁶) and consumer d −0.285 (p <10⁻¹⁵), also Innovator-Isolated. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

The effect-size threshold is met narrowly. Producer d at family rank (0.309) just exceeds d ≥ 0.3 and order rank (0.288) is just below, but both ranks have correct directions and p-values well below α/4 = 0.0125. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] The strict d ≥ 0.3 criterion is met cleanly only at family rank. The test at order rank passes through the producer_high, consumer_low and Bonferroni-significant criteria. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] The order-rank result is weaker but directionally consistent with family rank. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

The mycolic-acid candidate set has 715 KOs, including general fatty-acid biosynthesis (ko00061, ko00071, ko01040). It is therefore broader than the strict pathway, and the test is somewhat conservative. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] A stricter definition might amplify the signal at the cost of sample size. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

### Acquisition depth

Mycobacteriaceae × mycolic-acid gains (n=53,916) had acquisition-depth percentages of recent 79.87, older_recent 16.72, mid 3.41, older 0.00 and ancient 0.00. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] All mycolic-acid gains atlas-wide (n=1.47M) had recent 48.79, older_recent 31.81, mid 9.60, older 6.12 and ancient 3.68. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] The report describes essentially all 53,916 gain events as genus-rank (recent, 79.9%) or family-rank (older_recent, 16.7%). [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

The report calls this a textbook Innovator-Isolated signature: high paralog expansion plus within-clade-only recent acquisitions plus zero deep cross-clade exchange events. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] Innovator-Isolated enrichment is 2.0× the atlas baseline rate, read as elevated lineage-specific paralog expansion and within-clade containment. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] Innovator-Exchange is essentially absent, with 1 tuple versus ≥ 50K typical of HGT-active classes. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

The report offers two interpretations. Mycolic-acid biosynthesis evolved in the Corynebacteriales common ancestor, and within-clade paralog expansion drove pathway diversity in Mycobacteriaceae. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] The zero ancient-or-older gains signature (M22) is read as a cell-envelope coupling constraint: the pathway did not move across phyla because it cannot. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

### Sub-clade recomputation

| Subset | Genera | Producer d | Consumer d |
|---|---|---|---|
| Family rank (original NB12) | 13 | +0.309 | −0.193 [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] |
| All genera, genus rank, no LC filter | 13 | +0.326 | +0.020 [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] |
| Mycolic-positive (LC ≥ 0.5) | 10 | +0.394 | +0.006 [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] |
| Mycolic-low (LC < 0.5) | 3 | +0.211 | +0.043 [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] |

The producer effect is modestly amplified in the mycolic-positive sub-clade, so heterogeneity does not dramatically dilute the finding. [src: discoveries, gene_function_ecological_agora] The consumer signal vanishes at genus rank (d=+0.006 versus −0.193 at family rank). The report attributes the family-rank consumer effect to family-rank aggregation rather than sub-clade-specific signal. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] The consumer-side Innovator-Isolated component is therefore weaker than the family-rank framing implied. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

Surprisingly, g__Mycobacterium itself sits in the mycolic-low sub-clade, with mean LC=0.36 and median 0.08. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] The report's explanation is that the 11-KO panel is calibrated to long-chain Mycobacterium-style mycolate biosynthesis. Only the M. tuberculosis complex carries all panel KOs at high prevalence, unlike many environmental species. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

The refined supported claim is that the mycolate-producing sub-clade (10 of 13 genera) is mycolic-acid Innovator-Isolated, with producer d=0.39 at genus rank. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] The synthesis narrows the claim from "Mycobacteriaceae innovate mycolic-acid" to sub-clades such as the M. tuberculosis and M. leprae complexes, treating the family-rank d=0.31 as a population-mixture average. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] It also attributes the 7.88× host-pathogen biome enrichment (P4-D1 NB23) primarily to mycolic-positive pathogenic sub-clades. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] The mycolic effect at order rank, d=0.288, is marginal and already recorded as a limitation. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

## Bacteroidota PUL: falsified at the absolute criterion, a phylum tendency in LC

The pre-registered hypothesis defined Innovator-Exchange as both producer and consumer 95 % CI (confidence interval) lower bound > 0. Under it, the Bacteroidota PUL CAZyme UniRef50 hypothesis was falsified at all 4 deep ranks. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] Bacteroidota PUL CAZymes have producer z ∈ [−0.07, −0.10] across deep ranks, with 95 % CIs entirely below zero. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] They have consumer z ∈ [−9.3, −2.8] at parent ranks, so both legs fail the absolute criterion. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

Under the relative criterion, CAZymes still discriminate from housekeeping, but Bacteroidota CAZymes show no additional lift relative to other phyla's CAZymes. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] The hypothesis therefore remains falsified under either criterion. The relative framing changes only how the control classes are described. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] The report states the falsification stands at the pre-registered absolute-zero criterion, including at UniRef50. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

Bacteroidota × PUL has focal n of 6,070 and median LC of 0.41 against the 0.20 atlas reference, which is 2.0× higher. The report reads this as a phylum tendency with substantial within-phylum heterogeneity. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] See [[entities/bacteroidota]].

The report cites Yu et al. (2025), who find vertical inheritance dominating alginate-utilization-locus evolution at the Flagellimonas genus level. This complicates a simple "PUL = HGT" framing. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] The report considers its deep-rank absolute-zero falsification and small consumer-z signal consistent with PUL evolution being more vertical than horizontal at fine taxonomic resolution. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

## General lesson

A low LC beneath a supported clade-level result signals a population mixture of positive sub-clades and negative members. Sub-clade-restricted recomputation then sharpens the claim. [src: discoveries] The report's synthesis reads high LC (PSII 0.88) as class-defining, intermediate LC (PUL 0.41) as a phylum tendency, and LC below the atlas reference (mycolic 0.15) as within-clade heterogeneity. ## Open Directions

- Rerun the PSII test at intermediate ranks (for example, restricting to Cyanobacteria sub-clades that carry the full PSII repertoire). This would show whether the class-rank-only signal comes from pooling many clades with variable content.
- Replace the 11-KO mycolic panel with a panel tuned to shorter-chain mycolates. This would test whether g__Mycobacterium's low LC reflects panel calibration rather than biology.
- Rerun the mycolic test with a strict mycolic-acid KO set that excludes general fatty-acid pathways. This would show whether the order-rank d=0.288 rises above 0.3 or sample size becomes limiting.
- Apply sub-clade-restricted recomputation to Bacteroidota PUL (LC 0.41). This would test whether a positive sub-clade has a stronger Innovator-Exchange signal than the falsified phylum-level test.
- Correct for phylogenetic non-independence and quantify error propagation across phases, both listed as unresolved. This would test whether the PSII n=21 effect survives.
- Obtain reference consumer data at phylum rank. This would settle whether the PSII phylum-rank reversal is purely a data artifact.
