# Hydrogeological depth and flow structure subsurface microbial communities

This concept examines how hydrogeological depth zones, groundwater flow, possible contamination-plume pathways, sample habitat, and short-term temporal stability structure subsurface microbial communities. The primary evidence comes from [[summaries/enigma_sso_asv_ecology__REPORT]], which analyzed sediment and groundwater 16S amplicon sequence variant (ASV) communities from the SSO subsurface site. The Oak Ridge field-fitness comparison provides complementary evidence that groundwater communities also vary along a uranium gradient, while emphasizing that field composition reflects multiple geochemical and historical dimensions rather than metal tolerance alone. [src: lab_field_ecology]

## Depth and habitat structure communities

A PERMANOVA (permutational multivariate analysis of variance) of 37 sediment core segments found that hydrogeological zone explained 27.5% of community variance (F = 4.05, p = 0.0001), whereas well identity explained 19.2% and was not significant (F = 0.80, p = 0.979). [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Samples from the same well but different depths had median Bray–Curtis dissimilarity = 0.977, while samples from the same depth zone in different wells had median Bray–Curtis dissimilarity = 0.835. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] This **supports** the interpretation that depth and saturated-zone position structure sediment communities more strongly than horizontal well identity in the analyzed dataset, and **supports** [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]] by showing that hydrogeological position corresponds to reproducible community differences. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The depth classes were VZ (33 samples), VSZ (25), SZ1 (54), and SZ2 (45); ten of 12 dominant phyla had significant depth associations at p < 0.05. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Chloroflexi (Spearman ρ = −0.73), Patescibacteria (−0.70), Myxococcota (−0.54), and Spirochaetota (−0.53) were enriched toward shallow depths, whereas Firmicutes (ρ = +0.76), WPS-2 (+0.52), Bacteroidota (+0.50), and Proteobacteria (+0.49) were enriched toward deeper depths. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] These associations do not by themselves establish the environmental mechanisms producing the differences. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The report's discussion interprets this vertical zonation (PERMANOVA R² = 27.5%) as reflecting confinement of the contamination plume to the saturated zone rather than generic depth gradients. It also states that communities above the water table are unaffected by contamination. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] No direct geochemical measurement supports this reading in the report. It is therefore a hypothesis about why depth structures communities, not a measured contaminant profile. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

Groundwater and sediment collected at the same well had median Bray–Curtis dissimilarity = 0.424, with within-well values ranging from 0.364–0.450 across 5 wells. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] This **supports** habitat partitioning rather than an interpretation based only on geographically separated wells, and **refines** the spatial model: community composition varies across wells and hydrogeological zones and between sample habitats within wells. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] It connects to [[concepts/environment-embedding-geography]] and [[concepts/subsurface-bacillota-specialization]]. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

Groundwater was enriched in [[entities/rhodanobacter]] (sediment 1.23%, groundwater 3.62%, 2.9×), [[entities/gallionella]] (0.01%, 0.14%, 8.9×), and [[entities/sideroxydans]] (0.01%, 0.06%, 7.0×). [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Sediment was enriched in [[entities/anaeromyxobacter]] (1.24%, 0.03%, 0.02× in groundwater), [[entities/arcobacter]] (0.54%, 0.00%, 0×), and [[entities/methanoperedens]] (0.42%, 0.00%, 0×). [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Geobacter was 0.00% in sediment and 0.01% in groundwater, reported as 5.5× enriched in groundwater. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] This taxonomic contrast is consistent with groundwater enrichment in denitrifiers and iron oxidizers and sediment enrichment in anaerobic taxa, but that metabolic interpretation remains a hypothesis requiring direct genomic and geochemical validation. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The report places two of these genera in Oak Ridge Reservation literature context. Citing Green et al. (2012), it notes that [[entities/rhodanobacter]] species dominate the most contaminated ORR subsurface zones, accounting for up to 45% of 16S sequences in low-pH, high-nitrate wells. It reads its own 7.7% *Rhodanobacter* at M5 as consistent with moderate plume influence, lower than the most contaminated Area 2 wells but elevated above background. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] It describes *Anaeromyxobacter dehalogenans* ([[entities/anaeromyxobacter]]) as a model ENIGMA organism for iron and uranium reduction. The report says it is found throughout SSO sediments at 1.3% mean abundance and is 41× higher in sediment than groundwater, consistent with a biofilm-forming, surface-attached lifestyle. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] These discussion figures are expressed differently from the habitat-enrichment figures in the preceding paragraph; both are recorded here without reconciliation. The literature comparison is context, not a measurement of SSO plume exposure. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

A separate comparison of 10 deep-clay and 62 soil-baseline Bacillota_B genomes **supports** the plausibility of anaerobic-niche and persistence capacity among some deeper subsurface lineages: 547 eggNOG orthologous groups (OGs) were significantly enriched, including manually recognized anaerobic-respiration, sporulation-revival, mineral-attachment or exopolysaccharide, regulatory, and osmoadaptation functions. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] The comparison also found larger deep-clay genomes and more OGs rather than streamlining, but it was a lineage- and cohort-level comparison, not a direct test of SSO depth zones or realized activity. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] Thus, it **refines** the SSO taxonomic redox interpretation by providing genomic support for subsurface persistence and anaerobic potential without converting inferred functions into demonstrated hydrogeochemical processes. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

The cultured deep-clay evidence **supports** this narrower anaerobic-potential interpretation but **refines** its hydrogeological scope: among 9 cultured deep-clay genomes, 5 carried dissimilatory sulfate-reduction markers, with 5 observed versus 0.018 expected under a rock-attached null (binomial p = 4.0×10⁻¹²), whereas corrected iron-reduction comparisons were not significant. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] In pairwise sulfate-reduction completeness tests, anchor deep versus anchor shallow gave Fisher OR=∞ and p=2×10⁻⁴, whereas anchor shallow versus soil baseline was not significant (OR=0.0, p=0.59). [src: clay_confined_subsurface] After iron-reduction marker correction, the original "shallow >> deep" pattern reversed, with deep prevalence slightly higher than shallow, but none of the cohort comparisons were statistically significant (all Fisher p ≥ 0.46). [src: clay_confined_subsurface] The result matches a porewater-associated signal rather than demonstrating representation of the complete rock-attached community, so it does not by itself validate the SSO redox ladder or assign activity to any SSO zone. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]

The central digest **refines** this porewater caveat with site-level context. At the [[entities/mont-terri]] Opalinus clay site, the cited porewater and rock-attached studies describe qualitatively different microbial communities: sulfate reducers dominate porewater, and [[entities/geobacter]]/geothrix iron reducers dominate rock surfaces. [src: discoveries] This suggests the hypothesis that one site can produce both a sulfate-reduction signal in porewater-derived genomes and an iron-reduction expectation for rock-attached communities. Neither signal should therefore be read as whole-site hydrogeological zonation unless the sampled habitat is separated. [src: discoveries, clay_confined_subsurface]

## Meter-scale structure and a possible flow corridor

Across 9 wells arranged in a 3×3 grid spanning approximately 6 m, the sediment dataset contained 23,458 ASVs and 37 sediment core samples aggregated per well. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Mean Bray–Curtis dissimilarity was 0.747, ranging from 0.558 for U3–M6 to 0.872 for U3–L9. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] A Mantel test, which correlates ecological and geographic distance matrices, detected significant distance-decay of community similarity (Spearman ρ = 0.323, p = 0.029, 9,999 permutations). [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

NMDS (non-metric multidimensional scaling) produced stress = 0.067, while Procrustes correspondence between community ordination and the physical grid was marginal (m² = 0.379, p = 0.080). [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Community turnover was not aligned with the uphill–downhill direction (Mantel ρ = −0.049, p = 0.580), although its association with the east–west axis was stronger and non-significant (column Mantel ρ = 0.227, p = 0.092). [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The U3–M6–L7 pairs had the three most negative distance residuals: U3–M6 had Bray–Curtis = 0.558 and residual = −0.170, M6–L7 had Bray–Curtis = 0.615 and residual = −0.154, and U3–L7 had Bray–Curtis = 0.646 and residual = −0.133. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] U3–M4 had the largest positive residual (Bray–Curtis = 0.871, residual = +0.105). [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] These results **support** a northeast-to-southwest diagonal corridor of unusually similar communities, while the interpretation that it reflects contamination-plume flow remains a testable hypothesis rather than a directly measured hydrological result. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The report reads the corridor's shared composition as shared exposure to a plume from the Area 3 source, located uphill and northeast of the SSO. It reads the U3–M4 maximum as spanning the inferred plume entry point to the well farthest from the plume core. Both readings are inferred from community patterns. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The report's discussion states the model more strongly than its statistics. It says all findings converge on a single explanatory model, in which a contamination plume entering from the northeast and flowing southwest through the saturated zone governs SSO community structure. It also says the U3-M6-L7 corridor, identified purely from Bray-Curtis dissimilarity patterns, aligns with the expected NE→SW plume trajectory and demonstrates that 16S community similarity can map subsurface hydrology at meter scale. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] It places the [[entities/sso]] downhill and southwest of the Oak Ridge Reservation Area 3 source. That source is described as delivering high-nitrate, low-pH water laden with [[entities/uranium]], [[entities/chromium]] and [[entities/nickel]], with the plume entering near U3 and flowing diagonally toward L7. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The same report concedes that all environmental inferences come from community composition alone and that the plume model's predictions await geochemical confirmation. This page therefore grades the model as a hypothesis built from community similarity, not an independently verified flow path. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

## Hydrogeological position and inferred redox organization

The redox interpretation is an inference rather than a direct measurement: taxa or taxonomic groups were assigned to redox-associated processes, and their spatial enrichment was interpreted as a possible sequence of electron-acceptor zones. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The report describes this as the first spatially explicit mapping of biogeochemical process distributions across the SSO 3×3 grid. The mapping was inferred from 16S community composition at three taxonomic resolutions (phylum, class, genus); the processes themselves were not measured. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] This connects the SSO analysis to [[concepts/taxonomic-resolution-dependent-functional-inference]], [[concepts/occurrence-versus-catabolic-activity]], and [[concepts/subsurface-bacillota-specialization]]. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

Multi-resolution functional inference covered 22 classes at 78% coverage and 65 annotated genera at 21% coverage; genus-level analysis covered 12 biogeochemical process categories. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The class-level redox index ranged from 0.047 at M6 to 0.227 at U3. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Genus-level functional inference, which the report presents as spatially resolving the classic thermodynamic electron-acceptor sequence with process hotspots at specific wells, placed oxidative processes near U3, denitrification near M5, and fermentation near L9 along a proposed O₂ → NO₃⁻ → Fe(III) → SO₄²⁻ → fermentation sequence. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

Denitrification ranged from 1.9–7.7% and peaked at M5 at 7.7%, with [[entities/rhodanobacter]] as the key genus. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Iron oxidation peaked at U3 at 2.8%, with [[entities/sideroxydans]], while nitrification also peaked at U3 at 2.3%, with Ca. Nitrosotalea. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Iron reduction peaked at U1 at 2.3%, with [[entities/anaeromyxobacter]]; sulfur oxidation peaked at M4 at 1.9%, with [[entities/arcobacter]] and [[entities/thiobacillus]]; methanotrophy peaked at M4 at 1.9%, with [[entities/methanoperedens]]; and fermentation ranged from 2.0–5.3% and peaked at L9 at 5.3%, with [[entities/spirochaeta]] and [[entities/paenisporosarcina]]. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

M5, which has the highest inferred denitrification potential (7.7% [[entities/rhodanobacter]]), is hypothesized to be a plume mixing zone where nitrate-rich contaminated groundwater meets native organic carbon, and M6 is hypothesized to be an anaerobic dead zone with the lowest inferred iron oxidation, sulfur oxidation, and nitrification. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The report presents M5 as the location where plume nitrate meets background organic carbon. It frames this as a prediction that can be validated once SSO geochemistry is loaded into CORAL. These assignments are extrapolations from taxonomy-to-trait inference and support a sampling-prioritization framework, not a demonstrated geochemical map. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Class-level traits had 78% coverage and were consensus estimates based on literature-linked taxonomy rather than empirical measurements of the specific SSO populations. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Only 65 of 1,038 genera were annotated, genus-level functional annotation covered 21% of total reads, species-level classification was approximately 0%, genus-level taxonomy covered 44% of sediment reads, and 56% of reads remained outside genus-level inference. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

Across 9 wells, 65 annotated genera were assigned to 11 metabolic guilds. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Nitrifier × iron oxidizer correlation was ρ = +0.95, syntroph × fermenter correlation was ρ = +0.55, fermenter × predator correlation was ρ = +0.85, denitrifier × syntroph correlation was ρ = −0.67, and sulfate reducer × aerobic heterotroph correlation was ρ = −0.75. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] These associations support hypotheses about functional coupling or spatial separation across a redox gradient, but do not directly measure metabolic interactions or reaction rates. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The central digest names the corresponding taxon pair: *Candidatus Nitrosotalea* ([[entities/nitrosotalea]]), an archaeal ammonia oxidizer, and [[entities/sideroxydans]], an iron oxidizer. It reports that the two co-occur almost perfectly across the 9 SSO wells (ρ=+0.95) and are concentrated at U3. This **supports** the guild-level correlation, but a shared niche remains an inference from co-occurrence. [src: discoveries]

The clay-confined comparison **refines** the interpretation of anaerobic functions as a generic depth signature. Its combined Wood–Ljungdahl, group 1 [NiFe]-hydrogenase, and sulfate-reduction toolkit was strongly enriched in the cultured deep cohort, but within Bacillota_B only sulfate reduction remained associated with the deep cohort; Wood–Ljungdahl and [NiFe]-hydrogenase signals primarily tracked lineage. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] This **supports** treating sulfate reduction as a testable process-specific hypothesis while keeping broader anaerobic-toolkit assignments provisional. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]

## Persistent spatial structure in groundwater

Groundwater communities from 5 wells sampled 9 days apart showed that well identity explained 49.9% of variance (p = 0.001) and filter size explained 10.1% (p = 0.001). The report interprets the filter-size effect as showing that free-living and particle-associated fractions are distinct. Depth within the saturated zone explained 2.5% (p = 0.430), and date explained 0.8% (p = 0.998). [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The samples were collected on September 9 and September 18, 2024. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Median Bray–Curtis dissimilarity, a measure of compositional difference based on taxon abundances, was 0.351 temporally, 0.750 between filter sizes, and 0.917 spatially. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] This **supports** the interpretation that spatial separation contributed more community variation than the 9-day interval, although filter size was also a substantial source of variation. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Alongside these median dissimilarities, the date-1 and date-2 distance matrices correlated at Mantel ρ = 0.867 (p = 0.001). [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The date-1 versus date-2 distance matrices had Mantel ρ = 0.867 (p = 0.001), indicating that well-to-well similarity rankings were nearly unchanged over the 9-day interval. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] This **supports** short-term persistence of groundwater spatial organization rather than random short-term rearrangement, but does not establish seasonal, long-term, or redox stability. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The groundwater result **refines** the sediment spatial model: both materials show spatially structured communities, but the dominant measured variable differs by material. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Sediment hydrogeological zone explained 27.5% of variance, while groundwater well identity explained 49.9%; sediment and groundwater also differed in material, sampling period, and depth structure. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The Oak Ridge comparison **supports** the broader interpretation that groundwater community structure is environmentally multidimensional rather than reducible to a single stress-tolerance axis. Across 108 sites, five of 11 sufficiently prevalent Fitness Browser genera had significant uranium associations after Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction, with associations in both directions: *Herbaspirillum* and *Bacteroides* increased, whereas *Caulobacter*, *Sphingomonas*, and *Pedobacter* decreased. [src: lab_field_ecology] The aggregate laboratory metal-tolerance score had a positive but non-significant association with the high-uranium/low-uranium abundance ratio (Spearman rho=0.503, p=0.095, n=12 genera), and the report attributes the field–laboratory disconnect in part to pH, redox, carbon and energy sources, competition, cross-feeding, temporal history, and taxonomic-resolution mismatch. [src: lab_field_ecology] This **refines** the SSO interpretation: spatial or depth-associated taxonomic patterns may reflect interacting geochemical and historical gradients, while uranium-associated field sorting does not by itself demonstrate realized metal tolerance or a simple flow-driven redox sequence. [src: lab_field_ecology]

A wider Oak Ridge survey **refines** this multidimensional picture with community-level structure. Spearman correlation across 587 100-Well-Survey communities identified two anti-correlated genus clusters, with 47 significant pairs (|rho| > 0.2, p < 0.01). Cluster A (Brevundimonas-Caulobacter-Sphingomonas-Variovorax-Sphingobium; strongest pair rho = +0.56) and Cluster B (Rhodanobacter-Ralstonia-Dyella-Serratia-Comamonas; rho = +0.40) were negatively correlated with each other (Brevundimonas-Rhodanobacter rho = -0.34). [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma] The placement of [[entities/rhodanobacter]] opposite *Caulobacter* and *Sphingomonas* parallels the uranium-associated decreases of those two genera in the 108-site comparison. This suggests the hypothesis that the clusters partly track contamination. The survey correlations are co-occurrence patterns, however, and do not themselves identify the gradient separating the clusters. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma, lab_field_ecology]

Within wells at the [[entities/oak-ridge-field-research-center]] (ORFRC), a separate analysis reports a well gradient in a community-weighted mean (CWM) score that the report links to genus metal-resistance repertoires. CWM differed across eight wells (Kruskal–Wallis H = 29.10, n = 133 samples, p = 0.0001). Median CWM ranked FW215 (1.37) > FW216 (1.36) > GP01 (1.23) ≈ GP03 (1.22) > MLSB3 (1.14) > MLSA3 (1.09) ≈ FW202 (1.08) ≈ MLSG4 (1.08). [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] FW215 and FW216 are described as within the U/NO₃ contamination plume. Their higher CWM is consistent with, but does not establish, selection for genera with broader metal-resistance repertoires in contaminated subsurface environments. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] The CWM score also has a coverage limitation: only 1,637 OTUs (16.8% of reads) could be joined to genus metal-diversity data, and reads from genera without taxonomy matches are excluded. If those uncovered genera are predominantly metal-sensitive, CWM would be biased upward, and the report states that robustness to coverage-fraction variation has not been formally tested. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] With that caveat, this **supports** well-level spatial structuring aligned with plume position at a site independent of the SSO grid. It remains a correlational association with a composite community score. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

The ORFRC series showed directional change over time. This differs from the 9-day SSO groundwater comparison, in which date explained 0.8% of variance. CWM rose with time point across 133 samples (Spearman ρ = +0.383, p < 0.0001) and within well FW216 (ρ = +0.576, n = 40, p = 0.0001). [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology, enigma_sso_asv_ecology] *Sulfurimonas* (80 OTUs, 96.2% prevalence) showed a counterintuitive decline with time (ρ = −0.323, n = 133, p = 0.0002). The report gives a likely but untested explanation: carbon amendment depleting nitrate and oxygen, the genus's preferred electron acceptors. *Nitrospira* (25 OTUs, 88.7% prevalence) was most abundant in plume well FW215, and *Candidatus* Jorgensenbacteria (122 OTUs, 99.2% prevalence) was ubiquitous. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] *Citrobacter* and *Thermodesulfovibrio* were not detected; the report attributes this either to abundance below detection or to genus names mismatched under SILVA nomenclature, so the non-detection does not establish absence. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] This **refines** rather than contradicts the SSO short-term-stability result. The sites, sampling designs and possible amendment history differ, so short-term persistence at the SSO and directional temporal change in ORFRC wells can both hold. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology, enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

## Tensions and limits

The paired comparison supports groundwater–sediment partitioning, but the 18-month offset prevents clean attribution to habitat alone. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Sediment cores were collected during February–March 2023, whereas groundwater was sampled in September 2024; seasonal change or plume dynamics could therefore contribute to the difference, and sediment lacked within-well temporal replication. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Because sediment came from a single core sampling event, temporal dynamics of the plume and of the community response cannot be assessed from it. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The sediment analysis suggests that hydrogeological zone can dominate well identity, whereas the groundwater analysis found well identity explaining 49.9% of variance and depth within the saturated zone explaining 2.5% with p = 0.430. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] This is not a direct contradiction because the datasets differ in material, sampling period, and depth structure, but it **indicates** that the relative importance of spatial variables may be sample-type dependent. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The data show strong spatial structure and high similarity of spatial rankings between dates, but the design cannot distinguish persistent environmental forcing from stable well-specific properties, filter effects, or other sampling effects. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The Oak Ridge result **supports** this limitation rather than resolving it: significant uranium associations occurred in both positive and negative directions, while the aggregate laboratory-tolerance relationship was only suggestive. [src: lab_field_ecology] Because the Oak Ridge analysis did not control pH, dissolved oxygen, carbon sources, and other confounders, it cannot identify uranium as the sole driver of the observed community shifts; likewise, it does not directly validate the SSO proposed flow corridor or redox ladder. [src: lab_field_ecology]

The Bacillota_B comparison **refines** but does not directly validate the SSO redox ladder: its 10 deep-clay versus 62 soil-baseline genome analysis found enrichment of anaerobic-respiration-related functions, but the anchor cohort was borehole- and porewater-dominated and some enriched OGs may be lineage markers rather than recurrent subsurface-specialization features. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] In addition, a corrected multi-heme cytochrome detector found no significant deep-versus-shallow, deep-versus-baseline, or shallow-versus-baseline cohort differences (odds ratio 1.88 and p=0.46; 1.80 and p=0.49; and 0.96 and p=1.0, respectively), while the clay-project sulfite-reduction-side finding remained supported at 5/9 deep-cohort genomes versus a Mitzscherling rock-attached null rate of 0.2%, with binomial p=4×10⁻¹². [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] This **contradicts** any transfer of the original shallow-enrichment iron-reduction narrative to a general subsurface zonation claim, while **supporting** a narrower interpretation in which sulfite reduction and broader anaerobic capacity remain plausible but iron-reduction contrasts require corrected markers and direct validation. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

The clay-confined study adds a related limitation: its cultured deep cohort did not show elevated biosynthetic self-sufficiency, and the result was explicitly limited to cultivable porewater-cultured isolates rather than the full clay-confined community. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] This **refines** any inference that deeper or anaerobic communities should be broadly more self-sufficient: the cultured cohort does not test uncultured rock-attached, CPR/DPANN, or other MAG-dominated lineages, and the 18-pathway metric may saturate near 18. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] The finding is therefore not evidence against subsurface specialization, but it does not provide an additional community-level mechanism for the SSO depth pattern. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]

The strongest measured result is depth-associated community differentiation in sediment and short-term well-associated persistence in groundwater. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The proposed northeast-to-southwest contamination-plume explanation and redox ladder remain unconfirmed because direct SSO geochemistry was unavailable. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Although 221 registered SSO geochemistry sample tubes exist in CORAL, metals, ion chromatography/total organic carbon, isotopes, ammonia, and nitrite measurements had not been loaded. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The central digest adds that these 221 METALS/ICTOC/ISOTOPES/NH3NO2 sample tubes are registered in [[entities/enigma-coral]] (`sdt_sample`), but zero `Assay Geochemistry` processes are linked, so the analytical measurement values were never ingested. [src: discoveries]

The central discoveries digest states the SSO result more strongly than the project report. It says significant distance-decay (Mantel ρ=0.323, p=0.029) is driven by the east-west axis rather than the hillslope gradient, and that the U3-M6-L7 corridor shares community composition along the NE→SW plume flow path. It also describes an M5 [[entities/rhodanobacter]] denitrification hotspot (7.7%) at the plume mixing zone and temporally stable groundwater communities (well R²=49.9%, date R²=0.8% over 9 days), while noting that genus-level functional inference is what maps the redox ladder. [src: discoveries] The project report's statistics are more cautious, even though its own discussion states the plume model in similarly strong terms: its east–west association was not conventionally significant (column Mantel ρ = 0.227, p = 0.092), and the plume path, mixing zone and redox ladder rest on composition-based inference rather than measured hydrology or geochemistry. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The digest's wording therefore restates the same hypotheses and is not independent confirmation of them. [src: discoveries, enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

Groundwater ASV coverage exists for only 5 of 9 wells—L7, L9, M4, M6, and U2—and excludes inferred hotspot wells M5 and U3. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Consequently, the taxonomic contrast does not establish whether partitioning applies across the complete SSO well grid or at the proposed plume hotspots, and the persistent spatial pattern cannot fully test plume persistence. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The denitrification and iron-oxidation hotspot interpretations rest on sediment data. The report notes that groundwater validation at these wells would substantially strengthen the plume model.
