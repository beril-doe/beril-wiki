# Hydrogeological depth and flow structure subsurface microbial communities

This concept examines how hydrogeological depth zones, groundwater flow, and possible contamination-plume pathways structure subsurface microbial communities. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The primary evidence comes from [[summaries/enigma_sso_asv_ecology__REPORT]], which analyzed sediment and groundwater 16S amplicon sequence variant (ASV) communities from the SSO subsurface site. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

## Depth is a stronger structuring variable than well identity

A PERMANOVA (permutational multivariate analysis of variance) of 37 sediment core segments found that hydrogeological zone explained 27.5% of community variance (F = 4.05, p = 0.0001), whereas well identity explained 19.2% and was not significant (F = 0.80, p = 0.979). [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

Samples from the same well but different depths had median Bray–Curtis dissimilarity = 0.977, while samples from the same depth zone in different wells had median Bray–Curtis dissimilarity = 0.835. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

This supports the interpretation that depth and saturated-zone plume intersection structure communities more strongly than horizontal well identity in the analyzed sediment dataset. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The analyzed depth classes were VZ (33 samples), VSZ (25), SZ1 (54), and SZ2 (45). [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

Ten of 12 dominant phyla had significant depth associations at p < 0.05. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

Chloroflexi (Spearman ρ = −0.73), Patescibacteria (−0.70), Myxococcota (−0.54), and Spirochaetota (−0.53) were enriched toward shallow depths, whereas Firmicutes (ρ = +0.76), WPS-2 (+0.52), Bacteroidota (+0.50), and Proteobacteria (+0.49) were enriched toward deeper depths. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

These depth associations support [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]] by showing that hydrogeological position corresponds to reproducible differences in community composition, while they do not by themselves establish the environmental mechanisms producing those differences. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

## Meter-scale spatial structure and a possible flow corridor

Across 9 wells arranged in a 3×3 grid spanning approximately 6 m, the sediment dataset contained 23,458 ASVs and 37 sediment core samples aggregated per well. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

Mean Bray–Curtis dissimilarity was 0.747, ranging from 0.558 for U3–M6 to 0.872 for U3–L9. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

A Mantel test, which correlates ecological and geographic distance matrices, detected significant distance-decay of community similarity (Spearman ρ = 0.323, p = 0.029, 9,999 permutations). [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

NMDS (non-metric multidimensional scaling) produced stress = 0.067, while Procrustes correspondence between community ordination and the physical grid was marginal (m² = 0.379, p = 0.080). [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

Community turnover was not aligned with the uphill–downhill direction (Mantel ρ = −0.049, p = 0.580), but its association with the east–west axis was stronger though non-significant (column Mantel ρ = 0.227, p = 0.092). [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The U3–M6–L7 pairs had the three most negative distance residuals: U3–M6 had Bray–Curtis = 0.558 and residual = −0.170, M6–L7 had Bray–Curtis = 0.615 and residual = −0.154, and U3–L7 had Bray–Curtis = 0.646 and residual = −0.133. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

U3–M4 had the largest positive residual (Bray–Curtis = 0.871, residual = +0.105). [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

These results support a northeast-to-southwest diagonal corridor of unusually similar communities, but the interpretation that this corridor reflects contamination-plume flow is a hypothesis rather than a directly measured hydrological result. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The proposed plume model remains unconfirmed because direct SSO geochemistry was not available in the analyzed dataset. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

## Hydrogeological position and inferred redox organization

Multi-resolution functional inference covered 22 classes at 78% coverage and 65 annotated genera at 21% coverage. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The class-level redox index ranged from 0.047 at M6 to 0.227 at U3, and genus-level inference covered 12 biogeochemical process categories. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The inferred process pattern placed oxidative processes near U3, denitrification near M5, and fermentation near L9 along a proposed sequence of O₂ → NO₃⁻ → Fe(III) → SO₄²⁻ → fermentation. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

Denitrification ranged from 1.9–7.7% and peaked at M5 at 7.7%, with Rhodanobacter identified as the key genus. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

Iron oxidation peaked at U3 at 2.8%, with Sideroxydans, while nitrification also peaked at U3 at 2.3%, with Ca. Nitrosotalea. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

Iron reduction peaked at U1 at 2.3%, with Anaeromyxobacter; sulfur oxidation peaked at M4 at 1.9%, with Arcobacter and Thiobacillus; methanotrophy peaked at M4 at 1.9%, with Ca. Methanoperedens; and fermentation peaked at L9 at 5.3%, with Spirochaeta and Paenisporosarcina. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The M5 mixing-zone and M6 anaerobic-dead-zone assignments are extrapolations from taxonomy-to-trait inference and should therefore be treated as hypotheses awaiting geochemical validation. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The genus-level estimates are lower bounds because only 65 of 1,038 genera were annotated and genus-level functional annotation covered 21% of total reads. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

Class-level traits had 78% coverage and showed consistent redox patterns, but the report recommends sensitivity analysis against the lower-coverage genus-level results. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

This evidence extends [[concepts/subsurface-bacillota-specialization]] by linking depth-associated Firmicutes enrichment to a broader inferred subsurface redox gradient, while retaining the source report's warning that the functional assignments are not direct measurements. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

## Groundwater and sediment represent different hydrogeological compartments

Groundwater and sediment communities collected at the same well had median Bray–Curtis dissimilarity = 0.424, with within-well values ranging from 0.364–0.450 across 5 wells. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

Groundwater was enriched in Rhodanobacter (sediment 1.23%, groundwater 3.62%, 2.9×), Gallionella (0.01%, 0.14%, 8.9×), and Sideroxydans (0.01%, 0.06%, 7.0×). [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

Sediment was enriched in Anaeromyxobacter (1.24%, 0.03%, 0.02× in groundwater), Arcobacter (0.54%, 0.00%, 0×), and Ca. Methanoperedens (0.42%, 0.00%, 0×). [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

Geobacter was 0.00% in sediment and 0.01% in groundwater, reported as 5.5× enriched in groundwater. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

These differences support the interpretation that groundwater and sediment communities are distinct hydrogeological compartments rather than a simple detachment series. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The interpretation is constrained by an 18-month offset between the sediment and groundwater sampling periods, because sediment cores were collected during February–March 2023 and groundwater was sampled in September 2024. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

## Short-term persistence of groundwater spatial structure

Groundwater communities from 5 wells sampled 9 days apart showed that well identity explained 49.9% of variance (p = 0.001), filter size explained 10.1% (p = 0.001), depth within the saturated zone explained 2.5% (p = 0.430), and date explained 0.8% (p = 0.998). [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

Median Bray–Curtis variation was 0.351 temporally, 0.750 between filter sizes, and 0.917 spatially. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The date-1 versus date-2 distance matrices had Mantel ρ = 0.867 (p = 0.001), indicating that well-to-well similarity rankings were nearly unchanged over the 9-day interval. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

This supports persistent groundwater spatial structure over the observed 9-day interval, but it does not establish seasonal or long-term stability. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

## Tensions and limits

The strongest measured result is depth-associated community differentiation, whereas the proposed northeast-to-southwest contamination-plume explanation is not directly confirmed because the 221 registered SSO geochemistry sample tubes have not been loaded with metals, ion chromatography/total organic carbon, isotopes, ammonia, or nitrite measurements. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

Groundwater ASV coverage exists for only 5 of 9 wells—L7, L9, M4, M6, and U2—and excludes the inferred hotspot wells M5 and U3. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The report predicts that Rhodanobacter will be highest at M5 and lower at L8 and U2, but this prediction has not yet been tested with the available groundwater data. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

Trait scores at phylum and class levels are consensus estimates based on literature-linked taxonomy rather than empirical measurements of the specific SSO populations. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

Metagenomics at the same spatial resolution is therefore needed to test the inferred process assignments and recover functional capacity from reads not classified at genus level. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

## Open Directions

- Load the 221 SSO geochemistry samples into CORAL and use spatial-gradient analysis to test whether nitrate, pH, and metal concentrations follow the predicted northeast-to-southwest pattern. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]
- Extract pump-test ASVs from Brick 460-462 and compare M5, L8, and U2 to test whether Rhodanobacter is highest at M5. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]
- Add groundwater samples from M5 and U3 and use matched-well community comparisons to determine whether the inferred hotspots are represented in groundwater. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]
- Sequence metagenomes at the same spatial resolution and compare gene-level metabolic capacity with the 16S-derived redox and process assignments. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]
- Analyze the 18 M6-C2 isolate genomes for anaerobic metabolisms and compare their capacities with the inferred M6 anaerobic-dead-zone hypothesis. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]
- Apply weighted UniFrac to ASV sequences from Bricks 457/460/477 to test whether phylogenetic community structure reinforces the Bray–Curtis spatial pattern. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]
- Repeat 16S profiling across seasons and include sediment temporal replication to distinguish persistent hydrogeological structure from seasonal or plume-driven dynamics. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]
