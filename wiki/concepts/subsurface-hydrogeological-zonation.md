# Hydrogeological depth and flow structure subsurface microbial communities

This concept examines how hydrogeological depth zones, groundwater flow, possible contamination-plume pathways, sample habitat, and short-term temporal stability structure subsurface microbial communities. The primary evidence comes from [[summaries/enigma_sso_asv_ecology__REPORT]], which analyzed sediment and groundwater 16S amplicon sequence variant (ASV) communities from the SSO subsurface site. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

## Depth and habitat structure communities

A PERMANOVA (permutational multivariate analysis of variance) of 37 sediment core segments found that hydrogeological zone explained 27.5% of community variance (F = 4.05, p = 0.0001), whereas well identity explained 19.2% and was not significant (F = 0.80, p = 0.979). [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Samples from the same well but different depths had median Bray–Curtis dissimilarity = 0.977, while samples from the same depth zone in different wells had median Bray–Curtis dissimilarity = 0.835. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] This **supports** the interpretation that depth and saturated-zone position structure sediment communities more strongly than horizontal well identity in the analyzed dataset, and **supports** [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]] by showing that hydrogeological position corresponds to reproducible community differences. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The depth classes were VZ (33 samples), VSZ (25), SZ1 (54), and SZ2 (45); ten of 12 dominant phyla had significant depth associations at p < 0.05. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Chloroflexi (Spearman ρ = −0.73), Patescibacteria (−0.70), Myxococcota (−0.54), and Spirochaetota (−0.53) were enriched toward shallow depths, whereas Firmicutes (ρ = +0.76), WPS-2 (+0.52), Bacteroidota (+0.50), and Proteobacteria (+0.49) were enriched toward deeper depths. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] These associations do not by themselves establish the environmental mechanisms producing the differences. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

Groundwater and sediment collected at the same well had median Bray–Curtis dissimilarity = 0.424, with within-well values ranging from 0.364–0.450 across 5 wells. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] This **supports** habitat partitioning rather than an interpretation based only on geographically separated wells, and **refines** the spatial model: community composition varies across wells and hydrogeological zones and between sample habitats within wells. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] It connects to [[concepts/environmental-embedding-ecological-validity]] and [[concepts/subsurface-bacillota-specialization]]. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

Groundwater was enriched in [[entities/rhodanobacter]] (sediment 1.23%, groundwater 3.62%, 2.9×), [[entities/gallionella]] (0.01%, 0.14%, 8.9×), and [[entities/sideroxydans]] (0.01%, 0.06%, 7.0×). [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Sediment was enriched in [[entities/anaeromyxobacter]] (1.24%, 0.03%, 0.02× in groundwater), [[entities/arcobacter]] (0.54%, 0.00%, 0×), and [[entities/methanoperedens]] (0.42%, 0.00%, 0×). [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Geobacter was 0.00% in sediment and 0.01% in groundwater, reported as 5.5× enriched in groundwater. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] This taxonomic contrast is consistent with groundwater enrichment in denitrifiers and iron oxidizers and sediment enrichment in anaerobic taxa, but that metabolic interpretation remains a hypothesis requiring direct genomic and geochemical validation. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

A separate comparison of 10 deep-clay and 62 soil-baseline Bacillota_B genomes **supports** the plausibility of anaerobic-niche and persistence capacity among some deeper subsurface lineages: 547 eggNOG orthologous groups (OGs) were significantly enriched, including manually recognized anaerobic-respiration, sporulation-revival, mineral-attachment or exopolysaccharide, regulatory, and osmoadaptation functions. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] The comparison also found larger deep-clay genomes and more OGs rather than streamlining, but it was a lineage- and cohort-level comparison, not a direct test of SSO depth zones or realized activity. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] Thus, it **refines** the SSO taxonomic redox interpretation by providing genomic support for subsurface persistence and anaerobic potential without converting inferred functions into demonstrated hydrogeochemical processes. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

The cultured deep-clay evidence **supports** this narrower anaerobic-potential interpretation but **refines** its hydrogeological scope: among 9 cultured deep-clay genomes, 5 carried dissimilatory sulfate-reduction markers, with 5 observed versus 0.018 expected under a rock-attached null (binomial p = 4.0×10⁻¹²), whereas corrected iron-reduction comparisons were not significant. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] The result matches a porewater-associated signal rather than demonstrating representation of the complete rock-attached community, so it does not by itself validate the SSO redox ladder or assign activity to any SSO zone. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]

## Meter-scale structure and a possible flow corridor

Across 9 wells arranged in a 3×3 grid spanning approximately 6 m, the sediment dataset contained 23,458 ASVs and 37 sediment core samples aggregated per well. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Mean Bray–Curtis dissimilarity was 0.747, ranging from 0.558 for U3–M6 to 0.872 for U3–L9. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] A Mantel test, which correlates ecological and geographic distance matrices, detected significant distance-decay of community similarity (Spearman ρ = 0.323, p = 0.029, 9,999 permutations). [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

NMDS (non-metric multidimensional scaling) produced stress = 0.067, while Procrustes correspondence between community ordination and the physical grid was marginal (m² = 0.379, p = 0.080). [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Community turnover was not aligned with the uphill–downhill direction (Mantel ρ = −0.049, p = 0.580), although its association with the east–west axis was stronger and non-significant (column Mantel ρ = 0.227, p = 0.092). [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The U3–M6–L7 pairs had the three most negative distance residuals: U3–M6 had Bray–Curtis = 0.558 and residual = −0.170, M6–L7 had Bray–Curtis = 0.615 and residual = −0.154, and U3–L7 had Bray–Curtis = 0.646 and residual = −0.133. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] U3–M4 had the largest positive residual (Bray–Curtis = 0.871, residual = +0.105). [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] These results **support** a northeast-to-southwest diagonal corridor of unusually similar communities, while the interpretation that it reflects contamination-plume flow remains a testable hypothesis rather than a directly measured hydrological result. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

## Hydrogeological position and inferred redox organization

The redox interpretation is an inference rather than a direct measurement: taxa or taxonomic groups were assigned to redox-associated processes, and their spatial enrichment was interpreted as a possible sequence of electron-acceptor zones. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] This connects the SSO analysis to [[concepts/taxonomic-resolution-dependent-functional-inference]], [[concepts/occurrence-versus-catabolic-activity]], and [[concepts/subsurface-bacillota-specialization]]. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

Multi-resolution functional inference covered 22 classes at 78% coverage and 65 annotated genera at 21% coverage; genus-level analysis covered 12 biogeochemical process categories. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The class-level redox index ranged from 0.047 at M6 to 0.227 at U3. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The inferred pattern placed oxidative processes near U3, denitrification near M5, and fermentation near L9 along a proposed O₂ → NO₃⁻ → Fe(III) → SO₄²⁻ → fermentation sequence. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

Denitrification ranged from 1.9–7.7% and peaked at M5 at 7.7%, with [[entities/rhodanobacter]] as the key genus. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Iron oxidation peaked at U3 at 2.8%, with [[entities/sideroxydans]], while nitrification also peaked at U3 at 2.3%, with Ca. Nitrosotalea. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Iron reduction peaked at U1 at 2.3%, with [[entities/anaeromyxobacter]]; sulfur oxidation peaked at M4 at 1.9%, with [[entities/arcobacter]] and [[entities/thiobacillus]]; methanotrophy peaked at M4 at 1.9%, with [[entities/methanoperedens]]; and fermentation peaked at L9 at 5.3%, with [[entities/spirochaeta]] and [[entities/paenisporosarcina]]. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

M5 is hypothesized to be a plume mixing zone where nitrate-rich contaminated groundwater meets native organic carbon, and M6 is hypothesized to be an anaerobic dead zone with the lowest inferred iron oxidation, sulfur oxidation, and nitrification. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] These assignments are extrapolations from taxonomy-to-trait inference and support a sampling-prioritization framework, not a demonstrated geochemical map. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Class-level traits had 78% coverage and were consensus estimates based on literature-linked taxonomy rather than empirical measurements of the specific SSO populations. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Only 65 of 1,038 genera were annotated, genus-level functional annotation covered 21% of total reads, species-level classification was approximately 0%, genus-level taxonomy covered 44% of sediment reads, and 56% of reads remained outside genus-level inference. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

Across 9 wells, 65 annotated genera were assigned to 11 metabolic guilds. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Nitrifier × iron oxidizer correlation was ρ = +0.95, syntroph × fermenter correlation was ρ = +0.55, fermenter × predator correlation was ρ = +0.85, denitrifier × syntroph correlation was ρ = −0.67, and sulfate reducer × aerobic heterotroph correlation was ρ = −0.75. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] These associations support hypotheses about functional coupling or spatial separation across a redox gradient, but do not directly measure metabolic interactions or reaction rates. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The clay-confined comparison **refines** the interpretation of anaerobic functions as a generic depth signature. Its combined Wood–Ljungdahl, group 1 [NiFe]-hydrogenase, and sulfate-reduction toolkit was strongly enriched in the cultured deep cohort, but within Bacillota_B only sulfate reduction remained associated with the deep cohort; Wood–Ljungdahl and [NiFe]-hydrogenase signals primarily tracked lineage. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] This **supports** treating sulfate reduction as a testable process-specific hypothesis while keeping broader anaerobic-toolkit assignments provisional. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]

## Persistent spatial structure in groundwater

Groundwater communities from 5 wells sampled 9 days apart showed that well identity explained 49.9% of variance (p = 0.001), filter size explained 10.1% (p = 0.001), depth within the saturated zone explained 2.5% (p = 0.430), and date explained 0.8% (p = 0.998). [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The samples were collected on September 9 and September 18, 2024. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Median Bray–Curtis dissimilarity, a measure of compositional difference based on taxon abundances, was 0.351 temporally, 0.750 between filter sizes, and 0.917 spatially. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] This **supports** the interpretation that spatial separation contributed more community variation than the 9-day interval, although filter size was also a substantial source of variation. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The date-1 versus date-2 distance matrices had Mantel ρ = 0.867 (p = 0.001), indicating that well-to-well similarity rankings were nearly unchanged over the 9-day interval. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] This **supports** short-term persistence of groundwater spatial organization rather than random short-term rearrangement, but does not establish seasonal, long-term, or redox stability. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The groundwater result **refines** the sediment spatial model: both materials show spatially structured communities, but the dominant measured variable differs by material. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Sediment hydrogeological zone explained 27.5% of variance, while groundwater well identity explained 49.9%; sediment and groundwater also differed in material, sampling period, and depth structure. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

## Tensions and limits

The paired comparison supports groundwater–sediment partitioning, but the 18-month offset prevents clean attribution to habitat alone. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Sediment cores were collected during February–March 2023, whereas groundwater was sampled in September 2024; seasonal change or plume dynamics could therefore contribute to the difference, and sediment lacked within-well temporal replication. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The sediment analysis suggests that hydrogeological zone can dominate well identity, whereas the groundwater analysis found well identity explaining 49.9% of variance and depth within the saturated zone explaining 2.5% with p = 0.430. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] This is not a direct contradiction because the datasets differ in material, sampling period, and depth structure, but it **indicates** that the relative importance of spatial variables may be sample-type dependent. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The data show strong spatial structure and high similarity of spatial rankings between dates, but the design cannot distinguish persistent environmental forcing from stable well-specific properties, filter effects, or other sampling effects. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The Bacillota_B comparison **refines** but does not directly validate the SSO redox ladder: its 10 deep-clay versus 62 soil-baseline genome analysis found enrichment of anaerobic-respiration-related functions, but the anchor cohort was borehole- and porewater-dominated and some enriched OGs may be lineage markers rather than recurrent subsurface-specialization features. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] In addition, a corrected multi-heme cytochrome detector found no significant deep-versus-shallow, deep-versus-baseline, or shallow-versus-baseline cohort differences (odds ratio 1.88 and p=0.46; 1.80 and p=0.49; and 0.96 and p=1.0, respectively), while the clay-project sulfite-reduction-side finding remained supported at 5/9 deep-cohort genomes versus a Mitzscherling rock-attached null rate of 0.2%, with binomial p=4×10⁻¹². [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] This **contradicts** any transfer of the original shallow-enrichment iron-reduction narrative to a general subsurface zonation claim, while **supporting** a narrower interpretation in which sulfite reduction and broader anaerobic capacity remain plausible but iron-reduction contrasts require corrected markers and direct validation. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

The clay-confined study adds a related limitation: its cultured deep cohort did not show elevated biosynthetic self-sufficiency, and the result was explicitly limited to cultivable porewater-cultured isolates rather than the full clay-confined community. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] This **refines** any inference that deeper or anaerobic communities should be broadly more self-sufficient: the cultured cohort does not test uncultured rock-attached, CPR/DPANN, or other MAG-dominated lineages, and the 18-pathway metric may saturate near 18. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] The finding is therefore not evidence against subsurface specialization, but it does not provide an additional community-level mechanism for the SSO depth pattern. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]

The strongest measured result is depth-associated community differentiation in sediment and short-term well-associated persistence in groundwater. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The proposed northeast-to-southwest contamination-plume explanation and redox ladder remain unconfirmed because direct SSO geochemistry was unavailable. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Although 221 registered SSO geochemistry sample tubes exist in CORAL, metals, ion chromatography/total organic carbon, isotopes, ammonia, and nitrite measurements had not been loaded. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

Groundwater ASV coverage exists for only 5 of 9 wells—L7, L9, M4, M6, and U2—and excludes inferred hotspot wells M5 and U3. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Consequently, the taxonomic contrast does not establish whether partitioning applies across the complete SSO well grid or at the proposed plume hotspots, and the persistent spatial pattern cannot fully test plume persistence. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The report predicts that Rhodanobacter will be highest at M5 and lower at L8 and U2, but this prediction has not been tested with available groundwater data. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The composition-based redox hypothesis is coherent, but taxonomy-to-trait assignments cannot establish realized activity; metagenomics and matched geochemistry are needed to test inferred process assignments and recover functional capacity from reads not classified at genus level. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

## Open Directions

- Load the 221 SSO geochemistry samples into CORAL and use spatial-gradient analysis to test whether nitrate, pH, and metal concentrations form the predicted northeast-to-southwest pattern and explain the groundwater–sediment contrast. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]
- Extract pump-test ASVs from Brick 460-462, add groundwater samples from M5 and U3, and compare L8, M5, and U2 to test whether the predicted Rhodanobacter maximum occurs at M5. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]
- Repeat groundwater 16S sampling across seasons and test whether well identity continues to dominate date and seasonal effects, and whether the U3–M6–L7 corridor and inferred redox hotspots persist through time. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]
- Collect groundwater and sediment contemporaneously at the same wells, repeat paired Bray–Curtis comparisons across seasons, and include sediment temporal replication to separate habitat partitioning from the 18-month offset and test persistence during plume or environmental change. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]
- Standardize filter size and apply PERMANOVA with filter, well, depth, and date terms to determine how much of the apparent spatial structure is attributable to sampling configuration. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]
- Perform same-resolution metagenomic profiling of groundwater and sediment to test whether habitat-enriched taxa carry inferred denitrification, iron-oxidation, iron-reduction, sulfur-oxidation, nitrification, methanotrophy, and fermentation functions, and compare gene-level metabolic capacity with the 16S-derived assignments. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]
- Analyze the 18 M6-C2 isolate genomes for anaerobic metabolisms and compare their capacities with the inferred M6 anaerobic-dead-zone hypothesis. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]
- Apply weighted UniFrac to ASV sequences from Bricks 457/460/477 to test whether phylogenetic community structure reinforces the Bray–Curtis spatial pattern. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]
- Reanalyze the 547 enriched Bacillota_B OGs by genus and with corrected functional markers, then test matched deep, shallow, rock-attached, and soil cohorts to distinguish recurrent hydrogeological specialization from lineage and cultivation effects. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]
- Pair corrected multi-heme cytochrome and sulfite-reduction markers with SSO metagenomes and geochemistry to test whether inferred iron- and sulfur-linked processes vary across the proposed depth and flow zones. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]
- Add the cultured clay-confined cohort and deep-subsurface MAGs to the SSO metagenomic comparison, testing whether sulfate-reduction markers co-vary with hydrogeological zone and geochemistry while separating porewater, rock-attached, and lineage effects. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]
- Reassess biosynthetic self-sufficiency with finer-grained amino-acid pathway markers and MAG-augmented cohorts to determine whether the absence of elevated self-sufficiency is a cultivation artifact or a broader feature of clay-confined communities. [src: clay_confined_subsurface]
