# Physically Dispersed Genes Can Form Tightly Coupled Metabolic Systems

Genes do not need to share an operon or genomic neighborhood to participate in a tightly coupled metabolic system: coordinated dependencies can emerge when separate pathways jointly support the same biochemical objective. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] In *Acinetobacter baylyi* ADP1, quinate catabolism depends on genes distributed across aromatic degradation, respiration, iron acquisition, PQQ biosynthesis, and transcriptional regulation. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] This **supports** [[concepts/cofitness-network-architecture]] by showing that co-fitness—the comparison of gene-level fitness profiles across growth conditions—can reveal functional architecture beyond physical gene clustering. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

The [[summaries/cog_analysis__REPORT]] **supports** this principle at broader comparative scale: across 32 species spanning 9 phyla and 357,623 genes, core genes concentrated in conserved metabolic and housekeeping COG functions, whereas novel or singleton genes were enriched in mobile-element, defense, and unknown-function categories. [src: cog_analysis] COG L mobile-element functions showed +10.88% enrichment among novel or singleton genes, with 100% consistency across species; COG V defense functions showed +2.83% enrichment, also with 100% consistency. [src: cog_analysis] The report lists translation (-4.65%), nucleotide metabolism (-2.09%), coenzyme metabolism (-2.06%), amino acid metabolism (-1.81%), and energy production (-1.75%) under its heading of categories in which core genes are consistently enriched. [src: cog_analysis] The report gives these as negative signed enrichment values and labels translation the 'strongest depletion', so its sign convention is internally inconsistent. The heading places these categories on the core-enriched side, but the direction of the signed values should be confirmed against the underlying tables before it is relied on. [src: cog_analysis] Composite COG assignments were retained as biologically meaningful multifunctional categories, including the LV mobile-and-defense composite, which showed +0.34% enrichment with 76% consistency. [src: cog_analysis] This **refines** the dispersed-gene principle by suggesting that distributed functional organization can reflect both metabolic support systems and the acquisition of mobile or defense-associated functions, while COG distributions alone do not demonstrate physical dispersion or physiological coupling. Approximately 70% COG annotation coverage leaves unassigned genes as a source of distributional bias. [src: cog_analysis]

## The ADP1 quinate-support network

The analysis identified a 51-gene support network surrounding the [[entities/beta-ketoadipate-pathway]]. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Co-fitness assigned 44/51 genes (86%) to four functional subsystems: 8 aromatic-pathway genes, 21 Complex I genes, 7 iron-acquisition genes, and 2 PQQ-biosynthesis genes. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Six additional genes were transcriptional regulators and 7 remained unassigned. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Of 23 genes initially categorized as Other or Unknown, co-fitness assigned 16 to support subsystems with medium or high confidence. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] The central discoveries digest compares 8 core β-ketoadipate pathway genes with 51 genes that show quinate-specific growth defects. [src: discoveries] The digest also reports a 7:1 support-to-pathway ratio. That characterization is inconsistent with its own counts because the 51 genes include the pathway genes, so the ratio is not a support-only comparison and should not be read as one. [src: discoveries, aromatic_catabolism_network] In the digest's framing, the support genes form 3 biochemically rational subsystems: Complex I (21 genes, NADH reoxidation), iron acquisition (7 genes, Fe²⁺ for the ring-cleavage dioxygenase), and PQQ biosynthesis (2 genes, cofactor for quinate dehydrogenase). [src: discoveries] The digest's count of 3 subsystems leaves out the aromatic pathway, which the project report counts as the fourth. Its general principle, that the metabolic infrastructure required to run a pathway dwarfs the pathway itself, is extrapolated from a single organism and should be read as a hypothesis. [src: discoveries, aromatic_catabolism_network]

The pathway converts quinate through protocatechuate and β-ketoadipate to succinyl-CoA and acetyl-CoA, which enter the TCA cycle. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Quinate dehydrogenase (quiA) is a PQQ-dependent quinoprotein that requires the PQQ cofactor. The report uses this requirement to explain the 2 PQQ-biosynthesis genes, pqqC and pqqD. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Protocatechuate 3,4-dioxygenase (pcaGH) requires non-heme Fe²⁺ for ring cleavage. The report uses this requirement to explain the 7 iron-acquisition genes, which cover siderophore biosynthesis, ExbD/TolR transport, a ferrichrome receptor, and a TonB-dependent receptor. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] TCA-cycle oxidation also generates NADH that must be reoxidized by respiratory machinery. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] These biochemical dependencies **support** the concept that pathway dependence extends to cofactors, metals, electron transport, and regulation even when the corresponding genes are not physically adjacent. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

## Genomic separation and functional coupling

The support subsystems occupy distinct chromosomal regions rather than one shared genomic neighborhood. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] The Complex I operon lies at 714–729 kb, the pca/qui pathway at 1,709–1,724 kb, PQQ biosynthesis at 2,461 kb, and iron-acquisition genes are scattered across 4 loci. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] No cross-category operons were identified except within the aromatic pathway itself. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] The Complex I operon contains 13 nuoA–N subunits on the same strand with <100 bp intergenic distances, while the pca/qui region forms a 12-gene operon spanning pcaIJFBDCHG-quiABC plus transport genes. The report states that these operon predictions match Dal et al. (2005), an earlier experimental map of how the pca/qui genes are transcribed. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

Within the chromosome, 9 genomic clusters contain ≥2 quinate-specific genes, while the overall nearest-neighbor distance ratio is 0.89 observed/expected, indicating mild clustering rather than a single unified locus. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] In the separate cross-organism co-inheritance analysis, only 0.7% of cofit pairs were genomically adjacent (within 5 genes), and excluding them did not change the result pattern. [src: cofitness_coinheritance] These observations **support** the distinction between physical organization and functional coupling: local pathway modules may remain clustered, but pathway, respiratory, cofactor, metal, and regulatory loci can converge on quinate-supported growth despite genomic separation. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

## Physical clustering as a complementary signal

The [[summaries/fitness_modules__REPORT]] **refines** the dispersed-gene principle from the opposite direction. Fitness modules are gene sets with coordinated fitness profiles. They showed 22.7x genomic-adjacency enrichment, and the report states that module genes are co-located in operons. [src: fitness_modules] Coupling across distant loci, as in ADP1, is therefore one architecture among several. Operon co-location explains at least part of many coordinated modules, but the adjacency enrichment alone does not say what fraction of module genes are dispersed. [src: fitness_modules]

The [[summaries/functional_dark_matter__REPORT]] supplies the physically clustered counterpart for uncharacterized genes. Of 57,011 dark genes, 30,190 (52.9%) share a predicted operon with an annotated gene under a same-strand, gap < 300 bp heuristic. This supports guilt-by-association hypotheses about function, but an inferred function is not a measured one. [src: functional_dark_matter] The mean annotated-neighbor fraction was 63.6%, below the genome-wide 75% baseline. This is consistent with dark genes co-localizing with other dark genes in unannotated operonic clusters. [src: functional_dark_matter] A cross-species synteny analysis tested 21,011 dark gene–operon partner pairs for neighborhood conservation across up to 47 Fitness Browser organisms; only pairs with ortholog groups assigned to both genes were included. Of these, 17,058 pairs showed conserved neighborhoods in at least one other organism, and 10,150 pairs were conserved in ≥3 organisms. [src: functional_dark_matter] This **refines** the concept: conserved proximity is itself a linkage signal that supports, but does not prove, functional coupling. It also **supports** [[concepts/evidence-triangulation-for-functional-annotation]], because neighborhood conservation and co-fitness are complementary lines of evidence rather than substitutes. [src: functional_dark_matter]

The [[summaries/prophage_ecology__REPORT]] **refines** the boundary between clustered and scattered organization in mobile elements. Co-occurrence analysis across 15 phyla found that packaging, lysis, and lysogenic-regulation genes behave as physically linked functional units. The report proposes a two-tier model of prophage organization: a core backbone plus scattered accessory functions. [src: prophage_ecology] The report calls integration and anti-defense genes 'genomically independent', but this overstates its own results. Its co-occurrence table reports significant co-occurrence in 1/15 tests for integration and 3/11 tests for anti-defense, with mean contig co-localization of 0.420 and 0.281, respectively. These results show weaker co-occurrence and more scattered placement, not demonstrated independence. [src: prophage_ecology] The scattered accessory tier is therefore better treated as a hypothesis of looser linkage than as evidence of dispersed but coupled systems. This bears on [[concepts/phage-defense-syndromes-and-arms-race]]. [src: prophage_ecology]

## Respiratory support and model blind spots

Complex I, or NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreductase, was the largest support subsystem, containing 21/51 quinate-specific genes (41%). [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Flux-balance analysis (FBA), a constraint-based model that predicts steady-state metabolic fluxes, captured 1.76× higher Complex I flux on aromatic substrates than on the comparison condition, with fluxes of 0.55 versus 0.31, but predicted 0% essentiality. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] A total of 30/51 quinate-specific genes had no FBA reaction mappings, including cofactor-supply, iron-acquisition, regulatory, and newly identified Complex I-associated functions. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

The observed phenotype **supports** functional coupling between aromatic catabolism and respiration: 10/13 Complex I operon subunits independently produced quinate-specific growth defects even though the respiratory operon is physically distant from the pca/qui region. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] The contrast between increased modeled flux and 0% predicted essentiality **supports** [[concepts/metabolic-model-gapfilling]] and **refines** [[concepts/gene-essentiality]]: growth-optimizing linear programming can redistribute flux through alternative routes, whereas disruption of a single multi-subunit Complex I can eliminate complex function. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] This remains a mechanistic explanation rather than a definitive demonstration because the model does not establish that Complex I is the only limiting respiratory route under quinate growth. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] The result also connects to [[concepts/circularity-in-metabolic-model-validation]], because the model represents respiratory demand more successfully than the gene-level constraints that make the complex indispensable. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

The [[summaries/respiratory_chain_wiring__REPORT]] **supports** this interpretation by showing that ADP1 respiratory requirements change qualitatively with carbon source: Complex I is specifically required on quinate, whereas no listed respiratory component is specifically required on glucose. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] Complex I growth ratios are 0.37 on quinate, 1.44 on glucose, and 0.49 on acetate. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] The report hypothesizes that quinate-associated Complex I dependence reflects concentrated NADH production from aromatic-ring cleavage and TCA entry rather than total reducing-equivalent yield alone; this interpretation is based on theoretical stoichiometry rather than measured flux distributions. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]

Ortholog-transferred data from the [[entities/kescience-fitnessbrowser]] contained 12,241 entries covering 2,005 genes and 13 conditions. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Complex I orthologs had mean fitness values of -1.35 on aromatic conditions versus -0.77 on comparison conditions, with Mann–Whitney p < 0.0001. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] The largest relative defects occurred on acetate (-1.55) and succinate (-1.39), non-aromatic substrates that also generate high NADH flux through the TCA cycle. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Complex I was reported as dispensable on glucose and lactate, consistent with the hypothesis that an alternative NADH dehydrogenase, NDH-2, compensates under lower NADH flux. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Thus, this **refines** [[concepts/condition-specific-fitness]] by suggesting that coupling follows respiratory NADH load as well as carbon-substrate identity; the transferred data do not isolate aromatic chemistry because organisms differ in respiratory architecture. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

The respiratory-wiring analysis **further refines** this interpretation: after filtering likely false positives, validated NDH-2 was found in 5 of 14 organisms, and its presence did not predict reduced Complex I aromatic deficits (mean deficits −0.297 with NDH-2 versus −0.156 without; p = 0.52). [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] The comparison was underpowered, with only 4 organisms lacking NDH-2, so alternative-dehydrogenase compensation remains unresolved. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] FBA predicted zero flux through NDH-2 and ACIAD3522 because growth optimization favored the ATP-producing Complex I route, potentially missing alternative pathways required by capacity constraints. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] NDH-2 has no growth data, and ACIAD3522 may not be a respiratory NADH dehydrogenase. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]

Proteomics **supports** a flux-based interpretation: Complex I, NDH-2, and ACIAD3522 had similar protein levels under standard growth conditions, with values of 27.6, 27.0, and 26.2, respectively, against a genome median of 26.4. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] The condition dependence may therefore arise from substrate-specific flux and capacity constraints among proteins present simultaneously rather than from a transcriptional switch. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]

## Co-fitness and module-scale evidence

ACIAD3137 (UPF0234) and ACIAD2176 (DUF2280) correlated with Complex I genes at r > 0.98 and were proposed as candidate uncharacterized Complex I accessory factors. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] The discoveries digest reports the same r > 0.98 correlation across 8 growth conditions. It notes that both genes lack FBA reaction mappings and have no assigned metabolic function. It also states that their near-perfect co-fitness with the nuo operon suggests physical or regulatory association with Complex I. [src: discoveries] Within-category correlations were higher than between-category correlations, with mean r = 0.992 for Complex I and r = 0.961 for the aromatic pathway. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] These results **support** the inference that dispersed genes can form coherent functional modules, while correlation does not establish physical interaction or direct membership in the same protein complex. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

Independent component analysis (ICA), a decomposition method for identifying coordinated fitness modules, **supports** this architecture beyond ADP1: across 32 organisms, it identified 1,116 co-regulated modules enriched for core genes, with 86% core genes versus an 81.5% baseline (odds ratio 1.46; p=1.6e-87), and 59% of modules were more than 90% core genes. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] The [[summaries/cofitness_coinheritance__REPORT]] **refines** this result by directly testing module co-inheritance: across 195 ICA modules in 6 organisms, within-module co-occurrence exceeded a prevalence-matched null by delta phi=+0.053; 21/195 (11%) remained significant at q<0.05 after Benjamini-Hochberg FDR (false discovery rate) correction. [src: cofitness_coinheritance] Accessory modules showed mean delta phi +0.108, compared with +0.059 for core modules. [src: cofitness_coinheritance]

Pairwise co-fitness nevertheless provides only weak support for co-inheritance: across 9 organisms, the aggregate delta was +0.003, although 7 of 9 organism-level effects were positive and 8 of 9 were significant at p<0.05; the across-organism Wilcoxon signed-rank test was not significant (W=9, p=0.13). [src: cofitness_coinheritance] Near genomes had higher mean phi than medium-distance genomes (0.102 versus 0.067). [src: cofitness_coinheritance] Thus, module-scale coordination **supports** a distinction between functional architecture and simple pairwise similarity, while phylogenetic variation and prevalence ceilings limit pairwise inference. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis; cofitness_coinheritance]

The co-fitness matrix used only 8 conditions and 8-dimensional growth vectors, or approximately 5 independent dimensions, limiting subsystem resolution. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] The 11 Complex I-associated assignments beyond the core nuo operon may therefore represent indirect connections rather than physical association. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] This caveat links the concept to [[concepts/condition-space-dimensionality]] and [[concepts/cofitness-network-architecture]]. Co-fitness nevertheless **supports** [[concepts/evidence-triangulation-for-functional-annotation]] by narrowing functional hypotheses that can be tested against genomic organization, growth phenotypes, and biochemical requirements. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

## Scope and limitations

PQQ dependence is not exclusively aromatic: PQQ-biosynthesis genes also appeared as glucose-specific in the [[summaries/adp1_deletion_phenotypes__REPORT]] project through association with PQQ-dependent glucose dehydrogenase. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Prior transcriptomic evidence found 4/5 PQQ-biosynthesis genes upregulated on quinate versus succinate. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] This **refines** a pathway-specific interpretation by indicating that auxiliary networks may be condition-dependent rather than unique to one substrate. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

Cross-species transfer is confounded by organism-specific respiratory architectures, so the Complex I result is not definitive for ADP1 beyond its direct fitness observations. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] The co-fitness assignments are provisional because the 11 non-core Complex I associations are based on phenotypic correlation rather than direct physical evidence. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] The COG analysis covered 32 species, and a larger sample could reveal phylum-specific patterns; [[entities/eggnog]] v6 annotations may also differ from original COG assignments. [src: cog_analysis]

The co-inheritance analysis adds corresponding limits: most Fitness Browser genes mapped to core clusters (>95% prevalence), where phi approaches 0 for both cofit and random pairs; the two Ralstonia organisms were excluded because they had zero precomputed co-fitness data; and most species lacked genomes in the far stratum (>0.05 branch distance). [src: cofitness_coinheritance] These constraints mean that stronger module co-inheritance evidence does not establish direct transfer, physical interaction, or universal coupling of dispersed genes. [src: cofitness_coinheritance]

## Open Directions

- Add benzoate, catechol, vanillate, iron limitation, and respiratory-inhibitor conditions to the ADP1 fitness matrix, then test whether the dispersed support network separates aromatic-substrate effects from iron, cofactor, and respiratory-load effects. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]
- Measure Complex I and NDH-2 deletion fitness directly on quinate, glucose, acetate, and succinate, and measure NADH/NAD⁺ ratios to test whether dependency follows aromatic chemistry or NADH-generating load. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network; respiratory_chain_wiring]
- Test ACIAD3137 and ACIAD2176 with protein-protein interaction or co-purification assays to determine whether their r > 0.98 relationships reflect physical Complex I association. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]
- Add PQQ-biosynthesis, iron-homeostasis, and respiratory-chain capacity constraints to the ADP1 FBA model, then ask whether explicit support-system constraints reconcile the 0% predicted Complex I essentiality with defects in 10/13 Complex I operon subunits. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]
- Expand KO-based K03885 and K00330–K00343 searches across 27K species and compare Complex I/NDH-2 co-occurrence and fitness to test whether the ADP1 wiring model generalizes beyond one organism. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]
- Compare Complex I retention across aromatic-degrading species using pangenome data to test whether genomic dispersion and respiratory coupling are conserved beyond ADP1. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]
- Compare the 1,116 ICA modules across organisms with gene-neighborhood data, and extend the analysis to the 195 modules with prevalence-matched co-inheritance statistics, to test whether co-regulated, core-enriched modules are physically dispersed and whether accessory coupling predicts cross-genome retention. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis; cofitness_coinheritance]
- Restrict co-inheritance comparisons to auxiliary-only pairs below 95% prevalence, restore Ralstonia with raw Fitness Browser genefitness data, and use broader phylogenetic strata to distinguish functional coupling from shared ancestry. [src: cofitness_coinheritance]
- Combine COG L, V, and S enrichment with gene-neighborhood, co-fitness, and habitat metadata across additional taxa to test whether mobile, defense, and unknown-function novelty forms distributed ecological modules or remains primarily an annotation-level partition. [src: cog_analysis]
- Intersect the 10,150 dark gene–operon pairs conserved in ≥3 organisms with Fitness Browser co-fitness and module membership. This would separate couplings supported by both conserved proximity and shared fitness from those supported by only one, and test how often functional coupling among dark genes is physically dispersed. [src: functional_dark_matter]
- Re-test prophage integration and anti-defense genes against contig-position null models across the 15 phyla. This would show whether these genes are weakly linked to the packaging/lysis/lysogeny backbone or truly independent of it, and so settle the report's overstated 'genomically independent' wording. [src: prophage_ecology]
