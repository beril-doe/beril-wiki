# Cross-Cohort Portability of Microbiome Classifiers and Metabolomics

Cross-cohort portability is the ability of a microbiome classifier or metabolomics-based analysis to preserve its biological interpretation when applied to samples collected in a different study, laboratory, platform, or feature namespace. Evidence from [discoveries](../summaries/discoveries.md) shows that portability depends strongly on representation: relative-abundance features transferred more robustly than absolute-intensity metabolomics, while cohort effects could overwhelm disease or ecological signals. [^discoveries]

## Evidence

Pooling [hmp2](../entities/hmp2.md) and [franzosa](../entities/franzosa.md) metabolomics on 122 m/z-bridge metabolites produced principal-component analysis (PCA) plus K-means clustering with K=4 that separated completely by cohort rather than diagnosis. [^discoveries] The first principal component explained 79% of variance, and leave-one-study-out (LOSO) adjusted Rand index (ARI) was 0.000 for the metabolomics representation versus 0.113 for taxonomic ecotypes. [^discoveries] These results **support** treating absolute metabolite intensities as cohort-sensitive measurements rather than directly portable biological features. [^discoveries]

Relative-abundance spaces were more cross-cohort-portable because they are unitless and compositional, whereas absolute-intensity metabolomics required explicit batch correction such as ComBat, surrogate variable analysis (SVA), RUV, or quantile normalization. [^discoveries] This **refines** [cross-cohort-microbiome-portability](cross-cohort-microbiome-portability.md) from a classifier-performance problem into a measurement-scale and preprocessing problem. [^discoveries] The [webofmicrobes_explorer__REPORT](../summaries/webofmicrobes_explorer__REPORT.md) provides adjacent evidence that portability also depends on cross-collection feature identity: among 257 identified Web of Microbes compounds, 69 (26.8%) had definitive ModelSEED links, while formula-only matching yielded 107 compounds expanding to 900 candidate molecules. [^webofmicrobes_explorer] This **supports** treating namespace harmonization and match confidence as portability requirements, not merely implementation details. [^webofmicrobes_explorer]

Plant-associated comparative analyses **support** this representation warning at ecological scale. Pangenome-derived plant genera and MGnify rhizosphere genera had only 11.7% Jaccard overlap because isolation metadata and metagenomic detection measure different phenomena; the report therefore shows that apparently similar cohort labels can define materially different feature spaces. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Plant compartment effects also became much smaller after controlling for taxonomic composition: the refined 17-marker analysis gave PERMANOVA R² = 0.071, while db-RDA location-only R² = 0.060, and the earlier R² = 0.527 fell to R² = 0.072 after excluding three genome-rich species per compartment. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] This **supports** the existing conclusion that pooled ecological separation can reflect dominant taxa or sampling composition rather than a portable compartment classifier.

A K=4 linear discriminant analysis–Gaussian mixture model (LDA-GMM) consensus applied to 8,489 curatedMetagenomicData samples identified E0 diverse commensal (n=3,604), E1 Bacteroides2 transitional (n=2,601), E2 Prevotella copri enterotype (n=920), and E3 severe Bacteroides-expanded (n=1,364). [^discoveries] Healthy samples concentrated in E0 plus E2 at 84% combined, whereas Crohn’s disease and ulcerative colitis samples distributed across E1 and E3. [^discoveries] Cross-method validation selected K=4 because ARI was 0.131, within 0.02 of the peak at K=7 with ARI=0.140. [^discoveries]

Portability failures remained after apparently strong pooled validation. A pooled classifier achieved macro one-versus-rest area under the ROC curve (AUC) of 0.80 but only 41% agreement on UC Davis patients. [^discoveries] In that external cohort, `is_ibd=1` was constant and 19/22 patients were predicted as E1. [^discoveries] This **contradicts** the assumption that pooled AUC alone demonstrates transferability and supports external-cohort evaluation as a required validation step. [^discoveries]

Feature namespace also changed the projected biological result. Linear discriminant analysis projection across MetaPhlAn3 and Kaiju namespaces produced plausible Kuehl proportions of 27/42/31% across ecotypes, whereas centered log-ratio (CLR) plus PCA Gaussian-mixture-model projection assigned all 26 Kuehl samples to E3 at confidence greater than 0.97. [^discoveries] The latter projection was associated with Kuehl detecting only 54% of the training species. [^discoveries] This **supports** [environmental-resistome](environmental-resistome.md) and shows that missing feature coverage can create apparently confident but non-portable classifications. [^discoveries] The Web of Microbes–Fitness Browser bridge **reinforces** this concern from a metabolomics perspective: curated matching linked 19 produced metabolites from *Pseudomonas* FW300-N2E3 to Fitness Browser substrate experiments, but ambiguous compound matching and a small organism set limited interpretation. [^webofmicrobes_explorer] Thus, even when a cross-collection bridge exists, its biological portability depends on strain identity and feature-matching quality. [^webofmicrobes_explorer]

Functional annotation coverage adds a related portability constraint. COG (Clusters of Orthologous Groups) analysis across 32 species found that COG annotations covered approximately 70% of genes, while [eggnog](../entities/eggnog.md) v6 annotations may differ from original COG assignments. [^cog_analysis] This **refines** the namespace argument: cross-cohort functional comparisons require harmonized annotation versions and explicit treatment of unassigned genes, not only harmonized taxonomic or compound identifiers. [^cog_analysis] The same analysis found novel or singleton genes enriched in mobile-element and defense categories, including COG L at +10.88% and COG V at +2.83%, whereas core genes were depleted in translation (COG J) by -4.65%; these distributions show that functional representation can also track genome novelty and ecological adaptation rather than a stable portable signal. [^cog_analysis]

The plant analysis **supports** this annotation-schema concern: 12 of 22 marker Pfams were absent from bakta_pfam_domains despite being abundant in InterProScan, and the ten non-zero bakta Pfams returned only 10–35% of InterProScan hit counts. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Its use of InterProScan for secretion-system and cell-wall-degrading-enzyme markers therefore illustrates why apparently identical functional classifiers require version- and database-specific validation. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

A preliminary pooled analysis called *C. scindens* CD-enriched at log₂FC=+2.67, but independent evidence identified that result as a compositional and stratification artifact. [^discoveries] After leakage repair, *C. scindens* was genuinely CD-increased under within-substudy analysis, with pooled CLR-Δ +1.18, false discovery rate (FDR)=1e-8, and 4/4 sign concordance. [^discoveries] The correction **supports** [phenotype-database-coverage-bias](phenotype-database-coverage-bias.md) by showing that pooled associations can change when leakage and study structure are controlled. [^discoveries]

Independent evidence gating reduced the original 33 within-ecotype Tier-A candidates to 3: *Mediterraneibacter gnavus*, *Flavonifractor plautii*, and *Blautia wexleri*, all in E3; 0/18 E1 candidates passed. [^discoveries] This **refines** classifier interpretation by distinguishing reproducible, within-study-supported biomarkers from candidates generated by pooled compositional structure. [^discoveries]

Pathway category definitions were themselves non-portable across analytical specifications. Regex-based categories yielded 3 of 52 CD-up pathways in 7 themes and a structurally degenerate failure, whereas a MetaCyc hierarchy assigned 262/409 pathways to at least one of 12 IBD themes and supported iron/heme acquisition with OR=8.1, FDR=7e-6, and 15/52 CD-up pathways. [^discoveries] The evidence **supports** using curator-validated ontology hierarchies as the primary pathway representation and regex categories as sensitivity analyses. [^discoveries] Plant-associated pathway inference provides a complementary calibration: GapMind predictions agreed with BacDive metabolite-utilization data at 83.1% consistency, but its core-level completeness score was 0% across compartments, showing that threshold choice can remain non-portable even when two resources broadly agree. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

The plant marker analysis also **supports** external validation and continuous rather than categorical interpretation. After stricter marker gating, 878 of 1,115 plant-associated species, or 78.7%, were classified as dual-nature; in a curated panel, all four neutral controls were misclassified, while the continuous pathogenicity ratio distinguished known beneficial from pathogenic species with Mann–Whitney U = 9 and p = 0.027 for N = 7 versus 7. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes] This parallels the IBD result: high apparent class coverage or confidence does not establish biological portability when labels and feature definitions are coarse.

## Interpretation

The combined evidence indicates that cross-cohort portability is conditional rather than automatic: unitless relative-abundance representations, harmonized feature namespaces, leakage repair, within-study analyses, and independent evidence gating improve interpretability, while absolute intensities, incomplete species detection, and pooled compositional comparisons can produce cohort-specific results. [^discoveries] Functional annotation coverage and version differences **further refine** this interpretation by showing that portability can fail when unassigned genes and schema-dependent categories are treated as equivalent features. [^cog_analysis] Cross-collection metabolomics integration **supports** extending this interpretation beyond classifier transfer: Web of Microbes action codes distinguish amplified from de novo metabolites, but absent consumption data, ambiguous compound mappings, and a 2018 single-laboratory snapshot constrain transfer to broader metabolic or strain contexts. [^webofmicrobes_explorer] This conclusion **supports** [multi-omics-integration](multi-omics-integration.md) because microbiome abundance, metabolomics, pathway content, and external validation must be aligned before a cross-cohort biological claim is accepted. [^discoveries]

Portability should therefore be evaluated at several levels: feature-space compatibility, preprocessing stability, classifier agreement, calibration or confidence behavior, within-study replication, and orthogonal biological corroboration. [^discoveries] The findings do not establish that any single normalization method guarantees transfer; rather, they show that representation choice and validation design can materially alter the result. [^discoveries] The plant evidence **refines** this framework by adding label provenance, taxonomic-composition sensitivity, annotation-database coverage, and pathway-threshold calibration as explicit portability checks. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

## Open Directions

- Re-run the metabolomics comparison with ComBat, SVA, RUV, and quantile normalization on the same 122 m/z-bridge metabolites, then test whether cohort separation and LOSO ARI decline without erasing diagnosis-associated structure. [^discoveries]
- Evaluate external-cohort calibration using the pooled classifier on the UC Davis cohort, testing whether recalibration and a non-constant `is_ibd` design improve the 41% agreement and reduce the 19/22 E1 assignments. [^discoveries]
- Harmonize MetaPhlAn3 and Kaiju species namespaces, quantify the effect of restoring the 46% of training species not detected in Kuehl, and test whether projected ecotype proportions remain stable across feature spaces. [^discoveries]
- Reproduce the *C. scindens* and Tier-A candidate analyses with within-substudy CLR effects, leakage-free splits, and independent cohort replication to determine which biomarkers retain sign and effect size. [^discoveries]
- Compare curator-validated MetaCyc hierarchy assignments against regex categories in held-out cohorts, asking whether iron/heme acquisition remains associated with IBD when pathway definitions are fixed before testing. [^discoveries]
- Extend [multi-omics-integration](multi-omics-integration.md) by linking strain-level genomic content, metabolite measurements, and abundance across cohorts, while testing whether species-abundance-mediated and strain-content-mediated signals transfer differently. [^discoveries]
- Re-ingest current Web of Microbes or Northen laboratory data with consumption measurements, then test whether metabolite-production and consumption features transfer across laboratories when compound mappings are restricted to high-confidence identities. [^webofmicrobes_explorer]
- Benchmark COG and [eggnog](../entities/eggnog.md) annotation versions across cohorts, measuring whether harmonized functional namespaces and explicit unassigned-gene handling change classifier stability. [^cog_analysis]
- Test plant-compartment classifiers with taxonomically balanced resampling, InterProScan/bakta harmonization, and held-out host or metagenomic cohorts to determine whether the refined R² = 0.071 signal transfers beyond the original metadata and database boundaries. [^plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
