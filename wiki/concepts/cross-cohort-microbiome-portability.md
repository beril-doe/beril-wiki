# Cross-Cohort Portability of Microbiome Classifiers and Metabolomics

Cross-cohort portability is the ability of a microbiome classifier or metabolomics-based analysis to preserve its biological interpretation when applied to samples collected in a different study, laboratory, platform, or feature namespace. Evidence from [[summaries/discoveries]] shows that portability depends strongly on representation: relative-abundance features transferred more robustly than absolute-intensity metabolomics, while cohort effects could overwhelm disease or ecological signals. [src: discoveries]

## Evidence

Pooling [[entities/hmp2]] and [[entities/franzosa]] metabolomics on 122 m/z-bridge metabolites produced principal-component analysis (PCA) plus K-means clustering with K=4 that separated completely by cohort rather than diagnosis. [src: discoveries] The first principal component explained 79% of variance, and leave-one-study-out (LOSO) adjusted Rand index (ARI) was 0.000 for the metabolomics representation versus 0.113 for taxonomic ecotypes. [src: discoveries] These results **support** treating absolute metabolite intensities as cohort-sensitive measurements rather than directly portable biological features. [src: discoveries]

Relative-abundance spaces were more cross-cohort-portable because they are unitless and compositional, whereas absolute-intensity metabolomics required explicit batch correction such as ComBat, surrogate variable analysis (SVA), RUV, or quantile normalization. [src: discoveries] This **refines** [[concepts/cross-cohort-microbiome-portability]] from a classifier-performance problem into a measurement-scale and preprocessing problem. [src: discoveries]

A K=4 linear discriminant analysis–Gaussian mixture model (LDA-GMM) consensus applied to 8,489 curatedMetagenomicData samples identified E0 diverse commensal (n=3,604), E1 Bacteroides2 transitional (n=2,601), E2 Prevotella copri enterotype (n=920), and E3 severe Bacteroides-expanded (n=1,364). [src: discoveries] Healthy samples concentrated in E0 plus E2 at 84% combined, whereas Crohn’s disease and ulcerative colitis samples distributed across E1 and E3. [src: discoveries] Cross-method validation selected K=4 because ARI was 0.131, within 0.02 of the peak at K=7 with ARI=0.140. [src: discoveries]

Portability failures remained after apparently strong pooled validation. A pooled classifier achieved macro one-versus-rest area under the ROC curve (AUC) of 0.80 but only 41% agreement on UC Davis patients. [src: discoveries] In that external cohort, `is_ibd=1` was constant and 19/22 patients were predicted as E1. [src: discoveries] This **contradicts** the assumption that pooled AUC alone demonstrates transferability and supports external-cohort evaluation as a required validation step. [src: discoveries]

Feature namespace also changed the projected biological result. Linear discriminant analysis projection across MetaPhlAn3 and Kaiju namespaces produced plausible Kuehl proportions of 27/42/31% across ecotypes, whereas centered log-ratio (CLR) plus PCA Gaussian-mixture-model projection assigned all 26 Kuehl samples to E3 at confidence greater than 0.97. [src: discoveries] The latter projection was associated with Kuehl detecting only 54% of the training species. [src: discoveries] This **supports** [[concepts/environmental-resistome]] and shows that missing feature coverage can create apparently confident but non-portable classifications. [src: discoveries]

A preliminary pooled analysis called *C. scindens* CD-enriched at log₂FC=+2.67, but independent evidence identified that result as a compositional and stratification artifact. [src: discoveries] After leakage repair, *C. scindens* was genuinely CD-increased under within-substudy analysis, with pooled CLR-Δ +1.18, false discovery rate (FDR)=1e-8, and 4/4 sign concordance. [src: discoveries] The correction **supports** [[concepts/phenotype-database-coverage-bias]] by showing that pooled associations can change when leakage and study structure are controlled. [src: discoveries]

Independent evidence gating reduced the original 33 within-ecotype Tier-A candidates to 3: *Mediterraneibacter gnavus*, *Flavonifractor plautii*, and *Blautia wexlerae*, all in E3; 0/18 E1 candidates passed. [src: discoveries] This **refines** classifier interpretation by distinguishing reproducible, within-study-supported biomarkers from candidates generated by pooled compositional structure. [src: discoveries]

Pathway category definitions were themselves non-portable across analytical specifications. Regex-based categories yielded 3 of 52 CD-up pathways in 7 themes and a structurally degenerate failure, whereas a MetaCyc hierarchy assigned 262/409 pathways to at least one of 12 IBD themes and supported iron/heme acquisition with OR=8.1, FDR=7e-6, and 15/52 CD-up pathways. [src: discoveries] The evidence **supports** using curator-validated ontology hierarchies as the primary pathway representation and regex categories as sensitivity analyses. [src: discoveries]

## Interpretation

The combined evidence indicates that cross-cohort portability is conditional rather than automatic: unitless relative-abundance representations, harmonized feature namespaces, leakage repair, within-study analyses, and independent evidence gating improve interpretability, while absolute intensities, incomplete species detection, and pooled compositional comparisons can produce cohort-specific results. [src: discoveries] This conclusion **supports** [[concepts/multi-omics-integration]] because microbiome abundance, metabolomics, pathway content, and external validation must be aligned before a cross-cohort biological claim is accepted. [src: discoveries]

Portability should therefore be evaluated at several levels: feature-space compatibility, preprocessing stability, classifier agreement, calibration or confidence behavior, within-study replication, and orthogonal biological corroboration. [src: discoveries] The findings do not establish that any single normalization method guarantees transfer; rather, they show that representation choice and validation design can materially alter the result. [src: discoveries]

## Open Directions

- Re-run the metabolomics comparison with ComBat, SVA, RUV, and quantile normalization on the same 122 m/z-bridge metabolites, then test whether cohort separation and LOSO ARI decline without erasing diagnosis-associated structure. [src: discoveries]
- Evaluate external-cohort calibration using the pooled classifier on the UC Davis cohort, testing whether recalibration and a non-constant `is_ibd` design improve the 41% agreement and reduce the 19/22 E1 assignments. [src: discoveries]
- Harmonize MetaPhlAn3 and Kaiju species namespaces, quantify the effect of restoring the 46% of training species not detected in Kuehl, and test whether projected ecotype proportions remain stable across feature spaces. [src: discoveries]
- Reproduce the *C. scindens* and Tier-A candidate analyses with within-substudy CLR effects, leakage-free splits, and independent cohort replication to determine which biomarkers retain sign and effect size. [src: discoveries]
- Compare curator-validated MetaCyc hierarchy assignments against regex categories in held-out cohorts, asking whether iron/heme acquisition remains associated with IBD when pathway definitions are fixed before testing. [src: discoveries]
- Extend [[concepts/multi-omics-integration]] by linking strain-level genomic content, metabolite measurements, and abundance across cohorts, while testing whether species-abundance-mediated and strain-content-mediated signals transfer differently. [src: discoveries]
