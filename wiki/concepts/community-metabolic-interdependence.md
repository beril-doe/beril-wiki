# Community Metabolic Interdependence and Black Queen Predictions

Community metabolic interdependence is the hypothesis that organisms can rely on metabolites produced by neighboring organisms, allowing loss or reduction of otherwise costly biosynthetic functions. [src: discoveries] The Black Queen prediction tested here is that amino-acid biosynthetic capacity and environmental metabolite pools should show coordinated patterns consistent with community-level provisioning rather than uniformly independent synthesis. [src: discoveries] This page synthesizes evidence from [[summaries/discoveries]], [[summaries/nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology__REPORT]], and [[summaries/metabolic_capability_dependency__REPORT]], now extended by [[summaries/pathway_capability_dependency__REPORT]], and connects it to [[concepts/metabolic-model-gapfilling]] and [[concepts/multi-omics-integration]].

## Evidence for Black Queen-like structure

Community-scale NMDC metabolomics supported the Black Queen prediction for amino-acid pathways: 11 of 13 pathways trended in the predicted direction, with a binomial sign-test p=0.011. [src: discoveries] The NMDC analysis **supports** this result using 220 samples and 80 GapMind pathways, with 11 of 13 testable amino-acid pathways showing negative Spearman correlations between community pathway completeness and ambient amino-acid intensity; the sign test was p = 0.011. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] The strongest reported associations were for [[entities/leucine]], which had r=-0.390, q=0.022, n=62, and [[entities/arginine]], which had r=-0.297, q=0.049, n=80. [src: discoveries] The new analysis reports the same values and identifies these as the two associations surviving Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] [[entities/methionine]] had r=-0.496 but q=0.117 with n=18, so its direction was consistent with the prediction but did not meet the reported significance threshold. [src: discoveries]

The result was not universal across metabolites: tyrosine was an anti-Black-Queen outlier with r=+0.42. [src: discoveries] The NMDC analysis refines this value as r = +0.419 and likewise finds no significant isoleucine association (r = −0.057, q = 0.823, n = 18). [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] Thus, the dataset supports a graded, pathway-specific interpretation rather than the claim that all amino-acid biosynthesis follows one community-dependency rule. [src: discoveries] The negative associations therefore remain evidence compatible with community provisioning, not direct proof that organisms exchange the measured metabolites. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

A complementary organism-level analysis **supports** a community-context interpretation, but not direct exchange: among 1,695 complete pathway-organism pairs from 48 organisms, 267 (15.8%) were classified as fitness-neutral latent capabilities, while 881 (51.9%) were active dependencies. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] Latent capabilities were especially common for carbon-source utilization pathways (217 of 892, 24.3%) compared with amino-acid biosynthesis (48 of 735, 6.5%), and latent-capability rate correlated positively with pangenome openness at the clade level (Spearman ρ = 0.69, p = 0.0004, n = 22 clades). [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] This **refines** the Black Queen prediction: genome dynamics and environmental context are consistent with selective sharing or reduced autonomous synthesis, but pathway presence plus fitness neutrality does not identify the producer, recipient, or exchanged metabolite.

The new pathway-capability analysis **supports** the condition-dependent interpretation: in a separate set of 161 organism-pathway combinations across 7 model bacteria, all 66 pairs classified as Latent Capability became fitness-important under at least one condition type, especially nitrogen limitation, stress, or carbon limitation. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] However, this result is partly sensitive to applying a median-based importance threshold within condition subsets, so it does not by itself establish that every latent capability is biologically shared or conditionally required. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

## Carbon metabolism dominates community differentiation

Carbon utilization, rather than amino-acid biosynthesis, dominated metabolic differentiation in the analyzed communities. [src: discoveries] Principal-component analysis (PCA), an ordination method that summarizes major variance axes, found that PC1 explained 49.4% of variance and separated Soil from Freshwater at p<0.0001. [src: discoveries] Amino-acid pathways loaded more strongly on PC2, which explained 16.6%. [src: discoveries] The NMDC analysis **supports** this interpretation: PC1 explained 49.4% and PC2 16.6%, while carbon pathways including glucuronate, fumarate, succinate, cellobiose, and galactose had near-uniform positive PC1 loadings. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

This pattern **refines** the Black Queen interpretation: amino-acid associations are compatible with metabolite interdependence, but they explain less of the overall community metabolic structure than carbon-use differences in this analysis. [src: discoveries] The result therefore favors testing dependency within environmental and metabolic contexts rather than treating amino-acid exchange as the primary determinant of all community differentiation. [src: discoveries] The NMDC analysis further found significant ecosystem-type differences for 17 of 18 amino-acid pathways, but it could not determine whether these differences reflected habitat physicochemistry, taxonomic composition, or both. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

## Evidence limitations and tensions

The metabolomics dataset was dominated by one study, with 125 of 131 samples (95%) coming from `nmdc:sty-11-r2h77870`. [src: discoveries] This concentration limits how broadly the observed correlations can be generalized across environments and studies. [src: discoveries] The NMDC analysis **supports** this limitation: all 62 leucine samples came from that first study, although the arginine result was not driven by the minor study. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

Only approximately 2% of compounds had KEGG identifiers, and substring matching risked collisions such as leucine versus isoleucine. [src: discoveries] Three amino-acid pathways—cysteine, histidine, and lysine—were untestable because the relevant compounds were absent. [src: discoveries] The NMDC analysis confirms that the reported run corrected an isoleucine/leucine mapping collision, while cysteine, histidine, and lysine remained untestable. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] These limitations make the 11/13 directional result supportive but coverage-limited, because the untested pathways cannot distinguish universal from pathway-specific Black Queen behavior. [src: discoveries]

The positive tyrosine association is a direct tension with the simple prediction that community provisioning should produce a common negative association pattern across amino acids. [src: discoveries] The corpus does not establish whether this outlier reflects ecological specialization, measurement or mapping limitations, or a genuinely different exchange regime. [src: discoveries] The NMDC analysis **refines** that uncertainty by noting that GapMind measures genomic potential rather than expression and that abiotic measurements were unavailable: pH, temperature, and total organic carbon were all NaN in the 174-sample analysis matrix. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

There is also a tension between the small condition-specific study and the broader capability-dependency analysis. Across 7 Fitness Browser organisms and 23 GapMind pathways, 41.0% of pathway-organism pairs were Latent Capabilities, and all Latent Capabilities became fitness-important under condition-specific analyses. [src: discoveries] By contrast, the broader analysis classified 267 of 1,695 complete pairs (15.8%) as latent under its stated fitness thresholds, with the latent fraction remaining 4.7%–21.1% across 16 threshold combinations. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] The new 161-pair analysis **supports** the first study's condition-dependent result, but its 66 latent pairs and threshold caveat do not resolve the disagreement because it uses another organism set, pathway set, and median-based classification. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] The tension is whether latent capability is generally a condition-dependent dependency or whether a substantial fraction remains fitness-neutral across tested conditions.

Pathway-level conservation also did not distinguish latent capabilities from active dependencies: mean conservation was 0.869 for 248 latent pathways versus 0.829 for 755 active dependencies, with Mann–Whitney U p = 0.94 for active > latent and rank-biserial r = 0.052. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] This **refines** a simple gene-loss version of the Black Queen model: the reported signal appeared instead in the positive association between latent-capability rate and pangenome openness at the clade level. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]

## Relationship to pathway-capability evidence

The community metabolomics result should be interpreted alongside pathway-capability and fitness evidence rather than as a standalone demonstration of metabolite exchange. [src: discoveries] Across 7 Fitness Browser organisms and 23 GapMind pathways, 35.4% of pathway-organism pairs were Active Dependencies, 41.0% were Latent Capabilities, 14.9% were Incomplete but Important, and 8.7% were Missing. [src: discoveries] All Latent Capabilities became fitness-important under condition-specific analyses. [src: discoveries]

These results **support** the distinction between genomic capability and realized dependency: a pathway may be present yet become important only under particular environmental conditions. [src: discoveries] In FW300-N2E3, all 13 Web of Microbes metabolites that could be mapped to GapMind had complete pathways, and all 13 showed growth in Fitness Browser experiments. [src: discoveries] The overall 94% four-database concordance was structurally driven, however: Fitness Browser was 21/21, GapMind was 13/13, and BacDive was only 3/7. [src: discoveries] This means apparent agreement across resources cannot by itself establish ecological metabolite exchange. [src: discoveries] The NMDC results **reinforce** this caution because pathway completeness was compared with metabolite intensity, not pathway expression or experimentally demonstrated exchange. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

The broader capability-dependency study **supports** this distinction across more organisms: carbon pathways were more often complete but fitness-neutral than amino-acid biosynthesis, while only 48 organisms had fitness measurements among 293,000 genomes with pathway predictions. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] Its ecological clustering result is likewise observational: all 10 target species formed metabolic clusters, but environment-cluster associations were significant only for *Salmonella enterica* and *Phenylobacterium* sp., and explicit phylogenetic correction was not performed. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]

The new analysis **extends** this evidence from latent fitness states to pathway variation: among 2,810 GTDB species, variable pathway count was positively associated with pangenome openness after controlling for genome count (partial Spearman ρ=0.530, p=2.83e-203). [src: pathway_capability_dependency] It also found that ecotype count was associated with openness (partial ρ=0.322, p=8.0e-07), supporting a link between genome fluidity and within-species metabolic diversity rather than proving metabolite sharing. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] Accessory-dependent completeness was strongest for several amino-acid biosynthesis routes, including exact core-versus-all gaps of 0.146 for leucine and valine and 0.141–0.140 for arginine, lysine, and threonine; this **supports** a Black Queen-compatible hypothesis of community-level provisioning but does not demonstrate exchange. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

## Open Directions

- Reanalyze the NMDC metabolomics samples with study-stratified correlations and leave-one-study-out validation to test whether the 11/13 directional pattern persists after reducing the influence of `nmdc:sty-11-r2h77870`, which contributed 125/131 samples (95%). [src: discoveries]
- Replace substring metabolite matching with identifier-validated KEGG or equivalent mappings, then retest leucine, isoleucine, and the previously untestable cysteine, histidine, and lysine pathways to determine whether the Black Queen pattern is mapping-sensitive. [src: discoveries]
- Combine sample-level metabolite abundances with genome-resolved pathway presence and condition-specific fitness measurements to test whether negative metabolite associations identify producers, consumers, or both. [src: discoveries]
- Test the tyrosine outlier using independent cohorts and targeted metabolomics to distinguish a real anti-Black-Queen relationship from cohort, annotation, or measurement effects. [src: discoveries]
- Compare pathway presence, pathway completeness, and measured metabolite pools across the 7 Fitness Browser organisms and 23 GapMind pathways to ask when Latent Capabilities become realized community dependencies. [src: discoveries]
- Add pH, temperature, and total organic carbon to partial-correlation or mixed-effects models, and pair metabolomics with metatranscriptomics, to separate abiotic confounding and genomic potential from expressed biosynthetic activity. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]
- Obtain metabolomics for the 33 Freshwater samples and resolve the 61 Unknown-ecosystem samples through NMDC environmental annotations to test whether the Black Queen pattern generalizes beyond the predominantly soil H1 cohort. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]
- Reconcile the 7-organism condition-specific result with the 48-organism threshold analysis by applying matched pathway definitions and condition-stratified fitness models, testing whether latent capabilities remain neutral or become important under community-relevant media. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]
- Link clade-level pangenome openness to genome-resolved metabolite production and consumption, testing whether the ρ = 0.69 association reflects actual exchange networks rather than shared phylogeny or sampling structure. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]
- Validate the pathway-openness and ecotype-openness associations with phylogenetically independent models and genome-resolved metabolite production or consumption, testing whether accessory-dependent amino-acid pathways correspond to realized exchange. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]
