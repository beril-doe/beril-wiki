# BERIL Knowledge Wiki

Most of these projects analysed data already in the [KBase Data Lakehouse](https://hub.berdl.kbase.us); a few brought their own. See [[about|About This Wiki]] for what that means for citing anything here.

The BERIL Research Observatory's AI agents conducted every project in this corpus. Most of them analysed data already in the KBase Data Lakehouse: bacterial fitness experiments, pangenomes, genome annotations, amplicon surveys, metabolic models, and the metadata attached to them. The projects ask questions, run analyses, and write up what the data support and where the data run out. The corpus records both the findings and the failures, including the cases where an identifier mapping broke, a cohort turned out to be unrepresentative, or an annotation category hid the biology it was supposed to expose.

Topics are the entry points. Each topic gathers the concepts and projects that speak to one research problem, states what the corpus actually shows about it, and points to the evidence. Read a topic first when you want to know what this body of work has established on a subject. The reference layers sit underneath: concepts define recurring ideas and methods, entities cover the organisms, genes, datasets, tools, and databases the projects touch, and summaries pull cross-project patterns into two digests, one for discoveries and one for pitfalls.

Individual project reports hold the primary evidence. Every claim in a topic or concept page traces back to one or more reports, and the reports keep their original scope, including analyses that produced negative or ambiguous results. If you want the reasoning behind a statement, follow the link to the report rather than trusting the summary layer alone.

## Topics

- [[topics/biological-evidence-integration|Biological Evidence Integration]] (12 concepts): the disciplined combination of molecular measurements, phenotypes, ecological observations, metadata, and computational predictions to support a biological claim, tested here by projects that connect genome content to fitness, metabolism, community composition, environmental context, and therapeutic targeting.
- [[topics/cofitness-network-modules|Cofitness Network Modules]] (8 concepts): groups of genes whose fitness profiles vary together across conditions, studied through broad bacterial Fitness Browser datasets, independent component analysis, pangenome comparisons, and condition-specific patterns that operons and annotations do not capture.
- [[topics/ecological-genomic-inference-biases|Ecological Genomic Inference Biases]] (12 concepts): how representation shapes every stage that links environment, organismal traits, and genome content, from which organisms get sampled and which metadata get recorded to which taxa and functions databases can recognize.
- [[topics/functional-annotation-gaps|Functional Annotation Gaps]] (8 concepts): the places where a genome sequence, protein family, reaction, phenotype, or ecological signal cannot yet be tied to a defensible function, treated as a layered problem across sequence space, identifiers, experimental coverage, literature attention, and model assumptions.
- [[topics/gene-fitness-conservation-dynamics|Gene Fitness-Conservation Dynamics]] (12 concepts): how measured effects of disrupting a gene relate to its retention across bacterial genomes, using RB-TnSeq, complete-gene knockouts, core and accessory pangenome classifications, functional annotations, and environmental comparisons.
- [[topics/metabolic-capability-inference|Metabolic Capability Inference]] (8 concepts): estimating what organisms and communities can do from genome content, pathway annotations, fitness measurements, growth assays, metabolomics, and ecological context, and separating that potential from what they require, express, or perform in situ.
- [[topics/resistance-ecology-and-mobility|Resistance Ecology and Mobility]] (9 concepts): how resistance traits distribute across environments, lineages, strains, and genomic compartments, with pangenome surveys describing where resistance genes occur, fitness experiments showing when they matter, and genomic-context analyses tracking how they move.
- [[topics/schema-dependent-functional-inference|Schema-Dependent Functional Inference]] (4 concepts): translating genes, pathways, metabolites, phenotypes, and ecological observations into comparable categories, and how ontology choice, annotation system, identifier mapping, composite labels, feature namespace, and cohort representation change the result.
- [[topics/subsurface-ecological-genomics|Subsurface Ecological Genomics]] (3 concepts): linking community structure, recovered genomes, functional markers, and environmental context in sediments, groundwater, and confined clay, through 16S surveys of hydrogeological zones and comparative genome analyses of subsurface Bacillota_B.
