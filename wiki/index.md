# BERIL Knowledge Wiki

Most of these projects analysed data already in the [KBase Data Lakehouse](https://hub.berdl.kbase.us); a few brought their own. See [[about|About This Wiki]] for what that means for citing anything here.

The BERIL Research Observatory conducts microbial-biology research with AI over the KBase Data Lakehouse, a shared catalog of genomes, fitness assays, growth measurements, environmental metadata, and metabolic models. An AI system runs each project against that catalog and writes the report it produces. This wiki compiles those reports into a single cross-linked reference. The corpus covers bacterial gene fitness and essentiality, pangenome structure, metabolic capability, resistance and defense repertoires, subsurface and host-associated communities, and the statistical and infrastructural problems that show up when you ask all of those questions against the same catalog.

Topics are the entry points. Each topic page gathers the concepts that argue a single question across projects, states where the evidence agrees, and names the disagreements it cannot settle. Under the topics sit three reference layers. Concept pages carry the cross-project arguments, with every factual claim cited to the projects that back it and every disagreement recorded under a Tensions heading. Entity pages collect what the corpus says about one named thing: an organism, a gene or pathway, a compound, a method, a dataset. Summary pages hold one project report each, with a Slots Into section pointing back at the concepts that report feeds.

Read the wiki claim-first rather than project-first. A single project report is a snapshot of one analysis under one set of choices, and much of what this corpus learned comes from projects that re-ran the same question under different coverage, different annotation pipelines, or a different perturbation assay. The concept and topic pages are where those comparisons live. Numbers on every page are copied from their source reports without rounding or reconciliation, so where two projects report conflicting values you will see both.

## Topics

- [[topics/gene-fitness-landscapes-and-cofitness-modules|Gene Fitness Landscapes and Cofitness Modules]] (11 concepts). A gene fitness landscape is the matrix of growth effects obtained when every gene in a genome is disrupted and the resulting mutant pool is grown across many conditions. Most of this corpus's raw signal comes from RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing, a pooled assay that scores each insertion mutant's contribution to growth) collected in the Fitness Browser, and two derived objects dominate.
- [[topics/gene-essentiality-and-perturbation-assay-validity|Gene Essentiality and Perturbation-Assay Validity]] (6 concepts). "Essential" is the most load-bearing word in bacterial functional genomics: it decides which genes get annotated as indispensable, which become drug targets, and which are filtered out of screens because no mutant can be made. This corpus can interrogate the word directly, because several projects place two or more independent perturbation assays on the same genome.
- [[topics/laboratory-fitness-versus-natural-selection-and-gene-conservation|Laboratory Fitness Versus Natural Selection and Gene Conservation]] (6 concepts). Transposon-mutant fitness assays tell you what a gene costs its host in a flask; pangenome conservation tells you which genes lineages have kept across evolutionary time. The two are routinely used as if they were the same measurement of importance. Eleven projects here work with random barcode transposon sequencing (RB-TnSeq, a pooled assay that scores each insertion mutant's contribution to growth), which puts the corpus in an unusually good position to test that equivalence.
- [[topics/pangenome-structure-and-genome-evolution|Pangenome Structure and Genome Evolution]] (9 concepts). A pangenome is the union of gene families found across all sequenced genomes of a species: a core portion present in essentially every member genome and an accessory portion present in only some. How that partition is drawn, and what it predicts about function, fitness, ecology, and transfer, is one of the load-bearing questions of comparative microbial genomics.
- [[topics/ecotypes-and-environmental-gene-content-differentiation|Ecotypes and Environmental Gene-Content Differentiation]] (6 concepts). An ecotype is a population within a named species that is genetically and ecologically distinct, in comparative genomics usually a cluster of genomes that share accessory gene content and, by hypothesis, a habitat. This corpus attacks the ecotype concept empirically from both ends, asking first whether gene-content clusters exist and are functionally coherent inside species.
- [[topics/metabolic-traits-auxotrophy-and-community-dependence|Metabolic Traits, Auxotrophy, and Community Dependence]] (9 concepts). Whether a microbe can make its own building blocks, or must obtain them from neighbours, is one of the oldest questions in microbial physiology and one of the hardest to answer from sequence alone. This corpus attacks it from four directions at once, among them computational pathway calls across hundreds of thousands of genomes and pooled mutant-fitness assays that measure what a gene's loss actually costs.
- [[topics/metabolic-models-and-pathway-level-evidence|Metabolic Models and Pathway-Level Evidence]] (6 concepts). Genome-scale metabolic models and pathway-completeness predictors are the corpus's main route from a genome sequence to a statement about what an organism can do. Two tool families dominate: constraint-based models simulated with flux balance analysis (FBA, prediction of feasible steady-state reaction rates from a stoichiometric reconstruction, usually under a growth objective), and GapMind, a homology-based pathway caller.
- [[topics/functional-dark-matter-and-annotation-resolution|Functional Dark Matter and Annotation Resolution]] (9 concepts). A large fraction of bacterial genes carry no confident functional label. In this corpus that fraction is not a fixed biological quantity but a measurement: it moves when the annotation pipeline changes, when the reference database is swapped, when the ontology used to bin functions is redefined, and when a different literature or structural resource is consulted.
- [[topics/resistance-defense-systems-and-mobile-elements|Resistance, Defense Systems, and Mobile Elements]] (8 concepts). Bacteria carry overlapping protective repertoires: antimicrobial-resistance (AMR) genes, metal-tolerance determinants, anti-phage defense systems, and the prophages and resistance islands that move or co-inherit them. This corpus approaches the topic by joining a pan-bacterial pangenome, the union of gene families across the genomes assigned to a species split into core families and accessory ones, to those repertoires.
- [[topics/sampling-coverage-and-statistical-confounding|Sampling Coverage and Statistical Confounding]] (10 concepts). Every comparative claim in this corpus, that an environment shapes gene content, that a phenotype predicts metal tolerance, that a biome is genomically under-explored, is a claim about a sample, not about microbial life. Many projects re-ran the same question under different coverage, resolution, and validation regimes, which makes that gap examinable.
- [[topics/data-infrastructure-provenance-and-research-practice|Data Infrastructure, Provenance, and Research Practice]] (6 concepts). Every biological claim in this corpus comes from querying a shared, multi-tenant catalog, the KBase Data Lakehouse, joining tables built by different programs for different purposes, then interpreting the result. This topic covers what the corpus learned about that substrate: how catalog contents are distributed across owners, and why a resource name is unreliable evidence of what a resource holds.
- [[topics/translational-microbiome-and-phage-therapeutics|Translational Microbiome and Phage Therapeutics]] (3 concepts). This hub covers the part of the corpus that moves from describing microbial communities to designing interventions in them: defined commensal consortia meant to suppress a pathogen, and lytic-phage cocktails meant to remove specific gut pathobionts from specific patients. Two projects supply the primary design evidence.
- [[topics/subsurface-community-ecology|Subsurface Community Ecology]] (3 concepts). The deep subsurface, meaning saturated sediments, groundwater, and clay-confined rock formations, hosts microbial communities that are hard to sample, slow to grow, and thinly represented in genome collections. This hub gathers what the BERIL corpus, built over the KBase Data Lakehouse, can say about how those communities are organized, starting with which physical variables structure them.
