# BERIL Knowledge Wiki

Most of these projects analysed data already in the [KBase Data Lakehouse](https://hub.berdl.kbase.us); a few brought their own. See [[about|About This Wiki]] for what that means for citing anything here.

The BERIL Research Observatory runs AI-conducted microbial-biology research over the KBase Data Lakehouse. This wiki compiles what came out of it: 73 project reports, each a self-contained investigation, plus two cross-project digests, one of discoveries and one of pitfalls. The projects ask questions about gene fitness, pangenome structure, metabolic capability, community assembly and the data infrastructure underneath all of it, and they answer those questions against shared collections of genomes, reads and strains, mutant fitness matrices, and culture-collection records.

Topics are the entry points. Each of the ten topic hubs below states a question the corpus returns to and gathers the concept pages that argue about it, including the places where projects disagree. Start at a topic, follow it to the concepts, and follow the concepts to the evidence.

Concepts, entities and summaries are the reference layers beneath the topics. A concept page argues a claim across projects and cites each source by project id. An entity page collects what the corpus says about one named thing, such as an organism, a gene, a compound, a method or a dataset. A summary page condenses a single project report and lists the concepts its findings feed. Every factual statement carries the project it came from, so you can always walk back to the report that produced it.

## Topics

- [[topics/gene-fitness-landscapes-and-cofitness-modules|Gene Fitness Landscapes and Cofitness Modules]] (11 concepts): What a genome-wide matrix of mutant growth effects measures, and what it means when two genes move together across conditions.
- [[topics/gene-essentiality-and-perturbation-assay-validity|Gene Essentiality and Perturbation-Assay Validity]] (6 concepts): Essentiality looks binary, but every number here comes from an assay with its own blind spots; six concepts and nine conflict pages work out what a perturbation collection can and cannot see.
- [[topics/pangenome-structure-and-genome-evolution|Pangenome Structure and Genome Evolution]] (9 concepts): What the core/accessory split actually partitions, which functions land on each side, and how genes move between lineages.
- [[topics/metabolic-traits-auxotrophy-and-community-dependence|Metabolic Traits, Auxotrophy, and Community Dependence]] (9 concepts): Whether a microbe makes its own metabolites or scavenges them from neighbours, attacked at once by computational pathway-completeness calls and experimental gene-fitness measurements.
- [[topics/metabolic-models-and-pathway-level-evidence|Metabolic Models and Pathway-Level Evidence]] (6 concepts): Genome-scale models promise to turn a sequence into a capability, and the measured phenotypes keep answering a different question than the predictions ask.
- [[topics/functional-dark-matter-and-annotation-resolution|Functional Dark Matter and Annotation Resolution]] (9 concepts): 57,011 of 228,709 bacterial protein-coding genes across 48 Fitness Browser organisms (24.9%) carry no usable functional label, and this hub treats that gap as a question of how finely a pipeline, database or assay can localize function.
- [[topics/sampling-coverage-and-statistical-confounding|Sampling Coverage and Statistical Confounding]] (10 concepts): Every comparative claim rests on genomes, reads or strains that someone chose to sequence, and this hub covers what happens when that choice drives the result instead of biology.
- [[topics/data-infrastructure-provenance-and-research-practice|Data Infrastructure, Provenance, and Research Practice]] (6 concepts): How data are found, how origin and currency are established, how records from separate owners are joined, and how a finished claim is attacked before anyone believes it.
- [[topics/translational-microbiome-and-phage-therapeutics|Translational Microbiome and Phage Therapeutics]] (3 concepts): Turning ecological and host-range measurements into an intervention for a named patient: which organism, which agent, at what point in the disease course, on what evidence.
- [[topics/subsurface-community-ecology|Subsurface Community Ecology]] (3 concepts): What organizes microbial communities below the rooting zone, and how much of a community's present composition was set by its past rather than its current environment.
