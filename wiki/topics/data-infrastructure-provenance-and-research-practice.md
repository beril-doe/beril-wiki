# Data Infrastructure, Provenance, and Research Practice

Every biological claim in this corpus comes from querying a shared, multi-tenant catalog — the KBase Data Lakehouse — joining tables built by different programs for different purposes, then interpreting the result. This topic covers what the corpus learned about that substrate: how catalog contents are distributed across owners, why a resource name is unreliable evidence of what a resource holds, what separates a join that *executes* from one that *means* something, and which review practices catch errors ordinary review misses. Several projects took the infrastructure as their object of study — a catalog-wide inventory, a name-provenance audit, resource explorations, and two cross-project digests — while dozens more contributed failure reports from doing science on top of it.

## Literature Context

Published work on research data infrastructure converges on one diagnosis: heterogeneity is the binding constraint, and provenance capture is the remedy. ENCODE met a catalog of more than 19,000 functional genomics experiments across more than 1,000 cell lines and tissues with uniform processing pipelines that capture all data files, reference genome versions, software versions, and parameters; standardization there yields "comparable results from different ENCODE collections - a prerequisite for successful integrative analyses" ([PMID 37066421](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37066421/), [PMID 37503119](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37503119/)). LAGOS reached the same conclusion in ecology, pairing geospatial data on ~50,000 lakes with water-quality data compiled from ~100 individual datasets for a subset of ~10,000 lakes, and naming heterogeneity of data, formats, and metadata as the largest challenge — one requiring manual input from experts and explicit documentation of data provenance ([PMID 26140212](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26140212/)). Smaller curation efforts echo it: GdbMTB curates 365 magnetotactic-bacteria genomes previously scattered across multiple databases with inconsistent quality profiles and incomplete metadata, offering traceable per-genome provenance ([PMID 41537192](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41537192/)); MetaMP harmonizes 4,089 membrane-protein structures from resources differing in "scope, curation criteria, and identifier conventions" ([PMID 42626122](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42626122/)).

This corpus is consistent with that literature but sits on the other side of it. All four published efforts describe *curated* resources — harmonized deliberately, documented by their builders. The findings here describe an uncurated, multi-tenant catalog of 1,740 tables across 17 tenants and 10 funding programs, where the analyst rather than a coordination center must reconstruct provenance at query time. The identifier collisions reported — an ENIGMA strain label matching a taxonomically unrelated clinical species in GTDB, 12 of 32 pangenome linkages wrong at genus level, formula-only chemical matches expanding to hundreds of candidate molecules — are exactly the identifier-convention problem MetaMP names ([PMID 42626122](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42626122/)), observed downstream where no harmonization layer exists. [[concepts/taxonomic-nomenclature-reconciliation]]'s accession-first joining and recorded taxonomy versions is the same discipline ENCODE enforces by pinning reference genome and software versions ([PMID 37066421](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37066421/)).

Three contributions extend that frame. [[concepts/cross-tenant-data-bridging]]'s four-state ladder — shared keys, overlapping values, an executable join, a scientifically interpretable result — makes explicit a distinction curated resources assume away by construction; MetaMP's 121 broad-group conflicts (2.96% of 4,089 harmonized entries), where under strict label matching OPM agreed with expert annotations for 96 of 121 (79.34%) while under benchmark-aware evaluation MetaMP led at 88.43% against OPM's 80.99%, is the nearest published analogue, but concerns annotation disagreement rather than whether a join means anything ([PMID 42626122](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42626122/)). [[concepts/data-landscape-ownership-and-coverage-bias]] treats ownership and funding history as a measurable coverage variable — entropy differences between tenants covering 5 versus 10 topics — which no candidate paper attempts. And [[concepts/adversarial-research-quality-assurance]] addresses a failure class outside this literature: uniform pipelines guard against processing inconsistency ([PMID 37066421](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37066421/)) but not feature leakage, misapplied multiple-testing families, or hijacked and hallucinated citations. That adversarial review found 5 critical and 6 important issues where standard review reported none, and that leakage repair cut independent Tier-A candidates from 33 to 3, is novel relative to these papers rather than in tension with them.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Ownership is a coverage variable, not a footnote.** The inventory counts 1,740 deduplicated tables across 119 databases, 17 tenants, and 10 funding agencies or programs, covering 17 biological topics [src: berdl_data_atlas]. DOE accounts for approximately 78% of tables, DOE-BER alone 63% [src: berdl_data_atlas]. Topics are correspondingly lopsided: field_observational is 40% of tables, and six topics are more than 75% single-owner, including mobile_phage at 96% PhageFoundry ownership [src: berdl_data_atlas]. Volume is not breadth — ENIGMA holds 36% of tables but covers 5 topics at entropy 0.43, whereas KBase covers 10 topics at entropy 2.87 [src: berdl_data_atlas]. Metadata absence compounds this: 83,227 of 293,059 genomes (28.4%) have AlphaEarth environmental embeddings, and 52.7% of 180,025 genomes have unknown per-genome NCBI environment labels — gaps that can mean missing coordinates or unstructured text rather than biological absence [src: pitfalls]. [[concepts/data-landscape-ownership-and-coverage-bias]] argues the consequence: apparent topic richness tracks funding and curation history.

**A resource name is not a resource.** Twenty database entries containing `nmdc` resolve to 7 maintained resources spanning three tenants and six provenance classes, including NCBI and Pfam re-hosts, an Arkin Lab derivative, and `kbase.nmdc_neon`, a namesake collision representing NEON [src: nmdc_context_audit]. Scale hides inside the shared label: the genuine biosample universe is 16,640 samples, while the co-hosted NCBI mirror holds 51,711,888 — a roughly 3,000× scale trap [src: nmdc_context_audit]. Currency is invisible without effort; snapshot ages spanned roughly four months, and the freshest resource (62,346 metagenome-assembled genomes, or MAGs) sits in the `kbase` tenant rather than `nmdc` [src: nmdc_context_audit]. Other explorations agree from different angles: `kescience_paperblast` holds 12.4 million rows across 14 tables, yet literature evidence is extremely uneven (organism-level Gini coefficient 0.967; 65.6% of genes with a paper link have exactly one paper) [src: paperblast_explorer], and the Web of Microbes data here is a frozen 2018 snapshot of 37 organisms and 589 metabolites, 332 (56.4%) unidentified [src: webofmicrobes_explorer]. [[concepts/provenance-aware-resource-discovery]] develops the requirement: provenance, authority, scale, tenant, access surface, and currency belong on one resource card.

**Executable joins are only the first of four evidence states.** [[concepts/cross-tenant-data-bridging]] frames integration as shared schema keys, overlapping values, an executable join, then a scientifically interpretable result. The inventory populates the bottom rung: 536 unordered cross-tenant bridges through 29 canonical keys, with `sample_id` spanning 10 tenants [src: berdl_data_atlas]. Value-space failures live a rung up. ENIGMA MT20 (*Rhodanobacter glycinis*) matched GTDB MT20 (*Streptococcus pneumoniae*), an error involving 8,434 genomes including 1,751 clinical genomes, and 12 of 32 pangenome linkages through `ncbi_strain_identifiers` were wrong at the genus level [src: pitfalls]. Chemical identity fails the same way: 107 formula-only ModelSEED matches expand to 900 candidate molecules [src: webofmicrobes_explorer]. [[concepts/taxonomic-nomenclature-reconciliation]] collects remedies — prefer `GCF_*` accessions, record taxonomy version, join pangenome taxonomy through `genome_id` rather than `gtdb_taxonomy_id` [src: pitfalls]. Validated bridges do pay off: four-resource agreement for *Pseudomonas* FW300-N2E3 reached 17/21 (81%) fully concordant metabolites with mean concordance 0.94 [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency].

**Operational engineering sits inside the evidence chain.** A Delta-to-Iceberg migration changes live addresses, so discovery should prefer `catalog.namespace.table` forms and inspect live schemas rather than archived SQL; `data_lakehouse_ingest` is a governance-group name, not a database prefix [src: pitfalls]. Roughly 1B-row gene tables against a 1 GB driver cap make large `.toPandas()` collections unsafe, and joins touching the 2.5B-row UniProt identifier table need broadcast joins disabled [src: pitfalls, discoveries]. Access surfaces disagree with each other: `DESCRIBE DATABASE EXTENDED` raised `ForbiddenException` for a principal whose `COUNT(*)` on the same tables succeeded [src: nmdc_context_audit], and REST `/count` and `/schema` are unreliable for repeated or large-table discovery [src: pitfalls].

**Adversarial review is a distinct layer, not a sterner tone.** On one project, standard review reported no critical issues; adversarial review found 5 critical and 6 important issues, including feature leakage, hard-coded positive readings of non-significant results, absent confounder adjustment, missing null distributions, and sample-size overclaims [src: discoveries]. Across 9 adversarial rounds, two citation failures recurred — PMID hijacking (real PMIDs attached to unrelated papers) and hallucinated author lists — so load-bearing citations need author, title, journal, PMID, and DOI verified in PubMed [src: discoveries]. Multiple-testing correction must target real hypothesis families: methodology revisions M1–M26 were not one family; the 4 pre-registered hypotheses used Bonferroni α=0.0125 and all 4 survived, while only M14, M21, and M23 were genuine post-hoc metric corrections [src: discoveries]. Leakage repair has teeth — fixing selection-on-outcome leakage cut independent Tier-A candidates from 33 to 3 [src: pitfalls]. See [[concepts/adversarial-research-quality-assurance]].

**Documentation is itself a measurement instrument.** Realized reuse was measured by mining README files: 51 of 66 audited projects (77%) span multiple tenants, KBase appears in 53/66 (80%), and the kbase × kescience bridge accounts for 36 projects [src: berdl_data_atlas]. Because plans, notebooks, and query logs were not mined, every reuse count is a lower bound [src: berdl_data_atlas].

## Tensions and Caveats

The load-bearing disagreement is between what a discovery-time signal shows and what it licenses, catalogued in [[conflicts/conflict--nmdc_context_audit--paperblast_explorer--pitfalls--d6745105]]. Co-hosting is a deliberate service supplying useful re-hosts and derivatives, yet the same naming pattern lets users confuse NMDC, NCBI, Pfam/InterPro, Arkin-derived, and NEON resources, and prefix grouping keeps the catalog compact while hiding `kbase.nmdc_mags`, `kbase.nmdc_arkin`, and `kbase.nmdc_neon` from `nmdc`-scoped searches [src: nmdc_context_audit]. The ambiguity is observed; the *harm* is inferred — the audit never watched users choose wrongly, resting on gap analysis and prior-project usage skew [src: nmdc_context_audit]. The interface pair is not a contradiction but still yields opposite operational advice, so each claim should record the query path used [src: nmdc_context_audit, pitfalls].

Second, unused bridges: five high-leverage bridges had zero realized use at audit time, but UC1 was subsequently sample-executed and the count is a documentation lower bound, so "zero documented use" and "zero use" differ [src: berdl_data_atlas] ([[conflicts/conflict--berdl_data_atlas--cofitness_coinheritance--ecotype_env_reanalysis--ff2b02eb]]).

Third, pipeline dependence. Two runs of the same ecotype partial-correlation analysis report medians of 0.003 and 0.081 — a 27x difference attributed to different genome sets, full-genome extraction, and downsampling, explicitly not to be averaged [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] ([[conflicts/conflict--ecotype_analysis--ecotype_env_reanalysis--plant_microbiome_ecotypes--93da56a7]]). Instability is also not a specific leakage symptom: Jaccard thresholds 0.5 and 0.3 were project-specific decision rules, with no universal cutoff established [src: pitfalls] ([[conflicts/conflict--ecotype_analysis--ecotype_env_reanalysis--ecotype_functional_differentiation--0a908b43]]). And coverage-limited nulls are not biology: 74 of 83 compounds (89%) remained organism-dark in the ENIGMA census, a resource-limited frontier rather than demonstrated absence [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] ([[conflicts/conflict--ecotype_env_reanalysis--enigma_carbon_census_1]]).

Two inventory caveats bound all of the above: across-tenant deduplication was not performed, so apparent differences may include records duplicated across refdata, KBase, ENIGMA, and genome-depot layers; and two tenant-to-agency mappings (evaluation and lambda, 4 tables total) remain unverified against program documentation [src: berdl_data_atlas].
