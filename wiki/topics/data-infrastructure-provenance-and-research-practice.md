# Data Infrastructure, Provenance, and Research Practice

Biological conclusions in this corpus rest on data that someone else owned, named, migrated, and documented. This topic covers what happens between a table existing and a claim being defensible: how resources are discovered, how identifiers are reconciled, how cross-tenant joins are validated, and how claims are challenged after the analysis runs. The corpus speaks to it directly because one project audited the whole data landscape — 1,740 deduplicated tables across 119 databases, 17 tenants, and 10 funding agencies or programs [src: berdl_data_atlas] — while central pitfalls and adversarial-review digests record what went wrong in practice: an MT20 strain-name collision producing an erroneous match across 8,434 genomes [src: pitfalls], and adversarial review finding 5 critical and 6 important issues in a project that standard review called exceptionally sophisticated with no critical issues [src: discoveries]. Six member concepts and five conflict pages are synthesized below.

## Literature Context

Data-management literature has long held that provenance, not storage, is the limiting factor in reuse. The eGenVar system was built on the premise that files can remain in their original locations under existing access controls while a separate catalogue records three metadata types — file information, file provenance and data lineage, and content descriptions ([PMID 24682735](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24682735/)). SemPoD argued the same case for translational 'omics, using an ontology that reuses terms from PROV-O (the W3C provenance working group's PROV-ontology), MIAPE (minimum information about a proteomics experiment) and MIMIx (minimum information required for reporting a molecular interaction experiment) to make provenance queryable across projects, though its reported deployment is a single institution's 1,153 mass-spectrometry experiments from 20 projects, evaluated on initial user feedback ([PMID 23282161](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23282161/)). In ecology, LAGOS documented the assembly of geospatial data for ~50,000 lakes and water-quality data for ~10,000 lakes drawn from ~100 individual datasets across 17 US states, and reported heterogeneity of data, formats and metadata as the largest challenge, with many integration steps requiring manual input from domain experts ([PMID 26140212](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26140212/)). The broader open-data critique adds that standardization and robust metadata increase access but are *not sufficient*, and that sociological barriers — inadequate rewards for sharing — persist alongside technical ones ([PMID 21311007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21311007/)).

This corpus is consistent with that framing and sharpens it in three places. The NMDC audit's finding that 20 database names containing `nmdc` resolve to 7 maintained resources across six provenance classes — including a namesake that actually carries NEON rather than NMDC data — is a concrete instance of the claim that names and content descriptions alone do not suffice ([PMID 21311007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21311007/)); it argues that the catalogue field that resolves the ambiguity is provenance class and owning agency, which eGenVar's lineage metadata anticipates in principle but not at cross-tenant scale ([PMID 24682735](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24682735/)). The corpus's reconciliation work extends LAGOS's manual-curation lesson: a recorded synonymy layer over stable accessions, rather than string normalization, is the specific form that expert manual input must take when a single strain label maps to two different species ([PMID 26140212](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26140212/)).

Two contributions have no analogue among these papers and should be read as novel rather than confirmatory. First, ingestion currency as a discovery field — surfacing `max(committed_at)` because snapshot ages spanned months with no comment, property or changelog exposing them — goes beyond the file-level lineage these systems capture. Second, the four evidence states separating an executable join from a scientifically interpretable one, and the adversarial-review layer that catches citation-identity failures and statistical mis-specification, address post-integration claim validity, which the cited provenance literature does not attempt. See [[concepts/provenance-aware-resource-discovery]] and [[concepts/adversarial-research-quality-assurance]].

## What the Corpus Shows

**Ownership, not biology, sets the shape of what is available.** DOE accounts for approximately 78% of the KBase Data Lakehouse tables — DOE-BER 63%, DOE BRaVE 14%, DOE/NSF 0.6%, DOE-FE 0.4% — with ARPA-H at 4% and NSF at 3.5%, and DOE-BER is the only agency covering all 15 biological topics recorded in the agency-coverage analysis [src: berdl_data_atlas]. Six topics are more than 75% single-owner, including mobile_phage at 96% PhageFoundry ownership and pangenome at 79% KBase ownership [src: berdl_data_atlas]; field_observational alone is 40% of tables [src: berdl_data_atlas]. [[concepts/data-landscape-ownership-and-coverage-bias]] argues this must be carried as a coverage-bias variable: conclusions about pangenomes or mobile phage are coupled to the sampling frames and curation practices of one dominant owner [src: berdl_data_atlas]. Volume also diverges from breadth — KBase covers 10 topics at entropy 2.87 (a spread measure; higher means more even distribution across topics), while ENIGMA holds 36% of tables but covers only 5 topics at entropy 0.43 [src: berdl_data_atlas].

**A name is not a dataset boundary.** [[concepts/provenance-aware-resource-discovery]] rests on a direct audit: 20 database names containing `nmdc` resolve to 7 real, maintained resources across three tenants and six provenance classes, including an NCBI re-host, a Pfam re-host, an Arkin Lab derivative, and `kbase.nmdc_neon`, which represents NEON (NSF) rather than NMDC (DOE-BER) — reading the namesake as NMDC would be an agency-attribution error [src: nmdc_context_audit]. Scale is part of the same problem: the genuine NMDC biosample universe holds 16,640 samples in `nmdc.metadata.biosample_set` while the co-hosted NCBI mirror holds 51,711,888, a ~3,000× scale trap [src: nmdc_context_audit]. Currency is a third field and usually invisible — Iceberg snapshot ages spanned approximately four months across NMDC-labeled resources, from `2026-03-09` for `nmdc.ncbi_biosamples` to `2026-07-02` for `kbase.nmdc_mags`, with no comments, no database properties and no changelog exposing it, so discovery should surface `max(committed_at)` [src: nmdc_context_audit]. Temporal coverage is not ingestion currency: PaperBLAST spans papers from 1951 to 2026 but is an early-2026 snapshot [src: paperblast_explorer], and the Web of Microbes analysis used a 2018 export retrieved through the Wayback Machine [src: webofmicrobes_explorer].

**Identifiers break silently, and the breakage looks biological.** [[concepts/taxonomic-nomenclature-reconciliation]] documents the sharpest case: ENIGMA MT20 is *Rhodanobacter glycinis*, GTDB MT20 is *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, and the erroneous match touched 8,434 genomes including 1,751 clinical genomes [src: pitfalls]. Among 32 pangenome linkages through `ncbi_strain_identifiers`, 12 were incorrect genus matches [src: pitfalls]. Unreconciled names can also manufacture apparent effect sizes: in the CrohnsPhage mart, one cohort's abundance collapsed to a pseudocount and produced a log₂FC approximately 28, equivalent to approximately 2.68 × 10⁸ fold [src: pitfalls]. The prescription is stable accessions (`GCF_*`) over short strain labels, a recorded synonymy layer rather than string normalization, and explicit awareness that a column's taxonomic rank may not match its neighbour's — pangenome taxonomy must join through `genome_id`, not `gtdb_taxonomy_id`, because the genome table stores a genus-level string while `gtdb_taxonomy_r214v1` stores a species-level one [src: pitfalls].

**Connectivity has four evidence states, and most bridges sit in the first.** [[concepts/cross-tenant-data-bridging]] separates shared schema keys, overlapping values, an executable join, and a scientifically interpretable result [src: berdl_data_atlas]. The atlas identifies 536 unordered cross-tenant bridges at tenant-by-topic-cell granularity through 29 canonical keys, with `sample_id` spanning 10 tenants and `genome_id` and `ncbi_taxon_id` spanning 9 [src: berdl_data_atlas]. Realized use is narrower: 51 of 66 BERIL projects (77%) span multiple tenants, KBase appears in 53/66 (80%) and KEScience in 35/66 (53%), with the KBase × KEScience bridge alone accounting for 36 projects [src: berdl_data_atlas]. Five high-leverage bridges had zero realized use at audit time — UC1 (KEScience–refdata, 12 shared keys) through UC5 (NMDC–refdata, 9) — and only UC1 was later sample-validated [src: berdl_data_atlas]. A validated join can also produce a clean null: environment classifications, AlphaEarth embeddings, ANI distances and gene-cluster memberships were joined to show that environmental species did *not* have stronger partial correlations than human-associated species (U=1536, p=0.83) [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis].

**Claims survive scrutiny only when someone attacks them.** [[concepts/adversarial-research-quality-assurance]] treats adversarial review as a distinct layer, not a skeptical wording pass [src: discoveries]. Across 9 adversarial review rounds the recurring citation failures were PMID hijacking — real PubMed identifiers attached to unrelated papers — and hallucinated author lists, so load-bearing citations need author, title, journal, PMID and DOI verified through PubMed rather than DOI resolution alone [src: discoveries]. Statistical specification matters as much: methodology revisions M1–M26 were not automatically one multiple-testing family; the project had 4 pre-registered hypotheses, Bonferroni correction used α=0.0125, all 4 survived, and only M14, M21 and M23 were genuine post-hoc metric corrections [src: discoveries]. The other failure classes — feature leakage, non-significant results hard-coded as positive, absent confounder adjustment, missing null distributions, sample-size overclaims — are exactly the ones [[concepts/multi-omics-integration]] exposes when modalities are stacked [src: discoveries].

## Tensions and Caveats

The corpus's most consequential caveat is that its own use statistics are an observability artifact. The realized-use audit mined project README files, so data-source mentions in research plans, notebooks, or query histories were missed; reported tenant breadth and reuse are lower bounds, not a census [src: berdl_data_atlas]. "Zero realized use" for UC2–UC5 means zero use found in audited documentation, not proof of no use [src: berdl_data_atlas]. Two tenant-to-agency mappings (evaluation, lambda, 4 tables total) remain unverified, and across-tenant deduplication was not performed, so apparent inventory differences may include records duplicated across refdata, KBase, ENIGMA and genome-depot layers [src: berdl_data_atlas]. Coverage gaps can likewise be metadata gaps: 83,227 of 293,059 genomes (28.4%) have AlphaEarth embeddings and 52.7% of 180,025 genomes have unknown per-genome NCBI environment labels, which may reflect missing coordinates rather than biological absence [src: pitfalls].

Whether scale metadata can be quoted at face value is itself contested — see [[conflicts/conflict--provenance-aware-resource-discovery--f0c8f1e3]]. Iceberg metadata returned counts instantly, including 1.83B rows for `nmdc.results.annotation_kegg_orthology` via `SELECT COUNT(*)` [src: nmdc_context_audit], while the pitfalls digest records conditional unreliability for a REST `/count` looped over many tables and `/schema` on large tables [src: pitfalls]. The unresolved question is whether the difference tracks the interface, the query pattern, or the table; until it is settled, a discovery card should record how a count was obtained, not just the number.

Four conflicts concern bridge design rather than discovery: whether module-level aggregation rescues a weak gene-level fitness-to-genome bridge or only reframes it [[conflicts/conflict--cross-tenant-data-bridging--8325c1f4]]; whether a metabolite name such as tryptophan means the same thing on both sides of a join [[conflicts/conflict--cross-tenant-data-bridging--d89b8efa]]; whether one summary number per entity is portable across tenants or moves with the sample [[conflicts/conflict--cross-tenant-data-bridging--dd5b9877]]; and whether a near-perfect IBD taxonomy–metabolite correlation is under-validated or structurally unidentifiable [[conflicts/conflict--cross-tenant-data-bridging--34478c5a]].

Finally, execution is part of provenance. The Delta-to-Iceberg migration is incomplete, so live discovery should prefer `catalog.namespace.table` addresses and fall back to underscore forms only when necessary; `data_lakehouse_ingest` is a governance-group name, not a database prefix [src: pitfalls]. Pangenome tables (~1B-row `gene` and `gene_genecluster_junction`, ~421M `genome_ani`, ~833M `interproscan_domains`) against a 1 GB driver cap make large `.toPandas()` collections unsafe [src: pitfalls]; joins touching the 2.5B-row UniProt identifier table need `SET spark.sql.autoBroadcastJoinThreshold = -1`, and nbconvert runs should cache Spark output as CSV to avoid DeadKernelError [src: discoveries].
