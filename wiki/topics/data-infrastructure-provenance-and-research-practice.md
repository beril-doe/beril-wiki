# Data Infrastructure, Provenance, and Research Practice

This topic covers how data are organized, labelled and joined in the KBase Data Lakehouse, and how research practice (review, code history, ingestion) decides whether a result can be trusted. The corpus speaks to it because many projects hit the same infrastructure failures, and the central pitfalls digest records them. [src: pitfalls] One inventory report, berdl_data_atlas, counts 1,740 deduplicated tables across 119 databases, 17 tenants and 10 funding agencies or programs. [src: berdl_data_atlas]

## Literature Context

The candidate papers agree that data provenance and metadata structure determine whether research results can be reused and trusted. A review of FAIR (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable) practice in plant AI argues that progress is constrained less by algorithms than by the structure, semantics and interoperability of the underlying data. It also reports that integrative, transferable and reproducible analyses need richer metadata, explicit semantics and traceable provenance, while task-focused applications can succeed at lower readiness levels [PMID 42467611](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42467611/). A neuroimaging commentary likewise says that sharing depends on standards for metadata, provenance and other descriptors, and it names the lack of standard infrastructure and tools as a barrier [PMID 23587272](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23587272/). Systems work in neuroimaging describes a provenance service that tracks how workflows and datasets evolve [PMID 23763909](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23763909/). A hospital data-integration framework records data management and manipulation activities, with a provenance metadata schema and a process validation concept. Its authors say it is not a panacea for FAIR compliance [PMID 37189148](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37189148/). A review of workflow management systems compares Nextflow, CWL, WDL and Swift/T, with reproducibility and interoperability among the criteria [PMID 34737383](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34737383/).

The literature also shows the cost when provenance is missing. A critique of antenatal corticosteroid trials says that four randomised trials lack clear governance, so the provenance and reliability of their data are hard to verify [PMID 33940424](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33940424/). The closest match to the corpus's research-practice findings is a study of agentic science. It reports that 30 unguided local agents splintered into 18 incompatible pipelines on the same dataset. Public, fully traceable workflows exposed unstated modeling choices, reference mismatches and subsampled tutorial inputs in published studies [PMID 42990352](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42990352/). Other candidates cover LLM ethics in medicine, clinicogenomic equity and clinician guides to AI devices. They mention provenance only in passing and are not used as evidence here.

The corpus is consistent with this general picture, and it adds concrete, quantified failures from one platform. Its notebook finding is that three notebooks (NB08, NB09, NB10) existed only as outputs in genotype_to_phenotype_enigma. This fits the argument that work done in local, ephemeral environments leaves no examinable record [PMID 42990352](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42990352/). The corpus's reference-mismatch cases are the GTDB prefix problem, versioned versus unversioned Pfam accessions, and the genome-ID mismatch that gave a false null result (0/5 significant) in plant_microbiome_ecotypes. These parallel the mismatches reported in that study, but the candidate papers describe a different setting and do not test them on the KBase Data Lakehouse. The corpus's metadata gaps, such as NEON annotation covering 57% of workflows (1,799 / 3,137) and the habitat-biased MAG loss, illustrate the claim that metadata richness limits which analyses are feasible [PMID 42467611](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42467611/).

Some corpus findings have no counterpart in the candidates, so they are novel here. These include the mislabelling of 20 database names containing "nmdc", which resolve to 7 real resources, and the finding that adversarial review found 5 critical issues where standard review found none in ibd_phage_targeting. No candidate paper tests paired review. The corpus claim that label ambiguity drives poor resource choice is an inference, not an observed outcome. The candidates do not settle it either way.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Scale and ownership are well described, but only as inventory.** The inventory ran counts on 65 headline tables; most use COUNT(*), so a row total is not always a count of distinct biological objects. [src: berdl_data_atlas] The largest table, kbase_ke_pangenome.gene, has 1,011,650,903 rows. [src: berdl_data_atlas] DOE-BER supplies most tables and covers every topic. [src: berdl_data_atlas] Ownership is concentrated: PhageFoundry owns 96% of mobile_phage, and six topics are more than 75% single-owner. [src: berdl_data_atlas] See [[concepts/entity-class-depth-and-data-volume-of-a-biological-data-platform]] and [[concepts/funding-agency-and-topic-coverage-of-a-shared-data-platform]]. Use is narrower than availability: 77% (51/66) of audited projects span multiple tenants, but use concentrates on kbase × kescience with 36 projects, and five high-leverage bridges had no use found. [src: berdl_data_atlas] See [[concepts/realized-versus-theoretical-cross-collection-data-reuse]].

**Labels mislead.** The audit found 20 database names containing "nmdc" resolving to 7 real resources across three tenants and six provenance classes. [src: nmdc_context_audit] The genuine NMDC biosample universe is 16,640 samples, against 51.7M in the co-hosted NCBI mirror, a ~3,000× difference the names do not signal. [src: nmdc_context_audit] The catalog adds little context: no table comments, one broken schema link, and snapshot age as the only currency signal; snapshot ages differ by ~4 months across the NMDC label. [src: nmdc_context_audit] The claim that this drives poor resource choice is an inference, not an observed outcome. [src: nmdc_context_audit] Outside NMDC, pangenome_build_protocol, genomad_mobile_elements and IMG_env are referenced but do not exist, so protocol_id is a dangling reference. [src: pitfalls] See [[concepts/provenance-overloading-of-dataset-labels-in-the-data-lakehouse]] and [[concepts/documentation-discovery-and-currency-gaps-in-lakehouse-catalogs-and-tooling]].

**Joins fail silently.** The schema audit found 536 bridges from 29 keys, but this proves schema compatibility, not overlapping values; value-space validity is established only for UC1. [src: berdl_data_atlas] Documented failures include GTDB prefix mismatches (stripping GB_ or RS_ fixed one case), versioned versus unversioned Pfam accessions, and composite keys such as Fitness Browser's (orgId, locusId). [src: pitfalls, berdl_data_atlas] A genome-ID mismatch produced a false null result (0/5 significant) in plant_microbiome_ecotypes; after the fix, overlap was 1306/1306 genomes. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Name-based linkage fails too: matching short ENIGMA strain names caused 12 of 32 genus-level mismatches, and the MT20 collision injected 1,751 spurious clinical genomes into environmental profiles. [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma, pitfalls] BacDive species-name matching linked 42,227 strains (43.4% of 97,334). [src: bacdive_metal_validation] Table choice matters as well: bakta_pfam_domains had no hits for 7 of 33 marker Pfams, which would have made a cyanobacterial hypothesis test impossible on that table. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] See [[concepts/join-key-validity-and-identifier-format-mismatches]], [[concepts/linking-isolate-and-strain-records-to-reference-genome-databases]] and [[concepts/reference-database-table-choice-and-pfam-versioning-in-lakehouse-queries]].

**Metadata gaps limit what can be tested.** Per-gene KEGG annotation covers 57% of NEON workflows (1,799 / 3,137), and the gap is habitat-biased: the H4 cohort of 246 MAGs (metagenome-assembled genomes) collapsed to 73 MAGs across 19 genera. [src: neon_mag_functional_discovery, pitfalls] Harvard Forest abiotic features are all zeros, which the report calls a parsing artifact. [src: harvard_forest_warming] NMDC does not populate extraction kit, filtration or host-depletion fields, so wet-lab factors cannot be tested. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] Free-text isolation_source needs keyword harmonization: one 12-category mapping left 17% "Other" and 12.5% "Unknown". [src: env_embedding_explorer] Species-level majority votes cover far more (91% of species) than per-genome parsing, which gave 52.7% "Unknown". [src: amr_strain_variation, pitfalls] Coordinates can be institutional addresses: 30,469 genomes (36.6%) fall in suspicious clusters, though some flagged sites are legitimate field sites. [src: discoveries, env_embedding_explorer] See [[concepts/annotation-and-environmental-metadata-table-gaps-in-the-data-lakehouse]], [[concepts/unmeasured-wet-lab-metadata-as-a-limit-on-contamination-correlates]], [[concepts/harmonizing-free-text-isolation-source-and-environment-metadata]] and [[concepts/coordinate-quality-and-institutional-address-artifacts-in-genome-geolocation]].

**Access and tooling are moving targets.** Collections are migrating from Delta to Iceberg, with dotted names replacing underscore-joined ones. Underscore-form queries copied from older projects fail against migrated catalogs, but migration is unfinished and the underscore form remains correct for Delta-only collections. [src: pitfalls] The tenant field in ingestion is the governance group, not the database prefix. [src: pitfalls] See [[concepts/lakehouse-namespace-migration-tenancy-and-authentication]].

**Research practice decides trust.** On ibd_phage_targeting, standard review found no critical issues while adversarial review found 5 critical and 6 important ones. [src: discoveries, ibd_phage_targeting] The repair retracted two of three headline claims of the original analysis. [src: ibd_phage_targeting] In plant_microbiome_ecotypes, a circular 92.7% genus-level validation was replaced by a curated panel of 18 model organisms. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Code history matters as well: in genotype_to_phenotype_enigma, three notebooks (NB08, NB09, NB10) existed only as outputs. [src: pitfalls] See [[concepts/adversarial-review-pre-registration-and-multiple-testing-discipline]] and [[concepts/notebook-provenance-and-session-isolation-in-agent-assisted-analysis]].

## Tensions and Caveats

- **Coverage figures are not comparable across sources.** The ~2% KEGG compound annotation in nmdc_arkin.metabolomics_gold and the 2% in the community metabolic ecology report are not stated to describe the same population. [src: discoveries, nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] Isolation-source coverage also differs between datasets. [src: env_embedding_explorer, gc_ecotype_ecology]
- **Atlas reuse numbers are lower bounds.** The audit was README-based and may miss mentions in plans or notebooks. [src: berdl_data_atlas]
- **Heuristics are untested.** The isolation_source homogeneity refinement for coordinates is proposed, not tested. [src: env_embedding_explorer]
- **Remaining statistical limits.** The repaired ibd_phage_targeting consensus is partial: the plan required at least 2 of 3 methods to agree. [src: ibd_phage_targeting] In plant_microbiome_ecotypes, no multiple-testing correction was applied across 15 shuffle markers, and T3SS is marginal. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Elsewhere, 14 of 16 formal tests survived Bonferroni. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]
- **Single-source evidence.** Notebook-provenance and migration guidance rest mostly on the pitfalls digest, and the wet-lab metadata page on one report. [src: pitfalls, euk_in_prok_correlates]
