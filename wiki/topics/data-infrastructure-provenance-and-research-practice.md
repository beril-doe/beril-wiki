# Data Infrastructure, Provenance, and Research Practice

This topic covers how data are organized, labelled and joined in the KBase Data Lakehouse, and how research practice (review, code history, ingestion) decides whether a result can be trusted. The corpus speaks to it because one project inventoried the whole lakehouse [src: berdl_data_atlas], one audited a single overloaded label [src: nmdc_context_audit], and two central digests recorded what went wrong in practice [src: pitfalls, discoveries]. The page has 4 argument threads below and points to seven member concepts.

## Literature Context

The candidate literature treats data management, metadata and reproducibility as preconditions for trustworthy microbiome work. A bioinformatician's guide to metagenomics, written from Joint Genome Institute experience, walks through sample and metadata collection and ends with data management as a distinct issue [PMID 19052320](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19052320/). A forensic-microbiome review argues that results must be comparable and reproducible within and between laboratories, and that analysts must be aware of biases in a given sample; it also questions whether such resources can scale across institutions given the cost of maintaining databases [PMID 29521257](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29521257/). A plant-microbiome review likewise lists standardized controls, metadata, and reproducible workflows among the main obstacles [PMID 41304231](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41304231/). The FAIR Data Station describes machine-actionable metadata with a validation module, noting that few easy-to-adopt FAIR implementations exist [PMID 36879493](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36879493/). MicroEXPERT is a platform offering data management and cross-study metagenome-wide association analysis [PMID 38868224](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38868224/).
Reproducibility is also shown to depend on hidden modifiers. In mice, probiotic efficacy depended on the experimental obesogenic diet, which the authors say matters for the reproducibility of preclinical probiotic studies [PMID 36945120](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36945120/). Measurement reliability work on keratitis annotation (75 patients, two graders) found variability intrinsic to the task and called for optimized protocols [PMID 32833849](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32833849/). A comparison of two liquid phage susceptibility assays (55 phages) judged reproducibility good to fair [PMID 39171268](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39171268/).

Against this, the corpus's emphasis on metadata and reproducibility is consistent with the literature: its documented incidents, such as outputs committed without their notebooks and ingestion failures from foreign dumps, are concrete instances of the general call for reproducible workflows and validated metadata. The corpus extends the literature by quantifying how a name misleads, in the audit that resolved overloaded "nmdc" database names into provenance classes, and how shared keys produce false joins, such as strain-name collisions with GTDB; the metadata-focused papers above [PMID 36879493](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36879493/), [PMID 19052320](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19052320/) do not address these hazards in their abstracts, so they are novel relative to them. The corpus's ownership-concentration analysis of the KBase Data Lakehouse likewise has no counterpart here, though it loosely echoes the forensic review's concern about institutional scale and bias. The corpus's finding that adversarial review catches issues standard review misses is not tested in these abstracts; the annotation study measures grader reliability on images, not review of research [PMID 32833849](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32833849/). Several corpus practice findings rest on single incidents, so they illustrate the literature's concerns rather than test them.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Ownership shapes what data exist.** The BERDL Data Atlas inventories 1,740 deduplicated tables across 119 databases, 17 tenants (namespace owners) and 10 funding agencies or programs [src: berdl_data_atlas]. DOE accounts for approximately 78% of tables, and six topics are more than 75% single-owner [src: berdl_data_atlas]. Separately, organism-level coverage gaps exist (the sources do not link them causally to ownership): *Acinetobacter baylyi* ADP1 is not in the Fitness Browser, and *E. coli* K-12 had 0 GapMind predictions [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer, essential_metabolome]. Heavy presence is not heavy reuse: ENIGMA holds 36% of tables but appears in 6 projects, and among 66 audited projects 51 (77%) span multiple tenants [src: berdl_data_atlas]. See [[concepts/data-landscape-ownership-and-coverage-bias]].

**A name is not provenance.** The audit resolved 20 database names containing "nmdc" (National Microbiome Data Collaborative, NMDC) to 7 maintained resources in six provenance classes, and the genuine NMDC biosample universe (16,640 samples) sits beside an NCBI mirror of 51,711,888 biosamples [src: nmdc_context_audit]. Migration from Delta to Iceberg changes live table addresses, so archived underscore-form queries can fail and addresses must be discovered live [src: pitfalls]. Scale also misleads: the NCBI mirror is a roughly 3,000× scale trap relative to the NMDC samples, so discovery cards should show counts and resource role [src: nmdc_context_audit]. The 2018 Web of Microbes snapshot holds 37 organisms and 589 metabolites, a far narrower base than the resources it links to [src: webofmicrobes_explorer]. See [[concepts/provenance-aware-resource-discovery]].

**A shared key is not a shared identity.** The atlas counts 536 unordered cross-tenant bridges through 29 canonical keys, but these are schema-level potential, and only UC1 of five use cases was sample-validated [src: berdl_data_atlas]. Short strain names collided: ENIGMA MT20 (*Rhodanobacter glycinis*) matched the Genome Taxonomy Database (GTDB) entry for *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, and 12 of 32 pangenome linkages were incorrect genus matches [src: pitfalls]. The repair reduced verified linkages from 32 to 20 and eliminated all false matches [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma]. Name drift is a second hazard: unreconciled taxon names produced a log₂ fold-change (log₂FC) of approximately 28 artifact [src: pitfalls]. Where bridges were validated at organism level, four-resource concordance for *Pseudomonas* FW300-N2E3 was 17/21 (81%) fully concordant among testable metabolites, yet 37/58 metabolites (64%) were Web of Microbes only [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]. See [[concepts/cross-tenant-data-bridging]], [[concepts/taxonomic-nomenclature-reconciliation]] and the project-level integrations in [[concepts/multi-omics-integration]].

**Practice decides reproducibility and trust.** Outputs were committed without the notebooks that produced them (NB08, NB09, NB10), and `nbconvert --inplace` can exit 0 while saving zero cell outputs; each is a single documented incident [src: pitfalls]. See [[concepts/analysis-provenance-and-reproducible-outputs]]. Foreign dumps needed repair: MySQL data files lack headers, encode NULL as `\N`, and a leading inline comment made the ingest pipeline silently skip a whole table [src: pitfalls]. See [[concepts/foreign-dump-ingestion-fidelity]]. Review matters too: standard review found "no critical issues" where adversarial review of the same project found 5 critical and 6 important issues [src: discoveries, ibd_phage_targeting]. Reviews can themselves fabricate: the functional-atlas project documented three reviewer-side fabrications, including a PMID hijack, before REVIEW_8 recorded 0 fabrications and 7/7 DOIs auto-verified [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]. The same repair class took 7 notebooks when caught at notebook scope but a single plan-edit pass at plan-revision scope [src: discoveries]. See [[concepts/adversarial-research-quality-assurance]].

## Tensions and Caveats

- **Report prose versus tables.** Several reports state figures in prose that disagree with their own tables, so any single number carried forward takes a side [src: ibd_phage_targeting, pitfalls, snipe_defense_system]. The *E. coli* phage-susceptibility matrix is described as a 96-phage × 188-strain × 17,672-pair inventory, the stated multiplication is internally inconsistent, and a 94-phage denominator is used elsewhere [src: ibd_phage_targeting, snipe_defense_system]. See [[conflicts/conflict--adversarial-research-quality-assurance--a6ef7613]] and [[conflicts/conflict--adversarial-research-quality-assurance--eca086c5]].
- **Attribution of tenants.** The atlas tags some ontologies "NMDC integrated", which the audit reads as provenance blur; whether tenant and agency shares reflect origin or naming is a hypothesis [src: berdl_data_atlas, nmdc_context_audit]. See [[conflicts/conflict--data-landscape-ownership-and-coverage-bias--4b4b2ec9]] and [[conflicts/conflict--provenance-aware-resource-discovery--0fe66702]].
- **Metadata reliability.** Metadata counts succeeded for audited NMDC tables, while the digest reports requests through the REST (Representational State Transfer) interface as unreliable; the sources call this non-contradictory because the access surfaces differ [src: nmdc_context_audit, pitfalls]. See [[conflicts/conflict--provenance-aware-resource-discovery--f0c8f1e3]].
- **Do joins carry biology?** Modules recover signal in one project but not another [src: cofitness_coinheritance, field_vs_lab_fitness]; tryptophan evidence did not imply growth in one project, while another predicted binary growth with AUC (area under the receiver operating characteristic curve) 0.933; the endpoints differ, so whether these truly conflict is open [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency, genotype_to_phenotype_enigma]. See [[conflicts/conflict--cross-tenant-data-bridging--8325c1f4]] and [[conflicts/conflict--cross-tenant-data-bridging--d89b8efa]].
- **Name matching.** Stripping GTDB suffixes raises BacDive linkage but may merge distinct clades [src: bacdive_metal_validation, cf_formulation_design]; two projects report different coverage [src: bacdive_metal_validation, bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]. See [[conflicts/conflict--taxonomic-nomenclature-reconciliation--a209158c]] and [[conflicts/conflict--taxonomic-nomenclature-reconciliation--2208cd83]].
- **Limits.** Several practice findings rest on one project or one incident, so their frequency is unknown [src: pitfalls]. The atlas's provenance classes are inferences, because no ingestion manifest is exposed in-catalog [src: nmdc_context_audit].
