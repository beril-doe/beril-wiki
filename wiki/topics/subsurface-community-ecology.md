# Subsurface Community Ecology

Subsurface community ecology asks what organizes microbial communities below the rooting zone — how depth and groundwater flow partition them, whether life in confined clay and deep boreholes selects for a distinct genome content, and how much of a community's present composition is set by its past rather than its current environment. This corpus speaks to the question from four directions at once: 16S amplicon surveys of a contaminated sediment and groundwater field site, comparative genomics of cultivated deep-clay Bacillota_B, a global soil-genomics test of whether clay shielding makes communities more predictable, and a controlled carbon-passage experiment that isolates the effect of history from the effect of the present. The anchoring field result is that hydrogeological zone explained 27.5% of sediment-community variance (F = 4.05, p = 0.0001) while well identity explained 19.2% and was not significant (F = 0.80, p = 0.979) [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]; the anchoring genomic result is that 547 orthologous groups were significantly enriched in deep-clay Bacillota_B at q<0.05 [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]. The three member concepts below argue about what those numbers can and cannot bear.

## Literature Context

Reviews of the deep terrestrial subsurface describe a biosphere predicted to hold as many microbial cells as global surface soils, supported less by buried organic matter than by chemolithoautotrophic primary production with hydrogen as an important electron source, and populated by recently discovered groups including candidate phyla radiation bacteria and DPANN archaea whose syntrophic and episymbiotic interactions demand multifaceted rather than single-approach study ([PMID 38780093](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38780093/)). A contamination-aware meta-analysis of terrestrial deep-subsurface metagenomes reports dominance by Betaproteobacteria, Gammaproteobacteria and Firmicutes and a small consortium of prevalent Beta-/Gammaproteobacterial OTUs recurring irrespective of aquifer lithology and depth ([PMID 36748549](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36748549/)); national monitoring of 733 newly constructed wells in China found bacterial diversity declining with well depth, Patescibacteria acting as keystone taxa in shallower aquifers, and assembly predominantly deterministic ([PMID 37468948](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37468948/)). The corpus's sediment depth gradients — Firmicutes and Proteobacteria deep-enriched, Patescibacteria and Chloroflexi shallow-enriched, ten of 12 dominant phyla significant — sit squarely consistent with these patterns, and the dominance of hydrogeological zone (27.5%) over non-significant well identity for sediments is the field-scale version of the deterministic, depth-structured assembly those studies infer.

On metabolism, the literature places sulfur cycling and hydrogen centrally but not as a deep-specific signature: in Witwatersrand Basin fracture fluids, sulfur-oxidizing autotrophic denitrifiers dominated while sulfate reducers, methanogens and anaerobic methane oxidizers each comprised <5% of the community ([PMID 27872277](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27872277/)), and granitic subsurface rock to 55 m at two endmember Chilean sites was dominated by heterotrophs (Pseudarthrobacter, Janthinobacterium, Pseudomonas) with chemolithoautotroph-affiliated taxa rare and restricted to the arid site, sulfur, hydrogen and carbon monoxide appearing as candidate inorganic donors ([PMID 41321415](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41321415/)). Against that backdrop the corpus's within-phylum control is an extension rather than a restatement: Wood–Ljungdahl and group 1 [NiFe]-hydrogenase enrichments collapsed inside Bacillota_B (5/5 vs 15/19 and 14/19, p = 0.54) while dissimilatory sulfate reduction survived (5/5 vs 4/19, p_BH = 0.044) — see [[concepts/phylogenetic-confounding-of-pangenome-associations]]. The genome-expansion result (4,110,038 vs 3,046,124 bp) is novel against these community-level surveys, though it rests on 10 anchors and remains descriptive. Notably, the corpus's deep cohort essentially lacked the CPR/DPANN episymbionts the review highlights as a defining subsurface discovery, and its compartment labels came from isolation-source keywords, so "rock" entries may be porewater.

Two further alignments are worth naming. Distance-decay and a stochastic-to-deterministic shift with sediment depth were documented across 10 South China Sea seamount summits ([PMID 37404136](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37404136/)), matching the corpus's metre-scale Mantel signal (ρ = 0.323, p = 0.029); colonization history maintained by dispersal restriction within and among caves, inferred from 26 acidic cave biofilm samples ([PMID 36602326](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36602326/)), is what [[concepts/ecological-memory]] converts into a controlled measurement. A microfluidic chip representing porous subsurface environments separately showed that cooperative interactions between free-living and biofilm-forming bacteria drive active spatial segregation into distinct microhabitats ([PMID 37454222](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37454222/)), a mechanism the corpus's habitat split between groundwater and sediment describes but does not test. Finally, a synthesis of geodiversity–trait relationships reports that substrate heterogeneity exerts only marginal effects on trait distributions ([PMID 37003934](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37003934/)) — consistent with the corpus's null clay-shield test, whose 0.024 difference spanned −0.423 to 0.161.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Vertical position and habitat, not horizontal position, organize the sediment community.** In a PERMANOVA (permutational multivariate analysis of variance, a permutation test of group differences in composition) over 37 sediment core segments, hydrogeological zone outranked well identity [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]. The distance structure agrees: samples from the same well at different depths had median Bray–Curtis dissimilarity (a compositional difference measure on taxon abundances) of 0.977, while samples from the same depth zone in different wells had median 0.835 [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]. Ten of 12 dominant phyla had significant depth associations at p < 0.05, with Chloroflexi (Spearman ρ = −0.73) and Patescibacteria (−0.70) shallow-enriched and Firmicutes (ρ = +0.76) and Proteobacteria (+0.49) deep-enriched [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]. Habitat cuts across all of this: groundwater and sediment from the same well differed at median Bray–Curtis 0.424 (range 0.364–0.450 across 5 wells), with groundwater enriched in [[entities/rhodanobacter]] (sediment 1.23%, groundwater 3.62%, 2.9×) and Gallionella (0.01%, 0.14%, 8.9×), and sediment enriched in [[entities/anaeromyxobacter]] (1.24%, 0.03%) and Arcobacter (0.54%, 0.00%) [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]. [[concepts/subsurface-hydrogeological-zonation]] assembles this argument.

**Spatial structure is sharp at metre scale.** Across 9 wells in a 3×3 grid spanning approximately 6 m, 23,458 amplicon sequence variants (ASVs, exact-sequence taxonomic units) gave mean Bray–Curtis 0.747, with distance-decay by Mantel test (correlation of ecological with geographic distance matrices) at Spearman ρ = 0.323, p = 0.029 over 9,999 permutations [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]. In groundwater from 5 wells sampled 9 days apart, well identity explained 49.9% of variance (p = 0.001), filter size 10.1% (p = 0.001), depth within the saturated zone 2.5% (p = 0.430) and date 0.8% (p = 0.998); median Bray–Curtis was 0.351 temporally, 0.750 between filter sizes and 0.917 spatially, so spatial separation contributed more variation than the 9-day interval, although filter size was also a substantial source of variation [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology].

**Deep-clay genomes are larger, not streamlined.** Comparing 10 deep-clay against 62 soil-baseline Bacillota_B genomes, mean genome size was 4,110,038 bp versus 3,046,124 bp (Cohen's d = +1.39, Mann–Whitney p = 0.025), and CheckM-rescaled sizes were 4,323,230 bp versus 3,233,715 bp (d = +1.37, p = 0.013) [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]. Mean eggNOG orthologous-group counts (OGs, clusters of evolutionarily related proteins) were 2,630 versus 2,106 (d = +1.30, p = 0.022) [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]. Quality does not explain it: mean CheckM completeness was 94.7% versus 94.3% (d = +0.08, p = 0.93) and GC content 48.84% versus 47.76% (d = +0.21, p = 0.44) [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]. Of 14,109 OGs tested with Fisher's exact tests under Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate (FDR) correction, a ≥3-fold threshold and presence in ≥3 anchors, 547 were enriched; keyword scanning assigned 42 to anaerobic respiration, 24 to sporulation-revival, 12 to mineral attachment or exopolysaccharide, 4 to anaerobic regulators and 3 to osmoadaptation, leaving 462 "other or unannotated," which manual inspection revised upward to an estimated 80–100 anaerobic-respiration-related OGs of 547 [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]. [[concepts/subsurface-bacillota-specialization]] develops this self-sufficiency reading.

**Only sulfate reduction survives phylogenetic control.** A toolkit combining Wood–Ljungdahl (WL), group 1 [NiFe]-hydrogenase and dissimilatory sulfate reduction (SR) scored 1.889 in anchor_deep (n = 9) versus 0.033 in anchor_shallow (n = 30) and 0.393 in soil_baseline (n = 140), with all-three-module proportions of 0.556, 0.000 and 0.021 [src: clay_confined_subsurface]. Against soil baseline, WL gave OR = 10.4 (p_BH = 0.004), NiFe OR = 10.5 (p_BH = 0.004), SR OR = 33.8 (p_BH = 2.5×10⁻⁴), nitrogenase OR = 5.4 (p_BH = 0.035) and iron reduction OR = 0.46 (p_BH = 0.69) [src: clay_confined_subsurface]. Within Bacillota_B, however, WL was 5/5 deep versus 15/19 baseline (p = 0.54) and NiFe 5/5 versus 14/19 (p = 0.54), while SR was 5/5 versus 4/19 (OR = ∞, p = 0.003, p_BH = 0.044) — so WL and NiFe primarily track lineage, and SR is the process-specific claim [src: clay_confined_subsurface]. Against a rock-attached null, 5 observed sulfate-reduction positives versus 0.018 expected gave binomial p = 4.0×10⁻¹², whereas iron reduction gave 1 observed versus 0.63 expected, p = 0.87 [src: clay_confined_subsurface].

**History can outweigh the current environment.** The lignin-passage experiment supplies the controlled analogue the field data cannot: in a Round-2 factorial PERMANOVA on 12 samples, Round-1 carbon history explained 58.9% of variance (F = 14.31, R² = 0.589, p = 0.002) versus 32.7% for the current Round-2 carbon source (F = 4.85, R² = 0.327, p = 0.018), with a memory index of ~0.50 [src: lignin_community_enrichment]. Composition followed history: lignin-history groups were Pseudomonas-dominated at 52–53%, lignin-plus-labile-history groups Acinetobacter-dominant at 31–34% [src: lignin_community_enrichment]. This **refines** any purely environmental reading of subsurface zonation — see [[concepts/ecological-memory]].

## Tensions and Caveats

**Local differentiation does not scale globally.** Across 5,441 soil samples, measured stressors did not predict functional gene counts out of sample, and the clay-shield test gave low-clay cross-validation R² = −0.268 versus high-clay R² = −0.292, difference 0.024, 95% CI −0.423 to 0.161 [src: soil_frontier_genomics]. Because the interval includes zero, this does not support higher predictability in high-clay soils; it does not contradict the deep-clay genome comparison, which concerns selected genomes within one lineage and compartment, but it forbids generalizing that comparison into a global clay effect [src: soil_frontier_genomics].

**Cohort and compartment bias limit the genomic claim.** The Bacillota_B universe held 334 genomes in the KBase Data Lakehouse pangenome, and the 10 anchors clumped among 3 BRH-c8a, 2 BRH-c4a, 2 Desulfosporosinus, Desulforudis, Ch130 and 1 other genome, so effects resting on anchor counts of 3–5 are primarily descriptive and some enriched OGs may be lineage markers [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]. Compartment labels came from isolation-source keywords, so "rock" entries may be porewater; the deep cohort essentially lacked rock-attached Geobacter and Geothrix lineages and CPR/DPANN episymbionts — ultrasmall Candidate Phyla Radiation bacteria and DPANN archaea that live attached to the surface of host cells — and so matches a porewater paradigm rather than a rock-attached census [src: clay_confined_subsurface].

**The field redox ladder is inference, not measurement.** Class-level traits covered 22 classes at 78% coverage, while only 65 of 1,038 genera were annotated, covering 21% of reads; species-level classification was approximately 0%, and 56% of reads fell outside genus-level inference [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]. The proposed O₂ → NO₃⁻ → Fe(III) → SO₄²⁻ → fermentation sequence, the M5 plume-mixing zone and the M6 anaerobic dead zone are hypotheses awaiting geochemical and metagenomic validation, and flow direction is unresolved: turnover was not aligned uphill–downhill (Mantel ρ = −0.049, p = 0.580), and Procrustes analysis — a test of how well two ordination configurations superimpose — gave only marginal correspondence with the physical grid (m² = 0.379, p = 0.080) [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]. Field composition along a uranium gradient likewise reflects multiple geochemical and historical dimensions rather than metal tolerance alone [src: lab_field_ecology].

**Null and dispersion caveats stay as stated.** Fungal Round-2 history, measured with ITS2 (the fungal internal-transcribed-spacer amplicon marker), was not statistically detectable (R² = 0.142, p = 0.090), and that null stands rather than being read as weak support [src: lignin_community_enrichment]. PERMDISP, a test of within-group dispersion differences, was significant for 16S (p = 0.0004) and ITS (p = 0.0001), so the PERMANOVA signals mix location and dispersion; the design used 97% OTUs (operational taxonomic units, identity clusters rather than exact-sequence ASVs) and n=3 per group, with pairwise tests floored near p~0.10 [src: lignin_community_enrichment].
