# Subsurface Community Ecology

Subsurface community ecology asks what organizes microbial communities below the rooting zone — how depth and groundwater flow partition them, whether life in confined clay and deep boreholes selects for a distinct genome content, and how much of a community's present composition is set by its past rather than its current environment. This corpus speaks to the question from four directions at once: 16S amplicon surveys of a contaminated sediment and groundwater field site, comparative genomics of cultivated deep-clay Bacillota_B, a global soil-genomics test of whether clay shielding makes communities more predictable, and a controlled carbon-passage experiment that isolates the effect of history from the effect of the present. The anchoring field result is that hydrogeological zone explained 27.5% of sediment-community variance (F = 4.05, p = 0.0001) while well identity explained 19.2% and was not significant (F = 0.80, p = 0.979) [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]; the anchoring genomic result is that 547 orthologous groups were significantly enriched in deep-clay Bacillota_B at q<0.05 [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]. The three member concepts below argue about what those numbers can and cannot bear.

## Literature Context

The published work closest to this corpus's field arm establishes that sedimentary bacterial communities partition along depth and cross-shore position, and that the partition tracks the chemistry of dissolved organic matter (DOM) rather than geography alone. In intertidal beach sediments with a runnel–ridge system and subterranean groundwater discharge, Fourier-transform ion cyclotron resonance mass spectrometry paired with Illumina 16S rRNA gene sequencing resolved four bacterial clusters attributed to distinct areas, depths, or metabolic niches: Cluster I throughout surface sediments and probably involved in algal-polymer degradation, Cluster III prominent in ridge and low-water-line samples where porewaters indicated terrestrial DOM influence and release of aromatic compounds from reactive iron oxides, Cluster IV the most seasonal and associated with species previously reported from a subsurface bloom, and Cluster II harbouring several members of the candidate phyla radiation (CPR) and related to highly degraded DOM — offered as possibly one of the first geochemical proofs for a candidate-phyla role in refractory DOM degradation [PMID 34442799](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34442799/). That study's functional layer is predicted from 16S, not measured, and it describes one sandy-beach system, so its metabolic assignments are inferences from marker-gene composition.

The corpus's field result is **consistent** with this picture and sharpens it statistically. Where the beach clusters were attributed qualitatively to areas, depths or metabolic niches [PMID 34442799](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34442799/), [[concepts/subsurface-hydrogeological-zonation]] partitions the variance: hydrogeological zone explained 27.5% of sediment-community variance (F = 4.05, p = 0.0001) against 19.2% for well identity (F = 0.80, p = 0.979), and same-well/different-depth samples were more dissimilar (median Bray–Curtis 0.977) than same-depth/different-well samples (0.835). It inherits the same interpretive limit: the proposed O₂ → NO₃⁻ → Fe(III) → SO₄²⁻ → fermentation ladder rests on class-level traits covering 22 classes at 78% coverage and only 65 of 1,038 genera annotated (21% of reads, 56% falling outside genus-level inference) — the taxonomy-to-function step flagged in [[concepts/taxonomic-resolution-dependent-functional-inference]]. The beach study only partly escaped that step by pairing marker genes with FT-ICR-MS characterization of DOM, its own functions still predicted rather than measured [PMID 34442799](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34442799/).

Two corpus findings extend or strain this picture. The beach work links CPR-bearing Cluster II to highly degraded DOM [PMID 34442799](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34442799/), whereas the corpus finds Patescibacteria shallow-enriched (Spearman ρ = −0.70) and CPR/DPANN episymbionts essentially absent from the cultivated deep-clay cohort — read as porewater-biased culturing, not true scarcity, a sampling contrast rather than a refutation. The reactive-iron-oxide chemistry reported for porewaters of the ridge and low-water-line Cluster III samples [PMID 34442799](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34442799/) has no genomic counterpart here: iron reduction gave OR = 0.46 (p_BH = 0.69), 1 observed positive against 0.63 expected (p = 0.87), and only sulfate reduction survived within-lineage control — a null kept as a null. The controlled design in [[concepts/ecological-memory]], where Round-1 carbon history explained 58.9% of variance against 32.7% for the current source, is **novel** relative to the beach survey, which resolves clusters by area, depth and season, not by prior conditioning [PMID 34442799](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34442799/); but that PERMANOVA used 97% OTUs, n=3 per group, and significant PERMDISP (16S p = 0.0004, ITS p = 0.0001) mixing location and dispersion, and fungal Round-2 history was a stated null (R² = 0.142, p = 0.090).

## What the Corpus Shows

**Vertical position and habitat, not horizontal position, organize the sediment community.** In a PERMANOVA (permutational multivariate analysis of variance, a permutation test of group differences in composition) over 37 sediment core segments, hydrogeological zone outranked well identity [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]. The distance structure agrees: samples from the same well at different depths had median Bray–Curtis dissimilarity (a compositional difference measure on taxon abundances) of 0.977, while samples from the same depth zone in different wells had median 0.835 [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]. Ten of 12 dominant phyla had significant depth associations at p < 0.05, with Chloroflexi (Spearman ρ = −0.73) and Patescibacteria (−0.70) shallow-enriched and Firmicutes (ρ = +0.76) and Proteobacteria (+0.49) deep-enriched [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]. Habitat cuts across all of this: groundwater and sediment from the same well differed at median Bray–Curtis 0.424 (range 0.364–0.450 across 5 wells), with groundwater enriched in [[entities/rhodanobacter]] (sediment 1.23%, groundwater 3.62%, 2.9×) and Gallionella (0.01%, 0.14%, 8.9×), and sediment enriched in [[entities/anaeromyxobacter]] (1.24%, 0.03%) and Arcobacter (0.54%, 0.00%) [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]. [[concepts/subsurface-hydrogeological-zonation]] assembles this argument.

**Spatial structure is sharp at metre scale; filter size rivals time.** Across 9 wells in a 3×3 grid spanning approximately 6 m, 23,458 amplicon sequence variants (ASVs, exact-sequence taxonomic units) gave mean Bray–Curtis 0.747 (0.558 for U3–M6 to 0.872 for U3–L9), and a Mantel test (ecological against geographic distance matrices) detected distance-decay at Spearman ρ = 0.323, p = 0.029, 9,999 permutations [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]. A U3–M6–L7 diagonal carried the three most negative residuals (−0.170, −0.154, −0.133), read as a possible flow corridor [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]. In groundwater from 5 wells sampled 9 days apart (September 9 and September 18, 2024), well identity explained 49.9% of variance (p = 0.001), filter size 10.1% (p = 0.001), saturated-zone depth 2.5% (p = 0.430) and date 0.8% (p = 0.998); median Bray–Curtis was 0.351 temporally, 0.750 between filter sizes and 0.917 spatially, so spatial separation contributed more variation than the 9-day interval, although filter size was also a substantial source of variation [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology].

**Deep-clay genomes are larger, not streamlined.** Comparing 10 deep-clay against 62 soil-baseline Bacillota_B genomes, mean genome size was 4,110,038 bp versus 3,046,124 bp (Cohen's d = +1.39, Mann–Whitney p = 0.025), and CheckM-rescaled sizes were 4,323,230 bp versus 3,233,715 bp (d = +1.37, p = 0.013) [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]. Mean eggNOG orthologous-group counts (OGs, clusters of evolutionarily related proteins) were 2,630 versus 2,106 (d = +1.30, p = 0.022) [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]. Quality does not explain it: mean CheckM completeness was 94.7% versus 94.3% (d = +0.08, p = 0.93) and GC content 48.84% versus 47.76% (d = +0.21, p = 0.44) [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]. Of 14,109 OGs tested with Fisher's exact tests under Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate (FDR) correction, a ≥3-fold threshold and presence in ≥3 anchors, 547 were enriched; keyword scanning assigned 42 to anaerobic respiration, 24 to sporulation-revival, 12 to mineral attachment or exopolysaccharide, 4 to anaerobic regulators and 3 to osmoadaptation, leaving 462 "other or unannotated," which manual inspection revised upward to an estimated 80–100 anaerobic-respiration-related OGs of 547 [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]. [[concepts/subsurface-bacillota-specialization]] develops this self-sufficiency reading.

**Only sulfate reduction survives phylogenetic control.** A toolkit combining Wood–Ljungdahl (WL), group 1 [NiFe]-hydrogenase and dissimilatory sulfate reduction (SR) scored 1.889 in anchor_deep (n = 9) versus 0.033 in anchor_shallow (n = 30) and 0.393 in soil_baseline (n = 140), with all-three-module proportions of 0.556, 0.000 and 0.021 [src: clay_confined_subsurface]. Against soil baseline, WL gave OR = 10.4 (p_BH = 0.004), NiFe OR = 10.5 (p_BH = 0.004), SR OR = 33.8 (p_BH = 2.5×10⁻⁴), nitrogenase OR = 5.4 (p_BH = 0.035) and iron reduction OR = 0.46 (p_BH = 0.69) [src: clay_confined_subsurface]. Within Bacillota_B, however, WL was 5/5 deep versus 15/19 baseline (p = 0.54) and NiFe 5/5 versus 14/19 (p = 0.54), while SR was 5/5 versus 4/19 (OR = ∞, p = 0.003, p_BH = 0.044) — so WL and NiFe primarily track lineage, and SR is the process-specific claim [src: clay_confined_subsurface]. Against a rock-attached null, 5 observed sulfate-reduction positives versus 0.018 expected gave binomial p = 4.0×10⁻¹², whereas iron reduction gave 1 observed versus 0.63 expected, p = 0.87 [src: clay_confined_subsurface].

**History can outweigh the current environment.** The lignin-passage experiment supplies the controlled analogue the field data cannot: in a Round-2 factorial PERMANOVA on 12 samples, Round-1 carbon history explained 58.9% of variance (F = 14.31, R² = 0.589, p = 0.002) versus 32.7% for the current Round-2 carbon source (F = 4.85, R² = 0.327, p = 0.018), with a memory index of ~0.50 [src: lignin_community_enrichment]. Composition followed history: lignin-history groups were Pseudomonas-dominated at 52–53%, lignin-plus-labile-history groups Acinetobacter-dominant at 31–34% [src: lignin_community_enrichment]. This **refines** any purely environmental reading of subsurface zonation — see [[concepts/ecological-memory]].

## Tensions and Caveats

**Local differentiation does not scale globally.** Across 5,441 soil samples, measured stressors did not predict functional gene counts out of sample, and the clay-shield test gave low-clay cross-validation R² = −0.268 versus high-clay R² = −0.292, difference 0.024, 95% CI −0.423 to 0.161 [src: soil_frontier_genomics]. Because the interval includes zero, this does not support higher predictability in high-clay soils; it does not contradict the deep-clay genome comparison, which concerns selected genomes within one lineage and compartment, but it forbids generalizing that comparison into a global clay effect [src: soil_frontier_genomics].

**Cohort and compartment bias limit the genomic claim.** The Bacillota_B universe held 334 genomes in the KBase Data Lakehouse pangenome against a v1.1 plan estimate of 6,700, and the 10 anchors clumped among 3 BRH-c8a, 2 BRH-c4a, 2 Desulfosporosinus, Desulforudis, Ch130 and 1 other genome, so effects resting on anchor counts of 3–5 are primarily descriptive and some enriched OGs may be lineage markers [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]. Compartment labels came from isolation-source keywords, so "rock" entries may be porewater [src: clay_confined_subsurface]. Rock-attached Geobacter and Geothrix lineages were essentially absent, as were CPR/DPANN episymbionts — small-genome bacterial (Candidate Phyla Radiation) and archaeal (DPANN) lineages that live attached to host cells — so the cultured cohort matches a porewater paradigm rather than a rock-attached census [src: clay_confined_subsurface].

**The field redox ladder is inference, not measurement.** Class-level traits covered 22 classes at 78% coverage, while only 65 of 1,038 genera were annotated, covering 21% of reads; species-level classification was approximately 0% and 56% of reads fell outside genus-level inference [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]. The proposed O₂ → NO₃⁻ → Fe(III) → SO₄²⁻ → fermentation sequence, the M5 plume-mixing zone and the M6 anaerobic dead zone remain hypotheses awaiting geochemical and metagenomic validation [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]. Flow direction is unresolved: turnover was not aligned uphill–downhill (Mantel ρ = −0.049, p = 0.580), and Procrustes analysis — a test of how well two ordination configurations superimpose, m² being the residual misfit — gave only marginal correspondence with the physical grid (m² = 0.379, p = 0.080) [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]. Field composition along a uranium gradient likewise reflects multiple geochemical and historical dimensions rather than metal tolerance alone [src: lab_field_ecology].

**Null and dispersion caveats stay as stated.** Fungal Round-2 history, tested on ITS2 (the fungal ribosomal internal transcribed spacer marker), was not statistically detectable (R² = 0.142, p = 0.090), and that null stands [src: lignin_community_enrichment]. PERMDISP, a test of within-group dispersion differences, was significant for 16S (p = 0.0004) and ITS (p = 0.0001), so the PERMANOVA signals mix location and dispersion; the design used 97% operational taxonomic units (OTUs, sequence-identity clusters coarser than ASVs) and n=3 per group, flooring pairwise tests near p~0.10 [src: lignin_community_enrichment].
