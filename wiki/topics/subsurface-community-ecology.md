# Subsurface Community Ecology

The deep subsurface — saturated sediments, groundwater, and clay-confined rock formations — hosts microbial communities that are hard to sample, slow to grow, and thinly represented in genome collections. This hub gathers what the BERIL corpus, built over the KBase Data Lakehouse, can say about how those communities are organized: which physical variables structure them, whether their genomes look specialized, which functional markers survive scrutiny, and how much apparent structure is biology rather than sampling design. Seven projects contribute: a spatially resolved 16S survey of the SSO subsurface site [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]; two comparative-genomics studies of clay-confined Bacillota_B [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory, clay_confined_subsurface]; a laboratory carbon-passage experiment on community assembly [src: lignin_community_enrichment]; an Oak Ridge field-versus-laboratory comparison along a uranium gradient [src: lab_field_ecology]; and two larger-scale cautionary analyses, a global soil predictability test [src: soil_frontier_genomics] and a cross-clade genome-size control on ecological breadth [src: phb_granule_ecology].

## Literature Context

Published subsurface microbial ecology has converged on a few generalizations that this corpus can be read against. Depth is repeatedly the dominant structuring axis: in a national survey of 733 newly constructed pristine groundwater wells, bacterial diversity declined with increasing well depth, habitat niche breadth narrowed with depth, and community assembly was predominantly deterministic rather than stochastic ([PMID 37468948](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37468948/)); vertical gradients in diversity and in assembly process were likewise resolved in near-surface glacier-fed lake sediments, where homogeneous selection dominated but weakened with depth ([PMID 37542806](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37542806/)). In deep sedimentary aquifers used for gas storage, microbial diversity was significantly related to geological level, with sulfate-reducing *Desulfotomaculum* — a Firmicutes lineage — identified as a key group ([PMID 31314939](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31314939/)), and sulfate reduction, fermentation, and metal reduction recur as the core anaerobic repertoire of coal- and lignite-bearing aquifers ([PMID 25852663](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25852663/)). Separately, high sulfate concentrations together with detected sulfur-reducing and sulfur-oxidizing organisms were taken to suggest that sulfur-related metabolic strategies are prominent in ~150 m fractured-rock boreholes of a mine system, where communities also proved highly dynamic at very fine spatial scales (<20 cm) ([PMID 34756066](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34756066/)). Finally, mixing between oxic and anoxic sources repeatedly differentiates co-located habitats: planktonic and sediment communities in a circumneutral hot spring share little taxonomic overlap and split along aerobic-autotroph versus anaerobic-heterotroph lines ([PMID 34586901](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34586901/)), while hydrothermal plume communities derive from seawater rather than from the seafloor habitats beneath them ([PMID 22695860](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22695860/)).

The corpus's SSO results sit inside this consensus and sharpen it quantitatively. That hydrogeological zone outranks well identity for sediments, with Firmicutes deep-enriched and Patescibacteria shallow-enriched, matches the depth-primacy pattern of [PMID 37468948](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37468948/), which also identifies Patescibacteria as shallow-aquifer keystone taxa; [[concepts/subsurface-hydrogeological-zonation]] extends it by partitioning variance explicitly and adding a meter-scale distance-decay term. Notably, the sediment result — well identity not significant — contrasts with the fine-scale borehole-to-borehole distinctiveness reported at <20 cm in fractured rock ([PMID 34756066](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34756066/)), and the corpus's own groundwater analysis, where well identity dominated, suggests the discrepancy is a material and flow-regime difference rather than a contradiction. The large groundwater-versus-sediment dissimilarity, with iron-oxidizing *Gallionella* and *Sideroxydans* in water against *Anaeromyxobacter* and *Arcobacter* in sediment, is a compartment dichotomy of the kind documented for planktonic versus sediment habitats elsewhere ([PMID 34586901](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34586901/)), now shown for a contaminated shallow aquifer. That sulfate reduction is the one anaerobic marker surviving phylogenetic control in clay-confined Bacillota_B is consistent with sulfate-reducing Firmicutes being a signature group of deep sedimentary aquifers ([PMID 31314939](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31314939/)); the corpus's addition is methodological — separating lineage from habitat signal, which these descriptive surveys do not attempt.

Three corpus results have no clear counterpart here. The genome-expansion result on [[concepts/subsurface-bacillota-specialization]] runs against the narrowing-niche-breadth expectation of [PMID 37468948](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37468948/), though the two measure different things (genome content versus occupancy breadth) and cultivation bias may explain the divergence. The demonstration that miscurated marker genes reversed an iron-reduction narrative is a caution none of the marker-based surveys above address. And the carbon-passage memory result on [[concepts/ecological-memory]] gives experimental support to what is only inferred in the field, where decades-old transient gas storage still appears to shape aquifer communities ([PMID 31314939](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31314939/)) — legacy as a measured, dominant variance term rather than a post-hoc explanation.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Depth zone organizes sediment communities more strongly than well identity.** In a PERMANOVA (permutational multivariate analysis of variance, a permutation test for community-composition differences) over 37 sediment core segments, hydrogeological zone explained 27.5% of variance (F = 4.05, p = 0.0001), while well identity explained 19.2% and was not significant (F = 0.80, p = 0.979) [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]. The distance structure matches: samples from the same well but different depths had median Bray–Curtis dissimilarity 0.977, versus 0.835 for the same depth zone in different wells [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]. Ten of 12 dominant phyla showed significant depth associations at p < 0.05, with Chloroflexi (Spearman ρ = −0.73) and Patescibacteria (−0.70) shallow-enriched and Firmicutes (ρ = +0.76) and Bacteroidota (+0.50) deep-enriched [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]. [[concepts/subsurface-hydrogeological-zonation]] carries the full depth-class breakdown and the meter-scale grid analysis, including a Mantel distance-decay result (ρ = 0.323, p = 0.029, 9,999 permutations) and a hypothesized northeast-to-southwest corridor of unusually similar communities through wells U3–M6–L7, whose attribution to contamination-plume flow remains a testable hypothesis [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology].

**Habitat compartment is a first-order variable, and the dominant spatial term differs by material.** Groundwater and sediment sampled at the same well differed with median Bray–Curtis 0.424 (range 0.364–0.450 across 5 wells) [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]. Groundwater was enriched in [[entities/rhodanobacter]] (1.23% → 3.62%, 2.9×), [[entities/gallionella]] (0.01% → 0.14%, 8.9×), and [[entities/sideroxydans]] (0.01% → 0.06%, 7.0×); sediment was enriched in [[entities/anaeromyxobacter]] (1.24% vs 0.03%) and [[entities/arcobacter]] (0.54% vs 0.00%) [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]. In groundwater from 5 wells sampled 9 days apart, well identity explained 49.9% of variance (p = 0.001), filter size 10.1% (p = 0.001), depth within the saturated zone 2.5% (p = 0.430), and date 0.8% (p = 0.998) [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]. Well-to-well similarity rankings were nearly unchanged across the interval (Mantel ρ = 0.867, p = 0.001), indicating short-term persistence of spatial organization — but filter size was also a substantial source of variation, and the design cannot separate persistent environmental forcing from stable well-specific properties, filter effects, or other sampling effects [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology].

**Cultured deep-clay genomes are expanded, not streamlined.** Comparing 10 deep-clay to 62 soil-baseline Bacillota_B genomes, mean genome size was 4,110,038 bp versus 3,046,124 bp (Cohen's d = +1.39, Mann–Whitney p = 0.025) and mean eggNOG orthologous-group (OG, a cluster of genes inferred to share ancestry and function) counts were 2,630 versus 2,106 (d = +1.30, p = 0.022), while completeness estimated by CheckM — a tool that scores genome completeness from lineage-specific single-copy marker genes — was statistically indistinguishable (94.7% vs 94.3%, d = +0.08, p = 0.93), so the size difference is not a quality artifact [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]. Per-OG Fisher tests across 14,109 Firmicutes-level OGs with Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate (FDR) correction returned 547 significantly enriched OGs at q < 0.05 [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]. Keyword classification assigned 42 to anaerobic respiration, 24 to sporulation-revival, 12 to mineral attachment or exopolysaccharide, and 462 to "other or unannotated"; manual inspection estimated the true anaerobic-respiration-related total at 80–100 of 547 [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]. [[concepts/subsurface-bacillota-specialization]] develops this into a self-sufficiency-versus-streamlining argument.

**Under phylogenetic control, only sulfate reduction survives as a compartment-specific signal.** In the compartment study, an anaerobic toolkit of Wood–Ljungdahl (WL, the reductive acetyl-CoA pathway for CO₂ fixation), group 1 [NiFe]-hydrogenase (NiFe), and dissimilatory sulfate reduction (SR) scored 1.889 in anchor_deep (n = 9) versus 0.033 in anchor_shallow (n = 30) and 0.393 in soil_baseline (n = 140) [src: clay_confined_subsurface]. Against soil baseline, WL had OR = 10.4 (p_BH = 0.004), NiFe OR = 10.5 (p_BH = 0.004), and SR OR = 33.8 (p_BH = 2.5×10⁻⁴), but *within* Bacillota_B only SR remained (5/5 deep vs 4/19 baseline, OR = ∞, p = 0.003, p_BH = 0.044); WL and NiFe tracked lineage [src: clay_confined_subsurface]. Five of 9 deep genomes carried SR markers against a rock-attached null expectation of 0.018 (binomial p = 4.0×10⁻¹²) [src: clay_confined_subsurface]. The corresponding iron-reduction result collapsed once markers were corrected — see [[entities/multi-heme-cytochrome-detection]] and the tension below.

**Community history persists after conditions change.** A two-round carbon-passage experiment provides the corpus's cleanest assembly result: Round-1 carbon history explained 58.9% of Round-2 community variance (F = 14.31, R² = 0.589, p = 0.002) versus 32.7% for the current Round-2 carbon source (F = 4.85, R² = 0.327, p = 0.018), with a reported memory index of ~0.50 [src: lignin_community_enrichment]. At 97% OTU resolution, lignin-history groups were Pseudomonas-dominated at 52–53% while lignin-plus-labile-history groups were Acinetobacter-dominant at 31–34% — genus-level amplicon assignments, not strain identifications [src: lignin_community_enrichment]. [[concepts/ecological-memory]] argues that prior selective regimes constrain later responses — directly relevant to reading subsurface spatial patterns, where contamination history and flow path are legacies as much as current conditions.

## Tensions and Caveats

**Genome expansion versus measured pathway completeness.** The expansion result supports a self-sufficiency model, but GapMind — an annotation tool scoring how completely a genome encodes defined amino-acid biosynthesis pathways — found completeness across 18 pathways of 16.22/18 versus 16.66/18 before filtering (p = 0.153, d = −0.17) and 15.50/18 versus 17.14/18 after (p = 0.009, d = −0.84), i.e. *lower* in the deep cohort [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory, clay_confined_subsurface]. That post-filter comparison rests on a shrunken cohort: quality filtering took the deep cohort from 9 to 6 genomes (shallow 30 to 30, baseline 150 to 137), GapMind saturates near 18 for many cultured bacteria, and with n = 9 before filtering only large effects (Cohen's d > 0.7) are reliably detectable [src: clay_confined_subsurface]. These metrics measure different things and must not be averaged; the full five-strand dispute is on [[conflicts/conflict--bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory--clay_confined_subsurface--conservation_fitness_synthesis--912a2415]].

**Marker definition can reverse an ecological narrative.** The original iron-reduction markers — KEGG Orthology identifiers K07811, K17324, K17323, KEGG Orthology being a controlled vocabulary of gene-function groups — are TMAO reductase and glycerol ABC transporter genes, not canonical iron-reduction markers [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]. Under a corrected detector built on PFAM protein-family domains (PF02085, PF22678) and a count of ≥4 CXXCH heme-binding motifs, all pairwise comparisons were nonsignificant (deep vs shallow OR 1.88, p = 0.46; deep vs baseline OR 1.80, p = 0.49; shallow vs baseline OR 0.96, p = 1.0) [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]. The two reports also label the retained signal differently — "sulfite reduction" versus dissimilatory "sulfate reduction" — while both report 5/9 positives [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory, clay_confined_subsurface]. See [[conflicts/conflict--bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory--clay_confined_subsurface--functional_dark_matter--cb2c2770]].

**Local structure does not generalize to a global clay effect.** Across 5,441 soil samples, the clay-shield test found low-clay cross-validation R² = −0.268 versus high-clay R² = −0.292, difference 0.024, 95% CI −0.423 to 0.161 — an interval including zero [src: soil_frontier_genomics]. Negative out-of-sample R² values do not separate genuine unpredictability from distributional shift or outlier leverage [src: soil_frontier_genomics].

**Genome size confounds ecological-breadth claims.** Across 27,690 species in GTDB (the Genome Taxonomy Database), polyhydroxybutyrate-positive species had median genome size 4.34 Mbp versus 2.44 Mbp, and the raw niche-breadth association (rho = 0.106) reversed to partial rho = −0.047 (p = 0.037) after genome-size control — though enrichment persisted within all four size quartiles (1.4–4.6×, all p < 1e-11) [src: phb_granule_ecology]. Related measurement-artifact disputes are collected on [[conflicts/conflict--ecotype_analysis--ecotype_env_reanalysis--env_embedding_explorer--ffd6c359]].

**Load-bearing sampling limits.** SSO redox assignments are taxonomy-to-trait inferences: genus-level functional annotation covered 21% of reads, class-level traits 78%, and SSO geochemistry was unavailable, with 221 registered sample tubes in [[entities/enigma-coral|CORAL]] not yet loaded [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]. Sediment was collected February–March 2023 and groundwater in September 2024, an 18-month offset [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]. The clay cohorts are cultivation-biased porewater isolates, with rock-attached Geobacter and Geothrix lineages and CPR/DPANN episymbionts essentially absent [src: clay_confined_subsurface]. The memory experiment used n = 3 per group, and PERMDISP (a test for differences in within-group dispersion) was significant for 16S (p = 0.0004) and ITS (p = 0.0001), so location and dispersion effects are entangled; the fungal history effect was not detectable (R² = 0.142, p = 0.090) [src: lignin_community_enrichment]. Field sorting is also multidimensional: across 108 Oak Ridge sites, 5 of 11 sufficiently prevalent genera showed significant uranium associations in both directions after FDR correction, while aggregate laboratory metal tolerance was only suggestive (Spearman rho = 0.503, p = 0.095, n = 12 genera) [src: lab_field_ecology].
