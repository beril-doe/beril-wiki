# Pangenome Structure and Genome Evolution

A *pangenome* is the union of gene families found across all sequenced genomes of a species: a **core** portion present in essentially every member genome and an **accessory** portion present in only some. How that partition is drawn — and what it predicts about function, fitness, ecology and transfer — is one of the load-bearing questions of comparative microbial genomics, because nearly every downstream inference in this corpus (which genes are essential, which are horizontally acquired, which respond to environment) is conditioned on it. The BERIL corpus speaks to this question at unusual scale and from unusually many angles at once: the KBase Data Lakehouse holds 1,011,650,903 gene rows spanning 293,059 genomes, 27,690 GTDB species clades or pangenomes, and 132.5M gene clusters [src: berdl_data_atlas], and projects here join that gene-content layer to mutant-fitness measurements, annotation pipelines, phylogenies and environmental metadata. The result is a body of work that both supports a classical picture of bacterial genome evolution and repeatedly shows that picture's quantitative edges to be artifacts of sampling.

## Literature Context

The pangenome concept originated in the observation that new genes keep appearing as more strains of a species are sequenced, formalized as a core genome present in all strains plus a dispensable genome present in only some, the total potentially orders of magnitude larger than any single genome ([PMID 16185861](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16185861/)). Reviews since frame this variation as horizontal transfer plus differential loss, structured by conflict between host chromosomes and the mobile elements that mediate exchange, and call the prevailing view "phenomenological and incomplete" ([PMID 31639358](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31639358/)); others attribute it to drift, selection, gain/loss balance and ecological and epistatic interactions, whose relative contributions were argued to need new theory and empirical data ([PMID 33423895](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33423895/)). Fitting the infinitely many genes model to 172 Bacilli genomes favoured three gene classes — essential, slow and fast — with most newly originating genes short-lived ([PMID 22752048](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22752048/)).

The corpus's functional signature matches single-species precedents: carbohydrate metabolism dominated the *Clostridium butyricum* core while its accessory genome carried numerous mobile elements of COG category L ([PMID 33105087](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33105087/)); metabolism genes were overrepresented in the *Burkholderia pseudomallei* core and mobile-element, disease and motility genes in its accessory fraction ([PMID 26484663](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26484663/)); the *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* core is enriched for carbohydrate and amino-acid metabolism and cell maintenance ([PMID 36258067](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36258067/)); and *Clostridium neonatale* genomes were predicted to carry 201 genomic islands, 13 insertion sequence families and one CRISPR-Cas type I-B system ([PMID 35550024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35550024/)). [[concepts/two-speed-bacterial-genome]] reproduces the pattern with per-species consistency statistics across 32 species and 9 phyla — an extension in scope, not a new claim. Its transfer results ([[concepts/horizontal-gene-transfer-driven-innovation]]) instantiate the chromosome/mobile-element conflict framing ([PMID 31639358](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31639358/)) but remain observational, with validation pending; the plasmid negative for carbohydrate-active enzymes is ICEfinder-conditioned, so the chromosomal/integrative reading ([[concepts/chromosomal-and-integrative-gene-transfer]]) is only suggestive beside island-mediated recombination in *B. pseudomallei* ([PMID 26484663](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26484663/)).

Three contributions go beyond this literature. The published work is overwhelmingly gene-content-only; joining core/accessory membership to mutant fitness makes the "essential class" posited by [PMID 22752048](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22752048/) directly measurable, and finds the essentiality–conservation gradient real but modest, with fitness breadth only weakly tied to core status. Second, where reviews leave openness drivers unranked, [[concepts/pangenome-openness-determinants]] finds metabolic pathway variability tracking openness where environment and phylogeny effects do not — though the corpus keeps that split as an open tension, the pathway association was positive in 13 of 18 genera but significant in only 5, and the latent-capability correlation rests on 22 clades. Third, the arguments that core fractions are sampling- and coverage-conditioned ([[concepts/pangenome-core-boundary-and-clade-size-bias]]) and that pooled cross-species correlations can attenuate to near zero relative to within-lineage ones ([[concepts/phylogenetic-confounding-of-pangenome-associations]]) sit awkwardly with quoting one core size or one "open" verdict per species ([PMID 33105087](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33105087/), [PMID 26484663](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26484663/)). Population-structure work anticipates this — *P. aeruginosa* phylogroups differ in accessory content and resistance profiles ([PMID 36258067](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36258067/)), and combined core/accessory/regulatory analysis of *E. coli* ST131 resolved substructure invisible to core-genome phylogeny ([PMID 27618184](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27618184/)) — but the corpus generalizes the warning to cross-phylum comparison.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Bacterial genomes run at two speeds, and the split has a clear functional signature.** Comparing COG categories — Clusters of Orthologous Groups, a controlled functional vocabulary — across 32 species spanning 9 phyla and 357,623 genes, novel or singleton genes were enriched in COG L (replication, recombination, mobile elements) by +10.88% with 100% consistency across species; in COG V (defense) by +2.83% with 100% consistency; and in COG S (unknown function) by +1.64% with 69% consistency [src: cog_analysis]. Core genes were correspondingly depleted relative to the novel class in translation (COG J, −4.65%, 97% consistency), nucleotide metabolism (−2.09%), coenzyme metabolism (−2.06%), amino-acid metabolism (−1.81%) and energy production (−1.75%) [src: cog_analysis]. [[concepts/two-speed-bacterial-genome]] develops this as a conserved metabolic engine plus an innovative, mobility- and defense-associated periphery — but essentiality does not map cleanly onto it: universally essential genes were 91.7% core versus 49.5% for orphan essential genes, and of 17,222 ortholog families, 859 (5.0%) were universally essential, 4,799 (27.9%) variably essential and 11,564 (67.1%) never essential [src: essential_genome].

**The core/accessory boundary is a measurement with known failure modes.** Linking mutant-fitness data to pangenome clusters produced 177,863 gene-to-cluster links at 100.0% median protein identity and 94.2% median gene coverage, assigning 145,821 genes (82.0%) to core clusters and 32,042 (18.0%) to auxiliary clusters [src: conservation_vs_fitness]. Clades of only 2 genomes give trivially high core fractions; the main *Escherichia coli* clade was unavailable for containing too many genomes; Keio mapped to a small clade at 26.1% coverage; and Dyella79 was dropped entirely for a locus-tag format mismatch producing a 0% join rate [src: conservation_vs_fitness]. Essential genes were 86.1% core versus 81.2% for non-essential genes (median odds ratio 1.56; 18 of 33 organisms significant after Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction, which bounds the expected share of false positives) [src: conservation_vs_fitness] — real, but modest, because most genes are core regardless. A separate ~194,000-gene, 43-bacterium analysis put the gradient at 82% core for essential genes versus 66% for always-neutral genes, with fitness breadth only weakly tied to core status (Spearman rho=0.086, p=8.1e-230) [src: fitness_effects_conservation]. [[concepts/pangenome-core-boundary-and-clade-size-bias]] treats core membership accordingly as sampling- and coverage-conditioned rather than a fixed biological label.

**Conservation extends to functional units, but only to units the assay can see.** Independent component analysis (ICA — a decomposition recovering co-varying fitness modules) found module genes 86.0% core versus 81.5% for all genes (OR=1.46, p=1.6e-87), with 577 of 974 modules (59%) >90% core, 349 (36%) mixed and 48 (5%) <50% core [src: module_conservation]. Module-family breadth did not predict core fraction (Spearman rho=-0.01, p=0.914) [src: module_conservation]. Critically, essential genes are absent from these modules because essential loci lack usable transposon-insertion data, so the module figure describes the measurable, non-essential genome [src: module_conservation].

**Openness tracks metabolic variability, not environment or phylogeny.** Pangenome *openness* — the tendency of a repertoire to keep expanding as genomes are added — showed no significant relationship to environment effect (Spearman rho = -0.05, p = 0.54) or phylogeny effect (rho = 0.03, p = 0.73) [src: pangenome_openness]. Yet variable metabolic pathways predicted openness strongly (rho=0.327, p=7.2e-71), rising to partial rho=0.530, p=2.83e-203 after controlling for genome count [src: discoveries], reproduced across 2,810 GTDB species with ≥10 genomes using 80 GapMind pathways, positive in 13 of 18 genera and significant in 5 [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. Latent capability rate — complete pathways with low observed fitness dependence — also correlated with openness (rho=0.69, p=0.0004, n=22 clades) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. See [[concepts/pangenome-openness-determinants]].

**Transfer evidence upgrades from functional enrichment to gene-tree and synteny data.** Among 30,497 high-quality environmental MAGs (metagenome-assembled genomes), 6,652 (21.8%) carried type IV secretion system (T4SS) or conjugative machinery, and T4SS-positive genomes had 10× higher mobile-element density (p<0.001) [src: t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt]. A GT2 glycosyltransferase gene tree yielded 77 HGT events — 65 spanning 2 phyla, 12 spanning ≥3 (15.6%) — with the strongest spanning 8 phyla, 35 genes, 82.9% syntenic; divergence and synteny were negatively correlated (Spearman ρ = −0.615, p<0.001) [src: t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt]. Carbohydrate-active-enzyme genes were not detected on plasmids by ICEfinder while 12 integrative mobilizable elements appeared among the top 100 accumulators, pointing to chromosomal or integrative routes ([[concepts/chromosomal-and-integrative-gene-transfer]]) [src: t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt]. Convergently, costly-and-dispensable genes were 7.45× more likely than costly-conserved genes to carry mobile-element keywords (OR=7.45, p=4.6e-71) [src: costly_dispensable_genes]; see [[concepts/horizontal-gene-transfer-driven-innovation]]. Acquisition depth itself is quantified in [[concepts/gene-function-acquisition-depth]]: 17,073,194 Sankoff-parsimony gain events give recent:ancient ratios of 24.5× for CRISPR-Cas (58.7% recent, 2.4% ancient) against 2.3× for tRNA-synthetase controls (24.7%, 10.7%) [src: gene_function_ecological_agora].

**Pooling species hides lineage structure.** Pangenome openness correlated positively with antimicrobial-resistance count in 8/10 tested phyla while the pooled correlation was near zero (rho=0.006), with the strongest within-phylum signals in Bacillota (rho=0.219, p=1.0e-16) and Bacillota_C (rho=0.374, p=3.1e-5) [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]. [[concepts/phylogenetic-confounding-of-pangenome-associations]] generalizes this; [[concepts/within-species-conservation-between-species-functional-divergence]] shows the mirror image, where within-species conservation coexists with between-species divergence (metal-important genes 87.4% core versus 76.9% baseline, OR=2.08, p=4.3e-162) [src: metal_fitness_atlas].

## Tensions and Caveats

Two headline conservation gradients — 86.1% versus 81.2%, and 82% versus 66% — come from different organism sets, phenotype categories and integration procedures and must not be silently reconciled [src: conservation_vs_fitness, fitness_effects_conservation]; this and the ambiguity of "conserved" itself (ortholog breadth versus graded taxonomic rank versus clade-core membership) are argued in [[conflicts/conflict--conservation_fitness_synthesis--conservation_vs_fitness--core_gene_tradeoffs--57107100]] and [[conflicts/conflict--amr_fitness_cost--conservation_fitness_synthesis--conservation_vs_fitness--314d9fee]]. Whether module core enrichment licenses any fitness inference is disputed in [[conflicts/conflict--conservation_fitness_synthesis--fitness_effects_conservation--module_conservation]]. The environment-versus-metabolism split in openness is held open in [[conflicts/conflict--discoveries--fitness_effects_conservation--metabolic_capability_dependency--db725c69]], and the pooled-versus-stratified association problem in [[conflicts/conflict--amr_environmental_resistome--amr_pangenome_atlas--amr_strain_variation--a54e27a7]]; whether missing genes in perturbation collections index biology or assay coverage is unresolved in [[conflicts/conflict--adp1_deletion_phenotypes--core_gene_tradeoffs--costly_dispensable_genes--470fcab9]]. Further load-bearing limits: core is not uniformly cheap — 25,271 genes (17.8%) showed condition-dependent trade-offs and were 1.29× more likely to be core (p=1.2e-44), with core-gene burden reversed for Cell Wall genes (−14.1 percentage points) [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]; COG annotations covered only ~70% of genes across 32 species [src: cog_analysis]; and the T4SS transfer interpretation remains observational, with threshold, BLAST and null-baseline validation pending [src: t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt].
