# Pangenome Structure and Genome Evolution

A bacterial pangenome is the union of gene families found across the sampled genomes of a species or clade, conventionally split into a *core* (present in effectively all sampled genomes) and an *auxiliary* or accessory fraction (present in only some). This topic asks what that split means biologically: whether it marks a real two-speed architecture in which conserved metabolism coexists with faster-turning accessory innovation, what drives a repertoire to stay open rather than saturate, and how much of any observed pattern is a property of bacteria rather than of which genomes were sequenced and successfully joined. The corpus speaks to this unusually directly because it holds both sides of the comparison at once: KBase Data Lakehouse pangenome clusters covering 1,011,650,903 gene rows across 293,059 genomes and 27,690 GTDB species clades [src: berdl_data_atlas], and gene-level fitness measurements that can be joined to them — 177,863 gene-to-cluster links at 100.0% median protein identity and 94.2% median gene coverage [src: conservation_vs_fitness]. The member concepts and conflict pages linked below organize what that join does and does not support.

## Literature Context

The pangenome framing this topic inherits is two decades old: a species is described by a core genome present in all strains plus a dispensable genome of genes found in some or single strains, with new genes still appearing after many strains are sequenced and modeling predicting discovery to continue past hundreds of genomes per species [PMID 16185861](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16185861/). Single-species studies supply the usual scale: *Burkholderia pseudomallei* was open at roughly 136 new genes per genome with a global core of 4568±16 homologs across 37 isolates [PMID 26484663](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26484663/); *Clostridium butyricum* was open with 1011 core, 4543 accessory and 1473 strain-specific genes over 24 strains [PMID 33105087](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33105087/); *Clostridium neonatale* gave a 6840-gene pangenome against a 3387-gene core from nine assemblies [PMID 35550024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35550024/); the *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* core is about 62% of average gene content [PMID 36258067](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36258067/). Mechanistically the field attributes pangenomes to gene gain by horizontal transfer plus differential loss, structured by chromosome–mobile-element conflict, drift, selection and ecology, while conceding the current view is phenomenological and incomplete [PMID 31639358](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31639358/), [PMID 33423895](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33423895/).

The corpus's functional signature is **consistent** with this literature and extends its scope. That accessory and singleton genes are enriched in COG L replication/recombination/mobile-element functions matches the numerous mobile genetic elements of the replication, recombination and repair (L) category found in the accessory genome of *C. butyricum* [PMID 33105087](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33105087/), and the accessory enrichment of mobile elements, disease and motility in *B. pseudomallei* [PMID 26484663](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26484663/) — but those are single species, whereas the corpus reports the pattern with 100% cross-species consistency over 32 species and 9 phyla. The mirror claim is not symmetrical: metabolism is reported as *over*-represented in the core of both *P. aeruginosa* [PMID 36258067](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36258067/) and *B. pseudomallei* [PMID 26484663](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26484663/), which should be read carefully against the corpus's negative core percentages for amino-acid, coenzyme and nucleotide metabolism, since the two are computed against different comparison classes.

Two corpus results are **novel** against these candidates. No cited paper tests pangenome openness against variable metabolic capability, so the corpus's reproducible metabolic correlate and its latent-capability result have no counterpart here; conversely, the corpus's null correlations of openness with environment and phylogeny effects sit **in tension** with reviews placing ecology and transfer barriers at the centre of pangenome structure [PMID 31639358](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31639358/), [PMID 33423895](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33423895/) — though the corpus's own coverage limit keeps that null provisional (see [[concepts/pangenome-openness-determinants]]). The corpus's sampling caveats are directly **supported**: across 172 complete Bacilli genomes, predicted core and pangenome sizes depended on the assumed tree shape, pangenome size rising linearly on a star tree but logarithmically on a coalescent tree, which fit the data better [PMID 22752048](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22752048/) — the same sampling-geometry sensitivity that makes the corpus's core fractions clade-size-conditioned. Annotation dependence is likewise corroborated: in *Mycobacterium marinum*, eggNOG and InterProScan gave complementary rather than identical functional assignments, and significant differences in gene gain and loss rates were themselves read as signalling variation in annotation accuracy and the presence of mobile genetic elements [PMID 40600140](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40600140/) — which is why the corpus's ~70% COG coverage matters. Finally, that the precise phenotypes of the majority of the *P. aeruginosa* accessory genome remain undetermined, a single-species review claim [PMID 21119020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21119020/), names the gap the corpus's fitness-joined accessory analyses begin to fill.

## What the Corpus Shows

**The core/accessory split carries a reproducible functional signature.** Across 32 species spanning 9 phyla and 357,623 genes, novel or singleton genes were enriched in COG L — Clusters of Orthologous Groups category L, covering replication, recombination and mobile elements — by +10.88% with 100% consistency across species, and in COG V (defense) by +2.83% and COG S (unknown function) by +1.64% with 69% consistency [src: cog_analysis]. Core genes were correspondingly depleted in translation (COG J, -4.65%), nucleotide metabolism (-2.09%), coenzyme metabolism (-2.06%), amino-acid metabolism (-1.81%) and energy production (-1.75%) [src: cog_analysis]. This is the empirical backbone of [[concepts/two-speed-bacterial-genome]]. It is a distribution, not a transfer measurement: the report itself treats horizontal gene transfer (HGT) as an interpretation of functional-category distributions rather than a direct observation of transfer events [src: cog_analysis].

**Independent gene-level evidence converges on mobile accessory material, without pinning the route.** Among 142,190 genes from 43 bacteria, 5,526 were both costly in laboratory fitness assays and dispensable, and this class was 7.45× more likely than costly-and-conserved genes to carry mobile-element keywords (OR=7.45, p=4.6e-71), with 44.5% orphan genes versus 13.1%, and median ortholog breadth 15 organisms versus 31 [src: costly_dispensable_genes]. Transfer-resolved evidence goes further: among 30,497 high-quality environmental metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs), 6,652 (21.8%) carried type IV secretion system (T4SS) or conjugative machinery, and T4SS-positive genomes had 10× higher mobile genetic element density (p<0.001) [src: t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt]. GT2 glycosyltransferase gene trees contained 77 detected HGT events including 32 normalized high-confidence cross-phylum events; 65 spanned 2 phyla and 12 spanned ≥3 (15.6%), with divergence and synteny negatively correlated (Spearman ρ = −0.615, p<0.001) [src: t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt]. [[concepts/horizontal-gene-transfer-driven-innovation]] and [[concepts/chromosomal-and-integrative-gene-transfer]] argue these lines together; the latter records that CAZy (carbohydrate-active enzyme) genes were not detected on plasmids by ICEfinder while 12 integrative mobilizable elements occurred among the top 100 accumulators — a detection-dependent negative, not proof that plasmids never mobilize these genes [src: t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt].

**But the conserved core is neither cheap nor uniformly indispensable, which blunts the simplest two-speed reading.** Module genes identified by independent component analysis (ICA, a decomposition method for finding coordinated fitness modules) were 86.0% core versus 81.5% for all genes (OR=1.46, p=1.6e-87), and 577 of 974 modules with ≥3 mapped genes (59%) were >90% core [src: module_conservation]. Yet 25,271 genes, or 17.8% of those examined, showed fitness below -1 in some conditions and above 1 in others, and these trade-off genes were 1.29× more likely to be core (OR=1.29, p=1.2e-44) [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]. Core-gene burden was function-specific, higher in Protein Metabolism (+6.2), Motility (+7.8) and RNA Metabolism (+12.9 percentage points) but reversed for Cell Wall genes (-14.1) [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]. Among 17,222 ortholog families, only 859 (5.0%) were universally essential, 4,799 (27.9%) variably essential and 11,564 (67.1%) never essential [src: essential_genome].

**Openness has one reproducible correlate — metabolic variability — and a string of nulls elsewhere.** Variable metabolic pathways predicted openness at rho=0.327, p=7.2e-71, strengthening to partial rho=0.530, p=2.83e-203 after controlling for genome count, across 2,810 GTDB species with at least 10 genomes using 80 GapMind pathways [src: discoveries, pathway_capability_dependency]. Within-genus correlations were positive in 13 of 18 genera and significant in 5, strongest for Clostridium (rho=0.534) and Eubacterium (rho=0.506) [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. Latent-capability rate — the fraction of genomically complete pathways with low observed fitness dependence — correlated with openness at Spearman rho=0.69, p=0.0004 across 22 clades [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Against this, openness showed no significant relationship with either environment effect (rho = -0.05, p = 0.54) or phylogeny effect (rho = 0.03, p = 0.73) [src: pangenome_openness]. [[concepts/pangenome-openness-determinants]] holds both results.

**Sampling and identifier geometry are load-bearing, not incidental.** In the integrated set, 145,821 linked genes (82.0%) fell in core clusters and 32,042 (18.0%) in auxiliary, with 7,574 singletons inside the auxiliary category [src: conservation_vs_fitness]. Clades with only 2 genomes can reach trivially high core fractions; 44 of 48 Fitness Browser organisms mapped to pangenome clades but only 33 entered downstream analysis, ten dropped for <90% DIAMOND coverage [src: conservation_vs_fitness]. The main *Escherichia coli* clade was absent for containing too many genomes, while Keio mapped to s__Escherichia_coli_E at only 26.1% coverage, and Dyella79 joined at 0% because gene tables used N515DRAFT_* while proteins used ABZR86_RS* [src: conservation_vs_fitness]. [[concepts/pangenome-core-boundary-and-clade-size-bias]] and [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] treat these as first-class constraints. Lineage structure is the companion problem: pangenome openness correlated positively with AMR (antimicrobial resistance — genes conferring resistance to antibiotics) count in 8/10 tested phyla, yet the pooled correlation was rho=0.006 [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] — the central case in [[concepts/phylogenetic-confounding-of-pangenome-associations]].

## Tensions and Caveats

The sharpest unresolved disagreement is over **what openness measures at all**. One line treats variable metabolic capability as a reproducible determinant or proxy; another rests on null correlations against the environment-versus-phylogeny axis; a third argues the environmental associations are coverage-limited, since only 6.8% of species (1,872 of 27,690) had sufficient AlphaEarth coverage [src: discoveries]. See [[conflicts/conflict--pangenome-openness-determinants--0ec0a9e1]] and [[conflicts/conflict--pangenome-openness-determinants--813fc4ab]]. A related scope tension — whether openness carries lineage signal or none — is recorded at [[conflicts/conflict--phylogenetic-confounding-of-pangenome-associations--5a54a7b7]], and the prophage-density case at [[conflicts/conflict--phylogenetic-confounding-of-pangenome-associations--7b9c5a6c]], where prophage marker density tracked AMR breadth at rho=0.572, p<10^-300, n=4,770 species, with partial rho=0.464 after genome-count control, yet local prophage proximity gave only OR=1.10 at 10 genes and a median species-level odds ratio of 0.85 [src: prophage_amr_comobilization].

Second, the **essentiality–conservation gap is directionally agreed but not dimensionally agreed**: 86.1% core for essential versus 81.2% for non-essential genes (median OR 1.56, 18 of 33 organisms significant after Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction) [src: conservation_vs_fitness], against 82% versus 66% for always-neutral genes across ~194,000 genes from 43 bacteria [src: fitness_effects_conservation]. The comparison classes and organism sets differ; see [[conflicts/conflict--core-genome-burden-paradox--52b853b5]] and [[conflicts/conflict--pangenome-core-boundary-and-clade-size-bias--f5ff24c2]]. Fitness breadth was only weakly associated with core status (Spearman rho=0.086, p=8.1e-230) [src: fitness_effects_conservation], so a highly significant gradient is still a weak predictor.

Third, several nulls must stay null. Module-family breadth did not predict core fraction (rho=-0.01, p=0.914) [src: module_conservation]. A genome-size-normalized metal repertoire score did not predict metal fitness [src: metal_fitness_atlas], and the metal-score-versus-isolation disagreement is held open at [[conflicts/conflict--composite-resistance-score-limitations--a9245bd1]], as is the ecology-versus-lineage AMR question at [[conflicts/conflict--phylogenetic-confounding-of-pangenome-associations--782017fa]]. Finally, COG coverage was approximately 70% of genes, so unassigned genes may skew the functional distributions, and 32 species may miss phylum-specific patterns [src: cog_analysis]; essential genes are structurally absent from ICA modules because essential loci lack usable transposon-insertion data [src: module_conservation].
