# Sampling Coverage and Statistical Confounding

Every comparative claim in this corpus rests on a genome, read, or strain collection assembled for another purpose. This topic asks what those collections license: when an apparent environment–genome or phenotype–genotype association reflects biology, and when it reflects which organisms were cultured, which coordinates carried satellite embeddings, which reference database the classifier used, or which study contributed the batch. The corpus speaks to this directly because several projects tested their own confounders instead of reporting the headline effect — a study-held-out validation that reversed a cross-study prediction, a direct test of clinical sampling skew as the cause of a weak signal, a compartment effect that lost most of its variance when three genome-rich species were dropped per group. The ten member concepts below span read classification, genome gene content, cultivated-strain phenotype and spatial coverage; nine recorded conflict pages hold tensions no single project resolves.

## Literature Context

The methodological literature available here speaks to one of this topic's central claims: that accuracy measured within a pool of studies does not survive validation across studies. A systematic simulation of cross-study validation (CSV) versus traditional cross-validation (CV), built by hybrid parametric/non-parametric bootstrap resampling of publicly available compendia of microarray, RNA-seq and whole metagenome shotgun microbiome studies of health outcomes, found lower accuracy under CSV than CV across multiple disease outcomes and omics platforms [PMID 30202918](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30202918/). That work also reports a result that is explicitly null for the usual suspects: imbalances in the prevalence of clinical and pathological covariates, and differences in gene covariance structure of the kind caused by batch, platform or tumor purity effects, had "very limited" contributions to the loss of accuracy when validating in new studies; only forcing identical generative models across studies greatly reduced the within-versus-across-study gap, leading the authors to suggest that unidentified heterogeneity, such as could arise from unmeasured confounding, may matter more than the easily identifiable sources [PMID 30202918](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30202918/). These conclusions come from simulation on resampled compendia, not from prospective field data.

This corpus is **consistent with** that expectation and, in one place, extends it past degradation into reversal. The GroupKFold result — random cross-validation giving R²=0.35 for environment and R²=0.24 for `study_id` alone, against R²=−0.30 for environment and R²=−0.39 for environment plus sequencing when complete studies are held out, with out-of-study detection AUC 0.56, approximately chance — is a more extreme instance of the CSV accuracy loss simulated above, obtained on 2,759 NMDC ReadbasedAnalysis runs from 9 studies in which biomes were approximately 80–100% nested within a single study. Negative R² means worse than predicting the mean, i.e. the environmental signal did not merely attenuate out of study; it failed to transfer at all. See [[concepts/study-batch-confounding-of-environmental-associations]].

The corpus's coverage-bias tests **echo the literature's null** in an unexpected register. Just as covariate imbalance failed to explain CSV accuracy loss [PMID 30202918](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30202918/), the demonstrably clinical skew of the AlphaEarth subset failed to explain the weak environment–gene-content relationship: environmental species did not show stronger partial correlations than human-associated ones (medians 0.051 versus 0.084; Mann–Whitney U=1536, p=0.83), and differential missingness of 21% versus 7% did not leave a stronger environmental signal after filtering. Both literature and corpus point the same way — the identifiable, measurable confounder was not the operative one, and a null stays null on each side. Where the corpus departs from the simulation is that its sharpest open disagreement has a fully documented, not hidden, source: the ecotype reanalysis used all embedded genomes (up to 3,505 per species) against the original's diversity-maximizing downsampling capped at 250, so absolute values are not comparable and neither is established as the more accurate estimate. That dispute remains recorded rather than resolved in the corpus's conflict pages; see [[concepts/sampling-depth-and-downsampling-effects]].

## What the Corpus Shows

**Coverage is the first filter, and it is severe at every layer.** AlphaEarth satellite-imagery embeddings — numeric vectors summarizing the landscape around a sampling coordinate — covered only 28.4% of genomes in an analysis of 13,381 genomes across 224 species, yielding correlation results for 172 species [src: ecotype_analysis]. In curated strains, 42,227 of 97,334 BacDive strains (43.4%) matched 6,426 GTDB species while 55,107 (56.6%) remained unmatched, and only 25,089 matched strains carried isolation-source metadata [src: bacdive_metal_validation]; a parallel bridge matched 37,368 strains (38.4%) to a pangenome species and metal score, and across its ten tested phenotype features matched strains ranged from 22,581 to 475 [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]. Host-associated labels are sparser: only 7,995 of 293,059 genomes (2.7%) had plant-associated annotations, and endophytes comprised only 29 species [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]. Spatial coverage is uneven in a way that tracks soil chemistry — frontier areas with Genomic Discovery Index (GDI, OTU Richness / (Mean Genome Completeness + 1)) above 1000 had mean pH = 6.74 versus 5.94 in mapped areas, a +0.8 unit gap the report frames as a hypothesis of systematic under-sampling of alkaline soil microbiomes rather than an established mechanism [src: soil_frontier_genomics]. Annotation coverage bounds functional inference likewise: roughly 38% of gene clusters had COG (Clusters of Orthologous Groups, a coarse functional binning scheme) annotations, leaving 62% unannotated [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation].

**Coverage bias is real and separately measurable, yet testing it as *the* explanation has repeatedly failed.** The AlphaEarth subset is demonstrably clinically skewed: among 83,287 genomes with embeddings, 38% were human-associated, including 16,390 Human clinical (20%), 13,466 Human gut (16%) and 1,669 Human other (2%), against Soil 6,073 (7%), Marine 5,850 (7%) and Freshwater 5,840 (7%) [src: env_embedding_explorer]; at species level, 106 of 224 species (47%) were majority human-associated, 47 (21%) majority environmental, 71 (32%) Mixed/Other [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]. But when that skew was tested as the cause of the weak environment–gene-content relationship, environmental species did not show stronger partial correlations than human-associated ones — medians 0.051 versus 0.084, with Mixed/Other at 0.109, and a one-sided Environmental > Human-associated Mann–Whitney U test giving U=1536, p=0.83 [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]. The continuous versions were equally null: environmental-genome fraction versus correlation strength rho=-0.085, p=0.25, and human-associated fraction rho=0.030, p=0.69 [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]. Differential missingness (NaN entries in embeddings or derived statistics) was likewise unequal — 21% for Environmental species versus 7% for Human-associated — yet filtering did not leave a stronger environmental signal behind [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis].

**Study nesting can invert a result outright.** Biomes were approximately 80–100% nested within a single study across 2,759 NMDC ReadbasedAnalysis runs from 9 studies, so environment and batch cannot be separated in cross-study univariate comparisons [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]. Random cross-validation gave R²=0.35 for environment and R²=0.24 for `study_id` alone; under GroupKFold, which holds complete studies out as groups, the environment model gave R²=−0.30 and environment plus sequencing gave R²=−0.39 — worse than predicting the mean — with out-of-study detection AUC 0.56, approximately chance [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]. This is the corpus's sharpest single demonstration that an apparently strong environmental association need not transfer.

**A reference database that cannot represent a taxon cannot testify to its absence.** GOTTCHA2 detected eukaryotic reads in 77% of the 2,759 runs (median fraction 2.7%, mean 13.3%, 20% of runs above 20%), while Kraken2 and Centrifuge produced approximately 0 domain-level Eukaryota signal because their NMDC reference databases were prokaryote-restricted [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]. Matrix differences in the GOTTCHA2 response were significant (Kruskal–Wallis H=77.8, p=1.3×10⁻¹⁷, all pairwise contrasts significant after Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction), but those values are classifier-derived and should be read as ordinal, not calibrated [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]. Taxonomic aggregation acts the same way on functional inference: strict and relaxed genus modes retained a mean mapped abundance fraction of 0.343 versus 0.031 for the species-proxy mode, and 380 mapped genera were multi-clade with a maximum of 433 clades per genus [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential].

**Taxonomic composition, not ecology, carries much of what looks like an environmental effect.** Across 607 root, rhizosphere and phyllosphere species, the original compartment effect of R² = 0.527 fell to R² = 0.072 after excluding three genome-rich species per compartment — an 86% loss — with refined PERMANOVA (permutational multivariate analysis of variance) R² = 0.071 against location-only db-RDA (distance-based redundancy analysis) R² = 0.060 [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]. Metal-ecology inference at genus level showed the same dependence: sequenced-genome coverage correlated with inferred metal-type diversity (r = 0.35, p = 2.6×10⁻¹⁹), and single-genome genera had lower metal-type counts than multi-genome genera (Mann–Whitney p = 5.1×10⁻¹⁰) [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]. Small matched sets inflate effects: only 8 H₂S-negative species were present, making d = -0.87 unreliable [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance].

**Confirmatory tests fail where exploratory ones succeed, and pooling muddies the unit of analysis.** Predeclared Spearman tests of site defense score against contamination were null in both genus modes (relaxed rho = 0.0587, p = 0.546, FDR q = 0.862; strict rho = 0.0682, p = 0.483, q = 0.849), robust across four contamination-index definitions, with all eight variant tests non-significant (q = 0.546) [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]. Separately, 1,067 of 2,759 runs were pooled from multiple biosamples, forcing run-level analysis and inheriting environment labels from one representative biosample by `MIN(biosample_id)` — a conservative bias that can weaken associations rather than manufacture them [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]. Clustering on features later tested reversed direction, not just power: *C. scindens* moved from non-significant to CD (Crohn's disease) increases in E1 (CI +0.68 to +0.87) and E3 (CI +1.13 to +1.71), two of the K=4 latent Dirichlet allocation ecotypes, with independent within-substudy evidence cutting a 33-species Tier-A list to 3 [src: pitfalls].

## Tensions and Caveats

The central unresolved disagreement is scale: the original ecotype analysis places the median environment–gene-content partial correlation near zero (0.0025 for environment versus 0.0143 for phylogeny across 172 species) while the reanalysis reports 0.081 across 183 species, and the reanalysis's own quotation of the original does not match the original's number — recorded in [[conflicts/conflict--confirmatory-exploratory-ecological-association-discordance--c1d44874]] and [[conflicts/conflict--selection-on-outcome-leakage--c66707fc]] [src: ecotype_analysis, ecotype_env_reanalysis]. The reanalysis used all embedded genomes (up to 3,505 per species) against the original's diversity-maximizing downsampling capped at 250, so absolute values are not comparable and neither is established as the more accurate estimate [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]. Whether clinical skew explains the weak signal stays open in [[conflicts/conflict--sampling-depth-and-downsampling-effects--e2e1ad79]], and whether the missingness null retires missingness as an explanation in [[conflicts/conflict--sampling-depth-and-downsampling-effects--8542320b]]. The pangenome-openness nulls (openness versus environment rho = -0.05, p = 0.54; versus phylogeny rho = 0.03, p = 0.73) show only that this one summary did not predict effect sizes in the available overlap, not that pangenome structure is independent of eco-phylogenetic dynamics; [[conflicts/conflict--sampling-depth-and-downsampling-effects--cd13abd8]] records the dispute over what that null closes [src: pangenome_openness]. Resolution cuts both ways: within-species ecotypes showed 170 of 257 COG tests significant after BH-FDR, with adaptive categories exceeding housekeeping (0.0136 versus 0.0064, p = 2.53 x 10^-6), although the effects were small and the analysis lacked within-species phylogenetic controls — against a genus-level null on contamination gradients (see [[conflicts/conflict--taxonomic-resolution-dependent-functional-inference--326d40ea]], [[conflicts/conflict--taxonomic-resolution-dependent-functional-inference--4b4aa6ed]], [[conflicts/conflict--phenotype-database-coverage-bias--539e6d8e]]) [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation, enigma_contamination_functional_potential]. Two caveats are load-bearing: classifier-derived and gene-content responses are not interchangeable measurements of "environment" [[conflicts/conflict--classifier-database-compatibility-in-taxonomic-quantification--8df7057b]], and missing laboratory protocol fields prevent adjusting for extraction, filtration or library-preparation effects at all [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]. Field–lab agreement stayed non-significant (Spearman rho=0.503, p=0.095, n=12 genera) even though five of 11 tested genera had FDR-significant bidirectional uranium associations [src: lab_field_ecology].
