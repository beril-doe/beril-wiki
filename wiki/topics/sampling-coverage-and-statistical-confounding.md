# Sampling Coverage and Statistical Confounding

Many apparent ecological and genomic patterns in this corpus depend on who was sampled, what the reference databases could represent, and which observations were treated as independent. This hub summarizes how the corpus handles that problem: uneven genome and sample collection, classifier and database scope, study and batch structure, pseudoreplication, and design-limited statistics. Several projects in the corpus report signals that shrink, reverse or were never testable once such structure is examined. This page has 5 evidence sub-themes, followed by tensions and a reading path.

## Literature Context

The candidate literature establishes that sequence-based microbiome inference is vulnerable to sampling and technical artifacts. Microbes are frequently difficult to culture, which confounds cataloguing of community members, and both assembly-based and mapping-based profiling struggle with organisms of limited similarity to sequenced genomes [PMID 28898207](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28898207/). Contaminating DNA is ubiquitous in commonly used extraction kits, varies greatly between kits and kit batches, and critically affects low-biomass samples in both 16S and shotgun work; the authors advise sequencing negative controls [PMID 25387460](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25387460/). A later review reaches the same conclusion and proposes a minimal "RIDE" checklist of controls [PMID 30497919](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30497919/). Primer choice likewise biases PCR-based diversity surveys, as shown by an in silico evaluation of 175 primers and 512 primer pairs against a SILVA reference [PMID 22933715](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22933715/).

Human gut studies offer examples of cross-study validation. A meta-analysis of eight geographically and technically diverse colorectal cancer metagenomic studies (n = 768) found that signatures derived from single studies maintained their accuracy in other studies, and that training on multiple studies improved detection [PMID 30936547](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30936547/). A more recent atherosclerosis integration used leave-one-study-out validation, while cautioning that heterogeneity across the integrated datasets remains [PMID 40785047](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40785047/). Drug use was likewise treated as a confounder in a cardiovascular cohort of 218 cases and 187 controls, where disease status rather than current drug use was the main distinguishing feature [PMID 29018189](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29018189/).

Against this background, the corpus's held-out-study result is consistent in spirit with the field's emphasis on cross-study validation but differs in outcome. The environmental signal in the National Microbiome Data Collaborative (NMDC) runs lost its predictive value when whole studies were held out, whereas the colorectal cancer signatures transferred across studies. That contrast is not a contradiction, because the two systems differ in host, response variable and design, and the corpus result rests on one dataset with biomes mostly nested within single studies. The corpus's classifier-scope findings, such as prokaryote-restricted databases returning almost no eukaryotic signal, extend the reference-limitation concern raised for metagenomic profiling [PMID 28898207](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28898207/), though the cited papers do not test classifier databases directly. The unmeasured extraction kit in the NMDC analysis falls squarely within the contamination and batch concern documented in the literature [PMID 25387460](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25387460/), so it is a reasonable hypothesis, not a demonstrated cause.

Cultivation difficulty is already noted in the literature, so the corpus's quantified skew toward human-associated genomes extends it. Several other corpus themes appear novel relative to these candidates: pseudoreplication from pooled runs, selection-on-outcome leakage, and the interpretation of nulls under limited statistical resolution. The candidate papers do not address these directly, so the corpus treatment is an extension drawn from its own analyses and not a replication of published findings.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Collections are skewed before any analysis starts.** Genome collections come disproportionately from culturable, clinical or well-studied lineages. In the AlphaEarth-embedded genome subset (AlphaEarth embeddings are learned numerical representations of a location's environment), 38% of 83,287 genomes were human-associated, while embeddings covered only 28.4% of 293,059 genomes [src: env_embedding_explorer]. Plant-associated annotations existed for only 7,995 of 293,059 genomes (2.7%), and endophytes comprised 29 species, below the 30-species testing threshold; the report frames this as a cultivation gap rather than ecological absence [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]. In BacDive (a curated strain-phenotype database), 37,368 of 97,334 strains (38.4%) matched a pangenome species and metal score [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]. See [[concepts/cultivation-collection-bias-in-ecological-genomics]] and [[concepts/phenotype-database-coverage-bias]]. Genomic under-representation also appears geographically: the Genomic Discovery Index (GDI, OTU richness divided by mean genome completeness plus one) was 902.36 for forest and 890.82 for cropland, versus 503.42 for grassland, and the report treats the alkaline-soil gap as a hypothesis, not an established finding [src: soil_frontier_genomics]. See [[concepts/genomic-under-representation]]. For hotspot maps, 11 significant hotspots were found among 289 grid cells, but the report requires sampling-effort and study-origin checks before interpreting them; those checks are untested [src: metal_resistance_global_biogeography]. See [[concepts/spatial-sampling-effort-confounding]].

**Sampling choices change the answer, but not always in the expected direction.** The ecotype reanalysis used all genomes with embeddings and reported a median partial correlation (association between environmental and gene-content similarity after removing covariate influence) of 0.081 versus 0.003 in the original downsampled analysis, yet the report cautions that absolute values are not comparable across methodologies [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]. Clinical bias was real, but environmental species (n=37) did not show stronger environment–gene-content correlations than human-associated species (U=1536, p=0.83), and the environmental-genome fraction showed no relationship with correlation strength (rho=-0.085, p=0.25) [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]. Across 172 species, significant positive environmental effects appeared in only 12 species (7.0%), significant negative effects in 4 (2.3%), and no significant effect in 156 (90.7%) [src: ecotype_analysis]. See [[concepts/sampling-depth-and-downsampling-effects]]. In the plant analysis, an initial compartment effect lost 86% of its R² after three genome-rich species per compartment were excluded, which the report attributes mainly to taxonomic sampling [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]. Confirmatory tests tell the same story: predeclared contamination–defense tests were null (for example rho = 0.0587, false-discovery-rate-adjusted q = 0.862), while exploratory, coverage-adjusted analyses are sensitive to mapping and covariates [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]. See [[concepts/confirmatory-exploratory-ecological-association-discordance]].

**Classifier scope and taxonomic resolution decide what is detectable.** In 2,759 NMDC (National Microbiome Data Collaborative) read-based runs from 9 studies, GOTTCHA2 detected eukaryotic reads in 77% of runs, but Kraken2 and Centrifuge returned approximately 0 domain-level Eukaryota signal because their databases were prokaryote-restricted [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]. A database that cannot represent a taxon cannot show that it is absent, and GOTTCHA2 values are relative or ordinal, not calibrated [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]. See [[concepts/classifier-database-compatibility-in-taxonomic-quantification]] and [[concepts/eukaryotic-dna-admixture-in-prokaryotic-metagenomes]]. The same logic applies to taxon-to-pangenome bridging: of 1,392 ENIGMA genera, 530 mapped and 862 were unmapped, and the strict-unique-clade proxy mode retained a mean mapped abundance fraction of 0.031 versus 0.343 for the other modes [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]. See [[concepts/taxonomic-resolution-dependent-functional-inference]].

**Study structure can masquerade as environment.** Each biome in the NMDC data was approximately 80–100% nested within a single study. For the logit-transformed eukaryotic-fraction response, random cross-validation gave R²=0.35 for environment versus R²=0.24 for study alone; with whole studies held out (GroupKFold), environment gave R²=−0.30 and environment plus sequencing R²=−0.39, and out-of-study detection AUC (area under the ROC curve, a discrimination score) was 0.56 [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]. See [[concepts/study-batch-confounding-of-environmental-associations]]. Unit-of-analysis errors are a related hazard: 1,067 of 2,759 runs were pooled from multiple biosamples, so the run was used as the statistical unit, and representative-biosample labels may add predictor noise [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]. In another project, treating each metagenome as independent inflated significance so that all 83 genera reached q<0.05 [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]. See [[concepts/pooled-run-pseudoreplication-and-metadata-label-noise]].

**Analysis design can manufacture or hide signal.** Clustering taxa and then testing the same taxa within clusters leaks the outcome into the grouping: held-out-species Jaccard overlaps were 0.230 (E1) and 0.064 (E3), below the 0.3 bound at which leakage dominates, and the 33-species candidate list was retracted [src: pitfalls, ibd_phage_targeting]. See [[concepts/selection-on-outcome-leakage]]. Correlated features similarly limit attribution: SHAP (a method assigning each input feature a share of a prediction) credit splits across a 63-feature genome-scale block, and Fitness Browser concordance was only 1.19×, although the report is inconsistent on whether that figure reflects the completed correlation-expanded test or a partial one [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma]. See [[concepts/feature-attribution-with-correlated-genomic-features]]. Finally, nulls are not all equal: with n=3 per group the minimum pairwise p-value is 0.10, so per-OTU nulls are uninformative, whereas the AMR temporal null across 513 species stands as observed, and the report's explanation that sparse date metadata (only 70% of genomes had parseable dates) hides real trends remains an untested hypothesis [src: lignin_community_enrichment, amr_strain_variation]. See [[concepts/null-results-under-limited-statistical-resolution]].

## Tensions and Caveats

- **Is the reanalysis effect really 27x larger?** The reanalysis comparator does not match the median the original analysis reports, so the absolute difference should not be read as biological [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis, ecotype_analysis]. See [[conflicts/conflict--classifier-database-compatibility-in-taxonomic-quantification--2b613a7b]] and [[conflicts/conflict--confirmatory-exploratory-ecological-association-discordance--c1d44874]].
- **Phylogeny versus classifier-derived environment.** Phylogeny generally outweighed environment for gene content, while classifier-derived eukaryotic fractions showed strong habitat contrasts that failed to transfer across studies [src: ecotype_analysis, euk_in_prok_correlates]. See [[conflicts/conflict--classifier-database-compatibility-in-taxonomic-quantification--8df7057b]].
- **Denominators.** The Kuehl overlap is quoted as 54% of feature rows outside the training space in one place and as 54% of training species detected in another [src: ibd_phage_targeting, discoveries]. See [[conflicts/conflict--classifier-database-compatibility-in-taxonomic-quantification--eb662e56]]. The BacDive report likewise gives 38.4% for two different denominators [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance].
- **Missingness versus ecology.** Environmental species lose data more often, yet removing them did not strengthen the signal [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]. See [[conflicts/conflict--sampling-depth-and-downsampling-effects--8542320b]] and [[conflicts/conflict--sampling-depth-and-downsampling-effects--e2e1ad79]]. Openness of the pangenome did not predict environment or phylogeny effects (rho = -0.05, p = 0.54; rho = 0.03, p = 0.73), which does not settle the sampling explanation [src: pangenome_openness]. See [[conflicts/conflict--sampling-depth-and-downsampling-effects--cd13abd8]].
- **Ecotypes versus lineage.** COG (Clusters of Orthologous Groups, a gene-function classification) differentiation (170 of 257 tests significant after Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction) lacks within-species phylogenetic controls [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. See [[conflicts/conflict--phenotype-database-coverage-bias--539e6d8e]] and [[conflicts/conflict--taxonomic-resolution-dependent-functional-inference--326d40ea]].
- **Load-bearing limits.** The NMDC environment-versus-study wording is not fully consistent with its own table, and extraction kit remains unmeasured [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]. The BacDive "validation" framing is stronger than the direct evidence [src: discoveries, bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance, bacdive_metal_validation]; see [[conflicts/conflict--phenotype-database-coverage-bias--40968e6d]]. Which rank reveals signal is unresolved [[conflicts/conflict--taxonomic-resolution-dependent-functional-inference--d921d3c1]], as is whether coarse COG structure is robust [[conflicts/conflict--taxonomic-resolution-dependent-functional-inference--4b4aa6ed]]. Shared-coordinate filtering is contested [[conflicts/conflict--spatial-sampling-effort-confounding--f75b7c12]], and a pipeline gap in magnitude should not be read as leakage [[conflicts/conflict--selection-on-outcome-leakage--c66707fc]].
