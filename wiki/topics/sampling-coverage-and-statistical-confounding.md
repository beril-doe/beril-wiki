# Sampling Coverage and Statistical Confounding

Every comparative claim in this corpus — that an environment shapes gene content, that a phenotype predicts metal tolerance, that a biome is genomically under-explored — is a claim about a sample, not about microbial life. The BERIL corpus is unusually well suited to examining that gap, because many of its projects re-ran the same question under different coverage, resolution, and validation regimes and reported what moved. What emerges is a consistent methodological result: across genome collections, phenotype databases, taxonomic classifiers, and remote-sensing embeddings, the design of the survey routinely explains as much of the reported signal as the biology does — and in several cases the corpus cannot yet say which is which. This hub summarizes what the projects collectively establish about coverage limits, lineage and batch confounding, and the analysis choices that move effect sizes by orders of magnitude.

## Literature Context

Published genomic epidemiology has already made sampling design an explicit precondition of inference rather than a caveat appended to it. In the SARS-CoV-2 work of [PMID 34713795](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34713795/), the authors state at the outset that spatio-temporal bias in genome sequence sampling precludes phylogeographical inference unless additional contextual data are supplied, and their design follows from that premise: 174 near-full-length genomes from 289 COVID-19 cases at a single Munich university hospital were interpretable only in combination with anamnestic travel history, records of interpersonal interactions, and perceived high-risk exposures. The payoff of that integration was resolution — transmission chains dissected to single events and single persons, and multiple independent introductions attributed mainly to travellers returning from Alpine skiing areas; in those early weeks of the first wave the reported rates of *presumable* hospital-acquired infection were 9.6% among patients and 54% among healthcare workers. The instructive structure of that result is that high-confidence inference was achieved *within* a densely characterized, metadata-rich collection, not by treating the global genome set as an unbiased sample of spread.

This corpus is consistent with that premise and pushes it into a domain where the rescuing metadata is itself missing. Where the published work could recover context by interview, the projects here find context partial by construction: geographic coordinates often missing or imprecise, environmental embeddings covering well under a third of genomes in one analysis, isolation-source records attached to only a subset even of the strains that could be matched to reference genomes, and functional annotation covering well under half of gene clusters — the material documented in [[concepts/cultivation-collection-bias-in-ecological-genomics]] and [[concepts/phenotype-database-coverage-bias]]. The corpus also reproduces the literature's structural lesson about the locality of inference in a purely ecological setting: an environment model fits respectably within-collection under random cross-validation, fails to generalize when complete studies are held out (R² = −0.30, worse than predicting the mean, with out-of-study detection near chance), yet recovers a modest positive fit inside a single protocol-constant soil survey — the argument developed in [[concepts/study-batch-confounding-of-environmental-associations]].

What the corpus contributes is quantitative and, against this candidate literature, unbenchmarked. No cited work here speaks to the magnitude of design leverage the projects measure: a median partial correlation of 0.003 versus 0.081 across downsampling regimes, characterized as 27x by the reporting project while that same report cautions that absolute values are not comparable across the two methodologies; an 86% loss of compartment R² after removing three genome-rich species; and FDR verdicts that flip with the strictness of coverage adjustment ([[concepts/sampling-depth-and-downsampling-effects]], [[concepts/confirmatory-exploratory-ecological-association-discordance]]). Equally novel is the corpus's *null* test of its own artifact hypothesis, where a clinically skewed genome subset did not yield the expected bias direction. These results should be read as internally supported but externally uncorroborated: the one relevant candidate is a viral outbreak investigation, not a microbial-ecology benchmark, so it can validate the framing of these findings but not their effect sizes.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Coverage sets a ceiling on what can be detected, before any statistics are run.** Reference and metadata coverage is partial everywhere the corpus looks. AlphaEarth environmental embeddings — fixed-length numeric vectors summarizing a sampling location's remotely sensed context — covered only 28.4% of genomes in the ecotype analysis, with geographic coordinates often missing or imprecise [src: ecotype_analysis]; a larger audit found embeddings for 83,287 of 293,059 genomes, of which 79,449 had valid values across all 64 dimensions [src: env_embedding_explorer]. Of 293,059 GTDB r214 genomes, only 7,995 (2.7%) carried plant-associated annotations, and endophytes comprised only 29 species [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]. In the BacDive bridge, 42,227 of 97,334 strains (43.4%) matched 6,426 GTDB species while 55,107 (56.6%) remained unmatched, and only 25,089 matched strains had isolation-source metadata [src: bacdive_metal_validation]. Functional annotation is equally partial: roughly 38% of gene clusters had COG (Clusters of Orthologous Groups, a coarse functional classification) annotations, leaving 62% unannotated [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. Most sharply, a reference database that cannot represent a taxon cannot supply evidence of its absence: Kraken2 and Centrifuge produced approximately 0 domain-level Eukaryota signal because their NMDC reference databases were prokaryote-restricted, while GOTTCHA2 detected eukaryotic reads in 77% of 2,759 runs (median 2.7%, mean 13.3%) [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]. See [[concepts/classifier-database-compatibility-in-taxonomic-quantification]] and [[concepts/cultivation-collection-bias-in-ecological-genomics]].

**Lineage composition and a handful of over-sampled taxa carry much of the apparent ecological signal.** In the BacDive phenotype analysis, taxonomy alone explained 35.4% of metal-tolerance variance while phenotype features alone explained 16.3%, and adding phenotypes to taxonomy gave R² = 0.345 [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]. The Gram-stain association — the strongest univariate result, Cohen's d = -0.61, p < 1e-60, n = 3,272 species — could not be tested within taxonomic classes because the signal was almost entirely between lineages, chiefly Actinomycetes versus Proteobacteria [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]. The plant compartment analysis makes the same point quantitatively: an initial R² = 0.527 fell to R² = 0.072 after excluding three genome-rich species per compartment, an 86% loss, with the refined effect at PERMANOVA R² = 0.071 [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]. At genome scale, phylogeny generally outweighed environment: median partial correlation 0.0143 for phylogeny versus 0.0025 for environment across 172 species, with phylogeny dominating in 60.5% [src: ecotype_analysis]. [[concepts/phenotype-database-coverage-bias]] develops this.

**Composition bias is real, and the corpus's own test of it came back null.** The embedding subset is clinically skewed: of 83,287 genomes with embeddings, 38% were human-associated, including 16,390 Human clinical (20%) and 13,466 Human gut (16%), against Soil, Marine, and Freshwater at 6,073 (7%), 5,850 (7%), and 5,840 (7%) [src: env_embedding_explorer]; of 224 reanalysis species, 106 (47%) were majority human-associated [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]. But the direct test did not support the artifact hypothesis: environmental species had median partial correlation 0.051 versus 0.084 for human-associated species (one-sided Mann–Whitney U = 1536, p = 0.83), and the environmental-genome fraction showed no relation to correlation strength (rho = -0.085, p = 0.25) [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]. Missingness was itself uneven — NaN rates of 21% for Environmental, 20% for Mixed/Other, and 7% for Human-associated species — a pattern the report argues would, if anything, bias toward a stronger environmental signal that was not observed [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]. Detail in [[concepts/sampling-depth-and-downsampling-effects]].

**Analysis-design choices move effect sizes by more than an order of magnitude.** Using all embedded genomes (up to 3,505 per species) rather than diversity-maximizing downsampling at 250 per species shifted the median partial correlation from 0.003 to 0.081, a difference the report characterizes as 27x, while stating that absolute values are not comparable across the two methodologies [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]. Coverage handling has the same leverage: the ENIGMA contamination–defense association gave FDR (false discovery rate) q = 0.0462 under relaxed coverage adjustment and q = 0.130 under strict adjustment, while the predeclared genus-level Spearman tests were null in both mapping modes (rho = 0.0587, p = 0.546, q = 0.862; rho = 0.0682, p = 0.483, q = 0.849) [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]. Prevalence filtering behaves similarly: a strict 5% within-environment threshold cut the MicrobeAtlas PGLS (phylogenetic generalized least squares) sample from 606 to 379 genera and moved the metal-type association from β = +0.0215, p = 1.5×10⁻⁴ to β = +0.0166, p = 0.092 [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]. See [[concepts/confirmatory-exploratory-ecological-association-discordance]] and [[concepts/taxonomic-resolution-dependent-functional-inference]].

**Study and batch structure decide whether an association travels.** Each biome was approximately 80–100% nested within a single study, so environment and batch could not be cleanly separated [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]. An environment model reached R² = 0.35 under random cross-validation — against R² = 0.24 for `study_id` alone — but R² = −0.30 under GroupKFold, in which complete studies are held out, with out-of-study detection AUC = 0.56, approximately chance [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]. Within the single NEON soil study, where protocol and batch were approximately constant, the same class of model gave R² = +0.17 ± 0.06 [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]. Two structural hazards compound this: 1,067 of 2,759 runs were pooled from multiple biosamples, forcing analysis at the run level to avoid pseudoreplication, with each pooled run inheriting metadata from one representative biosample and thereby acquiring predictor label noise [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]; and grouping observations on the same features later tested can inflate associations, as when held-out-species sensitivity returned Jaccard values of 0.230 for E1 and 0.064 for E3 [src: pitfalls]. See [[concepts/study-batch-confounding-of-environmental-associations]], [[concepts/pooled-run-pseudoreplication-and-metadata-label-noise]], [[concepts/selection-on-outcome-leakage]].

**Indices of "what is missing" inherit the survey that produced them.** The Genomic Discovery Index (GDI = OTU Richness / (Mean Genome Completeness + 1)) reached 902.36 for forest and 890.82 for cropland versus 503.42 grassland and 525.13 wetland, with high-GDI frontier areas at mean pH 6.74 against 5.94 in mapped areas [src: soil_frontier_genomics]. The report itself notes GDI can equal 902 with zero genomes, since completeness = 0 makes the denominator 1, and that the 1.3% forest–cropland gap is not distinguishable without bootstrap intervals [src: soil_frontier_genomics]. Likewise, dark-gene fractions from more than 35% to less than 15% across 48 organisms are read as annotation-depth variation rather than established functional differences [src: functional_dark_matter]. [[concepts/genomic-under-representation]] carries this.

## Tensions and Caveats

The central unresolved dispute is whether sampling skew *explains* the weak environment–gene-content signal or merely coexists with it: the skew is independently confirmed [src: env_embedding_explorer], yet the corrections it implies did not strengthen the correlation [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] — see [[conflicts/conflict--core_gene_tradeoffs--ecotype_analysis--ecotype_env_reanalysis--267f9584]]. A second, partly separable dispute is whether the 0.003-versus-0.081 magnitudes are even comparable [[conflicts/conflict--ecotype_analysis--ecotype_env_reanalysis--env_embedding_explorer--ffd6c359]]. A third asks whether finer resolution and looser coverage *reveal* functional structure or *manufacture* it, given the relaxed/strict FDR split [[conflicts/conflict--cog_analysis--ecotype_functional_differentiation--enigma_contamination_functional_potential--9514b16e]]. Fourth, trait effects that vanish under lineage or sampling controls — and one, the ecotype-functional result, whose phylogenetic control was never applied [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] — are collected in [[conflicts/conflict--bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance--ecotype_analysis--ecotype_functional_differentiation--d8143c37]]. Fifth, the corpus reports two different BacDive–GTDB bridge sizes, 37,368 strains across 5,647 species [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] versus 42,227 across 6,426 [src: bacdive_metal_validation]; these must not be pooled [[conflicts/conflict--bacdive_metal_validation--bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance--metal_cross_resistance--62152797]]. Finally, whether under-representation indices measure missing biology or survey design is itself contested [[conflicts/conflict--conservation_vs_fitness--functional_dark_matter--soil_frontier_genomics--dc6d2058]], and instability under refitting is not a specific diagnostic of leakage [[conflicts/conflict--ecotype_analysis--ecotype_env_reanalysis--ecotype_functional_differentiation--0a908b43]]. Two load-bearing caveats: strong within-collection association and failed cross-study transfer are not mutually exclusive [src: euk_in_prok_correlates], and small-n validations remain fragile — the heavy-metal isolate result (d = +1.00, n = 10) barely exceeded the ~0.93 minimum detectable effect at 80% power [src: bacdive_metal_validation].
