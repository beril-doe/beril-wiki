# Laboratory Fitness Versus Natural Selection and Gene Conservation

Laboratory fitness assays — principally RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing, which estimates mutant fitness from barcode abundance after pooled growth) — measure what a gene does for growth in a tested flask condition, whereas pangenome conservation records whether a gene has been retained across the genomes of a clade over evolutionary time. This topic hub covers the corpus's attempt to use the first as a proxy for the second, and the six member concept pages below argue over one recurring result: essentiality and conservation are associated, but only modestly. Across 194,216 protein-coding genes from 43 diverse bacteria, essential genes were 82% core while always-neutral genes were 66% core [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis], and fitness breadth correlated with core status at Spearman rho=0.086, p=8.1e-230 — statistically overwhelming, biologically weak [src: fitness_effects_conservation]. The corpus speaks to this because it holds the two measurement types side by side at scale, and because it repeatedly catches the proxy straining: 28,017 genes are both costly in the laboratory and conserved [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]; 3,683 essential-auxiliary and 1,259 essential-unmapped genes meet a laboratory viability requirement outside confidently identified core clusters [src: conservation_vs_fitness]; and within one organism, fitness importance rather than a field-versus-laboratory condition label is the more consistent correlate of core status [src: field_vs_lab_fitness].

## Literature Context

The field has largely abandoned essentiality as a binary property: it is context dependent and evolvable across bacteria, yeasts, animals and human cells [PMID 29033457](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29033457/). CRISPR interference titration in *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* quantified this continuum, identifying highly vulnerable genes and *invulnerable* essential ones, and — reference strain versus a single hypervirulent isolate — found incomplete conservation of vulnerability [PMID 34297925](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34297925/). In *Streptococcus sanguinis* SK36, 32 previously "essential" genes were deleted, 23 mutants growing extremely slowly, and 243 evolved populations yielded >1,000 spontaneous suppressor mutations: conserved essential functions can be bypassed, in one species [PMID 39071448](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39071448/), [PMID 41170999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41170999/). Conservation and essentiality track each other imperfectly rather than not at all. Of 750 genes deemed essential in an experimental study of *Synechococcus elongatus* PCC 7942, 494 were conserved across the cyanobacterial phylum; separately, the conserved but non-essential genes broadly comprised genes required under stress conditions [PMID 28594867](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28594867/). A distinct transposon study of the same organism (~250,000 mutants) called 718 of 2,723 genes essential under laboratory conditions and reported tight overlap with well-conserved genes, the exceptions — a dispensable TCA segment — carrying the biological interest [PMID 26508635](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26508635/). Across hundreds of genome-scale metabolic models and 40 species over >60 conditions, essentiality was significantly more conserved over long evolutionary distances than nutrient utilization, and synthetic lethality significantly less so [PMID 25363780](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25363780/).

The corpus's central result is **consistent** with this picture and extends it in scale rather than in strength: essential genes 82% core against 66% for always-neutral genes, and fitness breadth correlating with core status at Spearman rho=0.086, p=8.1e-230 — statistically overwhelming, biologically weak, a gradient spanning only 16 percentage points, which the corpus reads as fitness importance being no strong standalone predictor of conservation. That is a 43-organism quantification of the partial overlap seen in single cyanobacterial genomes [PMID 28594867](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28594867/), [PMID 26508635](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26508635/). The callability worry in [[concepts/laboratory-fitness-versus-natural-selection]] — gene length lifting CV-AUC from 0.548 to 0.645 — turns on length-dependent insertion density, which no candidate paper addresses; the nearest literature is a different method dependence: in *Clostridioides difficile* R20291, CRISPRi confirmed >90% of 181 targeted genes while a new Tn-seq run called 346 essential genes, only 283 shared with the earlier 404-gene set [PMID 40919935](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40919935/).

Two corpus claims sit less comfortably. The ICA module result (86% core versus an 81.5% baseline, odds ratio 1.46) is in **tension** with protein-complex analyses, where fewer than 10% of 285 *E. coli* "gold standard" complexes were fully conserved across seven species, just 14 across 95% of 894 genomes, and no clear relationship between essentiality and complex conservation was observed — poorly conserved complexes still contained many essential proteins [PMID 25723151](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25723151/). Pangenome essentiality work remains small and in silico by comparison: 57 essential genes across 110 *Gardnerella* strains, 38.15% core reactions, four core-essential genes in simulated vaginal environments [PMID 36511689](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36511689/). Against that, the burden paradox of [[concepts/core-genome-burden-paradox]] — core genes more often positive-fitness on deletion, 24.4% versus 19.9%, with 28,017 genes both costly and conserved — and the AMR null of [[concepts/pangenome-conservation-fitness-decoupling]] have no counterpart in these candidates and are **novel** here.

## What the Corpus Shows

**The essentiality–conservation gradient is real, directionally consistent, and weak.** Three independent linkages find the same sign. The 43-bacterium synthesis puts essential genes at 82% core against 66% for always-neutral genes [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]; the companion ~194,000-gene analysis reproduces the gradient with genes affecting 20+ experiments at 79% core and genes affecting 1–5 experiments at 71% core [src: fitness_effects_conservation]; a separate 33-organism Fitness Browser–pangenome linkage reports 86.1% core for essential genes versus 81.2% for non-essential, median odds ratio 1.56, with 18 of 33 organisms significant after Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate correction (BH-FDR, a multiple-testing correction) [src: conservation_vs_fitness]. The gradient spans only 16 percentage points between the extreme categories, so fitness importance is not a strong standalone predictor of conservation [src: fitness_effects_conservation]. See [[concepts/laboratory-fitness-versus-natural-selection]] for the full argument.

**Conservation and laboratory cost point in opposite directions often enough to constitute a paradox.** Core genes are *more* likely to be burdensome when deleted: 24.4% of core genes showed positive deletion fitness (the mutant outgrew the reference strain) versus 19.9% of accessory genes [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]. This contradicts a simple genome-streamlining expectation — accessory genes were not systematically more burdensome, they were less costly under the tested conditions [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]. The joint matrix classifies 28,017 genes as both costly in the laboratory and conserved, the strongest available evidence for genes maintained despite measured cost, and 5,526 as costly-and-dispensable, candidates for ongoing loss [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]. [[concepts/core-genome-burden-paradox]] develops this; [[concepts/costly-dispensable-gene-loss]] takes the second class further, showing costly+dispensable genes are 7.45 times more likely than costly+conserved genes to carry mobile-element keywords (OR=7.45, p=4.6e-71) and contain 44.5% orphan genes versus 13.1% among costly+conserved [src: costly_dispensable_genes].

**The paradox is function-specific, not genome-wide.** Core-minus-non-core burden differences were +6.2 percentage points for Protein Metabolism, +7.8 for Motility, and +12.9 for RNA Metabolism, but reversed to −14.1 percentage points for Cell Wall genes, where non-core genes were the more burdensome [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]. The same analysis identified 25,271 true trade-off genes — fitness below −1 in some conditions and above 1 in others — representing 17.8% of genes examined, with core genes enriched at odds ratio 1.29, p=1.2e-44 [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]. Conservation therefore marks conditional cost-benefit structure as much as uniform importance.

**Conservation is partly a property of coordinated modules, not only single genes.** Independent component analysis (ICA, a decomposition method that extracts coordinated fitness modules from experiment matrices) found 1,116 modules across 32 organisms; module genes were 86% core against an 81.5% baseline (odds ratio 1.46, p=1.6e-87), and 59% of modules were more than 90% core [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]. The dedicated module analysis confirms 86.0% versus 81.5% and reports 577 of 974 modules with at least three mapped genes exceeding 90% core [src: module_conservation]. Critically, module-family *breadth* did not predict conservation (rho=-0.01, p=0.914) — a null the corpus keeps [src: module_conservation]. [[concepts/pangenome-conservation-fitness-decoupling]] carries this alongside a sharper null: core (N=638) and accessory (N=163) antimicrobial resistance (AMR) genes had identical mean fitness of −0.024, Cohen's d = 0.002, Mann-Whitney U p = 0.33 [src: amr_fitness_cost].

**Labelling a condition "field" does not rescue the proxy.** In *Desulfovibrio vulgaris* Hildenborough across 757 Fitness Browser experiments, field-stress-important genes were 83.6% core and field-core-important genes 82.4% core against a 76.3% non-essential baseline, but lab-nutrient genes reached 81.4% — importance, not ecological label, carried the signal [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]. Logistic models gave 10-fold cross-validated area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (CV-AUC) values of 0.517 for field fitness alone, 0.531 for laboratory fitness alone, and 0.548 for both, rising to 0.645 only after adding gene length [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]. [[concepts/lab-field-fitness-concordance]] tests the transfer directly: 29 of 47 testable clusters were concordant (61.7%), one-sided binomial p = 0.072 with Wilson 95% CI [0.474, 0.742], while Fisher's combined probability gave p = 0.031 — statistics answering different questions, not one resolved claim [src: functional_dark_matter]. A field abundance test across 108 Oak Ridge groundwater sites gave a positive but non-significant aggregate tolerance association (Spearman rho = 0.503, p = 0.095, n = 12 genera) with five of 11 genera significant in both directions [src: lab_field_ecology], and an ecotype analysis across 172 species found phylogeny dominant over environment in 60.5% of species, with no significant environmental effect in 156 species (90.7%) [src: ecotype_analysis].

## Tensions and Caveats

The headline numbers do not agree on magnitude. [[conflicts/conflict--core-genome-burden-paradox--52b853b5]] frames it exactly: 86.1% versus 81.2%, or 82% versus 66%? Both point the same direction, but the comparison classes and organism sets differ, and the corpus forbids averaging them [src: fitness_effects_conservation, conservation_vs_fitness]. [[conflicts/conflict--laboratory-fitness-versus-natural-selection--239d08e3]] raises the sharper worry: gene length lifted the DvH model from 0.548 to 0.645 CV-AUC, so the gradient may partly reflect callability — short genes receive fewer insertions and core genes tend to be longer [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]. The broader analyses did not test length as a covariate [src: fitness_effects_conservation, conservation_fitness_synthesis].

Whether conservation predicts cost at all is disputed by gene class. [[conflicts/conflict--pangenome-conservation-fitness-decoupling--e470a03a]] and [[conflicts/conflict--pangenome-conservation-fitness-decoupling--e503d1e4]] set the AMR null (p = 0.33; mechanism strongly predicted conservation status at χ² = 69.3, p = 1.4×10⁻¹³, but not cost, Kruskal-Wallis H = 0.65, p = 0.89) against the genome-wide gradient [src: amr_fitness_cost, fitness_effects_conservation]. A class-specific null and a modest average gradient can both be true.

Which conservation metric is used changes the answer. [[conflicts/conflict--comparative-conservation-metric-calibration--84eb9535]] contrasts binary core membership with graded taxonomic breadth; [[concepts/comparative-conservation-metric-calibration]] shows the cost concretely — a species-count scoring variant correlated with the original ranking at Spearman ρ = 0.982 yet retained only 62% top-50 and 58% top-100 overlap [src: functional_dark_matter].

Load-bearing limits: clades containing only 2 genomes could have trivially high core fractions, because a gene present in both genomes is 100% core [src: conservation_vs_fitness]; 44 of 48 organisms mapped but only 33 entered downstream analysis, with ten excluded for <90% DIAMOND coverage [src: conservation_vs_fitness]. Essentiality was inferred under library-construction conditions and essential genes were slightly shorter, consistent with insertion bias [src: conservation_vs_fitness]. Novel singleton genes showed near-zero mean fitness, but poor transposon coverage is an alternative to true neutrality [src: fitness_effects_conservation]. Condition-specific fitness did not establish niche-specific fitness [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]. AlphaEarth covered only 28% of genomes, 83K/293K [src: functional_dark_matter].
