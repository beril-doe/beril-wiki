# Laboratory Fitness Versus Natural Selection and Gene Conservation

Transposon-mutant fitness assays tell you what a gene costs its host *in a flask*; pangenome conservation tells you which genes lineages have kept *across evolutionary time*. The two are routinely used as if they were the same measurement of "importance," and this corpus is unusually well positioned to test that equivalence: eleven projects link random barcode transposon sequencing (RB-TnSeq — pooled mutant fitness estimated from barcode abundance after growth) data from the Fitness Browser to species pangenomes, to environmental metadata, and to field metagenomes. The result is a consistent but *weak* coupling. Laboratory fitness importance predicts conservation better than chance and better than the ecological label attached to a growth condition, yet it leaves most of the variance in gene retention unexplained — and conserved genes are, if anything, the ones most likely to look costly on the bench. This hub summarizes what the corpus establishes, where its numbers disagree, and which measurement artifacts are load-bearing.

## Literature Context

Published work treats laboratory essentiality and evolutionary conservation as correlated but loosely coupled. A cross-species survey reported a "universal exponential relation between gene essentiality and conservation," with essential, information-processing genes under stronger purifying selection than a metabolism-biased nonessential set, while confirming that orthologs essential in one species need not be essential in another ([PMID 29678658](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29678658/)). In cyanobacteria only 494 of 750 experimentally essential genes were conserved phylum-wide, leaving a conserved-but-nonessential class of stress-condition and specific metabolic genes ([PMID 28594867](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28594867/)). Comparative metabolic modelling across hundreds of species found essentiality significantly more conserved than nutrient-utilization ability and synthetic lethality significantly less conserved, with phenotypic similarity dropping mainly at the genus level ([PMID 25363780](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25363780/)). Thirteen dense transposon libraries spanning five Enterobacteriaceae genera found roughly a third of genes essential in one strain non-essential in a relative, a core essential genome of 201 genes, and an absence of evidence for genus-specific essential genes ([PMID 39207104](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39207104/)). Deleting 32 "essential" *Streptococcus sanguinis* genes yielded 23 viable, slow-growing mutants whose suppressors bypass widely conserved functions ([PMID 39071448](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39071448/), [PMID 41170999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41170999/)); pangenome re-analysis placed *S. pyogenes* essential genes at about a quarter of the core genome ([PMID 39012116](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39012116/)).

This corpus is consistent in direction and adds scale. Two separate linkages each place important genes more often in the core than their comparison set — 82% core for essential versus 66% for always-neutral genes in the 43-bacterium cohort, and 86.1% versus 81.2% for non-essential genes in the independent 33-organism linkage — kept apart here because their cohorts, filters and denominators differ. Both reproduce the qualitative relation of [PMID 29678658](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29678658/) while showing the gene-level association is weak. The conserved-but-costly class at [[concepts/core-genome-burden-paradox]] mirrors the conserved-but-nonessential class of [PMID 28594867](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28594867/): where that work invoked unassayed stress conditions, this corpus supplies the matching observation that core genes carry the strong condition-specific and trade-off phenotypes, and that 28,017 genes are costly in the flask yet retained.

Two contributions are less well covered. First, ecological labelling is tested directly: field-stress and lab-nutrient classes sat above the *Desulfovibrio vulgaris* Hildenborough non-essential baseline, while heavy-metal-important genes only trended below it non-significantly (OR=0.77, q=0.14), and cross-validated prediction of core status from fitness barely exceeded chance ([[concepts/lab-field-fitness-concordance]]) — a comparison the strain- and condition-focused literature does not anticipate. Second, the costly-and-dispensable class, dominated by mobile-element signatures, ties laboratory burden to gene loss, complementing endosymbiont phylogenomics where lifestyle shifts spike gene loss and constrained, conserved functions are lost at lower rates ([PMID 41500269](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41500269/)). The corpus's caveats — apparent neutrality from poor transposon coverage, 678 uncallable essential DvH genes, an unmodelled gene-length bias — track the transposon-density effects and insertion-sequencing biases documented in [PMID 39207104](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39207104/), and are what inducible mutagenesis targets by converting binary essentiality into quantitative fitness ([PMID 38826325](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38826325/), [PMID 40148565](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40148565/)); CRISPRi titration in *M. tuberculosis* argues likewise that all-or-none essentiality discards a continuous, incompletely conserved vulnerability axis ([PMID 34297925](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34297925/)).

## What the Corpus Shows

**A conservation–essentiality gradient exists, and it is modest.** Across approximately 194,000 genes from 43 diverse bacteria, essential genes were 82% core while always-neutral genes were 66% core, and fitness breadth correlated with core status only weakly despite overwhelming significance (Spearman rho=0.086, p=8.1e-230) [src: fitness_effects_conservation]. The 194,216-gene synthesis over the same 43-bacterium cohort reports the same 82%-versus-66% contrast [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]. An independent 33-organism linkage of Fitness Browser essentiality calls to pangenome clusters reproduces the *direction* at a smaller effect size: 27,693 putative essential genes were 86.1% core versus 81.2% for non-essential genes, median odds ratio 1.56, with 18 of 33 organisms significantly enriched after Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction (BH-FDR, a multiple-testing adjustment) at q < 0.05 [src: conservation_vs_fitness]. The two headline fractions come from different cohorts, filters and denominators and must not be averaged — see [[concepts/laboratory-fitness-versus-natural-selection]].

**Conserved genes are disproportionately *burdensome* in the laboratory.** 24.4% of core genes showed positive fitness when deleted — the mutant outgrew the reference — versus 19.9% of accessory genes [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]. The effect is function-specific rather than genome-wide: core-minus-non-core burden differences were +6.2, +7.8 and +12.9 percentage points for Protein Metabolism, Motility and RNA Metabolism, but reversed to −14.1 percentage points for Cell Wall genes [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]. Conserved genes also carry the conditional phenotypes: core genes were 1.78x more likely to show strong condition-specific effects and 1.29x more likely to be trade-offs [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis], and 25,271 true trade-off genes (17.8% of genes examined) were enriched in the core at odds ratio 1.29, p=1.2e-44 [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]. Genes with strong condition-specific annotations were 77.3% core versus 70.3% without (OR=1.78, p=1.8e-97) [src: fitness_effects_conservation]. Elaborated at [[concepts/core-genome-burden-paradox]].

**Costly *and* dispensable is a separate, mobile-DNA-flavoured class.** Of 142,190 genes from 43 bacteria, 5,526 were both costly in the laboratory and non-conserved [src: costly_dispensable_genes]. These are 7.45 times more likely than costly+conserved genes to carry mobile-element keywords (OR=7.45, p=4.6e-71), and the SEED category "Phages, Prophages, Transposable elements, Plasmids" is enriched 11.7-fold (FDR=1.3e-17) [src: costly_dispensable_genes]. They are also shallower and shorter: median ortholog breadth 15 organisms versus 31, 44.5% orphan genes versus 13.1%, median length 615 bp versus 765 bp [src: costly_dispensable_genes]. The complementary cell — 28,017 genes costly in the lab yet conserved — is the corpus's strongest *signature* (not measurement) of purifying selection in unassayed environments [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]. See [[concepts/costly-dispensable-gene-loss]].

**Fitness importance beats the field-versus-lab label.** Within *Desulfovibrio vulgaris* Hildenborough, 757 Fitness Browser experiments and 2,725 non-essential genes with pangenome links were partitioned by condition class: field-stress-important genes were 83.6% core (n=298, OR=1.58, q=0.026) and lab-nutrient genes 81.4% core (n=452, OR=1.36, q=0.037), against a 76.3% non-essential baseline — but heavy-metal-important genes were 71.2% core (n=198, OR=0.77, q=0.14), *below* baseline [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]. Prediction was poor throughout: cross-validated AUC was 0.517 for field fitness, 0.531 for laboratory fitness, 0.548 combined, and 0.645 only after adding gene length [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]. Ecological labelling of a condition is thus not the axis that carries conservation signal.

**Lab phenotypes predict field distributions only conditionally.** A pre-registered mapping found 29 of 47 testable clusters concordant (61.7%), with 100% concordance across 4 pH tests and 78% across 9 nitrogen-source tests; the significance is equivocal (one-sided binomial p = 0.072, Wilson 95% CI [0.474, 0.742]; Fisher's combined p = 0.031 — different questions, not one resolved claim) [src: functional_dark_matter]. Of 137 clusters tested for environmental-category enrichment, only 10 reached FDR < 0.05 [src: functional_dark_matter]. At Oak Ridge, aggregate laboratory metal tolerance tracked the high/low-uranium abundance ratio across 108 groundwater sites only suggestively (Spearman rho = 0.503, p = 0.095, n = 12 genera), while 5 of 11 genera showed significant uranium associations in *both* directions [src: lab_field_ecology]. Whole-genome gene content across 172 species was better explained by phylogeny than environment (median partial correlation 0.0143 versus 0.0025; environment significant and positive in 12 species (7.0%), negative in 4 (2.3%), absent in 156 (90.7%)) [src: ecotype_analysis]. See [[concepts/lab-field-fitness-concordance]].

**"Conservation" is itself several non-interchangeable metrics.** An eggNOG-based breadth score assigned 30,721 of 30,756 dark-gene clusters (99.9%) to universal breadth — useless for ranking [src: functional_dark_matter]. A species-count variant correlated at Spearman ρ = 0.982 with the original ranking yet shared only 62% of the top 50 and 58% of the top 100 [src: functional_dark_matter]. A GTDB r214 re-analysis instead resolved 11,774 root ortholog groups into graded tiers (55.9% kingdom-level, 6.5% mobile, 0.5% species-level) [src: functional_dark_matter]. See [[concepts/comparative-conservation-metric-calibration]].

## Tensions and Caveats

The two core fractions for essential genes — 82% over ~194,000 genes from 43 bacteria [src: fitness_effects_conservation] and 86.1% over the 33-organism linkage [src: conservation_vs_fitness] — are not reconcilable by averaging; the full accounting is at [[conflicts/conflict--acinetobacter_adp1_explorer--adp1_triple_essentiality--alphafold_msa_annotation--cc872022]] and [[conflicts/conflict--amr_fitness_cost--conservation_fitness_synthesis--conservation_vs_fitness--314d9fee]].

The gradient does not hold in every gene set. Core (intrinsic) AMR genes (N=638) and accessory (acquired) AMR genes (N=163) had identical mean fitness of −0.024 (Cohen's d = 0.002, Mann–Whitney p = 0.33) [src: amr_fitness_cost] — a genuine null inside a subset where the genome-wide gradient predicts a difference; see [[concepts/pangenome-conservation-fitness-decoupling]].

The core boundary is sampling-dependent. Clades with only 2 genomes yield trivially 100% core calls; the main *Escherichia coli* clade was absent for having too many genomes, and the Keio BW25113 library mapped to a small clade at 26.1% coverage [src: conservation_vs_fitness]. Elsewhere, core was called at ≥95% prevalence over a median of 9 GTDB genomes per species (range 2–399) [src: amr_fitness_cost].

Apparent neutrality may be missing measurement. Singleton and novel genes had near-zero mean fitness, but poor transposon coverage may produce that neutrality [src: fitness_effects_conservation]; 678 essential DvH genes were excluded outright because no mutants were recovered, and those genes were 80.1% core [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]. Essential genes were also slightly shorter on average, consistent with insertion bias, and no length-adjusted conservation model was fitted [src: conservation_vs_fitness]. Both threads are developed at [[conflicts/conflict--conservation_fitness_synthesis--conservation_vs_fitness--field_vs_lab_fitness--e8524ac8]] and [[conflicts/conflict--core_gene_tradeoffs--field_vs_lab_fitness--fitness_effects_conservation--dbc9f83b]].

Finally, module-level and gene-level conservation disagree in strength: module genes were 86.0% core versus 81.5% of all genes (OR 1.46, p=1.6e-87) [src: module_conservation], a clear enrichment sitting beside a weak genome-wide association — see [[conflicts/conflict--conservation_fitness_synthesis--fitness_effects_conservation--module_conservation]] and, for whether environmental signal survives methodological choices at all, [[conflicts/conflict--ecotype_analysis--ecotype_env_reanalysis--env_embedding_explorer--ffd6c359]].
