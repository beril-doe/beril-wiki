# Subsurface and Soil Community Ecology

This topic asks what structures microbial communities in soils and the subsurface: depth and flow, contamination, treatment history, warming, and lineage-level genome content. The corpus speaks to it through amplicon and metagenome surveys of the Oak Ridge subsurface, a warming experiment at Harvard Forest, NEON soil metagenomes, lignin enrichment cultures and genome-collection comparisons. Most findings come from a single report and are correlational, so the sections below say how far each claim reaches.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Depth, flow and plume geometry organize subsurface communities.** In the Subsurface Observatory (SSO) sediment cores, hydrogeological zone explains 27.5% of community variance (PERMANOVA, permutational multivariate analysis of variance), while well identity explains 19.2% and is not significant [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]. See [[concepts/depth-and-hydrogeological-zonation-of-subsurface-microbial-communities]]. Across the 3×3 well grid, similarity decays with distance (Mantel ρ = 0.323, p = 0.029), and a NE→SW corridor of wells is more similar than distance predicts [src: discoveries, enigma_sso_asv_ecology]; see [[concepts/meter-scale-spatial-structure-and-distance-decay-of-subsurface-communities]]. The report explains these patterns with a contamination plume model [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]. That model is an interpretation, and its geochemical predictions are untested because SSO geochemistry is not loaded [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]; see [[concepts/geochemical-and-contamination-plume-control-of-subsurface-community-structure]]. Taxonomy-based process inference then maps a redox ladder onto the grid, with denitrification peaking at M5 and fermentation at L9. The trait assignments are literature-based, not direct genomic evidence [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]; see [[concepts/taxonomy-based-functional-inference-of-biogeochemical-processes-and-the-redox-ladder]].

**Lifestyle and pH partition communities.** Sediment-attached and groundwater communities differ, with median Bray-Curtis dissimilarity 0.424 within wells. An 18-month sampling offset confounds this comparison [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]. See [[concepts/attached-versus-planktonic-communities-in-groundwater-and-sediment]]. In Oak Ridge groundwater, two anti-correlated genus clusters appear, and globally the Rhodanobacter-Ralstonia-Dyella cluster occupies environments 1.35 pH units more acidic and 6.9°C warmer than the Brevundimonas-Caulobacter-Sphingomonas cluster [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma]. See [[concepts/ph-driven-niche-partitioning-and-genus-co-occurrence-in-contaminated-subsurface-communities]]. Cultured genome collections also carry a bias. Clay and mixed groundwater/aquifer comparisons both show enrichment of aerobic respiration, motility and denitrification markers in cultured genomes. The report suggests this is a general porewater bias, but it tested only two lithologies [src: oak_ridge_cultivation_gap, clay_confined_subsurface]. See [[concepts/porewater-bias-as-a-general-signature-of-cultivation-based-subsurface-collections]]. In clay cohorts, the corrected iron-reduction comparison between cohorts is non-significant, and the sulfate-reduction signal in deep clay stands [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory, discoveries]; see [[concepts/iron-and-sulfate-reduction-signatures-in-clay-subsurface-porewater-versus-rock-attached-communities]].

**Contamination and metals: null where tested most strictly.** In the ENIGMA subset, confirmatory tests of contamination against stress-related functional potential were null, including against uranium-only and other index variants. Exploratory coverage-aware defense signals weakened under global multiple-testing control [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential, soil_metal_functional_genomics]. See [[concepts/contamination-and-metal-gradients-as-drivers-of-community-functional-potential]]. At the genus level, metal-gene-rich genera have more positive co-occurrence partners in all three MicrobeAtlas strata (PGLS, phylogenetic generalized least squares, β = 138.4–210.5). The signal is correlated with niche breadth, and the report did not run the control that would separate the two [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology]. See [[concepts/co-occurrence-network-structure-and-metal-tolerance-guilds]]. Metal-gene density predicts narrower pH and composite niches, and environment predicts community metal-gene content poorly [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology]; see [[concepts/environmental-drivers-of-genus-niche-breadth-and-metal-gene-density]]. In groundwater, metal-diversity genera show a modest prevalence association (Spearman ρ = +0.112, p = 0.0019) but no enrichment (ρ = +0.042, p = 0.242) [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]; see [[concepts/field-validation-of-metal-diversity-genera-in-contaminated-groundwater]]. At the extreme, 37 acid mine drainage MAGs (metagenome assembled genomes) are led by acidophilic, metal-tolerant lineages and carry stress-response genes. The evidence is descriptive, with n=37 [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]; see [[concepts/community-function-of-rare-earth-acid-mine-drainage-mags]].

**Soil chemistry, habitat and warming.** NEON KEGG ortholog profiles separate by habitat (pseudo-F of 137, p = 0.001), and within soil pH explains more than horizon does [src: neon_mag_functional_discovery]; see [[concepts/habitat-and-soil-chemistry-structuring-of-metagenomic-functional-gene-content]]. Within one batch-controlled NEON soil study, eukaryotic read fraction varies with vegetation and site (Kruskal H=119.1 and H=310.4) [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]; see [[concepts/within-study-environmental-drivers-of-eukaryotic-dna-in-soil-metagenomes]]. At Harvard Forest, warming shifts organic-horizon Actinobacteria up and Acidobacteria down (both q 0.049), but the treatment effect on whole-community structure is not significant (R²=7.6%, p=0.069) [src: harvard_forest_warming]; see [[concepts/warming-response-of-soil-microbial-community-composition]]. A curated carbon-cycling KO list is enriched among warming-up KOs only in organic-horizon DNA. No individual RNA KO survives FDR (false discovery rate) correction [src: harvard_forest_warming]; see [[concepts/functional-gene-responses-to-soil-warming]]. Heated mineral soil has fewer detectable metabolites (155 vs 167 ChEBI per sample, MW p=0.012), which is suggestive only [src: harvard_forest_warming]; see [[concepts/soil-metabolite-richness-under-warming-and-substrate-depletion]].

**Treatment history and selection in enrichments.** One lignin round collapses bacterial Shannon diversity from 6.46 to 3.16 and favors Pseudomonas and Acinetobacter [src: lignin_community_enrichment]; see [[concepts/selective-filtering-of-communities-by-lignin-enrichment]]. Fungi respond more extremely but noisily, so taxon-level patterns are suggestive [src: lignin_community_enrichment]; see [[concepts/fungal-community-responses-to-lignin-and-labile-carbon]]. In Round 2, bacterial history explains 58.9% of variance versus 32.7% for current carbon source, which the report reads as ecological memory. The fungal signal is not statistically detectable [src: lignin_community_enrichment]; see [[concepts/ecological-memory-and-historical-contingency-in-serially-passaged-communities]].

**Lineage-level genome content and inference from abundance.** Genus abundance atlases show where candidate carbon-compound utilizers live (83/86 genera detected in 1719 NMDC metagenomes) but not whether they are active [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]; see [[concepts/biome-abundance-atlases-as-a-proxy-for-catabolic-potential]]. Taxonomy-inferred prophage burden correlates with pH and temperature across 6,365 samples [src: prophage_ecology]; see [[concepts/abiotic-correlates-of-prophage-burden-in-metagenomes]]. Pre-registered trait predictions were confirmed in NMDC, though compositional coupling may inflate the signal [src: functional_dark_matter]; see [[concepts/metagenome-based-cross-validation-of-genomic-trait-predictions]]. Fifteen cold peat Acidobacteriota MAGs carry an ε-lactone hydrolase gene that cultured relatives lack. No enzyme was expressed [src: neon_mag_functional_discovery]; see [[concepts/biocatalyst-potential-of-uncultured-soil-bacterial-enzymes]].

## Tensions and Caveats

**Contamination effects are inconsistent across scales.** ENIGMA confirmatory tests are null and robust to index definition [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]. Genus-level metal-gene analyses report positive associations [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology], and the groundwater analysis says it does not uniquely support a metal-contamination mechanism [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]. These designs differ in unit and scale, so they are not directly reconcilable.

**Clay shielding is not supported.** The one test found no gain in predictive accuracy in high-clay soils (difference 0.024, 95% CI −0.423 to 0.161). All models have negative out-of-sample R² [src: soil_frontier_genomics]; see [[concepts/clay-content-as-a-protective-modifier-of-soil-microbial-functional-potential]].

**Plume explanations are interpretation.** The depth, corridor and hotspot patterns come from one site and one sampling event [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]. The Procrustes correspondence between the ordination and the grid is only marginal (p = 0.080) [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology].

**Inference chains are unvalidated.** The prophage inference assumes genus-level conservation of prophage content, which may fail for recently acquired or lost prophages. It was validated for the PHB project but not independently for prophage genes [src: prophage_ecology]. Separately, the functional_dark_matter report says its NMDC signal is inflated by compositional coupling, and its high rate of significant tests (76 of 105) likely reflects confounding [src: functional_dark_matter].

**Single-site and low-power evidence.** The warming results rest on one site, with no individual RNA KO passing FDR [src: harvard_forest_warming]. The lignin fungal data are noisy [src: lignin_community_enrichment].
