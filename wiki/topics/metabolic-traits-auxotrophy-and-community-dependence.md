# Metabolic Traits, Auxotrophy, and Community Dependence

Whether a microbe can make its own building blocks, or must obtain them from neighbours, is one of the oldest questions in microbial physiology and one of the hardest to answer from sequence alone. This corpus attacks it from four directions at once: computational pathway calls across hundreds of thousands of genomes, pooled mutant-fitness assays that measure what a gene's loss actually costs, community metabolomics that measures what is floating in the environment, and cultured-isolate phenotype databases. The result is a coherent but deflationary picture. Encoded metabolic capability is abundant and reasonably well predicted; *dependence* — on a pathway, on a substrate, on a partner organism — is condition-specific, sparsely measured, and repeatedly smaller than the genomic potential would suggest. Reading these projects together, the corpus's main contribution is a set of quantitative boundaries on what a "complete pathway" licenses you to conclude.

## Literature Context

Published work has established that amino-acid auxotrophy is common but far from universal, and that it is ecologically structured. A pathway-model survey of 26,277 bacterial genomes across 12 phyla conservatively estimated that 78.4% of taxa can synthesize all amino acids, with auxotrophy concentrated in obligate intracellular parasites and streamlined free-living lineages, and auxotrophic taxa more abundant in host-associated and fermented-food habitats than in soil or aquatic systems ([PMID 37993466](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37993466/)). Genome-scale metabolic models (GEMs) have supplied the complementary mechanistic view: across 55 *E. coli* and *Shigella* strains, most pan-genome metabolic content consisted of alternate catabolic routes for unique nutrient sources rather than biosynthetic variation, while a minority of strains had lost pathways for methionine, tryptophan or leucine ([PMID 24277855](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24277855/)); 439 *Lactococcus* GEMs found strain-level differences in carbon-source utilization and auxotrophies, with cysteine synthesizable in only 11% of strains ([PMID 41910316](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41910316/)). Community modelling has repeatedly inferred that these gaps are filled by exchange — glucogenic amino-acid trade overcoming auxotrophies from an incomplete TCA cycle in 836 anaerobic-digestion models ([PMID 32905861](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32905861/)), conserved amino-acid and B-vitamin cross-feeding in Tara Oceans bacterioplankton where streamlining and auxotrophy jointly shape assembly ([PMID 38548725](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38548725/)), and cost-driven auxotrophic cross-feeding structuring 507 lactic-acid-bacteria communities ([PMID 41074291](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41074291/)).

This corpus is broadly consistent with the biosynthetic side of that picture and sharply more cautious about the inferential side. Its finding that amino-acid biosynthesis is near-universally complete in mapped model bacteria, and only rarely latent at scale, matches the 78.4% prototrophy estimate and the *E. coli* pan-genome result that variation concentrates in catabolism, not biosynthesis ([PMID 37993466](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37993466/), [PMID 24277855](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24277855/)); the corpus's much higher latency rate for carbon-source utilization than for amino-acid pathways, described on [[concepts/metabolic-capacity-specialization]], extends that observation from encoded content to measured fitness cost. Likewise, the corpus's ecotype and accessory-genome results refine the strain-variation theme of the *Lactococcus* and *E. coli* GEM studies with a genome-fluidity correlate.

Where the corpus departs is in what it refuses to conclude. The published cross-feeding literature infers exchange from model-predicted complementarity ([PMID 32905861](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32905861/), [PMID 38548725](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38548725/), [PMID 41074291](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41074291/)); this corpus tests provisioning directly against measured ambient metabolite intensity and finds the signal real but weak, pathway-specific, with only two amino acids surviving FDR correction, one running the opposite direction, and several pathways untestable — an empirical check on [[concepts/community-metabolic-interdependence]] that the modelling papers do not provide. Its central novelty is the capability-versus-dependency split itself: fitness assays show a large minority of complete pathways to be fitness-neutral and some incomplete pathways to carry fitness-important genes, which reframes a "complete pathway" as a hypothesis rather than a requirement — precisely the kind of discrepancy between model and organism flagged, but not quantified at scale, in single-strain GEM curation against transposon and Biolog data ([PMID 39158303](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39158303/)). The corpus's demonstration that genomes predict whether an organism grows far better than how fast, argued on [[concepts/capability-versus-kinetic-predictability]], has no counterpart among these candidates and stands as a genuine addition.

## What the Corpus Shows

**A complete pathway is not a measured requirement, and a sizeable minority are fitness-neutral.** The corpus's central distinction, developed on [[concepts/biosynthetic-prototrophy-and-auxotrophy]] and [[concepts/capability-versus-kinetic-predictability]], separates predicted pathway capability from experimentally observed fitness dependency. Across 161 organism–pathway combinations from 7 model bacteria and 23 [[entities/gapmind]] pathways, 57 pairs (35.4%) were Active Dependency, 66 (41.0%) Latent Capability, 24 (14.9%) Incomplete but Important, and 14 (8.7%) Missing [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. A larger analysis of 1,695 complete pathway–organism pairs from 48 organisms classified 267 (15.8%) latent, 547 (32.3%) intermediate, and 881 (51.9%) active, with pathway category strongly predicting dependency class (χ²=163.6, df=4, p=2.5×10⁻³⁴) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Crucially, the "Incomplete but Important" class runs the other way too: fitness-important genes occur in pathways GapMind calls incomplete [src: pathway_capability_dependency], so a predicted gap is a testable hypothesis rather than an auxotrophy call.

**Amino-acid biosynthesis is the conserved, actively required end of the spectrum; carbon catabolism is where latency lives.** In a seven-organism pilot, 17 of 18 amino-acid biosynthesis pathways were complete in all 7 mapped organisms, six organisms scoring 18/18 and *Desulfovibrio vulgaris* 17/18 (94.4%) [src: essential_metabolome]. Scaled up, amino-acid biosynthesis was predominantly active — only 48 of 735 pairs (6.5%) latent — whereas carbon-source utilization was latent in 217 of 892 (24.3%) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. The single amino-acid gap, serine biosynthesis in *D. vulgaris*, is explicitly held as a hypothesis: a divergent enzyme, non-canonical route, or annotation gap could produce the same signal, and no growth test was performed [src: essential_metabolome].

**The community-provisioning (Black Queen) signal is real but weak, pathway-specific, and coverage-limited.** On [[concepts/community-metabolic-interdependence]], community-weighted pathway completeness correlated negatively with ambient amino-acid intensity for 11 of 13 testable pathways (binomial sign test p = 0.011) across 220 samples and 80 pathways [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology, discoveries]. Only two associations survived Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction (FDR — the expected share of false positives among calls declared significant): leucine (r = −0.390, q = 0.022, n = 62) and [[entities/arginine]] (r = −0.297, q = 0.049, n = 80) [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology, discoveries]. Tyrosine ran the other way at r = +0.419 [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology], methionine was directionally consistent but non-significant (r = −0.496, q = 0.117, n = 18) [src: discoveries], and cysteine, histidine and lysine were untestable for lack of matching compounds [src: discoveries, nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]. Carbon use, not amino-acid biosynthesis, dominated community structure: PC1 explained 49.4% of variance and separated soil from freshwater at p<0.0001, while amino-acid pathways loaded on PC2 at 16.6% [src: discoveries, nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]. This is evidence *compatible with* provisioning, not proof of exchange — GapMind measures genomic potential, not expression or flux [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology].

**Metabolic capacity is specialist-dominated, accessory-borne, and tracks genome fluidity.** In the ENIGMA carbon census, mean versatility was approximately 1.19 capacities per genome (median 1, maximum 4); of 3,109 genomes, 675 carried exactly 1 of 8 callable capacities and only 18 carried at least 3 [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] — see [[concepts/metabolic-capacity-specialization]]. Capability is partly accessory: core-only versus all-gene completeness gaps were 0.146 for leucine and valine, 0.141 for arginine, 0.140 for lysine, and 0.140 for threonine biosynthesis [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. Variable pathway count tracked pangenome openness after controlling for genome count (partial Spearman rho=0.530, p=2.83e-203) [src: pathway_capability_dependency], and latent-capability rate correlated with openness at the clade level (Spearman ρ = 0.69, p = 0.0004, n = 22 clades) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Pathway-completeness clustering across 225 species yielded a median of 4 metabolic ecotypes per species (maximum 8) [src: pathway_capability_dependency].

**Genomes predict *whether* an organism grows far better than *how fast*.** Across 46,389 genome × condition pairs from 727 genomes with genus-blocked holdouts, binary-growth AUC (area under the receiver-operating-characteristic curve) was 0.620 overall, rising to 0.775 for amino acids and 0.780 for nucleosides but falling to 0.435 for nitrogen [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma]. Continuous µmax, lag and yield were not predictable across genera, giving negative R² in both the pair model and a bulk-feature regression, even though n_unique_KOs correlated with µmax at r = +0.42 — a phylogenetically confounded association with zero cross-genus predictive power [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma].

## Tensions and Caveats

**How much latent capability is really latent?** The seven-organism analysis found that all 66 Latent Capability pairs became fitness-important under at least one condition type, especially nitrogen limitation, stress, or carbon limitation — but this can arise by construction from a median-based importance threshold [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. The 48-organism analysis held 15.8% latent, with the fraction ranging from 4.7% to 21.1% across 16 threshold combinations [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. These are different organism sets, pathway sets and classification rules and must not be averaged; see [[conflicts/conflict--adp1_deletion_phenotypes--adp1_triple_essentiality--metabolic_capability_dependency--61c559d9]].

**Conservation does not separate latent from active.** Mean pathway conservation was 0.869 for 248 latent capabilities versus 0.829 for 755 active dependencies, Mann–Whitney U p = 0.94 for active > latent, rank-biserial r = 0.052 [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] — the pathways an organism has but does not need were, if anything, the more conserved set.

**Matched-assay agreement can look perfect while the broader picture is latent.** In FW300-N2E3, concordance was 100% across 21 [[entities/kescience-fitnessbrowser]] comparisons and 13 GapMind comparisons [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency], yet complete pathways are routinely latent at scale [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]; see [[conflicts/conflict--essential_metabolome--fw300_metabolic_consistency--metabolic_capability_dependency--96fcb005]].

**Occurrence and completeness are not activity.** 3-hydroxybenzoic-acid utilizers reached 0.90 field prevalence at genus resolution, while the same project classes its environmental atlas as an occurrence proxy, not evidence of degradation [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] — the boundary argued on [[concepts/occurrence-versus-catabolic-activity]] and [[conflicts/conflict--enigma_carbon_census_1--essential_metabolome--metabolic_capability_dependency--8ff1555a]].

**Cultivation selects the sample.** After filtering for CheckM completeness ≥80% and contamination ≤5%, deep clay-confined cultured genomes had *lower* amino-acid pathway completeness than the soil baseline, 15.50/18 versus 17.14/18 (p = 0.009, Cohen's d = −0.84) [src: clay_confined_subsurface], even though deep-clay Bacillota_B genomes were larger (4,110,038 bp versus 3,046,124 bp; d=+1.39, p=0.025) [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]. These measure different things and should not be reconciled by treating genome expansion as proof of self-sufficiency [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory, clay_confined_subsurface].

**Denominators are small.** Only 48 organisms were fitness-tested among approximately 293,000 genomes with pathway predictions [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]; the amino-acid pilot mapped 7 of 45 organisms [src: essential_metabolome]; and species-level phenotypes hide strain variation — 0 of 50 *P. fluorescens* strains utilized tryptophan, but that does not establish FW300-N2E3 cannot [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency].
