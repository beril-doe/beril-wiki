# Metabolic Traits, Auxotrophy, and Community Dependence

Whether a bacterium can make its own amino acids, catabolize a given carbon source, or must scavenge those molecules from neighbours is the pivot on which cultivation, community modelling, and genotype-to-phenotype prediction all turn. This corpus speaks to that question from an unusual position: it holds genome-scale pathway predictions across hundreds of thousands of genomes, laboratory gene-fitness measurements on a few dozen organisms, community metabolomics, and isolate phenotype databases — and it repeatedly finds that these evidence types disagree in structured, informative ways. The central result across the member concepts is a gap between *encoded capability* and *measured dependency*: among 1,695 complete pathway–organism pairs from 48 organisms, 267 (15.8%) were fitness-neutral latent capabilities, 547 (32.3%) intermediate, and 881 (51.9%) active dependencies [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. This page argues across five sub-themes, records two promoted conflicts, and closes with a reading path.

## Literature Context

Genome-based auxotrophy calling is the field's default instrument, and the candidate literature shows both its reach and its limits. Metagenomic reconstruction of the MAST-3 stramenopile *Incisomonas marina* — a 68 Mbp haploid genome recovered to an estimated 93% completeness, alongside assembled genomes for 23 co-cultured bacteria — *predicted* auxotrophy for vitamins B1, B2, B6, B7 and B12 but not C, B3, B5 or B9, and found the protist unable to synthesize half the protein amino acids; because several of the co-cultured bacteria also lacked complete B-vitamin pathways, exchange of vitamins or precursors was inferred rather than measured [PMID 41231233](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41231233/). Work in the Jiangsu Oil Reservoir made the capability/activity distinction explicit, pairing metagenomics with metatranscriptomics to separate what community members encode from which are transcriptionally active in situ, and likewise treated amino-acid and vitamin auxotrophies as evidence for a dependency network beyond classical syntrophy [PMID 29304850](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29304850/).

This corpus sits on that axis but inverts it, and the inversion is the novel part. The published pattern infers dependency from pathway *absence*; the corpus asks what *presence* buys. Of 1,695 complete pathway–organism pairs, 267 (15.8%) were fitness-neutral latent capabilities against 881 (51.9%) active dependencies, the latent fraction being threshold-sensitive (4.7%–21.1% across 16 tested threshold combinations). In a seven-organism cross-match with GapMind — a computational method scoring pathway completeness from genome annotation — Latent Capability was the largest class, 66 of 161 pairs (41.0%), though all 66 became fitness-important under some condition, on a median-based importance threshold that can force reclassification by construction. It also runs the inference backwards: 24 of 161 pairs were Incomplete but Important — fitness-relevant despite a predicted gap — so annotation-only auxotrophy calls are hypotheses, as the corpus flags its own *D. vulgaris* serine case. A transport-side literature result echoes this: *Streptococcus suis* TrpX is a tryptophan-uptake substrate-binding protein under T-box riboswitch control, conditionally essential in vivo in a tryptophan auxotroph — a single-species, single-mutant study showing scavenging machinery, not biosynthesis, can carry the fitness signal [PMID 35978073](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35978073/).

On community dependence the corpus is weaker than the literature might suggest, and says so. A multi-scale mathematical model of proteome partition, compared with co-cultures of auxotrophic *E. coli* K12 strains, concludes that the stringent response promotes cooperation — a modelling result, not a direct measurement [PMID 35592760](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35592760/). In vitro, obligate amino-acid cross-feeders proved *more* stress-sensitive than a prototrophic control, with over 80% of populations rescued only when one partner metabolically bypassed its auxotrophy — a defined two-strain consortium [PMID 40216843](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40216843/). Against that backdrop, the corpus's field-scale Black Queen signal — 11 of 13 amino-acid pathways directionally correct (p=0.011), yet only leucine (r=−0.390, q=0.022) and arginine (r=−0.297, q=0.049) surviving Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery-rate (FDR) correction, with tyrosine running the wrong way at r=+0.419 — is consistent in sign but far weaker in force, and supports [[concepts/community-metabolic-interdependence]] treating cross-feeding as pathway-specific rather than universal. No candidate paper addresses kinetic predictability, so the corpus's null result — binary-growth AUC (area under the receiver-operating-characteristic curve) 0.620 but negative R² for maximum growth rate, lag and the yield-related asymptotic OD increase when test genera are held out entirely — stands without literature counterpart.

## What the Corpus Shows

**A complete pathway is not a dependency, and an incomplete pathway is not an auxotrophy.** The sharpest statement of this comes from a four-way classification of 161 organism–pathway combinations across 7 model bacteria and 23 GapMind pathways — GapMind being a computational method that scores biosynthetic and catabolic pathway completeness from genome annotation. Active Dependency comprised 57 pairs (35.4%), Latent Capability 66 (41.0%), Incomplete but Important 24 (14.9%), and Missing 14 (8.7%) [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. The 24 Incomplete-but-Important pairs matter as much as the latent ones: fitness-important genes occur even where GapMind reports a gap, so a pathway hole is a testable hypothesis, not an auxotrophy call [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. At larger scale, pathway category strongly predicted dependency class (χ²=163.6, df=4, p=2.5×10⁻³⁴), though the latent fraction was threshold-sensitive, ranging from 4.7% to 21.1% across 16 tested threshold combinations [src: metabolic_capability_dependency].

**Biosynthesis and catabolism behave differently.** Amino-acid biosynthesis pathways were latent in only 48 of 735 pairs (6.5%), whereas carbon-source utilization pathways were latent in 217 of 892 (24.3%) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Consistent with that, a seven-organism pilot found 17 of 18 amino-acid biosynthesis pathways complete in all 7 mapped organisms, drawn from an underlying 45-organism essential-gene collection [src: essential_metabolome]. The single exception is the corpus's canonical auxotrophy candidate: serine biosynthesis was complete in 6 of 7 organisms (85.7%), with the gap assigned to *Desulfovibrio vulgaris* — a result the report explicitly treats as a hypothesis, since a divergent enzyme or annotation gap can produce an apparent GapMind hole [src: essential_metabolome]. Catabolic capacity is likewise specialist-concentrated rather than broadly shared: among 3,109 genomes, 675 carried exactly 1 of 8 aromatic or alkaloid capacities and only 18 carried at least 3, with a maximum of 4 [src: enigma_carbon_census_1].

**Capability prediction works; kinetic prediction does not transfer.** Across 46,389 genome × condition pairs from 727 genomes with 106 genus-blocked holdouts, overall binary-growth AUC — area under the receiver-operating-characteristic curve — was 0.620, but ranged from 0.775 for amino acids (7,765 pairs) and 0.780 for nucleosides (829 pairs) down to 0.435 for nitrogen (152 pairs) [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma]. The continuous kinetic parameters — µmax, the maximum growth rate (median 0.028 h⁻¹ among fit-ok curves); lag, the delay before growth begins (median 11.4 h); and max_A, the yield-related asymptotic OD increase (median A = 0.315) — were *not* predictable under genus-blocked holdout, producing negative R² in both the 46K-pair model and a dedicated bulk-feature regression; n_unique_KOs correlated with µmax at r = +0.42 but gave zero cross-genus predictive power [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma]. This null result stays null — it is the corpus's clearest demonstration that "can grow" and "how fast" are different phenotype resolutions.

**Community dependence is visible as a weak, pathway-specific signal — not a universal rule.** Community-weighted GapMind completeness correlated negatively with ambient metabolite intensity for 11 of 13 testable amino-acid pathways (binomial sign test p=0.011) across 220 samples and 80 pathways, the Black Queen prediction being that costly biosynthesis is lost when neighbours provision the metabolite [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]. Only leucine (r=−0.390, q=0.022, n=62) and arginine (r=−0.297, q=0.049, n=80) survived Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction, methionine trended correctly but failed it (r=−0.496, q=0.117, n=18), and tyrosine ran the wrong way at r=+0.419 [src: discoveries, nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]. Carbon utilization, not amino-acid biosynthesis, dominated community structure: principal-component analysis (PCA), an ordination method that summarizes major variance axes, gave a PC1 explaining 49.4% of variance and separating Soil from Freshwater at p<0.0001, while amino-acid pathways loaded on PC2 at 16.6% [src: discoveries]. A genomic correlate exists — latent-capability rate rose with pangenome openness (Spearman ρ = 0.69, p = 0.0004, n = 22 clades) — but presence-plus-neutrality never identifies producer, recipient, or exchanged metabolite [src: metabolic_capability_dependency].

**Accessory genes carry part of the biosynthetic repertoire.** Core-only versus all-gene GapMind comparisons showed completeness gaps of 0.146 for leucine and valine, 0.141 for arginine, and 0.140 for lysine and threonine biosynthesis [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. Across 2,810 GTDB species with ≥10 genomes and 80 pathways (18 amino-acid, 62 carbon-source), variable pathway count tracked pangenome openness after controlling for genome count (partial Spearman rho=0.530, p=2.83e-203) [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. This makes biosynthetic capacity a within-species variable, which is why species-level phenotype claims need strain denominators: BacDive recorded 0 of 50 *P. fluorescens* strains utilizing tryptophan (high confidence), but glycine rested on 0/1 [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency].

## Tensions and Caveats

The first promoted disagreement is [[conflicts/conflict--biosynthetic-self-sufficiency-and-cultivation--6f70a5cd]]. Ten deep-clay Bacillota_B genomes were larger and richer than 62 soil-baseline genomes — 4,110,038 bp versus 3,046,124 bp (Cohen's d=+1.39, p=0.025) and 2,630 versus 2,106 eggNOG orthologous groups (d=+1.30, p=0.022), with CheckM completeness effectively identical at 94.7% versus 94.3% (p=0.93) [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]. Yet direct GapMind measurement over the 18-pathway universe found the deep cohort *lower* after quality filtering: 15.50/18 versus 17.14/18 (p = 0.009, Cohen's d = −0.84), against a non-significant unfiltered comparison of 16.22/18 versus 16.66/18 (p = 0.153, d = −0.17) [src: clay_confined_subsurface]. Gene-content expansion and pathway completeness are not interchangeable evidence, and the deep cohort shrank from 9 → 6 genomes on filtering [src: clay_confined_subsurface].

The second is [[conflicts/conflict--respiratory-capacity-and-nadh-load--10ef6928]], on whether the dispensability of Complex I — NDH-1, the proton-pumping NADH:quinone oxidoreductase — on glucose reflects compensation by NDH-2, a single-subunit type II NADH dehydrogenase that oxidizes NADH without translocating protons, or an organism-specific wiring pattern; the ADP1 analysis reports a Complex I growth ratio of 1.44 on glucose and no significant cross-species compensation pattern (p = 0.52) [src: respiratory_chain_wiring].

Two caveats are load-bearing everywhere. First, denominators: only 48 organisms had fitness measurements among approximately 293,000 genomes with pathway predictions [src: metabolic_capability_dependency], and only 7 of those had matching GapMind data [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. Second, thresholds: the claim that all 66 Latent Capability pairs became fitness-important under some condition rests on a median-based importance threshold that can force reclassification by construction [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. Pathway-level conservation did *not* separate latent from active pathways (0.869 versus 0.829; Mann–Whitney U p = 0.94) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency], and RB-TnSeq — random-barcode transposon sequencing — run in rich media cannot distinguish biosynthetic from viability essentiality [src: essential_metabolome]. Annotation is a third limit: SEED subsystem roles served as a pathway-membership proxy, deoxyribonate and myoinositol were excluded for want of matching SEED descriptions, and alanine was dropped for fewer than 3 qualifying genes [src: metabolic_capability_dependency].
