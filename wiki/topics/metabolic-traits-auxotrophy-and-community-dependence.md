# Metabolic Traits, Auxotrophy, and Community Dependence

Whether a microbe can make its own amino acids, catabolize a given carbon source, or must scavenge those metabolites from neighbours is the axis along which this topic runs. The corpus attacks it from three directions at once: computational pathway-completeness calls (GapMind, which scores whether a genome encodes all steps of a defined biosynthetic or catabolic pathway), experimental gene-fitness data from transposon mutant pools, and community-scale metabolomics. The central lesson across the member concepts is a separation of two things that are easy to conflate — *encoded capability* and *measured dependency*. Across 1,695 complete pathway–organism pairs from 48 organisms, 267 (15.8%) were latent, 547 (32.3%) intermediate, and 881 (51.9%) active [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]; in an independent 161-pair set, 57 (35.4%) were Active Dependency, 66 (41.0%) Latent Capability, 24 (14.9%) Incomplete but Important, and 14 (8.7%) Missing [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. Nine member concepts and one conflict page feed this hub, organised below into four sub-themes.

## What the Corpus Shows

**A complete pathway is not a dependency, and an incomplete pathway is not an auxotrophy.** This is the load-bearing claim. Complete pathways could be either Active Dependency or Latent Capability, while incomplete pathways could still contain fitness-important genes — the 24 Incomplete but Important pairs, 14.9% of the 161, make gap calls testable hypotheses rather than auxotrophy verdicts [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. Pathway category strongly predicted dependency class (χ²=163.6, df=4, p=2.5×10⁻³⁴), yet pathway-level conservation did *not* separate latent from active: mean conservation 0.869 for 248 latent versus 0.829 for 755 active dependencies, Mann–Whitney U p = 0.94 [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. [[concepts/biosynthetic-prototrophy-and-auxotrophy]] applies this to the amino-acid case: 17 of 18 amino-acid pathways were complete in all 7 mapped organisms, with serine the lone exception, absent in *Desulfovibrio vulgaris* [src: essential_metabolome]. That report explicitly treats serine auxotrophy as a hypothesis, since a divergent enzyme or annotation gap can produce the same signal, and no growth test was performed [src: essential_metabolome].

**Catabolic capacity is far more often latent than biosynthetic capacity.** Carbon-source utilization pathways were latent in 217 of 892 cases (24.3%) versus 48 of 735 (6.5%) for amino-acid biosynthesis [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. [[concepts/metabolic-capacity-specialization]] shows the same asymmetry from the compound side: across genomes carrying at least 1 of the 8 ENIGMA-isolate-callable capacities, mean versatility was approximately 1.19 capacities per genome, with median 1 and maximum 4; among 3109 genomes, 675 carried exactly 1 of the 8 aromatic or alkaloid capacities and 18 carried at least 3 [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]. Specialism, not generalism, is the norm, and it is phylogenetically concentrated — Castellaniella and Hylemonella at approximately 100% for 3-hydroxybenzoic acid, Paenarthrobacter at 100% for phthalic acid [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]. Co-occurrence of capacities does not imply a portable module: only 25 of 3109 genomes, or 0.8%, carried both an aromatic and a non-aromatic capacity [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]. [[concepts/phb-granule-ecology]] finds comparably uneven distribution for a storage trait: phaC, the committed polyhydroxybutyrate step, occurred in 21.9% of 27,690 GTDB species, from 44.0% prevalence in plant-associated species down to 3.3% in animal-associated species, though after controlling for genome size the association between polyhydroxybutyrate and niche breadth fell to partial rho = -0.047 with p = 0.037 [src: phb_granule_ecology].

**Latency is conditional, not permanent — but the reclassification is threshold-fragile.** All 66 aggregate Latent Capability pairs became fitness-important under at least one condition type, most often nitrogen limitation, stress, or carbon limitation [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. The same report warns that its median-based condition-specific importance threshold can force pathways across the line by construction [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. The broader analysis reports the latent fraction ranging 4.7%–21.1% across 16 tested threshold combinations, while holding the carbon-versus-amino-acid contrast qualitatively [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. [[concepts/capability-versus-kinetic-predictability]] extends this to prediction: binary growth capability is partially learnable — overall AUC 0.620, where AUC means area under the receiver-operating-characteristic curve, across 46,389 genome × condition pairs from 727 genomes under genus-blocked holdout, with amino acids 0.775, nucleosides 0.780, carbon sources 0.695 and nitrogen 0.435 — whereas the continuous kinetic parameters µmax (maximum growth rate), lag (delay before growth begins), and max_A (the yield-related asymptotic OD increase) gave negative R² under the same holdout [src: genotype_to_phenotype_enigma]. [[concepts/respiratory-capacity-and-nadh-load]] raises the hypothesis that condition-specific reducing-equivalent load, rather than substrate chemistry, drives respiratory dependence: Complex I orthologs had mean fitness −1.35 on aromatic versus −0.77 on comparison conditions (Mann–Whitney p < 0.0001), yet the largest Complex I defects relative to background were −1.55 on acetate and −1.39 on succinate, both non-aromatic [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]. That inference remains a hypothesis, not an established rule, because the transferred fitness data mixes organisms with different respiratory-chain architectures [src: aromatic_catabolism_network].

**Community-level dependence is supported directionally but never demonstrated as exchange.** The Black Queen hypothesis here is that organisms can rely on metabolites produced by neighbouring organisms, allowing loss or reduction of otherwise costly biosynthetic functions [src: discoveries]. [[concepts/community-metabolic-interdependence]] reports 11 of 13 testable amino-acid pathways trending in the predicted direction (binomial sign-test p=0.011), with leucine r=−0.390, q=0.022, n=62 and arginine r=−0.297, q=0.049, n=80 surviving Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction [src: discoveries, nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]. Tyrosine ran the other way at r=+0.42 [src: discoveries]. Carbon, not amino acids, dominated community structure: in principal-component analysis, an ordination method that summarises the major variance axes, the first axis PC1 explained 49.4% of variance and separated Soil from Freshwater at p<0.0001, while amino-acid pathways loaded on the second axis PC2, which explained 16.6% [src: discoveries]. Accessory genes carry part of biosynthetic completeness — all-gene versus core-only gaps of 0.146 for leucine and valine, 0.141 for arginine, 0.140 for lysine and threonine [src: pathway_capability_dependency] — and latent-capability rate tracked pangenome openness (Spearman ρ = 0.69, p = 0.0004, n = 22 clades) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. None of this identifies a producer, recipient, or exchanged metabolite [src: metabolic_capability_dependency].

## Tensions and Caveats

The sharpest disagreement is recorded in [[conflicts/conflict--biosynthetic-self-sufficiency-and-cultivation--6f70a5cd]]: deep-clay Bacillota_B genomes were larger and functionally richer than soil baselines (4,110,038 bp versus 3,046,124 bp, Cohen's d=+1.39, p=0.025; 2,630 versus 2,106 orthologous groups, d=+1.30, p=0.022, with CheckM completeness effectively identical at 94.7% versus 94.3%, p=0.93) [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory], while the direct GapMind measurement found *no* elevated amino-acid completeness and, after quality filtering, a deficit: 15.50/18 versus 17.14/18, p = 0.009, Cohen's d = −0.84 [src: clay_confined_subsurface]. These must not be averaged — they measure different things, and 462 of 547 enriched orthologous groups were unannotated or "other" [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]. [[concepts/biosynthetic-self-sufficiency-and-cultivation]] holds the full argument, including the opposite shallow-clay result (17.87/18 versus 16.66/18, p = 0.006, d = +0.52) and the small filtered cohorts (9 → 6 anchor_deep genomes) [src: clay_confined_subsurface].

A second tension concerns how much latency is real. The 161-pair study finds every Latent Capability conditionally required [src: pathway_capability_dependency]; the 1,695-pair study finds 15.8% latent under its thresholds with a threshold range of 4.7%–21.1% [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Different organism sets, pathway sets, and classification rules mean neither resolves the other.

Denominators are the recurring caveat. Only 48 organisms had fitness measurements among approximately 293,000 genomes with pathway predictions [src: metabolic_capability_dependency], and only 7 of the essential-gene collection's 45 organisms mapped successfully [src: essential_metabolome]. RB-TnSeq (random-barcode transposon sequencing) essentiality was measured in rich media, where supplementation masks biosynthetic requirement [src: essential_metabolome]. Pathway–fitness mapping used SEED subsystem annotations as a membership proxy, excluding deoxyribonate and myoinositol for lack of matching roles and alanine for insufficient coverage [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Metabolomics was dominated by one study (125 of 131 samples, 95%), only ~2% of compounds had KEGG identifiers, and cysteine, histidine, and lysine were untestable [src: discoveries]. [[concepts/sample-size-aware-phenotype-consensus]] makes the denominator itself the evidence: BacDive supported 0/50 *P. fluorescens* strains utilizing tryptophan at high confidence, but glycine rested on 0/1 [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]. Finally, [[concepts/occurrence-versus-catabolic-activity]] insists that none of these signals is an activity measurement — 74 of 83 census compounds were organism-dark, and GapMind measures genomic potential, not expression or flux [src: enigma_carbon_census_1, nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology].
