# Resistance, Defense Systems, and Mobile Elements

Bacteria carry overlapping protective repertoires: antimicrobial-resistance (AMR) genes, metal-tolerance determinants, anti-phage defense systems, and the prophages and resistance islands that move or co-inherit them. This corpus approaches the topic from an unusual angle — it joins a pan-bacterial pangenome (the union of gene families across the genomes assigned to a species, split into *core* families present in essentially all members and *accessory*/*auxiliary*/*singleton* families present in fewer) to genome-wide competitive fitness measurements, isolate phenotype databases, environmental metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs), and geospatial metadata on the KBase Data Lakehouse. That lets the same question be asked at several scales at once: where resistance genes sit in the genome, what they cost, what they are worth under challenge, where they occur, and whether they travel. The recurring lesson is that the scale and catalog of measurement, not biology alone, decide what a "resistance gene" appears to be.

## Literature Context

Published pangenome work consistently frames the accessory genome as the flexible, adaptation-bearing compartment in which acquired resistance preferentially sits. In *Neisseria gonorrhoeae* the accessory pool is small (247 genes) and dominated by mobile-element, phage-associated and secretion-system genes, with increased prevalence of DNA-exchange regulators significantly associated with AMR ([PMID 35604129](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35604129/)); *Elizabethkingia anophelis* shows an open pangenome whose resistance factors are both core-genome and accessory, the latter tied to genomic islands, integrative and conjugative elements and prophages ([PMID 39472795](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39472795/)); *Pseudomonas paraeruginosa* strains share very similar cores while differing mainly in "regions of genomic plasticity," and all strains tend to be multiresistant, with a significant proportion carbapenem-resistant ([PMID 40709931](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40709931/)). Crucially, the core/accessory split is already known to be species- and gene-dependent: culture-enriched gut metagenomes placed *Enterobacter cloacae* resistance genes in the species core while *Escherichia coli* carried a large accessory resistome proximal to mobile elements ([PMID 30953542](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30953542/)), and in a clonal *Salmonella enteritidis* population 14 of 23 resistance genes were common to all 43 isolates ([PMID 38833480](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38833480/)).

This corpus is **consistent** with that picture and **extends** it from a handful of taxa to pan-bacterial scale: among the 4,252 species with at least five AMR clusters, 63.7% show AMR less core than their own pangenome baseline, with the auxiliary compartment enriched roughly two-fold overall. What it adds is the per-class gradient developed in [[concepts/environmental-resistome]] — intrinsic efflux and many beta-lactamases behaving as core while blaTEM, tet(C) and ant(2'')-Ia are never core — which turns the two-species contrast reported for *E. cloacae* versus *E. coli* ([PMID 30953542](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30953542/)) into a systematic, mechanism-resolved gradient, and which shows that the apparent compartment of a "resistance gene" depends heavily on catalog scope: the prominent mercury and arsenic stress families counted here are not resistance in the clinical sense addressed by these candidate studies.

The corpus stands in **productive tension** with the mobility literature. Single-species studies routinely identify concrete vehicles — prophages as the primary carrier of resistance genes in *Streptococcus suis* serotype 4 ([PMID 38703011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38703011/)), Tn2006/Tn2008 transposons and plasmid *dif* modules in *Acinetobacter baumannii* ST15 ([PMID 40673430](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40673430/)), ICEs in *E. anophelis* ([PMID 39472795](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39472795/)). The corpus reproduces a strong species-level prophage–AMR association, and contig-scale co-occurrence is common (over half of sampled AMR instances share a contig with a strict prophage marker); it is specifically the tight gene-neighborhood window that is weak (5.5% within five genes; median nearest-marker distance 34 genes). Candidate explanations remain open — immediate phage neighbors being core phage genes rather than acquired cargo, or open-pangenome species independently accumulating both prophages and AMR, alongside differences between annotation-based markers and dedicated element detection — and this corpus routes the disagreement to [[conflicts/conflict--phage_defense_arsenal--prophage_amr_comobilization--prophage_ecology--9f1135e8]] rather than settling it. Relatedly, the epistatic accounting of transferred versus core alleles in recombining *Campylobacter* ([PMID 38683013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38683013/)) offers a frame for, but no measurement of, the small relative fitness contrast in [[concepts/antimicrobial-resistance-fitness-cost]].

Several strands here are **unmatched** by these candidates, which are dominated by clinical-isolate genomics (with a gut-metagenome and an agricultural-population exception): genome-wide competitive fitness costs of AMR knockouts, the tiered metal-tolerance architecture in [[concepts/metal-cross-resistance]], anti-phage defense syndromes, and the sampling-effort caveats of [[concepts/spatial-sampling-effort-confounding]]. The candidates do document how heavily multidrug-resistant clinical isolates can be — 95.83% of *S. suis* SS4 lab isolates multidrug-resistant ([PMID 38703011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38703011/)), CC17 vancomycin-resistant *Enterococcus faecium* resistant to last-resort agents ([PMID 40762484](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40762484/)) — but none compares clinical against soil, aquatic or host-associated sources, so they cannot corroborate the corpus's between-environment contrast, and they offer no external control for the environmental taxa it contributes.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Resistance is disproportionately accessory — but the gradient is per-class, and the catalog defines the phenotype.** A uniform annotation of 27,690 pangenome species found 83,008 AMRFinderPlus hits spanning 82,908 clusters and 14,723 species, with 53.2% of species carrying at least one hit [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]. Only 30.3% of AMR genes were core versus 46.8% for the pangenome baseline, the auxiliary compartment was 2.2x enriched (33.6% versus 15.3%), and among 4,252 species with ≥5 AMR clusters, 63.7% had AMR less core than their own baseline (Wilcoxon p=1.1e-130, mean difference −0.102) [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]. But beta-lactamases were 54.9% core and intrinsic emhABC efflux pumps >95% core, while regulatory genes were 6.5% core and blaTEM, tet(C) and ant(2'')-Ia were 0% core [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]. The [[entities/amrfinderplus]] catalog also includes stress-response genes: the most abundant families were *bla* beta-lactamases (6,115 hits), *merA* mercury reductase (4,506), *arsD* (2,611), *merP* (2,222) and *vanR* (1,929), with mercury families contributing roughly 15,000 hits and 18% of annotations [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]. [[concepts/environmental-resistome]] develops this compartment logic into an intrinsic-versus-acquired distinction.

**Environment structures resistome size and composition — as association, not demonstrated selection.** Clinical species carried a median of 5 AMR clusters versus 2 for soil, aquatic and host-associated species, with environment effects persisting in 5 of 6 tested major phyla [src: amr_environmental_resistome]; clinical AMR was also less core than soil or plant AMR [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]. Geography adds a further layer and its own warning: of 22,356 coordinate-bearing environmental MAGs, 2.8% carried a metal-resistance type; soil (7,939 MAGs) was enriched at 5.8% prevalence (OR=5.05, q=1.8e-82), and the strongest cell, Atacama/Andean Chile, reached 21.8% (OR=9.83, q=7.6e-12, n=101 MAGs) — a contrast the report declines to read as environmental selection before sampling-effort correction [src: metal_resistance_global_biogeography]. See [[concepts/spatial-sampling-effort-confounding]].

**The cost of resistance is relative, small, and thinly resolved per organism; the benefit is conditional.** Using RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing, which measures competitive fitness of insertion mutants relative to a pool average) from the [[entities/kescience-fitnessbrowser]], 1,352 AMR genes were identified across 43 organisms, and 25 organisms had data sufficient for per-organism effects [src: amr_fitness_cost]. Across those 25, the pooled random-effects shift was **+0.086 [95% CI: +0.074, +0.098], z=14.3, p ~ 0**, median per-organism Cohen's d = 0.18, with heterogeneity I² = 54.3% and Cochran's Q = 52.54, p = 0.0007 [src: amr_fitness_cost]. Only six organisms were nominally significant at p<0.05 and just two survived false-discovery-rate (FDR) correction at q<0.05 — acidovorax_3H11 and Koxy — so the per-organism evidence is thin even though the pooled estimate is tight [src: amr_fitness_cost]. The estimate is also a contrast *between knockout classes*: AMR knockouts averaged −0.024 against a non-AMR background near −0.11, and absolute AMR-knockout fitness did not differ from zero (one-sample Wilcoxon p = 0.999) [src: amr_fitness_cost]. Under antibiotic exposure, 57.0% of AMR genes became more important (N=797, p=0.0001), while resistance mechanism did not structure baseline cost (H=0.65, p=0.89) [src: amr_fitness_cost]. [[concepts/antimicrobial-resistance-fitness-cost]] explains why "compensation" remains an interpretation here, not a measurement.

**Metal tolerance is layered: a broad stress-vulnerability floor plus specialized determinants.** Across 452 metal experiments, 37 organisms and 14 metals, 311/317 organism–metal-pair gene-level correlations were positive (98.1%), and all 15 pairs tested in ≥5 organisms exceeded 90% sign consistency [src: metal_cross_resistance]. Magnitudes track chemistry — Fe–Zn mean r = 0.61, Co–Ni 0.56, [[entities/copper]]–[[entities/zinc]] 0.48, with aluminium most independent at 0.34 [src: metal_cross_resistance]. With explicit non-metal controls, 4,177 of 7,609 metal-important records (54.9%) were metal-specific, 2,888 (38.0%) generally sick and 544 (7.2%) metal+stress [src: metal_specificity]. The [[entities/metal-fitness-atlas]] anchors the conserved end: metal-important genes were 87.4% core versus 76.9% baseline (OR=2.08, p=4.3e-162), with essential metals showing a larger mean core-fraction delta (+0.148) than toxic metals (+0.081; one-sided Mann-Whitney U=39, p=0.015) [src: metal_fitness_atlas]. Aggregate tolerance scores therefore mix components: phenotype features alone explained 16.3% of variance but taxonomy alone explained 35.4%, and catalase's overall positive association reverses within Actinomycetes, Gammaproteobacteria and Betaproteobacteria [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]. See [[concepts/metal-cross-resistance]] and [[concepts/composite-resistance-score-limitations]].

**Anti-phage defense is near-universal, syndromic, and scales with prophage burden.** Markers for ≥1 defense system appeared in 27,626 of 27,690 species-level pangenomes (99.8%), ranging from 7.2% prevalence for CBASS to 96.1% for CRISPR-Cas [src: phage_defense_arsenal]. Of 28 system pairs, 27 co-occurred more than expected under 1,000 phylum-stratified permutations (q<0.05), the strongest being restriction–modification Type II × Gabija (2,429 observed versus null mean 1,555, z=46.1, OR=24.0) [src: phage_defense_arsenal]. Defense count tracked prophage-cluster burden at Spearman ρ=0.609 (n=7,323), still ρ=0.301 (p=1.6×10⁻¹⁵³) after residualizing on genome size and phylum, with each 10-fold genome-size increase associated with ≈2.1× more defense systems [src: phage_defense_arsenal]. SNIPE shows both the breadth and the fragility of such calls: 4,572 DUF4041/PF13250 clusters across 1,696 species and 33 phyla, 86.7% accessory or singleton, yet only 54 clusters carried the Mug113 co-annotation [src: snipe_defense_system]. [[concepts/phage-defense-syndromes-and-arms-race]] carries the syndrome table.

**Mobility conclusions flip with the scale of measurement.** Prophage-marker density correlated with AMR repertoire breadth across 4,770 species at Spearman rho=0.572 (partial rho=0.464 after controlling for genome count), holding in all five major phyla tested [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. Locally the same data are weak: only 5.5% of 36,041 AMR instances lay within 5 genes of a strict prophage marker, the median nearest-marker distance was 34 genes, and the accessory-enrichment odds ratio at 10 genes was 1.10 — a 2.1-percentage-point difference that slid from 0.78 at 3 genes to 1.28 at 50 genes, with a median species-level OR of 0.85 [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. Co-inheritance is nonetheless real: 1,517 resistance islands across 705 species (mean 6.2 genes, mean phi = 0.827), 88% spanning multiple mechanisms [src: amr_strain_variation]. See [[concepts/scale-dependent-mobile-element-associations]] and [[concepts/resistance-island-coinheritance]].

## Tensions and Caveats

The sharpest dispute is **what an annotation hit licenses**. CRISPR-Cas prevalence is 96% by EggNOG description matching but approximately 55% by the Cas1 Pfam marker PF01867 on the same pangenome, and the DISARM anchor also returns non-DISARM SNF2 helicases [src: phage_defense_arsenal]; prophage calls covered all 27,702 analyzed species using eggNOG functional annotations rather than dedicated prophage detection, so near-universality may include domesticated remnants [src: prophage_ecology]. [[conflicts/conflict--phage_defense_arsenal--prophage_amr_comobilization--prophage_ecology--9f1135e8]] sets this against the species-level prophage–AMR correlation.

The **ecological signal does not localize**. Environment explains 2–13% of AMR-composition variance (η² = 0.02–0.13), yet only 20 of 141 testable families (14%) retained significant within-family effects after FDR correction [src: amr_environmental_resistome], and whole-genome ecotype analysis found environmental effects significant and positive in just 12 species (7.0%) [src: amr_environmental_resistome, ecotype_analysis]. Pooled and stratified analyses also diverge: the openness–AMR correlation was near zero overall (rho=0.006) but positive in 8/10 tested phyla [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]. See [[conflicts/conflict--amr_environmental_resistome--amr_fitness_cost--amr_pangenome_atlas--76f2bfcc]].

**Validation of genome-based scores against ecology is contradictory.** A BacDive-to-pangenome test reports Cohen's d = +1.00 for heavy-metal-contamination isolates (n=10, barely above the approximately 0.93 minimum detectable effect) [src: bacdive_metal_validation], while a species-scale test found Spearman rho approximately −0.02, p > 0.8 [src: metal_cross_resistance]; these must not be averaged — see [[conflicts/conflict--bacdive_metal_validation--metal_cross_resistance--metal_fitness_atlas--d3b93f8c]].

**Fitness evidence is fragile under class matching**: the any-antibiotic flip (57.0%, N=797, p=0.0001) is not reproduced by the underpowered class-matched test (54.8%, 157 pairs, p=0.14) [src: amr_fitness_cost]; see [[conflicts/conflict--amr_cofitness_networks--amr_fitness_cost--amr_strain_variation--90a27ef2]]. Finally, **metadata coverage is load-bearing**: 15,550 AMR clusters (18.7%) were mechanism-unassigned [src: amr_environmental_resistome], and 30.8% of ENA sample records lacked valid coordinates [src: metal_resistance_global_biogeography].
