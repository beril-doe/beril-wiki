# Resistance, Defense Systems, and Mobile Elements

Bacteria carry overlapping systems for surviving chemical and biological attack: antimicrobial-resistance (AMR) genes, metal-tolerance determinants, anti-phage defense systems, and the mobile genetic elements (MGEs: prophages, plasmids, integrative elements) that can move these functions between genomes. This corpus speaks to the topic through separate analyses of species pangenomes, fitness screens, BacDive isolates and environmental metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs, genomes reconstructed from metagenomes). Most mobility claims are proxy-based, so they are associations rather than demonstrations of transfer.

## Literature Context

The candidate papers describe mobile genetic elements (MGEs) as carriers of both antimicrobial resistance (AMR) genes and anti-phage defense. A review of enterococcal genetics centers on mobile elements, conjugative plasmids, integrated conjugative elements and the resistance genes they frequently carry [PMID 30848235](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30848235/). A census of 6,549 Tn7-like transposons found that 96% carry at least one cargo gene. The cargo is enriched in anti-MGE defense and antibiotic resistance genes, and 22 transposons carry both [PMID 34872347](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34872347/). Mobile integrons carry cassettes that encode phage resistance. These can be mixed with drug-resistance cassettes, and their fitness costs vary with genetic context and cassette order [PMID 40338999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40338999/). These papers support the hub's framing of resistance and defense as overlapping, mobile functions.

The papers disagree on how defense and resistance relate. A scan of >20 000 genomes found over 13 000 chromosomally integrated MGEs, and defense, AMR and virulence genes were negatively correlated within ICEs and IMEs [PMID 37078595](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37078595/). In Staphylococcus haemolyticus (692 genomes), CRISPR-Cas-encoding genomes had fewer MGEs and AMR genes, suggesting a trade-off. Defense and anti-defense elements were also found on plasmids and prophages [PMID 40516885](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40516885/). A review of enterococcal CRISPR-Cas notes that CRISPR may prevent acquisition of resistance and virulence genes [PMID 39438415](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39438415/). In contrast, 1,045 Pseudomonas aeruginosa genomes showed a highly connected network of co-occurring MGEs and AMR determinants, with IncP linked to the most (n = 93) [PMID 41612191](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41612191/). Salmonella serovars differ in prophage, conjugative-element and AMR content, with S. Paratyphi A carrying fewer than S. Typhi [PMID 40394957](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40394957/). A riverine plastisphere study, using in situ cultivation, linked MGE and defense-system interplay to the resistome. Defense systems and ARGs were carried by the same resistant bacteria, which took part in 24.6% of all horizontal gene transfer events [PMID 41864008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41864008/). Reviews of bacterial immune systems [PMID 42501080](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42501080/) and of gene transfer in respiratory and oral bacteria [PMID 29928825](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29928825/) give background.

The corpus is consistent with this literature in several places. Its defense-island reading of 27 of 28 co-occurring defense pairs fits the clustering and MGE association seen in S. haemolyticus. Its accessory, strain-variable AMR content and its prophage-AMR correlation (Spearman rho=0.572, n=4,770 species) fit the mobile-element picture. The corpus extends the literature by working across species pangenomes, where the candidates mostly examine single species or MGE classes. Its caveat that these are proxy associations, not demonstrations of transfer, applies equally to the genomic candidates.

There is partial tension. The corpus found that species with more prophage clusters carry more defense systems (partial rho 0.301), and prophage-proximal AMR genes only slightly more often accessory (OR 1.10). The negative defense-AMR correlation in ICEs and IMEs and the CRISPR trade-off in S. haemolyticus point the other way, though they concern different element classes and taxa. The integron paper's context-dependent fitness costs of phage-resistance cassettes are a loose counterpart to the corpus's cost finding. That finding is that 57% of AMR genes become more important when knocked out under any antibiotic (N = 797), while the stricter class-matched result is not significant. The metal-tolerance results and the SNIPE pore-acting defense have no counterpart among the candidates and remain novel here.

## What the Corpus Shows

**AMR is largely accessory and strain-variable.** In the bacterial pangenome atlas, 30.3% of AMR genes are core versus a 46.8% baseline (OR=0.49, odds ratio). [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] Within species, 51.3% of AMR gene-species occurrences are rare (present in <=5% of strains) and only 7.5% are fixed (>=95%). [src: amr_strain_variation] Conservation differs by mechanism, so intrinsic and acquired resistance are separable only by interpretation. [src: amr_fitness_cost] See [[concepts/intrinsic-versus-acquired-resistance-gene-conservation]] and [[concepts/within-species-variation-of-antimicrobial-resistance-gene-content]]. Resistance genes also co-occur as modules: 1,517 resistance islands were detected across 705 species, and 88% (1,343/1,517) mix mechanisms. [src: amr_strain_variation] The mobile-element reading is an interpretation. [src: amr_strain_variation] See [[concepts/resistance-islands-and-co-occurrence-of-amr-genes]]. AMR catalogs also contain metal genes: mercury genes account for ~15,000 hits, or 18% of AMR annotations. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] See [[concepts/metal-resistance-genes-within-amr-catalogs]].

**Environment shapes resistance content.** Clinical species have a median of 5 AMR gene clusters against 2 for soil, aquatic and host-associated species. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] Metal resistance dominates soil and aquatic AMR, while target modification is more prominent in gut and clinical species; the niche-selection reading is the report's interpretation. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] See [[concepts/environmental-structuring-of-antimicrobial-resistance-gene-content]] and [[concepts/environment-dependent-resistance-mechanism-composition]]. Metal resistance is rare across georeferenced MAGs (2.8% of 22,356 carry at least one type) but clusters in grid cells and biomes; the hotspots are descriptive. [src: metal_resistance_global_biogeography] See [[concepts/geographic-and-biome-distribution-of-environmental-metal-resistance]].

**Fitness costs and support networks.** Under any antibiotic, 57% of AMR genes become more important when knocked out (N = 797); the stricter class-matched result is not significant. [src: amr_fitness_cost] See [[concepts/antibiotic-dependent-fitness-flip-of-resistance-genes]]. Cofitness support networks are organism-specific rather than mechanism-specific (mean GO Jaccard 0.375 within organism across mechanisms versus 0.207 for the same mechanism across organisms). [src: amr_cofitness_networks] Flagellar and amino acid biosynthesis terms are enriched; no mechanism-specific enrichment survives correction. [src: amr_cofitness_networks] See [[concepts/organism-specific-versus-mechanism-specific-wiring-of-amr-support-networks]] and [[concepts/functional-composition-of-antimicrobial-resistance-cofitness-support-networks]].

**Metal tolerance mixes shared stress, genome content and ecology.** Among genes important for metals, 39.8% were also NaCl-important across 19 organisms and 14 metals, and genome-encoded resistance count predicts tolerance better than phenotypes such as urease status. [src: discoveries] See [[concepts/metal-tolerance-determinants-and-shared-stress-confounding]]. The metal_cross_resistance report identified 318 ortholog groups important for >=2 metals in >=2 organisms; their use for engineering is a proposal. [src: metal_cross_resistance] See [[concepts/conserved-multi-metal-tolerance-gene-families]]. In BacDive, taxonomy explained more variance in genome-based tolerance scores than phenotypes, and the scores are themselves genome-derived. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] See [[concepts/genome-encoded-metal-resistance-repertoire-versus-phenotype-as-predictor-of-metal-tolerance]]. Genome-derived scores are higher for heavy-metal contamination isolates (Cohen's d = +1.00, n=10, a small group). [src: bacdive_metal_validation] See [[concepts/isolation-environment-validation-of-genome-predicted-metal-tolerance]]. Genera with more diverse metal repertoires have broader niches after phylogenetic control (a correlation, not causation), and the effect looks distributed across metals. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] See [[concepts/metal-resistance-repertoire-breadth-and-ecological-niche-breadth]] and [[concepts/single-metal-dominance-versus-distributed-metal-type-diversity-effects]]. Cofactor-biosynthesis genes track niche breadth like housekeeping genes, while resistance genes show no association. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] See [[concepts/constitutive-versus-inducible-gene-classes-in-niche-breadth-associations]].

**Anti-phage defense is near-universal and clustered.** Detection of seven defense families covered 27,626 of 27,690 species-level pangenomes; after strict/stringent filtering, 27,565 species have at least one defense system. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] CRISPR numbers depend strongly on detection method. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] See [[concepts/pan-bacterial-prevalence-phylum-distribution-and-mobility-of-anti-phage-defense-systems]]. 27 of 28 tested defense pairs co-occur significantly under a phylum-stratified null, consistent with defense islands (the mechanistic reading is a hypothesis). [src: phage_defense_arsenal] See [[concepts/defense-system-co-occurrence-syndromes-and-defense-islands]]. Species with more prophage clusters carry more defense systems (partial rho 0.301 after genome size and phylum), consistent with but not a test of an arms race. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] See [[concepts/defense-system-burden-scaling-with-prophage-load-anti-phage-arms-race]]. Receptor loss can buy phage resistance at a metabolic cost (ManYZ and phage lambda, using published knockout data); the SNIPE report concludes a pore-acting defense avoids that price. [src: snipe_defense_system] See [[concepts/phage-resistance-versus-metabolic-cost-trade-off-at-receptor-transporters]].

**Prophages and AMR.** Prophage marker density correlates with AMR repertoire breadth (Spearman rho=0.572, n=4,770 species). [src: prophage_amr_comobilization] See [[concepts/prophage-density-and-amr-repertoire-breadth-across-species]]. Prophage-proximal AMR genes are only slightly more often accessory (OR 1.10), and the effect is modest and threshold-dependent. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization] See [[concepts/prophage-proximity-and-amr-gene-conservation]]. Prophage modules track environment beyond host phylogeny in PERMANOVA (a multivariate variance test). [src: prophage_ecology] See [[concepts/environmental-structuring-of-prophage-gene-modules-beyond-host-phylogeny]]. Within-species co-occurrence in one species per phylum suggests packaging, lysis and lysogenic-regulation genes are physically linked; this is an inference from limited sampling. [src: prophage_ecology] See [[concepts/modular-architecture-and-co-occurrence-of-prophage-genes]]. Anti-defense genes are the most environmentally responsive module, and the arms-race reading is interpretive. [src: prophage_ecology] See [[concepts/prophage-anti-defense-genes-and-host-phage-arms-races]].

**Mobile elements as a source of novelty.** Novel/singleton genes are enriched for COG L (mobile elements) by +10.88% and V (defense) by +2.83%; the HGT conclusion is an interpretation. [src: cog_analysis, discoveries] See [[concepts/horizontal-gene-transfer-and-mobile-elements-as-a-source-of-novel-and-dark-genes]]. Defense (V) differentiated ecotypes in 11/12 species and L in 9/12; the phage-pressure link is a hypothesis. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] See [[concepts/defense-and-mobile-element-gene-content-as-drivers-of-ecotype-differences]]. Costly+dispensable genes were 7.45x more likely than costly+conserved genes to contain mobile-element keywords. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] See [[concepts/mobile-genetic-element-context-of-horizontally-acquired-gene-families]]. Transfer is also inferred for phaC [[concepts/horizontal-transfer-of-storage-pathway-genes-inferred-from-pangenome-core-accessory-status]], plant-interaction genes [[concepts/mobile-elements-and-horizontal-transfer-of-plant-interaction-genes]] and CAZy genes near type IV secretion systems [[concepts/horizontal-transfer-of-carbohydrate-active-enzymes-via-conjugative-machinery]]. Transposase-driven copy-number variation is an untested explanation. [src: gene_copy_number_variation] See [[concepts/mechanisms-and-confounders-of-copy-number-variation-in-pangenomes]]. Metal-resistance horizontal-transfer evidence is exploratory and modest. [src: comprehensive_metal_ecology] See [[concepts/horizontal-transfer-signatures-of-metal-resistance-genes]] and [[concepts/metal-resistance-co-occurrence-with-mobile-gene-transfer-hubs]].

**Envelope alternatives.** Caulobacter shows measured peptidoglycan expression changes after lipid A loss, but the Pal mechanism is an inference. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] Cross-species sphingolipid and resistance predictions rest on genome presence/absence and literature, are model-based, and were not functionally tested in the comparator species. [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss] See [[concepts/alternative-routes-to-tolerating-lipid-a-or-lps-loss-across-gram-negatives]] and [[concepts/peptidoglycan-remodeling-and-tol-pal-engagement-after-outer-membrane-lps-loss]].

## Tensions and Caveats

**Are metal and antibiotic resistance genes accessory?** A single-organism DvH analysis found a non-significant trend toward accessory enrichment. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness] A 22-organism atlas found strong core enrichment, and a follow-up only a modest accessory tilt for metal-specific genes. [src: metal_fitness_atlas, metal_specificity] See [[concepts/accessory-conservation-of-metal-and-antibiotic-resistance-genes]].

**Annotation proxies.** Prophage detection by annotation likely inflates prevalence by including domesticated remnants, and its false-positive rate is uncharacterized. [src: prophage_ecology] The keyword/Pfam approach may include defense systems and miss divergent prophages. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization] See [[concepts/annotation-based-prophage-detection-prevalence-and-domesticated-remnants]].

**Metal resistance and mobility.** GT2-neighbourhood MAGs carry more metal resistance, but another MAG set finds metal resistance and T4SS (type IV secretion system) not uniformly co-distributed. [src: t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt, metal_resistance_global_biogeography]

**Cultivation.** Conjugation markers were not depleted in isolates; the episomal-loss explanation is a hypothesis. [src: oak_ridge_cultivation_gap] See [[concepts/mobile-genetic-element-retention-in-cultured-isolates-versus-episomal-loss]].

**Internal inconsistency.** The resistome report's efflux and target-modification numbers disagree between sections. [src: amr_environmental_resistome, discoveries]
