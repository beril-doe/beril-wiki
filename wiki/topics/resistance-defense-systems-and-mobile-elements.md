# Resistance, Defense Systems, and Mobile Elements

Bacteria carry overlapping systems for surviving chemical and biological attack: antimicrobial-resistance (AMR) genes, metal-tolerance determinants, anti-phage defense systems, and the mobile genetic elements that may move any of them. Two instruments rarely meet here — pangenome inventories spanning tens of thousands of species, and RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing, which measures competitive fitness of pooled insertion mutants) across a few dozen lab organisms — so prevalence and cost are measured in different currencies and often disagree. The anchor inventories are 82,908 AMR gene clusters across 14,723 bacterial species and 293K genomes [src: amr_environmental_resistome], defense-system markers in 27,626 of 27,690 species-level pangenomes (99.8%) [src: phage_defense_arsenal], and 452 metal experiments across 37 organisms and 14 metals [src: metal_cross_resistance].

## Literature Context

The literature measures resistance burden through carriage and growth assays, not pooled knockout fitness, and it does not agree. A systematic review of mutation-conferred resistance in *Staphylococcus aureus* found 26 of 35 articles reporting a frequently associated fitness cost [PMID 33417813](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33417813/), and a high-throughput reverse-genetics study of *mcr-1* *Escherichia coli* variants measured 73·8% less growth per 24 h for wild-type *mcr-1* than for a non-functional construct, with 3,433 of 3,449 (99·5%) single mutants costly in colistin-free media and only 43 (0·3%) mutants costless, most of those containing multiple mutations [PMID 38870982](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38870982/). Against this, dissection of the multiresistance plasmid pUUH239.2, which reduces host fitness by 2.9% per generation, attributed the entire cost to *bla*CTX-M-15 and *tetAR* while most of its 13 resistance genes were cost-free even when overexpressed [PMID 35038907](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35038907/); *tet*(X3)/*tet*(X4) confer only a mild cost (relative fitness of >0.704) [PMID 31133751](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31133751/); and *bla*IMP-carrying IncN plasmids from nine clinical isolates showed 100% stability after 100 generations with minimal fitness cost [PMID 41347505](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41347505/). Transposon carriage can likewise be near-free: a macrolide-resistance transposon in *Streptococcus pneumoniae* gave transformants negligible cost [PMID 24532683](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24532683/), and an inducible VanB-type resistance transposon cost *Enterococcus* nothing in vitro or in gnotobiotic mice absent vancomycin, while induced or constitutive strains were severely impaired [PMID 20833818](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20833818/) — regulation as cost avoidance, a general theme [PMID 17223624](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17223624/).

This corpus's fitness-measured half sits with the low-cost pole of that literature rather than the high-cost pole, and it explains part of the split: because it compares AMR knockouts (mean −0.024) to a non-AMR knockout background of approximately −0.11, its pooled +0.086 shift is a relative contrast, not a carriage cost, so it neither confirms nor refutes the *mcr-1* result [PMID 38870982], which measures a different quantity in a different direction. The corpus finding that conservation class does not predict burden — core/intrinsic (N=638) and accessory/acquired (N=163) genes both at mean −0.024, Cohen's d = 0.002 — extends the gene-specific picture of pUUH239.2 [PMID 35038907] from one plasmid to 25 lab-adapted organisms, while remaining vulnerable to the same objection those authors avoid: knockouts in lab strains cannot see compensation, which reviews treat as the standard resolution [PMID 31492517, PMID 40276228].

The corpus's antibiotic-conditional sign flip for 57.0% of AMR genes, strongest for broad-spectrum efflux (+0.094 versus −0.001 enzymatic), is consistent with transposon-library evidence that intrinsic and acquired resistance genes confer a positive in vivo fitness advantage to *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, confirmed in *Acinetobacter baumannii* and *Vibrio cholerae* infection models [PMID 26203082, PMID 27248008], and with condition-dependence demonstrated by medium-specific CRISPRi-seq fitness costs in *Haemophilus influenzae* [PMID 41171899] and background-dependent costs of *rpoB* H526Y [PMID 34697460]. For the mobile-element and defense strands, mobile integrons carrying phage-resistance cassettes with variable, context- and order-dependent costs — "highly mobile, low-cost defense islands" [PMID 40338999] — anticipate the corpus's mostly non-core SNIPE marker (86.7% accessory or singleton), though the corpus's near-universal defense prevalence (99.8% of pangenomes) and its metal tiers have no counterpart among these candidates and stand as novel at pangenome scale.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Resistance genes sit disproportionately in the accessory genome, but conservation class is not a cost class.** Only 30.3% of AMR genes were core — present in essentially all genomes of a species — versus 46.8% for the pangenome baseline (odds ratio 0.49, chi-squared=23,117, p≈0), with the auxiliary genome 2.2x enriched for AMR (33.6% versus 15.3%) [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]. Within species, 51.3% of AMR gene-species records were rare, 41.3% variable, and 7.5% fixed [src: amr_strain_variation]. Yet when fitness is measured directly, core or intrinsic AMR genes (N=638) and accessory or acquired ones (N=163) had identical distributions: both means −0.024, Cohen's d = 0.002, Mann-Whitney U p = 0.33 [src: amr_fitness_cost]. Mechanism predicted *where* a gene sits — metal-resistance genes were accessory in 44% of cases versus 13% of efflux and 16% of enzymatic-inactivation genes (χ² = 69.3, p = 1.4×10⁻¹³) — but not its baseline knockout fitness (Kruskal-Wallis H = 0.65, p = 0.89) [src: amr_fitness_cost]. Genomic compartment therefore describes mode of maintenance far better than it describes burden.

**The measured "cost" of resistance is relative, small, and conditional.** A DerSimonian-Laird random-effects meta-analysis across 25 organisms pooled a shift of +0.086 [95% CI: +0.074, +0.098], z = 14.3, p ~ 0, with median per-organism Cohen's d = 0.18 [src: amr_fitness_cost]. This is a contrast between knockout classes: AMR knockouts averaged −0.024 against a non-AMR knockout background of approximately −0.11, and a one-sample Wilcoxon test of AMR knockout fitness against zero gave p = 0.999 [src: amr_fitness_cost]. Under antibiotics the sign of importance flips for 57.0% of AMR genes (N = 797, mean flip +0.045, Wilcoxon signed-rank p = 0.0001), and the flip is mechanism-dependent: broad-spectrum efflux genes averaged +0.094 versus −0.001 for enzymatic-inactivation genes (Mann-Whitney U p = 0.007) [src: amr_fitness_cost]. Class-matched validation was weaker (157 gene-antibiotic pairs, mean flip +0.113, flip rate 54.8%, Wilcoxon p = 0.14) [src: amr_fitness_cost]. Read the full argument in [[concepts/antimicrobial-resistance-fitness-cost]].

**Metal tolerance is a layered phenomenon, and aggregate scores cannot resolve the layers.** Gene-level fitness responses to different metals are almost uniformly positively correlated: 311/317 organism–metal-pair correlations were positive, and all 15 metal pairs tested in at least 5 organisms exceeded 90% sign consistency, with no systematically negative pair [src: metal_cross_resistance]. Magnitudes are chemistry-dependent — Fe–Zn mean r = 0.61 (n = 6 organisms), Co–Ni r = 0.56 (n = 28), while Al was the most independent metal at mean r = 0.34 [src: metal_cross_resistance]. Beneath this sit tiers: among 8,162 metal-important genes, 1,484 (18.2%) were general stress, 2,306 (28.3%) metal-shared, and 4,372 (53.6%) metal-specific, with fully core fractions of 57.2%, 50.4%, and 45.7% [src: metal_cross_resistance]. With explicit non-metal controls, 4,177 of 7,609 metal-important gene records (54.9%) were metal-specific (<5% sick rate across 5,945 non-metal experiments), 2,888 (38.0%) generally sick, 544 (7.2%) metal+stress [src: metal_specificity]. Composite scores mix these: taxonomy alone explained 35.4% of composite metal-tolerance variance, phenotypes only 16.3%, and adding phenotype to taxonomy *lowered* R² to 0.345 (delta R² = −0.009) [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]. See [[concepts/metal-cross-resistance]] and [[concepts/composite-resistance-score-limitations]].

**Anti-phage defense is near-universal, syndromic, and tracks prophage burden.** Species-level prevalence ranged from 7.2% for CBASS to 96.1% for CRISPR-Cas, with BREX at 80.1% and restriction-modification (R-M) Type I at 76.8% [src: phage_defense_arsenal]. Of 28 defense-system pairs, 27 co-occurred significantly more than a phylum-stratified permutation null at q < 0.05; the strongest was R-M Type II × Gabija (2,429 observed versus null mean 1,555, z = 46.1, OR = 24.0), while CRISPR-Cas × CBASS was the sole non-significant pair (z = 0.21, empirical p = 0.98) [src: phage_defense_arsenal]. Defense count rose with prophage-cluster burden (Spearman ρ = 0.609, n = 7,323; partial ρ = 0.301, p = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁵³ after residualizing on genome size and phylum), positively and significantly in all nine major phyla [src: phage_defense_arsenal]. SNIPE adds a mobile, mostly non-core case: its DUF4041/PF13250 marker spanned 4,572 clusters in 1,696 species, 86.7% accessory or singleton [src: snipe_defense_system]. Details in [[concepts/phage-defense-syndromes-and-arms-race]].

**Mobile-element evidence is scale-dependent, and the scales point different ways.** Across 4,770 species, prophage marker density tracked AMR repertoire breadth strongly (Spearman rho=0.572, p<10^-300; partial rho=0.464 after controlling for genome count) [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. Locally the association is modest and heterogeneous: of 36,041 AMR gene instances, 20,073 (55.7%) sat on contigs carrying strict prophage markers but only 1,991 (5.5%) within 5 genes, and the accessory-fraction odds ratio at a 10-gene window was 1.10 (one-sided p=0.005, bootstrap 95% CI [1.024, 1.185]) — significant but a 2.1 percentage point difference [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. It is threshold-dependent rather than uniformly positive: odds ratios ran 0.78 at 3 genes, 1.10 at 10, and 1.28 at 50, with a median species-level odds ratio of 0.85 [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. Meanwhile 1,517 resistance islands across 705 species (mean phi 0.827, 88% multi-mechanism) show tight co-inheritance without identifying a carrier [src: amr_strain_variation].

## Tensions and Caveats

The sharpest disagreement is whether AMR genes escape the genome-wide conservation–fitness gradient: essential genes were 82% core versus 66% for always-neutral genes [src: fitness_effects_conservation], yet the AMR subset shows d = 0.002, p = 0.33 [src: amr_fitness_cost]. [[conflicts/conflict--antimicrobial-resistance-fitness-cost--3139de09]] holds these apart rather than importing the all-gene gradient. A companion conflict, [[conflicts/conflict--antimicrobial-resistance-fitness-cost--cded18a3]], warns against reading co-inheritance as functional synergy: 96% of 801 AMR genes had an extra-operon cofitness partner at |r| > 0.3, but support-network size was uncorrelated with fitness cost (Spearman rho = −0.006, p = 0.87, N = 769) [src: amr_cofitness_networks].

Metal core fractions do not reconcile: 87.4% [src: metal_fitness_atlas], 84.8% [src: metal_specificity], and the 92.0%/91.0%/89.8% tier gradient [src: metal_cross_resistance] are tracked in [[conflicts/conflict--composite-resistance-score-limitations--ba02b0ad]] and [[conflicts/conflict--condition-specific-fitness--1a717775]], which add a 71.2%-core single-organism null [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]; they must not be averaged. External validation of metal scores splits: Cohen's d = +1.00 for heavy-metal isolation [src: bacdive_metal_validation] against Spearman rho approximately −0.02, p > 0.8 at Fitness Browser species scale [src: metal_cross_resistance] — see [[conflicts/conflict--composite-resistance-score-limitations--a9245bd1]] and [[conflicts/conflict--environmental-resistome--9415d508]] — while [[conflicts/conflict--composite-resistance-score-limitations--4a1c9395]] records incompatible BacDive-to-GTDB coverage denominators. Whether universal cross-metal positivity is metal-specific at all remains open, since the cross-resistance study ran no non-metal controls ([[conflicts/conflict--metal-cross-resistance--59068bf5]]) [src: metal_cross_resistance].

Catalog scope is load-bearing throughout. AMRFinderPlus includes stress-response genes: merA mercury reductase contributed 4,506 hits and mercury families approximately 18% of annotations [src: amr_pangenome_atlas], so "resistome" may name a stress landscape ([[conflicts/conflict--environmental-resistome--ecbc42fd]]). Mechanism labels came from keyword matching, leaving 18,448 hits (22.2%) Other/Unclassified [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] against 15,550 clusters (18.7%) unassigned elsewhere [src: amr_environmental_resistome] — different tables, not a numeric contradiction, but see [[conflicts/conflict--environmental-resistome--175dc8a6]]. Defense prevalence is equally annotation-sensitive: EggNOG description matching gave CRISPR-Cas 96% while Cas1 marker PF01867 gave approximately 55% on the same pangenome ([[conflicts/conflict--phage-defense-syndromes-and-arms-race--d33388e9]]) [src: phage_defense_arsenal].

Ecological framing carries caveats: environment effects are strong across taxa — clinical species median 5 AMR clusters versus 2 for soil, aquatic and host-associated (Kruskal–Wallis H = 781.9, p = 9.4×10⁻¹⁶⁷, η² = 0.056) [src: amr_environmental_resistome] — but weak within them ([[conflicts/conflict--environmental-resistome--86566e6b]]), and 46.8% of AMR-carrying species remained Other/Unknown for isolation source [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]. Phylogenetic signal in AMR content (701 of 1,261 species at false-discovery rate, or FDR, the multiple-testing correction behind the q-values above, below 0.05) may be lineage biology or uneven sampling [src: amr_strain_variation, ecotype_analysis] ([[conflicts/conflict--environmental-resistome--4d25f7e8]]); compartment may mark maintenance rather than origin ([[conflicts/conflict--environmental-resistome--1585d18c]]); and genome-wide association is not local linkage ([[conflicts/conflict--environmental-resistome--e8ac0fe1]]). Geography is least settled: 11 hotspots and 3 coldspots emerged from 289 grid cells with at least 5 MAGs (metagenome-assembled genomes), the strongest Atacama/Andean Chile (21.8% prevalence, OR=9.83, q=7.6e-12, n=101 MAGs), but the report requires sampling-effort correction by log(n_MAGs) before regional interpretation [src: metal_resistance_global_biogeography]. All 25 organisms in the fitness meta-analysis were lab-adapted strains, so compensation remains an interpretation, not a measurement [src: amr_fitness_cost].
