# Lanthanide-Dependent Methylotrophy

Lanthanide-dependent methylotrophy is the use of rare-earth elements (REEs, the lanthanides) as cofactors for methanol oxidation by pyrroloquinoline-quinone (PQQ) methanol dehydrogenases. The corpus speaks to it through a single project, the lanthanide methylotrophy atlas, which the report calls the first pangenome-scale atlas of the xoxF cassette. It surveyed 293,059 GTDB-r214 genomes (GTDB is the Genome Taxonomy Database) [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. The four member concepts cover how common xoxF is, where it occurs, how well its PQQ cofactor is supported, and how lanmodulin relates to it. All evidence comes from one report and rests on gene annotations, so the picture is a map of annotated potential, not a measurement of lanthanide use.

## Literature Context

The candidate papers establish that XoxF is a lanthanide-dependent methanol dehydrogenase whose discovery changed how methylotrophy is understood. Reviews describe XoxF as broadly distributed among bacteria, possibly an ancestral form of methylotrophy and the predominant form in nature [PMID 41636527](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41636527/). Another review notes that XoxF is encoded in methylotrophic and non-methylotrophic genomes alike [PMID 33867018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33867018/). In Methylobacterium aquaticum 22A, mxaF and xoxF1 expression were inversely correlated in response to La3+, so the calcium and lanthanide enzymes are regulated reciprocally [PMID 29404411](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29404411/). Genetic work in the same strain found XoxF1 and MxaFI as one Ln- and Ca-dependent MDH pair involved in methylotrophy [PMID 32486139](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32486139/).

Experimental work also extends to other lineages and settings. In Sinorhizobium meliloti, the XoxF-type enzyme encoded by smb20173 is the sole MDH supporting methylotrophic growth, and a xoxF mutant was outcompeted in alfalfa nodule occupancy only when lanthanides were available [PMID 41626782](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41626782/). A study of three named strains (Bradyrhizobium diazoefficiens USDA 110, Bradyrhizobium sp. USDA 3456 and Sinorhizobium meliloti 2011) gave multiple lines of evidence for an XoxF–Calvin-Benson-Bassham pathway in those strains. Its authors say that genomic analyses suggest the metabolism may be widespread in both genera [PMID 41117506](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41117506/). In the open ocean, TMED127/Methylaequorales bacteria show a diel pattern of xoxF5 transcription in Sargasso Sea surface water [PMID 40970701](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40970701/). In a wetland sediment co-assembly, xoxF/EC 1.1.2.10 was annotated on 1,996 genes, against 428 for mxa-like EC 1.1.2.7. That paper stresses that this is annotation-based functional potential, not activity [PMID 42678158](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42678158/). Enzyme assays add a caveat about lanthanide effects. For Methylacidiphilum fumariolicum SolV MDH, La-, Ce- and Pr-MDH had higher specific activity in a protein-coupled assay than in a dye-coupled assay, so the assay used changes the apparent lanthanide trends [PMID 38269599](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38269599/).

The atlas's headline result is that xoxF is annotated in 3,690 genomes against 195 for mxaF, a ratio of 18.92 : 1. This is consistent with the literature's view of XoxF as the more widespread enzyme [PMID 41636527](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41636527/). It also matches the wetland annotation skew toward xoxF [PMID 42678158](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42678158/). The atlas extends that work to 293,059 GTDB-r214 genomes with a formal test and confidence interval. Its soil and sediment enrichment (OR 1.92) fits the rhizosphere and nodule findings in rhizobia [PMID 41626782](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41626782/). The marine enrichment is smaller (OR 1.31), but the marine transcription data show that the lineages involved can be active [PMID 40970701](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40970701/). The high xoxF rates in Acidobacteriota, Gemmatimonadota and Methylomirabilota, and the clade-restricted lanmodulin (62 genomes), have no counterpart in these candidates. They are novel at pangenome scale and should be treated as hypotheses.

The atlas shares the wetland study's main limit. Gene annotation shows potential, not lanthanide use. The PQQ cofactor gap and the lanmodulin co-occurrence of 79.0 %, just under the 80 % threshold, are not addressed by any candidate paper. The literature neither supports nor contradicts them.

## What the Corpus Shows

**xoxF far outnumbers mxaF.** xoxF (the lanthanide-dependent methanol dehydrogenase gene, KEGG K00114) is annotated in 3,690 genomes and mxaF (the calcium-dependent counterpart, K14028) in 195. That is a ratio of 18.92 : 1, with Clopper-Pearson 95% CI [13.07, 27.69] [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. A one-sided binomial test against the pre-registered threshold (xoxF fraction > 10/11 ≈ 0.909) gives p = 7.6 × 10⁻²² [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. The report says this confirms Chistoserdova's hypothesis at larger scale and supports Pol et al.'s claim that XoxF is the more widespread enzyme [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. Several phyla are xoxF-only, with zero mxaF, including Bacteroidota, Cyanobacteriota and Chloroflexota [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. See [[concepts/taxonomic-distribution-and-relative-prevalence-of-xoxf-and-mxaf-methanol-dehydrogenases]].

**Carriage extends beyond classical methylotrophs.** The highest per-genome xoxF rates are in Acidobacteriota (28 %), Gemmatimonadota (25 %) and Methylomirabilota (29 %). The report reads this as widening the candidate set of free-living lanthanide users, which is an interpretation of annotation counts [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. Canonical families are also enriched: Beijerinckiaceae has 171 xoxF genomes in 508, and Hyphomicrobiaceae has 33 in 56 [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. Within Pseudomonadota, Pseudomonadaceae alone contributes 566 xoxF genomes versus 1 mxaF [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. In Methylobacterium, 102 of 209 genomes carry xoxF, which the report calls consistent with the lanthanide-switch model [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas].

**Soil and sediment carry the environmental signal.** Against the generic_environmental reference (3.69 % xoxF rate), soil_sediment is 6.84 % (OR = odds ratio, 1.92, p_BH 6.1 × 10⁻³⁹; p_BH is the Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery-rate-adjusted p-value). Marine is 4.76 % (OR 1.31). Host_associated is strongly depleted (OR 0.058), which the report reads as consistent with absence of methylotrophy in the gut/host niche [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. Within Acidobacteriota the soil enrichment persists (OR=2.16, p_BH=2.2 × 10⁻⁵), which suggests it is not purely phylogenetic confounding [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. See [[concepts/environmental-association-of-lanthanide-dependent-methanol-oxidation-genes]].

**The PQQ cofactor gap is mostly annotation.** PQQ is described as an obligate XoxF cofactor, yet complete eggNOG pqqA-E is found in only 33 xoxF genomes (0.9 %) [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. Of 2,185 genomes with no eggNOG pqq, 1,288 (59 %) have at least one bakta PQQ product, which the report identifies as pure annotation gaps [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. A further 897 genomes (24.3 %) show no PQQ evidence from either source and are treated as unresolved candidates, not established absences [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. For future pangenome PQQ work the report recommends the union of eggNOG and bakta sources [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. See [[concepts/pqq-cofactor-biosynthesis-support-in-xoxf-bearing-genomes]].

**Lanmodulin is clade-restricted.** Lanmodulin is a small REE-binding protein. All 62 lanmodulin-positive genomes (10 species) fall in Beijerinckiaceae, Acetobacteraceae or Hyphomicrobiaceae (one-sided binomial p = 9.8 × 10⁻⁷) [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. Methylobacterium extorquens is the dominant carrier, with 22 genomes and one copy each [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. The report concludes lanmodulin is not a phylogenetically widespread REE handler, but only at this 62-genome resolution [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. See [[concepts/lanmodulin-clade-restriction-and-xoxf-co-occurrence]].

## Tensions and Caveats

**Pooled versus phylogeny-corrected ratios.** The naive pooled xoxF fraction is 0.950 [0.942, 0.956]. The family-equal-weight bootstrap gives 0.960 [0.941, 0.976], and the Bayesian GLMM (generalised linear mixed model) gives 0.993 [0.992, 0.994]. The GLMM ratio is ~143 : 1 [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. All three put the lower bound above the 10 : 1 threshold [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. The report attributes the larger figure to random effects absorbing phylum-level heterogeneity. That is its explanation, not a separate measurement [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. The correction used taxonomic random effects, not an explicit tree [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas].

**Weak or null results.** Lanmodulin and xoxF co-occur in 49 / 62 = 79.0 % of lanmodulin-positive genomes. That is just under the 80 % threshold (p = 0.65), so H3b is not formally supported. The 13 lanmodulin-without-xoxF genomes may reflect annotation incompleteness or alternative pathways, and these readings are untested [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. Volcanic_geothermal (OR 0.86, p_BH 0.20) and mining (OR 1.12, p_BH 0.38) are null. The ree_impacted class (n=37, 10.81 %, OR 3.51, p_BH 0.082) is descriptive only and comes from a single bioproject [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. The report considers H2 only partially supported [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas].

**Annotation limits.** Bakta over-calls, and eggNOG is used as primary [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. The report did not screen for pseudogenes, truncated ORFs or assembly fragmentation, so the unresolved 24 % cannot be split into assembly incompleteness, pseudogenization or community-acquired PQQ [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. Gene-call presence of xoxF does not show that lanthanides are used.

**Coverage and classification.** AlphaEarth coverage of xoxF genomes is 39.5 % (1,457 / 3,690), which leaves 60.5 % without environmental coordinates [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. The ncbi_env classes come from regex text mining, and host_associated is broad. The report states that soil enrichment and host depletion survive possible misclassification [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. H2 relies on within-phylum stratification, not a fully phylogeny-aware model [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. Methylomirabilota (3.3 : 1) and Verrucomicrobiota (3.8 : 1) have ratios far below the global value [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas].
