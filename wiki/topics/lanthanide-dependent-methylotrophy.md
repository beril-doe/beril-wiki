# Lanthanide-Dependent Methylotrophy

Lanthanide-dependent methylotrophy is the use of rare-earth elements (REEs, the lanthanides) as cofactors for methanol oxidation by pyrroloquinoline-quinone (PQQ) methanol dehydrogenases. This corpus speaks to it through one large genome-annotation project, the lanthanide methylotrophy atlas, which scans 293,059 Genome Taxonomy Database (GTDB) genomes for lanthanide-related markers and tests where they occur [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. This hub arranges three member concepts into one narrative.

## Literature Context

Lanthanides were long considered biologically inert, but they are now known to strongly affect the metabolism of methylotrophic bacteria [PMID 31166187](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31166187/). They were first found in the active site of the XoxF-type methanol dehydrogenase of *Methylacidiphilum fumariolicum* SolV [PMID 30308436](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30308436/). XoxF enzymes are homodimers that carry a rare-earth element, whereas the calcium-dependent MxaFI enzyme is a heterotetramer, and XoxF-type enzymes with the REE-binding motif occur in methylotrophs, methanotrophs and organisms not known for that lifestyle [PMID 24816778](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24816778/). Reviews also argue that XoxF homologs are encoded in a wide variety of microbes, and that lanthanide-dependent enzymes may be more widespread than first thought [PMID 27357406](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27357406/), [PMID 31166187](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31166187/). Some organisms carry both enzymes, for example a thermophilic alphaproteobacterial isolate with a complete mxaF cluster and xoxF1 [PMID 33668875](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33668875/), and a lanthanide "switch" reciprocally regulates the two in other methylotrophs [PMID 40846390](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40846390/). The lanthanide-binding protein lanmodulin has been characterised in *Methylorubrum extorquens* AM1 and is described as a highly selective chelator for trivalent f-elements [PMID 33867019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33867019/), [PMID 37897703](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37897703/).

Environmental work links XoxF to light-lanthanide-rich settings: XoxF was found in the metaproteome of a community in shale rock [PMID 33547466](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33547466/) and in the metagenome and metaproteome of a deep underground mine mat [PMID 35409305](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35409305/). It also appears in marine isolates [PMID 29755426](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29755426/), and light REE removal accompanied methane consumption in Deepwater Horizon plume waters [PMID 28871146](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28871146/). A methanotroph requiring lanthanides was isolated from circumneutral pond sediment [PMID 32037377](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32037377/). These studies are single-site or single-isolate cases rather than genome-wide surveys.

The atlas's central result, xoxF in 3,690 genomes against 195 for mxaF, is consistent with and extends the claim of broad XoxF distribution, because it quantifies the ratio across 293,059 genomes and 29 testable phyla instead of inferring it from selected organisms. Lanmodulin restricted to 62 genomes in three alphaproteobacterial methylotroph families is new as a systematic distribution, though it fits lanmodulin's characterisation in a single model methylotroph. The 79.0% xoxF co-occurrence in lanmodulin genomes, just under the pre-registered 80% threshold, has no direct counterpart in these papers. The soil/sediment and marine enrichments are compatible with the shale, mine and marine observations. The null result for mining and volcanic/geothermal categories, and the low xoxF carriage in REE-containing acid mine drainage MAGs, weaken, without refuting, the idea that REE-rich settings select for XoxF. The 4/37 carriage is descriptive and from a single collection, not a comparative test, and REE-impacted genomes show a descriptive odds ratio of 3.51 (adjusted p 0.082) that did not clear FDR, so selection is neither established nor excluded. The corpus's annotation-based calls also cannot address the biochemical questions raised in the literature, such as lanthanide preference or activity.

## What the Corpus Shows

**The lanthanide-dependent enzyme is far more widespread than the calcium-dependent one.** eggNOG (a functional annotation tool) assigned the xoxF marker (lanthanide-dependent methanol dehydrogenase) to 3,690 genomes and the mxaF marker (calcium-dependent) to 195. The global xoxF:mxaF ratio is 18.92 : 1, with a Clopper-Pearson 95% confidence interval (CI) of [13.07, 27.69]. The xoxF fraction of joint calls is 0.9498 [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. This is a direct count with strong statistics. See [[concepts/lanthanide-dependent-methanol-dehydrogenase-predominance]].

**The pattern spans many lineages.** After Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) correction across 29 testable phyla, xoxF predominance held in every phylum with any methanol dehydrogenase calls and adequate sample size. Acidobacteriota showed 285 xoxF against 3 mxaF, and several phyla, including Bacteroidota and Chloroflexota, had no mxaF annotations at all [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. The claim concerns gene carriage inferred from annotation. That lanthanide-dependent oxidation is the ecologically dominant mode remains a hypothesis these data support [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas].

**The lanthanide-binding chelator is much narrower.** Bakta-validated lanmodulin (a REE-binding protein) occurs in 62 genomes from 10 species. All of them fall within Beijerinckiaceae, Acetobacteraceae or Hyphomicrobiaceae, the three α-Proteobacterial methylotroph families pre-specified in the hypothesis (one-sided binomial p = 9.8 × 10⁻⁷) [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. Within *Methylobacterium*, xoxF appears in 102/209 genomes and lanmodulin in 46/209 [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. See [[concepts/lanmodulin-narrow-distribution]].

**Habitat enrichment does not track REE richness.** xoxF is enriched in soil/sediment (odds ratio 1.92, adjusted p 6.1 × 10⁻³⁹) and marine samples (odds ratio 1.31, adjusted p 7.8 × 10⁻⁷). Mining (adjusted p 0.38) and volcanic/geothermal (adjusted p 0.20) categories are null, and host-associated samples are strongly depleted (odds ratio 0.058) [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. Among 37 metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs) from REE-containing acid mine drainage, only 4/37 carry any xoxF and 0/37 carry validated lanmodulin or xoxJ. In this descriptive analysis of a single sample collection, not a comparative test, their gene content reads as an acid and metal stress profile [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. This **weakens**, without refuting, a simple model in which REE availability drives the trait. See [[concepts/environmental-distribution-of-lanthanide-methylotrophy]].

**The dominant lanmodulin carrier is a single well-known methylotroph.** [[entities/methylobacterium-extorquens]] contributes 22 genomes with 1 lanmodulin copy each, so paralogs do not explain the pattern. Other contributors include an uncharacterised Acetobacteraceae genus (`g__BOG-930`, 12 genomes), *M. thiocyanatum* (6), *M. rhodesianum* (6), *M. aminovorans* (4), *Hyphomicrobium_B* (2) and *Methylocella* (2) [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. In this genus the dehydrogenase gene appears more often than the chelator, which **supports** the narrower-chelator claim [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas].

**Within Pseudomonadota, carriage is uneven.** Pseudomonadota hold 2,988 xoxF genomes versus 171 mxaF, but only a 2.5 % xoxF rate. *Pseudomonadaceae* alone contribute 566 xoxF genomes versus 1 mxaF. Canonical methylotroph-rich families are far richer in carriers: *Beijerinckiaceae* show 171 xoxF in 508 genomes (33.7 %) and *Hyphomicrobiaceae* 33 in 56 (58.9 %) [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. High carrier rates and strong dominance ratios do not always go together across phyla, and the report does not test their relationship formally [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas].

**The soil signal is not purely phylogenetic, at least in one phylum.** The soil/sediment enrichment holds within Acidobacteriota, the phylum with the highest per-genome xoxF carriage (within-phylum OR=2.16, p_BH=2.2 × 10⁻⁵) [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas]. The atlas suggests that this fits methanol oxidation as a known soil process and soil as a reservoir of lanthanide minerals, so habitat-level REE availability and methylotrophic niche cannot be separated with these data. That methanol supply rather than lanthanide abundance governs xoxF occurrence remains a hypothesis [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas].

**Taken together,** the marker for the enzyme is common and broad, the chelator marker is restricted, and the robust habitat enrichments are in broad soil/sediment and marine categories. The REE-impacted category shows only a non-significant descriptive elevation, so the evidence is partial and does not establish selection for or against the trait. Every figure comes from one project [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas].

## Tensions and Caveats

- **Odds ratio versus carriage in REE-impacted samples.** The 37 REE-impacted genomes show a descriptive odds ratio of 3.51 (adjusted p 0.082, too small to clear FDR), yet only 4/37 REE-AMD MAGs carry xoxF. Neither result establishes selection for or against the trait [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas].
- **Chelator versus dehydrogenase coupling.** xoxF co-occurred in 49/62 = 79.0% of lanmodulin genomes, just under the pre-registered 80% threshold (p = 0.65). The 13 exceptions may reflect annotation gaps or an alternative lanthanide pathway, and the report does not decide between them [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas].
- **Annotation-bounded detection.** All calls depend on eggNOG and Bakta. Novel REE-handling enzymes without KEGG/RefSeq homologs would be missed, so low carriage of known markers is not absence of capability [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas].
- **Unresolved PQQ gaps.** 897 xoxF-carrying genomes (24% of carriers) lack PQQ evidence from either annotation source. Assembly incompleteness, pseudogenization and community-acquired PQQ are all unresolved explanations [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas].
- **Confounding and sampling.** The within-phylum soil check covers only Acidobacteriota (odds ratio 2.16, p_BH = 2.2 × 10⁻⁵). Thinly sampled phyla such as Methylomirabilota (80 genomes) have marginal statistics [src: lanthanide_methylotrophy_atlas].
