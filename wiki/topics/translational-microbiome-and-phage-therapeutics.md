# Translational Microbiome and Phage Therapeutics

This topic asks how microbiome measurements become interventions: which species to remove or displace, in which patients, with what evidence that the intervention will work and what it will cost the community. The corpus speaks to it from two sides. One is a Crohn's disease (CD) phage-targeting project, which scores pathobionts (disease-associated gut residents) and designs cocktails [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. The other is a cystic fibrosis (CF) formulation project that builds protective commensal consortia against *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* PA14 [src: cf_formulation_design]. A phage defense-system analysis [src: snipe_defense_system] and the central digests [src: discoveries, pitfalls] add cross-cutting caveats. Ten concept pages are synthesized below.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Removal starts with target selection, and selection is fragile.** The CD project scored 71 rigor-controlled candidates and found 6 actionable targets [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. A separate rigor repair applied three evidence filters within each ecotype (a recurring gut community type). It passed 0 of 18 E1 candidates and 3 of 15 E3 candidates, so Tier-A shrank from 33 candidates to 3, all in E3 [src: discoveries]. An independent within-study check agrees that most E1 candidates are ecotype markers, not CD drivers [src: pitfalls]. See [[concepts/pathobiont-target-prioritization-for-ibd-phage-therapy]]. Within that picture, iron acquisition is narrowed to the adherent-invasive *E. coli* (AIEC) subset. Biosynthetic gene clusters (BGCs) with iron-siderophore matches numbered 54 in *E. coli* and 0 in the other 5 actionable species [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. This informs whether to target a species broadly or only its AIEC subset [src: discoveries]. See [[concepts/iron-acquisition-as-an-aiec-specific-genomic-specialization-in-crohn-s-disease]].

**Phage availability is the structural ceiling for gut anaerobes.** *E. coli* has clinical precedent and a 5-phage cocktail that covers 94.7 % of the 188 PhageFoundry strains [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. Three of the six actionable species have no lytic phage in the curated reference, and the two gap species, *H. hathewayi* and *F. plautii*, have the highest NB05 scores among the gaps, 4.0 and 3.3 [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. HMP2 viromics shows no observations of phages targeting five of the six pathobionts [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. The report ranks external database queries (INPHARED, IMG/VR) as the highest-priority follow-up [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. See [[concepts/phage-availability-for-gut-pathobionts-and-the-gut-anaerobe-phage-gap]].

**Per-patient design therefore has to be hybrid.** All 9 E1 patients carry the full 5-species Tier-A module, but only 3 of the 5 species have direct lytic phage options, so a pure phage cocktail is infeasible for any E1 patient [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. Drafts exist for 14 of 23 patients, and the largest group, 12 patients, is held in reserve for flare [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. *E. coli* is present in only 8 of 23 patients (35 %) [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. A state-dependent dosing rule comes from one patient, 6967, whose cocktail Jaccard overlap between visits is 0.60 [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. See [[concepts/ecotype-aware-per-patient-phage-cocktail-design-and-state-dependent-dosing]].

**Host range depends on receptors, with defense systems as a proposed modifier.** In the *E. coli* panel (188 strains, 96 phages, 17,672 binary outcomes), the underlying paper's key conclusion, as the report states it, is that adsorption factors (surface receptors) are the primary predictors of infection, not antiphage defense systems [src: snipe_defense_system]. Infection rates differ by family: Myoviridae 43.4 %, Podoviridae 13.0 %, Siphoviridae 9.7 % [src: snipe_defense_system]. The SNIPE defense domain DUF4041 appeared only in *Klebsiella* among four PhageFoundry pathogens, which led to a hypothesis that SNIPE could affect *Klebsiella* phage therapy [src: snipe_defense_system]. See [[concepts/adsorption-receptors-versus-defense-systems-as-determinants-of-phage-host-range]].

**Displacement: consortia designed against several criteria at once.** For CF airways, the report scored communities of 1–5 organisms on niche coverage, internal complementarity, inhibition, engraftability and safety [src: cf_formulation_design]. The recommended primary candidate is the k=2 pair *R. dentocariosa* + *N. mucosa*, with the k=3 community as an aspirational second line [src: cf_formulation_design]. Persistence is inferred from patient prevalence and transcriptional activity, and from pangenome isolation sources: 33–38 % of *N. mucosa* and *R. dentocariosa* genomes are respiratory [src: cf_formulation_design]. See [[concepts/multi-criterion-design-of-protective-microbiome-formulations]] and [[concepts/engraftability-and-lung-adaptation-of-candidate-commensals]].

**Mechanism: competition is real but partial.** Metabolic overlap with PA14 predicts inhibition (r = 0.384, p = 2.3×10⁻⁶), and the multivariate model gives R² = 0.274 [src: cf_formulation_design]. Adding genus-level taxonomy raises R² to 0.360, which the report reads as likely direct antagonism [src: cf_formulation_design]. The top FDA-safe inhibitors show 57–74 % more inhibition than metabolic overlap predicts [src: cf_formulation_design, discoveries]. See [[concepts/metabolic-competition-versus-direct-antagonism-in-pathogen-inhibition]].

**Supporting the consortium: prebiotics and interactions.** Among tested substrates, the assays found no selective prebiotic; PA14 outgrew the average commensal on every tested substrate [src: cf_formulation_design]. A genomic comparison instead nominated six pathways complete in at least one commensal but absent in PA14: xylitol, myoinositol, xylose, arabinose, fucose and rhamnose [src: cf_formulation_design, discoveries]. These are predictions that need experimental validation [src: cf_formulation_design]. See [[concepts/selective-prebiotics-for-commensal-consortia]]. Pairwise tests, the cost-to-community question, are thin: mean synergy was −5.8 %, with *N. mucosa* pairs near-additive [src: cf_formulation_design]. See [[concepts/pairwise-interaction-among-commensals-in-competition-assays]].

## Tensions and Caveats

**Small, single-source evidence.** The CD cocktail framework rests on 23 patients, and the drift rule on one patient, with visit confidences of 0.64 and 0.41 [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. E3 target status rests on one study [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. The CF work uses one pathogen strain, PA14 [src: cf_formulation_design].

**Scores versus repair.** The top NB05 scorers, *H. hathewayi* (4.0) and *M. gnavus* (3.8), have the weakest phage availability [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. The NB05 list and the repair results are reported separately, and the claims do not say how one maps onto the other [src: discoveries, ibd_phage_targeting].

**Receptors versus defense.** The *E. coli* paper points to receptors, yet the same report proposes SNIPE as a therapy-relevant defense in *Klebsiella*. Its argument is that SNIPE acts at the injection step during ManYZ transit, and no direct test exists [src: snipe_defense_system]. The *Klebsiella* link is an interpretation of annotation counts. The data have no fitness tables for it [src: snipe_defense_system].

**Coverage versus engraftability.** The digest says competitive exclusion needs 3+ organisms covering the niche, yet the report recommends k=2, which has 0.820 engraftability but 18 % coverage. k=3 has 100 % coverage but 0.140 engraftability [src: discoveries, cf_formulation_design]. Bootstrap intervals for k=2 through k=5 overlap, so those sizes are not statistically distinguishable [src: cf_formulation_design].

**Overfit and untested additivity.** Five-fold cross-validation gives CV R² = 0.145 ± 0.142 against a training R² of 0.274, so the metabolic-inhibition prediction model is overfit and its effect sizes should be read conservatively [src: cf_formulation_design]. All formulation rankings assume additive inhibition, and only 5 of the 10 possible pairs are covered [src: cf_formulation_design]. The report's own counts of pairs and comparisons do not reconcile [src: cf_formulation_design]. One untested antagonistic pair could invalidate the design [src: cf_formulation_design].

**Proxies for persistence and clinical applicability.** Patient prevalence is not a direct measure of colonization after administration [src: cf_formulation_design]. The 5-phage cocktail was assessed on PhageFoundry strains, not patient isolates, and the susceptibility matrix is binary, with no titer or burst size [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. HMP2 and PhageFoundry phages do not name-overlap, so in-vivo gut delivery is not validated [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. The odds ratio of 44 for iron BGCs is partly inflated by rarity of iron matches in the catalog and should not be read as a precise effect size [src: ibd_phage_targeting].
