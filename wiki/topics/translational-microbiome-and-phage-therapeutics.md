# Translational Microbiome and Phage Therapeutics

This topic asks how microbiome measurements become interventions: which species to remove or displace, in which patients, with what evidence that the intervention will work and what it will cost the community. The corpus speaks to it through two worked cases: a Crohn's disease (CD) phage-targeting project on gut metagenomes and metabolomes, and a cystic-fibrosis (CF) airway project designing a consortium to suppress *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* [src: ibd_phage_targeting, cf_formulation_design]. The cross-project digest adds portability and annotation lessons [src: discoveries]. The page below has four argued themes, then tensions and a reading path.

## Literature Context

The review literature frames IBD as arising from genetic risk, environment and gut microbiota, each necessary but not sufficient, and calls for moving beyond association toward mechanism and microbiome-based diagnostics and interventions ([PMID 34555295](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34555295/)). Precision approaches are argued to need attention to recipient and donor selection, diet and xenobiotics, and strain-specific interactions ([PMID 40190259](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40190259/)). Fecal microbiota transplantation is guideline-integrated for recurrent *Clostridioides difficile* infection, but its use in IBD is still under study ([PMID 38717124](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38717124/)). For Crohn's disease specifically, evidence is described as insufficient to endorse it as a definitive treatment ([PMID 39958442](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39958442/)). Microbial metabolites, notably bile acids, short-chain fatty acids and tryptophan metabolites, are implicated in IBD pathogenesis ([PMID 32076145](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32076145/)).

Phage targeting of pathobionts has direct precedent. Virulent phages reduced adherent-invasive *E. coli* (AIEC) strain LF82 in colonised transgenic mice and in ileal biopsy homogenates from CD patients, and a single dose of a three-phage cocktail reduced colitis symptoms in mice ([PMID 28130329](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28130329/)). A novel lytic phage against a clinical CD AIEC strain has since been isolated, with in vitro plaque morphology and adsorption depending on medium and carbon source ([PMID 41665752](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41665752/)). A five-phage combination against an IBD-associated *Klebsiella pneumoniae* clade suppressed the pathobiont in colitis-prone mice and was tolerated in healthy volunteers, in a proof-of-concept setting ([PMID 35931020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35931020/)). Caveats: LF82 carries five prophages and blocks lytic induction of its most active one in macrophages ([PMID 36730457](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36730457/)), and bile acids inhibited AIEC gut colonization in a mouse model ([PMID 39405098](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39405098/)).

Against this background, the corpus's ranking of *E. coli* as the species with clinical-trial-stage phage evidence is consistent with the AIEC-targeting literature, while its iron-centred mechanism for *E. coli* has no counterpart in these abstracts and is not tested by them. The reviews do not report a scoring scheme, a co-occurrence-module design, or within-carrier pathway testing, so the Tier-A framework, the finding that carriage prevalence rather than within-carrier shift drives several species' signals, and the *E. coli* CD-down pathway direction are extensions without direct literature counterpart. The bile-acid depletion cost for the 7α-dehydroxylating species is an inference in the corpus; the literature establishes only that bile-acid metabolites matter in IBD and that bile acids affect AIEC colonization in mice, so the corpus claim remains a hypothesis. The reviews' emphasis on strain-specific interactions and recipient selection bears on the corpus's species-level scoring and patient stratification, though none of these papers tests ecotypes or their reproducibility.

For the cystic-fibrosis case, the cited literature concerns *P. aeruginosa* ecology rather than consortium design: purine exchange between *P. aeruginosa* and *Staphylococcus aureus* shows that bidirectional nutrient exchange between these competitors is possible ([PMID 33535659](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33535659/)), and in liquid-culture experiments early longitudinal CF isolates out-competed their clonal progeny in co-culture, with early isolates growing faster alongside progeny in 10 of 12 examined patient courses ([PMID 31985137](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31985137/)). Both point to strain and context dependence, which supports the corpus's caution that PA14-only, planktonic results may not generalise. Corpus findings on metabolic-overlap-based inhibition are novel relative to these papers.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Target selection is a scoring problem, and phage evidence lags it.** The IBD project scored 71 unique rigor-controlled candidates and returned 6 actionable Tier-A species (those clearing the project's four-criterion score threshold). Their phage evidence ranged from clinical-trial-stage for *Escherichia coli* to lytic-literature for *Eggerthella lenta* and *Enterocloster bolteae*, temperate-only for *Mediterraneibacter gnavus*, and gaps for *Hungatella hathewayi* and *Flavonifractor plautii* [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. Host-range evidence is therefore treated as one input to a hybrid-cocktail design, not a proxy for efficacy ([[concepts/phage-therapy-evidence-translation]]). 5 of the 6 actionable species sit in one pathobiont co-occurrence module per subnet, which makes multi-target cocktails a reasonable design. That is a hypothesis built on cross-sectional correlation; no perturbation tested whether removing one member moves the others. Membership also shifts between community states (ecotypes E1, the Bacteroides2 transitional state, and E3, the severe Bacteroides-expanded state): *F. plautii* is in the main module in E1 but in the generalist module in E3 ([[concepts/species-cooccurrence-modules-as-intervention-units]]) [src: ibd_phage_targeting].

**A species can be "disease-associated" for three different reasons, and each implies a different intervention.** Within-carrier pathway testing finds substantial CD-versus-non-IBD shifts in *H. hathewayi* (33 passing pathways) and *E. coli* (20 passing), but almost none in four other Tier-A species. The report attributes those species' signal to carriage prevalence rather than within-carrier metabolic shift. That is a pathway-level null, not an absence of association ([[concepts/carriage-prevalence-versus-within-carrier-function]]) [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. For *E. coli* the within-carrier pathway direction is CD-down, opposite to its CD-up relative abundance. Two readings are offered, an AIEC-specialized subset or competition from other Enterobacteriaceae, and the data reported do not separate them [src: ibd_phage_targeting].

**Mechanism narratives converge on iron for *E. coli* but leave other targets in the dark.** Iron and heme acquisition is the dominant CD-up pathway theme (odds ratio, OR, 8.1; false discovery rate, FDR, 7e-6), and the genomic signal sits almost entirely in *E. coli* ([[concepts/iron-acquisition-and-pathobiont-expansion]]) [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. The larger genomic odds ratios (iron OR=44) use the full biosynthetic gene cluster (BGC) catalog as an unmatched comparator and should not be read as precise effect sizes. The formal verdict remained PARTIALLY SUPPORTED, because the species-by-BGC interaction test was dropped as a scope limit [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. The five other core species sit in "MIBiG dark matter", meaning significant BGC content that the curated MIBiG reference catalog cannot name, so an absent annotation is not an absent mechanism ([[concepts/pathobiont-biosynthetic-gene-cluster-repertoires]]) [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. At the metabolite level, polyamines (OR 14.6, FDR 0.008) and long-chain polyunsaturated fatty acids (OR 7.9, FDR 0.009) passed a class-enrichment gate. This is one cohort awaiting replication ([[concepts/metabolite-class-signatures-of-gut-inflammation]]) [src: ibd_phage_targeting].

**Removing a target has costs a priority score does not show.** *F. plautii*, *E. lenta* and *E. bolteae* form a bile-acid 7α-dehydroxylation network, supported by substrate–product correlations in 468 paired CSM* HMP2 samples, a single cohort. *M. gnavus* and *E. coli* are outside it. The expected shift of the bile-acid pool toward inflammatory primary tauro-conjugated forms on depletion is an inference, not a measured intervention outcome ([[concepts/ecological-cost-of-microbiome-target-depletion]]) [src: ibd_phage_targeting, discoveries]. The digest asks that every target also carry annotations for iron/AIEC (adherent-invasive *E. coli*) specialization and for abundance versus strain-content mediation [src: discoveries].

**Patient stratification is promising but fragile.** A K = 4 consensus on 8,489 samples gives four ecotypes (community-state clusters) that resemble published enterotypes, with CD and UC patients spread across E1 and E3 [src: discoveries]. Cross-method per-sample agreement is only 48.9 % at K = 4 and leave-one-study-out ARI (adjusted Rand index) is 0.113, so resemblance to the literature does not settle reproducibility ([[concepts/gut-microbiome-ecotypes-as-patient-strata]]) [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. Cheap proxies do not yet substitute: a covariate classifier reached macro one-versus-rest area under the curve (AUC) 0.80 in cross-validation but only 41 % agreement with metagenomics on UC Davis, so metagenomics remains required. The proposed 4–6-species quantitative PCR (qPCR) panel rests on one patient's trajectory and is a hypothesis ([[concepts/proxy-assays-for-community-state-assignment]]) [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. Metabolite-feature clustering did not beat taxonomy across cohorts (leave-one-study-out ARI 0.000 versus 0.113). Its verdict is bounded by an unbatch-corrected, 111-feature bridge ([[concepts/cross-cohort-microbiome-portability]]) [src: ibd_phage_targeting, discoveries].

**The same discipline applies to differential-abundance calls and to the CF consortium.** Normalization and contrast design can reverse a call: *Roseburia intestinalis* flips from raw CD↑ to centered-log-ratio CD↓, yet *Clostridium scindens* stays CD-enriched under both methods ([[concepts/compositional-robustness-of-differential-abundance-calls]]) [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. In the CF case, metabolic overlap with PA14 predicts inhibition (r = 0.384, p = 2.3×10⁻⁶). The multivariate training R² is 0.274, falling to a cross-validated 0.145 ± 0.142, so planktonic inhibition is only part of the story ([[concepts/competitive-exclusion-consortium-design]]) [src: cf_formulation_design]. All assays used PA14, the laboratory reference strain of *P. aeruginosa* (ExoU+) representing <5% of CF isolates, while CF isolates are 94% ExoS+ ([[concepts/reference-strain-representativeness]]) [src: cf_formulation_design].

## Tensions and Caveats

**Within-ecotype target lists disagree.** The digest reports 33 within-ecotype candidates collapsing to 3 under a stricter filter and calls the list not usable. The revised meta-analysis reports 51 E1 and 40 provisional E3 candidates. Both are recorded as stated; see [[conflicts/conflict--compositional-robustness-of-differential-abundance-calls--b440b98c]] [src: discoveries, ibd_phage_targeting].

**Internal discrepancies stand unreconciled.** The covariate classifier's AUC clears its threshold while patient-level agreement does not [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. The *E. lenta* CB-ORF (candidate-BGC open reading frame, quantified at the metagenomic read level) pattern is CD-down, although the group of dark-matter species is described as CB-ORF-enriched at read level [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. In the CF report, "no individual commensal outgrows PA14" sits beside a 13.8% maximum-growth-rate advantage and a 43.1% lag advantage on substrate comparisons; the source never clarifies whether "outgrows" means a different metric or comparison set, so the discrepancy is unresolved. The PA14-like share of CF isolates is given as both <5% and 5% [src: cf_formulation_design].

**Load-bearing limits.** The co-occurrence modules, the bile-acid network and the metabolite-class enrichment rest on cross-sectional, single-project analyses of one paired dataset or cohort each. Mechanisms such as drug metabolism in *E. lenta* or the bile-acid consequence of depletion are borrowed from literature or inferred, not tested here [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. The CF results are planktonic, not biofilm, and are specific to PA14 [src: cf_formulation_design].
