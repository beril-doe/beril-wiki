# Translational Microbiome and Phage Therapeutics

This hub covers the part of the corpus that moves from *describing* microbial communities to *designing interventions* in them: defined commensal consortia meant to suppress a pathogen, and lytic-phage cocktails meant to remove specific gut pathobionts from specific patients. Two projects supply the primary design evidence — a cystic-fibrosis airway formulation study built around suppressing [[entities/pseudomonas-aeruginosa]], and an inflammatory-bowel-disease (IBD) phage-targeting study built around a 23-patient Crohn's disease cohort — and a third body of work, on cross-cohort portability, governs whether the classifiers and biomarkers these designs depend on survive the move to a new cohort at all. Read together, the corpus's translational message is consistent: the assays reliably rank *candidates*, and almost never establish *efficacy*.

## Literature Context

Published work establishes the therapeutic frame this corpus operates in but rarely supplies the design-stage evidence the corpus interrogates. A review of artificial microbiome therapeutics positions microbiota consortia, bacteriophages, bacterial metabolites and engineered probiotics as an evolution of current modulators — probiotics, prebiotics and faecal microbiota transplantation, whose exploitation is hindered by poor precision, regulatory and safety issues, and the impossibility of reproducible, targeted treatment — and holds that artificial therapeutics promise safe and reproducible effects with variable levels of precision ([PMID 38604200](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38604200/)). The preclinical antibacterial pipeline shows the same shift at scale: 407 projects from 314 institutions as of 1 May 2019, an unprecedented number of pathogen-specific and adjunctive approaches including engineered phages and microbiome-modifying strategies, with translational hurdles — especially development pathways showing clinical impact for non-traditional approaches — explicitly not yet addressed ([PMID 31745331](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31745331/)). Phage-therapy reviews concur: intestinal-infection efficacy data come largely from animal models, and phage selection difficulty, host specificity and safety remain open ([PMID 41415829](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41415829/)); in hematologic malignancies direct clinical efficacy evidence is limited and largely preclinical or compassionate-use, with metagenomics-guided personalization and AI-optimized cocktail design named as the route forward ([PMID 41384994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41384994/)).

This corpus is consistent with that literature and sharper on several points. Cocktail design to broaden host range and delay resistance is precedented in *Klebsiella pneumoniae*: ΦK2044 killed only KL1 capsule-type strains while the two sewage-derived phages ΦKR1 and ΦKR8 targeted 13 different capsular serotypes, and the three-phage cocktail killed bacteria in biofilms, delayed phage resistance, and improved 7-day survival in *Galleria mellonella* and mouse models ([PMID 39543151](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39543151/)). The corpus's greedy set-cover cocktails achieve high coverage of a tested strain panel but lack the in-vivo delivery validation that study supplies, so its residual phage-resistant strain fraction and its refusal to call coverage efficacy sit inside the known resistance problem rather than resolving it (see [[concepts/phage-therapy-evidence-translation]]). Targeting one IBD-enriched pathobiont with a phage-derived agent is likewise precedented — the endolysin phiCS-1 lysed *Clostridium symbiosum* and attenuated colitis ([PMID 41881016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41881016/)) — supporting the corpus's hybrid strategy where lytic phages are absent or temperate-only.

Two contributions extend this picture. First, the corpus quantifies patient-level target heterogeneity and ecological-state assignment in a Crohn's cohort, shows population-level prioritization does not predict individual profiles, and shows targets shifting between visits — an operational counterpart to the metagenomics-guided personalization reviews advocate but do not measure ([PMID 41384994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41384994/), [PMID 41415829](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41415829/)). Second, its portability work — classifier agreement collapsing on a new cohort, metabolite structure separating entirely by study — supplies concrete failure modes that put the promised reproducibility of designed interventions ([PMID 38604200](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38604200/)) to an empirical test; see [[concepts/cross-cohort-microbiome-portability]]. The tradeoff surface in [[concepts/competitive-exclusion-consortium-design]] has no direct counterpart here; the closest is bacteria-and-bacteriophage consortia correlating with protective intestinal metabolites and motivating consortium formulations ([PMID 38172339](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38172339/)), which is correlative rather than a design objective. No candidate contradicts the corpus's conclusion that these assays rank candidates rather than establish efficacy.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Targets are patient-specific, and population-level prioritization does not predict them.** In 23 UC Davis Crohn's disease patients, *Mediterraneibacter gnavus* was present in 21 (91 %), *Hungatella hathewayi* in 19 (83 %), *Enterocloster bolteae* in 19 (83 %), *Flavonifractor plautii* in 18 (78 %), *Eggerthella lenta* in 16 (70 %), and [[entities/escherichia-coli]] in only 8 (35 %) [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. Because target prevalence and phage tractability diverged across the six actionable ("Tier-A") species, the patient-level target profile could not be inferred from population-level ranking alone [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. Ecological state adds a second axis: the same 23 patients split into 7 E0, 9 E1, 6 E3, and 1 mixed-ecotype assignment, with no patient in E2 [src: ibd_phage_targeting], where ecotypes are the four community states — E0 diverse commensal (n=3,604), E1 *Bacteroides2* transitional (n=2,601), E2 *Prevotella copri* (n=920), E3 severe *Bacteroides*-expanded (n=1,364) — derived from 8,489 curatedMetagenomicData samples [src: discoveries]. See [[concepts/phage-therapy-evidence-translation]].

**Phage host-range data is dense but panel-bound.** The PhageFoundry evidence layer held 96 phages, 188 *E. coli* strains, and 17,672 experimentally tested susceptibility pairs, of which 3,929 were susceptible — a 22 % susceptibility rate [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. A greedy minimum-set-cover search (repeatedly adding the phage that covers the most still-uncovered strains) selected five phages — DIJ07_P2, LF73_P1, AL505_Ev3, 55989_P2, LF110_P2 — covering 94.7 % of the 188 tested strains, and an eight-phage design reached 98.4 % [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. Twenty-six strains (14 %) were phage-resistant at ≤5 % susceptibility, so even a high-coverage cocktail leaves a measurable resistant fraction [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. Critically, these coverage values describe the PhageFoundry panel, not the patient isolates: no patient-isolate matching, AIEC-versus-commensal labels, burst sizes, titers, or in-vivo delivery validation were available [src: ibd_phage_targeting].

**Coverage gaps force hybrid, not pure-phage, designs.** Phage evidence ranged from clinical-trial-stage for *E. coli*, to lytic-literature evidence for *E. lenta* and *E. bolteae*, to temperate-only (lysogeny-capable, therefore unsuitable for straightforward lytic killing) for *M. gnavus*, to outright coverage gaps for *H. hathewayi* and *F. plautii* [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. All 9 E1 patients carried the full five-species E1 pathobiont module, which makes a pure phage cocktail structurally infeasible for them and supports a three-strategy hybrid: direct phage targeting where lytic options exist, alternatives such as GAG-degrading enzyme inhibitors or bile-acid co-therapy, and limited or engineered approaches for *M. gnavus* [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. *F. plautii* was deprioritized despite a Tier-A score because it combined the highest bile-acid coupling cost with a phage gap [src: ibd_phage_targeting].

**Consortium design is multi-objective, and the objectives actively conflict.** In synthetic cystic-fibrosis sputum, PA14 preferred amino acids (endpoint OD 0.60 proline, 0.56 histidine, 0.46 ornithine) over glucose at OD 0.22 [src: cf_formulation_design]. Across 142 isolates, metabolic overlap with PA14 predicted planktonic inhibition at r = 0.384, p = 2.3×10⁻⁶, with a multivariate model reaching R² = 0.274 (R² = 0.360 adding genus) but only CV R² = 0.145 ± 0.142 under five-fold cross-validation — roughly 73 % of inhibition variance unexplained [src: cf_formulation_design]. The tradeoff surface is explicit: the k=2 formulation ([[entities/neisseria-mucosa]] + *Rothia dentocariosa*) gave 18 % niche coverage, 84 % inhibition, engraftability 0.820, while the k=3 formulation reached 100 % coverage but 75 % inhibition and engraftability 0.140, because *Micrococcus luteus* — which grew on 9 of PA14's 11 preferred substrates — had best inhibition 38 % and engraftability 0.000 [src: cf_formulation_design]. Exhaustive enumeration of C(97,3) = 147,440 triples yielded 127,598 valid formulations with k=3 the global optimum at composite score 0.562, yet bootstrap 95 % intervals for k=2 through k=5 overlapped [src: cf_formulation_design]. See [[concepts/competitive-exclusion-consortium-design]].

**Both programs depend on classifiers that may not travel.** A pooled ecotype classifier achieved macro one-versus-rest AUC (area under the ROC curve) of 0.80 but only 41 % agreement on UC Davis patients, where `is_ibd=1` was constant and 19/22 patients were predicted E1 [src: discoveries]. Representation choice changed the answer outright: a centered-log-ratio (CLR) plus PCA Gaussian-mixture projection assigned all 26 Kuehl samples to E3 at confidence >0.97 while detecting only 54 % of training species, whereas a linear-discriminant projection across MetaPhlAn3 and Kaiju namespaces gave 27/42/31 % [src: discoveries]. Pooled metabolomics fared worse: 122 m/z-bridge metabolites separated entirely by cohort, first component 79 % of variance, leave-one-study-out adjusted Rand index (ARI, chance-corrected cluster agreement) 0.000 versus 0.113 for taxonomic ecotypes [src: discoveries]. See [[concepts/cross-cohort-microbiome-portability]].

**Therapy must be re-planned as state changes.** Patient 6967 shifted E1→E3 across two visits while *M. gnavus* rose from 0.53 to 7.45 reads, a 14.0× expansion, with *E. lenta* 3.1×, *E. bolteae* 2.1×, *F. plautii* 1.9×, *H. hathewayi* 1.3×, and *E. coli* absent; visit-level cocktail Jaccard similarity was 0.60 [src: ibd_phage_targeting]. The proposed 3–6-month reassessment interval and five-fold *M. gnavus* retesting trigger are explicitly labeled hypotheses requiring prospective validation [src: ibd_phage_targeting].

## Tensions and Caveats

**Which ecotype's targets are real?** The phage framework builds a five-species E1 pathobiont module and a universal Tier-1 trio of *M. gnavus*, *H. hathewayi*, and *E. lenta* [src: ibd_phage_targeting], while independent evidence gating reduced 33 within-ecotype Tier-A candidates to 3 — *M. gnavus*, *F. plautii*, and *Blautia wexleri*, all in E3, with 0/18 E1 candidates passing [src: discoveries]. Whether these two Tier-A lists are drawn from the same candidate pool and differ only in the evidence bar applied is not established by either source; the E1-versus-E3 discrepancy is therefore an open question about candidate scope and scoring, and should not be settled by reconciling or averaging the two lists.

**A valid join is not a demonstrated finding.** This family of disagreements is anchored on [[conflicts/conflict--berdl_data_atlas--cofitness_coinheritance--ecotype_env_reanalysis--ff2b02eb]]: of 257 identified Web of Microbes compounds only 69 (26.8 %) had definitive ModelSEED links, and 107 formula-only matches expanded to 900 candidate molecules, so a cross-collection bridge can execute while its chemical identity and metabolic direction remain unresolved [src: webofmicrobes_explorer].

**Finer classifiers can manufacture structure.** On [[conflicts/conflict--cog_analysis--ecotype_functional_differentiation--enigma_contamination_functional_potential--9514b16e]], the plant-associated marker scheme classified 78.7 % of 1,115 species as dual-nature yet misclassified all four neutral controls in a curated 18-organism panel, and an apparent R² = 0.527 compartment effect fell to R² = 0.072 after removing three genome-rich species per compartment [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]; COG (Clusters of Orthologous Groups) annotations covered approximately 70 % of genes across 32 species [src: cog_analysis]. No project in this corpus evaluates a marker classifier on patient stratification, so this evidence at most suggests the hypothesis that marker-based patient stratification is exposed to the same control-failure mode — a parallel worth testing, not a demonstrated transfer.

**Assay-to-application gaps.** CF inhibition was measured on planktonic PA14, whereas *P. aeruginosa* in CF airways occupies structured biofilms, and engraftability is a prioritization proxy rather than a measurement of post-administration establishment — *M. luteus* had engraftability 0.000 [src: cf_formulation_design]. On the phage side, coverage figures apply to the tested strain panel and lack burst-size, titer, and in-vivo delivery validation [src: ibd_phage_targeting].
