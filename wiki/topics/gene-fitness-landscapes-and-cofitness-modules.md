# Gene Fitness Landscapes and Cofitness Modules

A gene fitness landscape is the map of how much each gene in a genome matters — measured as the growth advantage or disadvantage of disrupting it — across many environments. Cofitness is the correlation between two genes' fitness profiles over those environments, and a cofitness module is a set of genes whose profiles move together. This corpus builds such landscapes at scale from RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing, a pooled method that measures per-gene fitness by tracking barcoded insertion mutants), covering 194,216 protein-coding genes across 43 bacteria in one synthesis [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] and 221,005 genes from 48 bacteria in another [src: essential_genome], with module decompositions spanning 32 organisms [src: fitness_modules]. Eleven member concept pages and this page's four sections organize what those landscapes support, what they do not, and where the projects disagree — a distinction that matters because the same cofitness edge can mean shared regulation or merely shared dispensability [src: amr_cofitness_networks].

## Literature Context

The published work that most directly frames this corpus is single-organism RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing) applied to an ecologically defined condition. In a plant-associated Bacteroidota, RB-TnSeq in *Mucilaginibacter yixingensis* YX-36 resolved pathways for rhizosphere colonization, gliding motility, stress tolerance and carbon metabolism, and found that phylum-specific genes — polysaccharide utilization loci and carbohydrate-active enzymes — are required for fitness in the plant niche ([PMID 42559327](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42559327/)); that study covers one strain, and the abstract reports no cross-organism comparison. In a synthetic mutualism, RB-TnSeq on *Escherichia coli* grown with *Rhodopseudomonas palustris* identified hundreds of genes that raised or lowered fitness in a mutualism-dependent manner, spanning not only nitrogen sensing and assimilation as the coculture design predicted but also energy production and cell wall and membrane biogenesis, and it uncovered unanticipated purine cross-feeding that rescued an *E. coli* purine auxotroph ([PMID 32332139](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32332139/)). Both are single-organism, condition-specific designs reported here from abstracts only.

This corpus is consistent with that literature on its core premise and extends it in scope. Where the published studies each measure one genome, the syntheses here span 194,216 protein-coding genes across 43 bacteria and 221,005 genes from 48 bacteria, with module decompositions across 32 organisms — a scale at which the field's condition-specific fitness observations can be asked whether they generalize. The finding that niche-specific, phylum-restricted loci carry the fitness signal ([PMID 42559327](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42559327/)) sits comfortably beside the corpus's weak conservation–fitness coupling (Spearman rho=0.086) and its 25,271 trade-off genes (17.8% of genes examined), which together argue that core status is a poor proxy for mattering; see [[concepts/condition-dependent-gene-tradeoffs]]. The mutualism study's recovery of fitness determinants across diverse cellular processes, including energy production and cell wall and membrane biogenesis ([PMID 32332139](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32332139/)), is a statement about functional breadth and reports nothing about genomic position, so it is at most analogous to — not independent support for — the corpus's dispersed-support-network claim, the *Acinetobacter baylyi* ADP1 quinate case in which 44/51 genes fall into four subsystems at physically separated loci and only 0.7% of cofit pairs are adjacent within 5 genes ([[concepts/genomic-dispersal-functional-coupling]]). On energy metabolism the corpus's own enrichment test is a null that stays null: enriched in 0/25 organisms, permutation fold 0.91.

Several corpus claims have no counterpart in these candidates and should be read as novel rather than corroborated: cofitness module structure and its threshold sensitivity (94.2% versus 59% enrichment), module-level coinheritance across pangenomes, and the capability-versus-dependence split that classes 267 of 1,695 pathway–organism pairs (15.8%) as latent. Notably, the purine cross-feeding discovered only by coculture ([PMID 32332139](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32332139/)) is the field's own demonstration that encoded or expected dependence is not measured dependence — an unanticipated-interaction result that makes the corpus's latent-pathway class more, not less, plausible, while leaving its calibration sensitivity untouched.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Modules are real structure, but their boundaries are a threshold choice.** Independent component analysis (ICA, a blind source-separation method that decomposes a fitness matrix into co-varying gene sets) applied with an absolute component-weight threshold of |weight| >= 0.3 and a 50-gene cap yielded 1,116 stable modules across 32 organisms; 94.2% showed significantly elevated within-module cofitness by Mann-Whitney U at p < 0.05, with within-module mean |r| of 0.34 versus 0.12 background — a 2.8x enrichment — and 22.7x genomic-adjacency enrichment [src: fitness_modules]. The alternative D'Agostino K-squared membership rule produced 100-280 gene modules with only 59% enrichment and 1-17x correlation enrichment [src: fitness_modules]. Cross-organism alignment found 156 module families spanning at least 2 organisms, 28 spanning 5+, and one spanning 21 of 32 [src: discoveries]. The adjacency enrichment shows operon co-localization contributes, while not reducing cofitness to adjacency [src: fitness_modules]. See [[concepts/fitness-module-detection-sensitivity]] and [[concepts/cofitness-network-architecture]].

**Cofitness reaches well beyond operons and beyond physical clustering.** Among 801 AMR (antimicrobial resistance) genes with fitness data, 769 (96%) had at least one extra-operon cofitness partner at |r| > 0.3; of 180,370 total cofitness partners, 179,375 were extra-operon, with only 0.6% excluded as near-operon pairs [src: amr_cofitness_networks]. Mean support-network size was 233 genes at |r| > 0.3, 110 at |r| > 0.4, and 71 at |r| > 0.5 [src: amr_cofitness_networks]. The clearest mechanistic case is *Acinetobacter baylyi* ADP1 quinate growth: a 51-gene support network in which cofitness assigned 44/51 (86%) to four subsystems — 8 aromatic-pathway, 21 Complex I, 7 iron-acquisition, 2 PQQ-biosynthesis genes — with the Complex I operon at 714–729 kb, the pca/qui pathway at 1,709–1,724 kb and PQQ biosynthesis at 2,461 kb, i.e. physically separated loci serving one objective [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]. Only 0.7% of cofit pairs were genomically adjacent within 5 genes, and excluding adjacent pairs did not change the result pattern [src: cofitness_coinheritance]. See [[concepts/genomic-dispersal-functional-coupling]] and [[concepts/module-level-coinheritance]].

**Fitness importance tracks conservation — as a gradient, and only weakly.** Across 194,216 genes from 43 bacteria, essential genes were 82% core, often-sick 78%, mixed 70%, sometimes-sick 72%, always-neutral 66% and sometimes-beneficial 70% [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]. Fitness breadth correlated with core status at Spearman rho=0.086, p=8.1e-230 — statistically overwhelming, biologically weak [src: fitness_effects_conservation]. A narrower directly linked analysis found essential genes 86.1% core versus 81.2% non-essential, median odds ratio 1.56, with 18 of 33 organisms significant after Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) correction [src: conservation_vs_fitness]. Conservation is not inertness: 24.4% of core genes had positive fitness when deleted versus 19.9% of accessory genes, and core genes were 1.78x more likely to have strong condition-specific phenotypes and 1.29x more likely to be important in some conditions but burdensome in others [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]. Trade-off genes — fitness below -1 in some conditions and above 1 in others — numbered 25,271, or 17.8% of genes examined, and were 1.29x more likely to be core (p=1.2e-44) [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]. See [[concepts/condition-dependent-gene-tradeoffs]].

**Encoded capability and measured dependence are different quantities.** Among 1,695 complete pathway–organism pairs from 48 organisms, thresholds based on pathway fitness t-scores and essential-gene fractions classed 267 (15.8%) as latent (complete but fitness-neutral), 547 (32.3%) intermediate and 881 (51.9%) active; the latent fraction ranged from 4.7% to 21.1% across the tested threshold combinations, and the large intermediate class makes the exact value calibration-sensitive [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Carbon-source pathways were latent far more often than amino-acid biosynthesis: 217 of 892 (24.3%) versus 48 of 735 (6.5%), with pathway category predicting class at χ²=163.6, df=4, p=2.5×10⁻³⁴ [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. An earlier analysis of 161 organism–pathway combinations found all 66 latent pairs became fitness-important under at least one condition type, though its median-based threshold can reclassify by construction [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. In ADP1, 5 principal components captured 82% of variance in a 2,034 gene × 8 carbon-source matrix, PC1 (general sensitivity) 36.7% and PC2 (urea, loading +0.75) 12.7%, with no component above 37% — a multidimensional condition space, not one sensitivity axis [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes]. See [[concepts/condition-space-dimensionality]] and [[concepts/condition-specific-fitness]].

**Shared stress is a large, quantifiable fraction of any "specific" fitness signal.** Across 71 NaCl experiments and 10,821 metal-important gene records spanning 14 metals and 19 organisms, 4,304 records (39.8%) were also NaCl-important, leaving 6,517 (60.2%) metal-specific [src: counter_ion_effects]. A stricter classification of 7,609 metal-important records put 4,177 (54.9%) metal-specific, 2,888 (38.0%) general sick and 544 (7.2%) metal+stress, with the metal-specific fraction ranging from approximately 41% at a 2% non-metal sick-rate threshold to approximately 67% at 10% [src: metal_specificity]. Shared-stress genes were important for a mean of 4.1 metals per gene versus 2.5 for metal-specific genes [src: counter_ion_effects]. See [[concepts/shared-stress-versus-stressor-specific-fitness]] and [[concepts/fitness-matched-null-models]].

## Tensions and Caveats

The corpus's sharpest methodological limit is what a fitness-derived inference licenses. Benchmarked against gene-level KEGG KO (KEGG Orthology identifier, a molecular-function label) identity, Module-ICA achieved <1% strict precision at 23.3% coverage while ortholog transfer achieved 95.8% precision at 91.2% coverage [src: fitness_modules] — the basis of [[conflicts/conflict--cofitness-network-architecture--80bc8754]], on whether cofitness nominates processes or assigns functions.

Whether modules or pairs are the right unit is contested. Delta phi — cofit-pair co-occurrence minus prevalence-matched-random co-occurrence, where phi measures association between two genes' binary presence/absence patterns — averaged +0.011 across 9 organisms, aggregate delta=+0.003 (Mann-Whitney p=1.66e-29), but the across-organism Wilcoxon signed-rank test was non-significant (W=9, p=0.13); 195 ICA modules in 6 organisms gave mean within-module phi=0.229 against a permuted null of 0.177, delta=+0.053, with 51/195 (26%) significant and 21/195 (11%) surviving FDR [src: cofitness_coinheritance] — see [[conflicts/conflict--cofitness-network-architecture--52cc7c49]]. A prevalence ceiling confounds this: most Fitness Browser genes map to core clusters above 95% prevalence where phi approaches zero for both groups, and Korea's negative result had 95.2% NaN phi values [src: cofitness_coinheritance]. Whether conservation tracks fitness tightly at module level or weakly genome-wide is itself disputed in [[conflicts/conflict--fitness-module-detection-sensitivity--485af690]].

Essential genes are absent from insertion-based modules entirely [src: module_conservation], which [[conflicts/conflict--cofitness-network-architecture--7a75ec8a]] frames as a measurement boundary rather than biology. Metal-gene core fractions do not reconcile — 87.4% core versus 76.9% baseline (OR=2.08, p=4.3e-162) in the atlas [src: metal_fitness_atlas] against 71.2% for heavy-metal conditions in a single organism [src: field_vs_lab_fitness] — and must not be averaged: see [[conflicts/conflict--condition-specific-fitness--1a717775]] and [[conflicts/conflict--shared-stress-versus-stressor-specific-fitness--e9b25de2]]. Whether universal positive metal cross-resistance (311/317 positive, 99.1% significant, but permutation p = 0.42 and no non-metal controls [src: metal_cross_resistance]) is one stress program is argued in [[conflicts/conflict--shared-stress-versus-stressor-specific-fitness--09b51730]] and [[conflicts/conflict--cofitness-network-architecture--2d1b0ad4]].

The AMR enrichment null matched conservation class but not mean fitness, so flagellar (OR 4.7–5.3 across 5 organisms) and amino-acid biosynthesis signals cannot yet be separated from shared dispensability; energy metabolism was enriched in 0/25 organisms, permutation fold 0.91 — a null that stays null [src: amr_cofitness_networks]. In ADP1, flux-balance analysis (FBA, a constraint-based model that predicts steady-state metabolic fluxes) predicted 1.76× higher Complex I flux on aromatics (0.55 versus 0.31) yet 0% essentiality, with 30/51 quinate-specific genes having no FBA reaction mapping at all [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]; the compensation explanation is contested in [[conflicts/conflict--cofitness-network-architecture--05940aec]].
