# Gene Fitness Landscapes and Cofitness Modules

A gene fitness landscape is the matrix of growth effects obtained when every gene in a genome is disrupted and the resulting mutant pool is grown across many conditions — most of this corpus's raw signal comes from RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing, a pooled assay that scores each insertion mutant's contribution to growth) collected in the Fitness Browser. Two derived objects dominate what the corpus can say. The first is *cofitness*: the correlation between two genes' fitness profiles across conditions, which flags a shared phenotypic response — not, on its own, shared transcriptional control [src: amr_cofitness_networks]. The second is the *fitness module*: a multi-gene unit recovered by independent component analysis (ICA, a decomposition of correlated measurements into additive components). Across roughly two dozen projects, the corpus asks what these objects measure, how much of their structure is an artifact of thresholds, whether they persist across species and pangenomes, and what they license about individual gene function. The honest headline is that modules are real and reproducible as *process-level* units, while nearly every stronger reading of them is contested.

## Literature Context

Transposon-insertion sequencing is by now a standard route to genome-wide fitness, and the published landscapes it has produced are almost all *single-organism, single-context* catalogues of required genes: 126 and 116 genes required by serotype M1 and M28 group A streptococcus during necrotizing myositis in nonhuman primates, with more than 25% of them encoding known or putative transporters ([PMID 30667377](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30667377/)); 624 genes required for optimal growth or in-host fitness in *Enterobacter cloacae* ATCC 13047 against a *Galleria mellonella* model, plus 63 genes where mutation was positively selected ([PMID 32754144](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32754144/)); 724 genes required in LB and 851 in SPI-2-inducing minimal medium for *Salmonella* Typhimurium ST313 D23580, with 68 further genes needed for intra-macrophage replication but not in vitro ([PMID 31560731](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31560731/)); and 1,465 predicted essential genes in *Cryptococcus neoformans*, together with a fluconazole fitness screen ([PMID 40402997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40402997/)). The published frame is thus a gene list per condition. This corpus's central move — treating the fitness matrix itself as the object, and asking what its covariance structure supports — is a genuine extension rather than a replication of that frame, and its 1,116 modules across 32 organisms have no direct analogue in these papers.

Several corpus cautions are strongly consistent with published methodology. The finding in *Vibrio cholerae* that the nucleoid protein H-NS systematically depresses transposon insertion into AT-rich loci, so that low insertion frequency need not indicate fitness importance ([PMID 27578758](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27578758/)), is independent support for this corpus's worry that near-neutral singleton and novel genes may reflect poor transposon coverage rather than true neutrality, and for the general position argued in [[concepts/fitness-matched-null-models]] that insertion-derived signal needs a matched null before it is read as biology. Likewise, the condition-dependence visible when *Salmonella* genes are required in macrophages but not in vitro ([PMID 31560731](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31560731/)), and the 5'-insertion tuning that made mitochondrial genes drive fluconazole resistance ([PMID 40402997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40402997/)), agree qualitatively with the sign-flipping and multi-axis picture in [[concepts/condition-dependent-gene-tradeoffs]] and [[concepts/condition-space-dimensionality]].

Where the literature does approach gene–gene structure, it does so mechanistically rather than statistically. Dual Tn-seq samples 73% of 1.3 million possible double deletions in *Streptococcus pneumoniae* and recovers genetic interactions spanning many biochemical processes, including a new cytidine triphosphate synthase in a well-studied pathway ([PMID 40997176](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40997176/)). That is the sharpest external comparator: it measures epistasis directly, whereas cofitness infers coupling from correlated single-mutant profiles. The corpus's negative result — Module-ICA and cofitness voting both below 1% strict precision for KEGG-level gene function, against 95.8% for ortholog transfer — is novel and cuts against any assumption that correlation-derived modules substitute for interaction mapping; dual Tn-seq is the obvious resolving assay. RB-TnSeq's reach into ecological settings is corroborated by the finding that most biosynthetic gene clusters in *Pseudomonas viridiflava* *reduce* fitness during *Arabidopsis* infection, with cost varying by host genotype ([PMID 42294911](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42294911/)) — a published parallel to this corpus's conserved-but-burdensome genes, and a reminder that laboratory dispensability and field relevance diverge, as the weak *Desulfovibrio vulgaris* Hildenborough field-versus-lab discrimination already indicates.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Fitness covariance resolves into modules, but the modules are threshold-constructed.** The core module collection comprises 1,116 stable modules across 32 organisms, each organism represented by at least 100 experiments, with a median size range of 7–50 genes [src: fitness_modules]. Of these, 94.2% show significantly elevated within-module cofitness (Mann–Whitney U, p < 0.05); within-module mean |r| is 0.34 against a background of 0.12, a 2.8× enrichment, and module genes show 22.7× genomic-adjacency enrichment [src: fitness_modules]. That result is not threshold-free: an initial D'Agostino K-squared membership rule produced modules of 100–280 genes with 59% enrichment and 1–17× correlation enrichment, while an absolute rule of |weight| ≥ 0.3 with a 50-gene cap produced 94% enrichment and 2.8× correlation enrichment [src: fitness_modules]. Depth constrains it too: a 40% component cap was required to avoid FastICA convergence failures, and Caulo, despite 198 experiments, showed only 2.9× correlation enrichment [src: fitness_modules]. [[concepts/fitness-module-detection-sensitivity]] treats this dependence in full.

**Modules give process context; they do not give gene-level function.** In a held-out benchmark withholding 20% of KEGG-annotated genes, ortholog transfer achieved 95.8% strict precision, 91.2% coverage and 0.934 F1, and domain-based prediction 29.1%/66.6%/0.401, whereas Module-ICA achieved <1% strict precision with 23.3% coverage and cofitness voting <1% precision with 73.0% coverage [src: fitness_modules]. The mismatch is structural — KEGG KO groups average about 1.2 genes per KO, while a module with 20 annotated members typically contains 20 different KOs [src: fitness_modules]. Annotation settings move the apparent yield independently of the data: lowering the enrichment-overlap threshold from 3 to 2 and adding PFam domains raised module annotation from 8% to 80%, from 92 to 890 annotated modules, unlocking 7.6× more function predictions [src: fitness_modules]. The same caution applies to unknown genes: of 57,011 dark genes, 33,105 (83.7%) were reclassified by Bakta and 6,427 remained hypothetical in both pipelines, and module or neighborhood membership prioritizes them without assigning function [src: truly_dark_genes, functional_dark_matter].

**Module architecture sits in the conserved genome and travels through pangenomes better than pairwise links do.** Module genes are 86.0% core versus 81.5% for all genes (OR = 1.46, p = 1.6e-87); among 974 modules with ≥3 mapped genes, 577 (59%) exceed 90% core, 349 (36%) are 50–90%, 48 (5%) are <50%, and the median module is 93.4% core [src: module_conservation]. Breadth does not buy conservation — family breadth and core fraction are uncorrelated (Spearman rho = −0.01, p = 0.914) — even though 156 families span ≥2 organisms, 28 span 5+, and one spans 21 of 32 [src: module_conservation, fitness_modules]. Co-inheritance sharpens the level argument: pairwise cofit pairs exceed prevalence-matched random pairs by only delta phi +0.003 (Mann–Whitney p = 1.66e-29) with a non-significant across-organism Wilcoxon test (W = 9, p = 0.13), while across 195 ICA modules in 6 organisms within-module co-occurrence exceeds the null by delta phi = +0.053, with 51/195 (26%) significant at p < 0.05 and 21/195 (11%) at FDR q < 0.05 (FDR = false discovery rate, the expected share of false positives among calls) [src: cofitness_coinheritance]. Accessory modules carry the strongest signal (+0.108) against core (+0.059) and mixed (+0.031), with the accessory-versus-core difference only trending (p = 0.051) [src: cofitness_coinheritance]. See [[concepts/module-level-coinheritance]].

**Support networks are dispersed and organism-shaped rather than operon-shaped.** In *Acinetobacter baylyi* ADP1, a 51-gene quinate support network assigns 44/51 genes (86%) to four subsystems — 8 aromatic-pathway, 21 Complex I, 7 iron-acquisition and 2 PQQ-biosynthesis genes — occupying separate chromosomal regions (Complex I at 714–729 kb, pca/qui at 1,709–1,724 kb, PQQ at 2,461 kb), with a nearest-neighbour distance ratio of 0.89 observed/expected [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]. Cofitness is likewise not reducible to adjacency: only 0.7% of cofit pairs are genomically adjacent within 5 genes, and excluding them does not change the result pattern [src: cofitness_coinheritance]. In the AMR setting, 769/801 AMR genes (96%) have an extra-operon cofitness partner at |r| > 0.3, mean support-network size falls from 233 genes at |r| > 0.3 to 110 at 0.4 and 71 at 0.5, and organism context structures networks more than mechanism does (within-organism Jaccard 0.375 versus 0.207 for the same mechanism across organisms, p = 4.3×10⁻¹³) [src: amr_cofitness_networks]. [[concepts/genomic-dispersal-functional-coupling]] and [[concepts/cofitness-network-architecture]] develop both strands.

**Correlated fitness needs a fitness-matched null before it is read as shared biology.** Enrichment around AMR neighbourhoods includes flagellum-dependent motility (5 organisms, mean OR 4.7), flagellum assembly (5, OR 5.3), histidine biosynthesis (3, OR 5.3) and tryptophan biosynthesis (3, OR 5.3), while energy metabolism is enriched in 0/25 organisms (permutation fold 0.91) — a pattern equally consistent with shared laboratory dispensability [src: amr_cofitness_networks]. The corpus's permutations matched conservation class but not mean fitness, which is the unresolved confound [src: amr_cofitness_networks]. A parallel decomposition problem appears in stress assays: of 10,821 metal-important gene records, 4,304 (39.8%) were also NaCl-important, leaving 6,517 (60.2%) metal-specific [src: counter_ion_effects], while a stricter non-metal control classified 4,177/7,609 (54.9%) as metal-specific, 2,888 (38.0%) as general sick and 544 (7.2%) as metal+stress [src: metal_specificity]. See [[concepts/fitness-matched-null-models]] and [[concepts/shared-stress-versus-stressor-specific-fitness]].

**Landscapes are multidimensional, and conserved genes are the most condition-active ones.** Across 194,216 protein-coding genes from 43 bacteria, essential genes are 82% core versus 66% for always-neutral genes, yet fitness breadth correlates with core status only weakly (Spearman rho = 0.086, p = 8.1e-230) [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis, fitness_effects_conservation]. Conservation does not mean inertness: 25,271 genes (17.8%) are true trade-off genes, 1.29× more likely to be core (p = 1.2e-44) [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]; 24.4% of core genes are burdensome when deleted versus 19.9% of accessory genes, with category-specific reversals (+12.9 pp for RNA metabolism, +7.8 pp motility, +6.2 pp protein metabolism, but −14.1 pp for cell wall) [src: core_gene_tradeoffs, conservation_fitness_synthesis]. Dimensionality is explicit in ADP1: 5 principal components capture 82% of variance in a 2,034 × 8 matrix, PC1 (general sensitivity) 36.7% and PC2 (urea, loading +0.75) 12.7%, with median pairwise condition correlation r = 0.25 and 625 genes (31%) concentrated on one carbon source [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes]. See [[concepts/condition-space-dimensionality]] and [[concepts/condition-dependent-gene-tradeoffs]].

## Tensions and Caveats

The sharpest internal disagreement is about how much module-level core enrichment licenses. Module genes are clearly more core than background (86.0% versus 81.5%) [src: module_conservation, conservation_fitness_synthesis], yet the genome-wide fitness–conservation association is weak and partly a detection limit [src: fitness_effects_conservation]; the two analyses do not cover the same gene set — see [[conflicts/conflict--conservation_fitness_synthesis--fitness_effects_conservation--module_conservation]].

Whether cofitness reflects co-regulation or shared dispensability remains open across eleven linked disputes catalogued in [[conflicts/conflict--amr_cofitness_networks--amr_strain_variation--aromatic_catabolism_network--d0b6ec8b]]; Pearson correlation removes each gene's mean fitness, but genes with similar condition-responsive dispensability still correlate without sharing regulation [src: amr_cofitness_networks].

Two blind spots are structural. Essential genes have no viable insertion mutants and are therefore absent from these modules, so module results are not representative of the genome-wide fitness spectrum [src: module_conservation]; and singleton or novel genes look near-neutral in laboratory assays, which may reflect poor transposon coverage rather than true neutrality [src: fitness_effects_conservation]. Pangenome inference has its own ceiling: most Fitness Browser genes map to clusters above 95% prevalence, where phi approaches zero for cofit and random pairs alike, and for Korea 95.2% of cofit pairs had NaN phi values [src: cofitness_coinheritance]. Coverage is incomplete — 29/32 organisms had usable pangenomes, since Cola, Kang and SB2B lacked sufficient GTDB genomes [src: module_conservation].

Mechanistic extrapolation across species is contested. NDH-2 compensation was proposed to explain variable Complex I dependence [src: aromatic_catabolism_network], but the cross-species test found the opposite ordering: among organisms retained after filtering, validated NDH-2 organisms had a *larger* mean Complex I aromatic deficit (−0.297 versus −0.156), with p = 0.52, and the comparison included only 4 organisms lacking NDH-2, so it may be underpowered [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] — see [[conflicts/conflict--aromatic_catabolism_network--respiratory_chain_wiring]] and [[conflicts/conflict--aromatic_catabolism_network--metabolic_capability_dependency--respiratory_chain_wiring]]. Similarly, flux-balance analysis captured 1.76× higher Complex I flux on aromatics (0.55 versus 0.31) yet predicted 0% essentiality, while 10/13 Complex I operon subunits produced quinate-specific defects [src: aromatic_catabolism_network].

Finally, what "conserved" and "essential" even mean differs by assay: essential genes were 86.1% core versus 81.2% non-essential with a median odds ratio of 1.56 in one 33-organism cohort [src: conservation_vs_fitness], a non-interchangeable framing relative to the 82%-versus-66% synthesis [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] — see [[conflicts/conflict--adp1_triple_essentiality--conservation_fitness_synthesis--conservation_vs_fitness--3d504d86]] and [[conflicts/conflict--conservation_fitness_synthesis--conservation_vs_fitness--core_gene_tradeoffs--57107100]]. Ecological condition labels discriminate weakly: in *Desulfovibrio vulgaris* Hildenborough, field fitness alone gave cross-validated AUC 0.517, lab fitness 0.531, combined 0.548, while adding gene length raised AUC to 0.645 [src: field_vs_lab_fitness].
