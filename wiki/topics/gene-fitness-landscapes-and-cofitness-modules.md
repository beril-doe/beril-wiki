# Gene Fitness Landscapes and Cofitness Modules

A gene fitness landscape is the matrix of growth effects produced by disrupting each gene in a genome across many experimental conditions — typically measured by RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing, a pooled mutant assay that tracks strain abundance over growth). Cofitness is the correlation between two genes' rows in that matrix, and modules are the gene sets that move together. This corpus interrogates fitness landscapes at three scales at once: genome-wide across 194,216 protein-coding genes from 43 bacteria [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis], module-wide across 1,116 stable modules in 32 organisms [src: fitness_modules], and organism-deep in *Acinetobacter baylyi* ADP1, where a 2,034-gene × 8-carbon-source growth matrix resolves phenotype dimensionality directly [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes]. The value of reading these together is that they disagree productively: the same fitness covariance that looks like co-regulation at module scale looks like shared dispensability at gene scale, and the corpus has not settled which. The four sub-themes below trace that argument, and the tensions section then turns to the conflicts it leaves unresolved.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Fitness matrices carry real, threshold-sensitive module structure.** Independent component analysis (ICA, a blind source-separation method that decomposes a fitness matrix into co-varying gene sets) applied to 32 organisms with at least 100 experiments each recovered 1,116 stable modules, of which 94.2% showed significantly elevated within-module cofitness by Mann–Whitney U at p < 0.05; within-module mean |r| was 0.34 against a background of 0.12, a 2.8x enrichment, with 22.7x genomic-adjacency enrichment [src: fitness_modules]. That structure is not free of analyst choices. An initial D'Agostino K-squared membership rule produced modules of 100–280 genes with 59% enrichment and 1–17x correlation enrichment; switching to an absolute component-weight threshold of |weight| >= 0.3 with a maximum of 50 genes per module produced 94% enrichment and 2.8x correlation enrichment [src: fitness_modules]. Depth does not guarantee quality either: Caulo, with 198 experiments, showed only 2.9x correlation enrichment [src: fitness_modules]. Modules also recur across taxa — 156 families span at least 2 organisms, 28 span 5 or more, and one spans 21 of 32 [src: discoveries].

**Modules are the better unit for predicting what travels together through genomes.** Pairwise cofitness predicts gene co-occurrence weakly: across 9 organisms, 2,253,491 cofit pairs against 22,534,910 prevalence-matched random pairs gave mean delta phi of +0.011, with 7 of 9 organisms positive and 8 of 9 significant at p<0.05, but the across-organism Wilcoxon signed-rank test was not significant (W=9, p=0.13) [src: cofitness_coinheritance]. Organism effects ranged from +0.093 (Ddia6719) to -0.042 (Korea), the latter hard to read because 95.2% of its cofit pairs had NaN phi values [src: cofitness_coinheritance]. At module level the signal is several times larger: across 195 ICA modules in 6 organisms, within-module co-occurrence had mean phi=0.229 versus a prevalence-matched null of 0.177, delta=+0.053, with 51/195 (26%) significant at p<0.05 and 21/195 (11%) surviving Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR, the expected proportion of false positives among calls) correction at q<0.05 [src: cofitness_coinheritance]. A prevalence ceiling constrains the pairwise test — most Fitness Browser genes map to core clusters above 95% prevalence, where phi approaches 0 for both cofit and random pairs, and cofitness strength was weakly *anti*-correlated with co-occurrence across 1.04M pairs (Spearman rho=-0.109, p<1e-300) [src: cofitness_coinheritance]. Operon proximity does not explain the weakness: only 0.7% of cofit pairs were adjacent within 5 genes, and excluding them changed nothing [src: cofitness_coinheritance].

**Fitness importance tracks conservation, but only as a gradient.** Across 194,216 genes from 43 bacteria, essential genes were 82% core, often-sick 78%, mixed 70%, sometimes-sick 72%, always-neutral 66%, and sometimes-beneficial 70% [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]. Breadth of fitness effect is a weak predictor despite overwhelming significance (Spearman rho=0.086, p=8.1e-230) [src: fitness_effects_conservation]. Module genes were 86.0% core versus 81.5% for all genes (OR=1.46, p=1.6e-87) [src: module_conservation], and a linked analysis of 148,826 genes from 33 organisms found essential genes 86.1% core versus 81.2% non-essential, median odds ratio 1.56, with 18 of 33 organisms significant after Benjamini-Hochberg correction [src: conservation_vs_fitness]. Conservation also does not mean inertness: core genes were 1.78x more likely to have strong condition-specific phenotypes, 24.4% had positive fitness when deleted versus 19.9% of accessory genes, and 25,271 genes (17.8% of those examined) were true trade-off genes with fitness below -1 in some conditions and above 1 in others [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis, core_gene_tradeoffs].

**Condition space is multidimensional, and encoded capability is not measured dependency.** In the ADP1 deletion collection, principal component analysis (PCA, summarizing correlated variation into orthogonal components) needed 5 components for 82% of variance; PC1 explained 36.7%, general growth sensitivity with all conditions loading positively, and PC2 12.7%, isolating urea at loading +0.75, while the median correlation across the 28 condition pairs was only r = 0.25 [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes]. Among 1,695 complete pathway–organism pairs from 48 organisms, 267 (15.8%) were latent capabilities, 547 (32.3%) intermediate and 881 (51.9%) active, and carbon-source pathways were latent more often than amino-acid biosynthesis pathways, 24.3% versus 6.5% [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. That 15.8% is calibration-sensitive: the latent fraction ranged from 4.7% to 21.1% across tested threshold combinations [src: metabolic_capability_dependency], and a median-based condition-specific threshold can reclassify by construction [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. Dispersed genes still form one system ([[concepts/genomic-dispersal-functional-coupling]]): quinate growth in ADP1 needs a 51-gene support network where cofitness assigned 44/51 (86%) to four subsystems — 8 aromatic-pathway, 21 Complex I (NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreductase), 7 iron-acquisition and 2 PQQ (pyrroloquinoline quinone, a redox cofactor) biosynthesis genes — in distinct chromosomal regions, not one shared neighborhood [src: aromatic_catabolism_network].

## Tensions and Caveats

The load-bearing caveat is what a cofitness edge licenses. A cofitness edge is evidence of shared phenotypic response, not of transcriptional control: 769/801 AMR genes (96%) had an extra-operon cofitness partner at |r| > 0.3, and the observed enrichments — flagellum-dependent motility in 5 organisms at mean OR 4.7, histidine biosynthesis in 3 at mean OR 5.3 — are equally consistent with shared dispensability in shaken laboratory culture, because the permutation matched conservation class but not mean fitness [src: amr_cofitness_networks]. [[conflicts/conflict--cofitness-network-architecture--80bc8754]] sharpens this into a claim-type dispute: benchmarked on whether they can assign a gene its strict KEGG KO (KEGG Orthology identifier, a molecular-function label), the fitness-derived predictors Module-ICA and cofitness voting scored <1% precision against ortholog transfer's 95.8% [src: fitness_modules], so fitness methods nominate processes rather than gene-level functions. [[conflicts/conflict--cofitness-network-architecture--52cc7c49]] contests whether modules generalize better than pairs or whether the module effect reflects small unrepresentative subsets, and [[conflicts/conflict--cofitness-network-architecture--f152c7da]] asks whether cross-genome similarity is coupling or shared ancestry — cofit-pair phi was higher among near genomes than medium-distance genomes (0.102 versus 0.067), with most species lacking far-stratum genomes [src: cofitness_coinheritance]. Organism depth does not dissolve the problem: in [[concepts/outer-membrane-lipid-homeostasis]], RB-TnSeq fitness is one input among transcriptomics, published regulons and a single-replicate outer-membrane proteome that limits mechanistic inference [src: caulobacter_fur_lipida_loss].

Module membership has a boundary: [[conflicts/conflict--cofitness-network-architecture--7a75ec8a]] records that essential genes are absent from insertion-based modules [src: module_conservation], leaving open whether that is biology or a measurement limit; [[conflicts/conflict--fitness-module-detection-sensitivity--485af690]] pits module-level core enrichment against that weak genome-wide association. [[conflicts/conflict--condition-specific-fitness--1a717775]] holds four irreconcilable core fractions (87.4% core versus 76.9% baseline in the atlas [src: metal_fitness_atlas]; 71.2% and not significant after correction for heavy-metal conditions — a null that stays null [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]), which must not be averaged: definitions, thresholds and organism sets differ. [[conflicts/conflict--shared-stress-versus-stressor-specific-fitness--e9b25de2]] repeats it per metal: manganese, zinc and iron core-conservation deltas that disagree between the counter-ion analysis and the atlas. [[conflicts/conflict--shared-stress-versus-stressor-specific-fitness--09b51730]] instead asks whether universal positive metal cross-resistance is one shared-stress program or needs per-stressor decomposition, as [[concepts/shared-stress-versus-stressor-specific-fitness]] requires: 4,304/10,821 metal-important records (39.8%) were also NaCl-important [src: counter_ion_effects], while the cross-resistance study's metal-label permutation was non-significant (p = 0.42) with no non-metal controls [src: metal_cross_resistance] — the control missing in [[conflicts/conflict--cofitness-network-architecture--2d1b0ad4]]. Finally, [[conflicts/conflict--cofitness-network-architecture--05940aec]] and [[conflicts/conflict--condition-space-dimensionality--aeb98adb]] record flux balance analysis (FBA, a constraint-based model predicting steady-state fluxes) predicting 0% Complex I essentiality despite 10/13 operon subunits producing quinate-specific defects, and 30/51 quinate-specific genes lacking an FBA reaction mapping [src: aromatic_catabolism_network].
