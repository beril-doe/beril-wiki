# Gene Fitness-Conservation Dynamics

Gene fitness-conservation dynamics describes how experimentally measured effects of disrupting a gene relate to its retention across bacterial genomes. The corpus speaks directly to this topic by combining random-barcode transposon sequencing (RB-TnSeq), complete-gene knockouts, pangenome core/accessory classifications, functional annotations, and environmental comparisons. Its central message is not that conservation predicts a single, universal fitness state, but that genome retention, laboratory burden, condition-specific importance, and natural selection form related yet distinct axes.

## Literature Context

Bosch et al. used genome-wide expression tuning in *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* to show that gene importance is not adequately represented by a binary essential/non-essential classification. By progressively reducing expression, they identified a spectrum of dosage-dependent vulnerabilities: some genes produced strong growth defects only after substantial depletion, whereas others were sensitive to relatively modest expression changes. [PMID 34297925](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34297925/) Their results also showed that vulnerability is distributed across diverse cellular processes rather than restricted to a small set of conventionally essential functions. [PMID 34297925](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34297925/) This work therefore frames gene fitness as a quantitative phenotype shaped by perturbation strength, pathway context, and the biological state of the cell. [PMID 34297925](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34297925/)

The present corpus is consistent with that literature in treating fitness as graded, context-dependent, and broader than a single essentiality call. Its discordance between RB-TnSeq and complete-knockout classifications, together with the stronger predictive performance of continuous fitness values, directly reinforces the dosage-and-threshold perspective developed by Bosch et al. [PMID 34297925](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34297925/) The corpus extends that perspective beyond one organism and one perturbation framework by comparing measured deletion effects across dozens of bacteria with pangenome conservation. Its findings that core genes show stronger condition-specific phenotypes, that thousands of genes switch from beneficial to deleterious effects across conditions, and that some genes are neutral overall but critical in a particular environment are compatible with the literature’s rejection of universal gene labels. These results further connect quantitative vulnerability to evolutionary retention through [[concepts/condition-dependent-gene-tradeoffs]] and [[concepts/comparative-conservation-metric-calibration]].

Several corpus conclusions go beyond what the candidate paper directly establishes. The observation that conserved core genes can impose positive laboratory deletion fitness, and that costly-conserved and costly-dispensable genes differ in mobile-element-associated annotation, introduces an evolutionary comparison between laboratory burden and genome retention that expression tuning in *M. tuberculosis* does not test. Likewise, the weak conservation–fitness association and the near-null average difference for the AMR subset caution against treating experimentally measured vulnerability as a universal predictor of conservation. These are not direct contradictions of Bosch et al.; they reflect different biological questions and perturbation scales. The novel synthesis here is that gene importance occupies multiple axes—expression dosage, condition-specific fitness, pangenome retention, and ecological history—and that agreement on one axis need not imply agreement on the others. This motivates [[concepts/laboratory-fitness-versus-natural-selection]] and [[concepts/essentiality-assay-discordance]] as necessary interpretive safeguards.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Conservation provides a real but weak prioritization signal.** Across 194,216 protein-coding genes from 43 bacteria, essential genes were 82% core, whereas genes that were always neutral in tested experiments were 66% core. Fitness breadth was positively associated with core status, but only weakly: Spearman rho=0.086, p=8.1e-230. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis, fitness_effects_conservation] A separate integration across 33 organisms found essential genes were 86.1% core versus 81.2% of non-essential genes, with a median odds ratio of 1.56; 18 of 33 organisms showed significant enrichment after Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate correction. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] These results support conservation as a useful ranking feature, but not as a standalone definition of biological importance. The distinction is developed in [[concepts/comparative-conservation-metric-calibration]] and [[concepts/laboratory-fitness-versus-natural-selection]].

The metric itself changes what “conserved” means. In one dark-gene analysis, 99.9% of 30,756 clusters were assigned to universal breadth using an eggNOG-based classification, making that category poorly discriminative. Replacing it with species-count scoring produced Spearman ρ = 0.982 with the original ranking, but top-50 and top-100 overlaps were only 62% and 58%. [src: functional_dark_matter] A broader GTDB r214 analysis expanded conservation coverage from 32,791 (57.5%) to 37,997 (66.6%) dark genes and resolved taxonomic categories from kingdom-level to species-level and mobile groups. [src: functional_dark_matter] Thus, conservation-based prioritization should specify its reference database, taxonomic breadth, and decision objective.

**Core genes are active, condition-dependent, and sometimes burdensome.** The corpus identifies a core-genome burden paradox: genes retained broadly can improve laboratory growth when deleted. Across the 43-bacterium synthesis, 24.4% of core genes had positive deletion fitness compared with 19.9% of accessory genes. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] Here, positive deletion fitness means that the mutant grew better than the reference strain under the assay conditions. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] Core genes also had heavier fitness-effect tails in both negative and positive directions, rather than being uniformly beneficial or costly to retain. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

This pattern is strongly condition-dependent. The corpus identified 25,271 true trade-off genes, defined as genes with fitness below -1 in some conditions and above 1 in others; they represented 17.8% of genes examined and were 1.29 times more likely to be core than non-core genes, with odds ratio 1.29 and p=1.2e-44. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] Strong condition-specific phenotypes were found in 77.3% of core genes versus 70.3% of genes without such annotations, with OR=1.78 and p=1.8e-97. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] Another analysis identified 4,450 genes (2.7%) that were neutral overall but critical in one condition; these “ephemeral-niche” genes were 3.0% of core genes, 1.7% of auxiliary genes, and 1.6% of singleton genes. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

The burden is also function-specific rather than a universal property of core status. Core genes were more burdensome in Protein Metabolism, Motility, and RNA Metabolism, with core-minus-non-core differences of +6.2, +7.8, and +12.9 percentage points, respectively. Cell Wall genes showed the reverse pattern, with a difference of -14.1 percentage points. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] [[concepts/condition-dependent-gene-tradeoffs]] and [[concepts/core-genome-burden-paradox]] explain why conservation should be interpreted alongside the condition and functional category of each fitness measurement.

**Laboratory fitness is not natural selection.** RB-TnSeq measures the growth effect of perturbation under particular library-construction and laboratory conditions, while pangenome conservation accumulates evolutionary retention across environments and histories. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] The 28,017 genes classified as both costly in the laboratory and conserved are therefore candidates for retention despite laboratory burden, but they are evidence for possible purifying selection rather than direct measurements of selection in nature. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis, core_gene_tradeoffs] Conversely, 5,526 genes were costly in the laboratory but dispensable in the pangenome; these are candidates for ongoing loss, recent acquisition, or unstable accessory material, not proof that every such gene is being eliminated. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis, costly_dispensable_genes]

The costly-dispensable class provides a mechanistic contrast. Such genes were 7.45 times more likely than costly-conserved genes to contain mobile-element keywords, with OR=7.45 and p=4.6e-71. The SEED category “Phages, Prophages, Transposable elements, Plasmids” was enriched 11.7-fold, with FDR=1.3e-17. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] They also had lower annotation rates: SEED annotations occurred in 50.8% versus 74.9% of costly-conserved genes, and KEGG annotations in 20.0% versus 42.7%. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] These observations support a model in which some burdensome accessory genes are mobile or recently acquired, while emphasizing that annotation keywords do not directly measure mobility or natural selection. See [[concepts/costly-dispensable-gene-loss]] and [[concepts/laboratory-fitness-versus-natural-selection]].

**Fitness architecture is organized, but measurement coverage shapes the signal.** Independent component analysis (ICA), a decomposition method for detecting coordinated fitness profiles, identified 1,116 modules across 32 organisms. Module genes were 86.0% core versus 81.5% for all genes, with OR=1.46 and p=1.6e-87; 59% of modules were more than 90% core. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis, module_conservation] This supports coordinated conservation, but not the claim that every module is uniformly essential or functionally characterized. Module discovery used thresholds such as absolute component weight |weight| >= 0.3 and a maximum of 50 genes; changing the membership rule altered module size and enrichment. [src: fitness_modules]

Fitness-matched null models are consequently important. Cofitness, the similarity of fitness profiles across conditions, can reflect shared regulation, shared dispensability, or common stress responses. A conservation-matched null does not reproduce mean fitness, so it may leave these confounders intact. [src: amr_cofitness_networks] The broader fitness analysis reinforces this concern: core genes were more likely to show strong condition-specific phenotypes, while singleton genes appeared near-neutral in tested conditions but may have suffered poor transposon coverage. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] [[concepts/fitness-matched-null-models]] and [[concepts/fitness-module-detection-sensitivity]] provide the methodological route for distinguishing coordinated biology from observation structure.

## Tensions and Caveats

The principal disagreement is captured by [[conflicts/conflict--acinetobacter_adp1_explorer--adp1_triple_essentiality--alphafold_msa_annotation--8af84dcc]]. Genome-wide analyses support a conservation–essentiality gradient, but the AMR-specific comparison found virtually identical baseline mean fitness for core and accessory AMR genes: −0.024 for both, with Cohen’s d = 0.002 and p = 0.33. [src: conservation_vs_fitness, amr_fitness_cost] This is not necessarily a contradiction: the analyses use different gene sets, fitness summaries, and biological contexts. Conservation can track broad importance without predicting a particular subset’s average deletion cost.

Assay definitions also disagree. In ADP1, only 18 of 1,933 genes (0.9%) were called essential by both RB-TnSeq and complete-knockout assays; 211 (10.9%) were knockout-essential but RB-TnSeq-dispensable, and 293 (15.2%) showed the reverse pattern. At the 0.05 RB-TnSeq threshold, Cohen’s kappa was -0.081, while continuous inverted fitness achieved AUC values of 0.700 in rich medium and 0.725 in minimal medium. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] As summarized in [[conflicts/conflict--adp1_triple_essentiality--conservation_fitness_synthesis--conservation_vs_fitness--3d504d86]], continuous fitness is more informative than a binary call, but neither assay establishes natural-environment essentiality.

Coverage is a load-bearing limitation. The ADP1 deletion collection excluded 499 essential genes and 316 genes with incomplete data. Among TnSeq-dispensable genes, 10.5% lacked deletion-collection growth data; missing genes were less likely to be core and had lower annotation rates. [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes] Transposon essentiality can also be inflated by non-callability from small genes, low-complexity sequence, or scaffold edges, while stress-specific requirements can be missed in rich-medium assays. [src: essential_genome, conservation_vs_fitness] These limitations motivate [[concepts/genetic-perturbation-coverage-bias]] and [[concepts/transposon-callability-bias]].

Finally, unequal condition coverage can make well-profiled organisms appear biologically richer. The Fitness Browser included 48 organisms, with MR-1 represented by 121 historical conditions; organisms with deeper panels have more opportunities to reveal condition-specific effects. [src: functional_dark_matter] Field-versus-lab labels did not solve this problem: in *Desulfovibrio vulgaris*, field fitness alone had cross-validated AUC 0.517, laboratory fitness 0.531, and combined fitness 0.548, while adding gene length increased AUC to 0.645. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness] Rankings should therefore model condition depth, gene length, callability, pangenome sampling, and functional category rather than treating conservation or ecological labels as universal corrections.
