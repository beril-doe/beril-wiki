# Metabolic Models and Pathway-Level Evidence

Genome-scale metabolic models and pathway-completeness predictors are the corpus's main route from a genome sequence to a statement about what an organism can do. Two tool families dominate: constraint-based models simulated with flux balance analysis (FBA — prediction of feasible steady-state reaction rates from a stoichiometric reconstruction, usually under a growth objective), and GapMind, a homology-based tool that scores whether a genome encodes a complete amino-acid biosynthesis or carbon-utilization pathway. Both are cheap, genome-wide, and available across thousands of organisms in the KBase Data Lakehouse; both make claims about *capability* that the corpus's experimental layers — RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing, a pooled mutant assay scoring each gene's contribution to growth), clean deletion collections, proteomics, exometabolomics, curated growth phenotypes — test only partially. What this corpus contributes is not a verdict on whether models "work," but a quantified map of exactly where the inference from encoded reaction to measured requirement breaks, and what each break looks like.

## Literature Context

Published genome-scale metabolic model (GEM) work is dominated by intensively curated single-organism reconstructions that report high accuracy. EcoCyc-18.0-GEM reaches 95.2% accuracy on knockout essentiality for its 1,445 genes and 80.7% on nutrient utilization across 431 media, while still cataloguing 70 incorrect essentiality calls on glucose and 83 incorrect nutrient-utilization calls ([PMID 24974895](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24974895/)); a curated *Bordetella pertussis* model predicts essentiality at 83% against a TraDIS transposon screen ([PMID 28715411](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28715411/)); iCH492 for *Streptococcus thermophilus* reports 95.83% predictive accuracy with 100% specificity ([PMID 37814322](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37814322/)); and iSep23 for *Staphylococcus epidermidis* reports growth and carbon-source predictions aligning with experiment ([PMID 40396730](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40396730/)). The literature also contains the counterweight: when semi-curated, automatically generated GEMs are evaluated at scale, predicted growth rates and interaction strengths fail to correlate with in vitro data, and only curated models perform ([PMID 38262921](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38262921/)). Separately, high-throughput mutant fitness data has been established as the appropriate yardstick for GEM accuracy, with an *E. coli* evaluation across thousands of genes and 25 carbon sources identifying medium-absent vitamins/cofactors and isoenzyme gene-protein-reaction mapping as principal error sources, and recommending precision-recall-style metrics over simple accuracy ([PMID 37888487](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37888487/)).

This corpus sits squarely on the semi-curated side of that divide, and its numbers are consistent with it: 42.5% baseline accuracy with 86.5% recall and 330 false positives across 574 organism–carbon-source combinations is the permissive, false-positive-dominated regime one expects from automated reconstructions, not the 80–95% of hand-curated models. The corpus extends the published critique in two ways. First, it moves the semi-curated accuracy problem from community-interaction prediction ([PMID 38262921](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38262921/)) to single-organism carbon-source growth across many non-model bacteria, and it pairs FBA against RB-TnSeq fitness data in organisms other than *E. coli*, applying the validation design of [PMID 37888487](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37888487/) well outside its original scope. Second, it localises the error: the heavy gapfilling dependence (87% of 121,519 *Acinetobacter* predictions requiring at least one gapfilled reaction) and the EC-number stratification of gene assignment name a failure mode distinct from the isoenzyme-GPR and unmodelled-cofactor causes previously identified. See [[concepts/metabolic-model-gapfilling]] and [[concepts/ec-less-reaction-annotation]].

Two corpus results refine or go beyond the candidates. Multistrain modelling of 183 *Bacillus subtilis* GEMs attributes panphenome breadth largely to accessory metabolism ([PMID 37108252](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37108252/)); the *Acinetobacter* finding that 1,248 of 1,330 reactions (94%) are shared across 14 genomes while gapfilling supplies 7.7% **refines** this by locating the variable margin in inferred rather than observed content. The corpus's zero predicted NDH-2 flux under growth optimisation is consistent with the literature's motivation for enzyme- and proteome-constrained dynamic FBA, which improves agreement precisely where growth-rate optimisation misprices capacity ([PMID 36607958](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36607958/), [PMID 37428554](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37428554/)). Genuinely unaddressed by any candidate are the circularity of in-silico knockouts of gapfilled reactions ([[concepts/circularity-in-metabolic-model-validation]]) and the latent-capability fractions from pathway-completeness calls ([[concepts/computational-pathway-prediction-validation]]) — these remain corpus-internal and untested against published benchmarks.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Model growth predictions are permissive, and most of them lean on inferred reactions.** Across 574 organism–carbon-source combinations, baseline FBA achieved 42.5% overall accuracy with 86.5% recall (244 of 282 growth-positive conditions) and 42.5% precision, generating 330 false positives — the dominant error mode is predicting growth that does not occur. [src: annotation_gap_discovery] The dependence on gapfilling is heavy: of 121,519 growth-phenotype predictions across 14 *Acinetobacter* genomes, 105,376 (87%) required at least one gapfilled reaction, and false negatives carried higher mean gap counts than correct predictions. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] Reaction content itself is stable — 1,248 of 1,330 reactions (94%) were shared by all 14 genomes, with gapfilling accounting for 7.7% of reactions on average and 243 missing functions cataloged — so the conserved backbone is not what varies; the inferred margin is. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] See [[concepts/metabolic-model-gapfilling]].

**Gapfilling repairs a prediction; it does not certify the biology, and testing it inside the model is circular.** Conditional gapfilling of 38 false-negative cases added 219 reactions (201 enzymatic, 14 transport, 12 exchange), averaging 5.8 per case. [src: annotation_gap_discovery] When 23 gene-protein-reaction rules were inserted into SBML models and knocked out *in silico*, the simulations returned zero wildtype growth — but the models required those same gapfilled reactions to grow on the selected carbon sources, so the knockout re-tested a dependency built during construction rather than a biological requirement. [src: annotation_gap_discovery] Default ModelSEED gapfilling minimizes added reactions without guaranteeing biological optimality, and multiple valid solutions can explain one false negative. [src: annotation_gap_discovery] [[concepts/circularity-in-metabolic-model-validation]] develops the repair-versus-validation distinction.

**Assigning genes to gapfilled reactions requires triangulation, and reactions without EC numbers resist it.** Combining Fitness Browser phenotypes, pangenome annotations, Bakta annotations, GapMind evidence and BLAST homology resolved 96 of 201 gapfilled enzymatic reaction–organism pairs (47.8%) — 44 high-confidence (21.9%), 19 medium (9.5%), 33 low (16.4%) — leaving 105 (52.2%) unresolved; BLAST alone reached 70 (34.8%), a 13-percentage-point deficit against the full pipeline. [src: annotation_gap_discovery] The sharpest stratifier is the enzyme-classification identifier: 50 of 201 reactions (24.9%) lacked an EC number in ModelSEED and only 8 of those 50 (16%) received assignments, versus 88 of 151 (58.3%) with known ECs. [src: annotation_gap_discovery] Structural evidence is unevenly distributed in a way that may compound this: only 28.7% of 132,531,501 gene clusters bridged to AlphaFold MSA depths while 83.8% carried at least one InterProScan domain, and MSA depth correlated with domain-hit count at Spearman ρ = 0.7563. [src: alphafold_msa_annotation] See [[concepts/ec-less-reaction-annotation]] and [[concepts/pathway-versus-reaction-evidence-resolution]].

**Pathway completeness measures capability, and a large fraction of complete pathways register no dependency under tested conditions.** Among 1,695 complete organism–pathway pairs from 48 organisms, 881 (51.9%) were active dependencies, 547 (32.3%) intermediate, and 267 (15.8%) latent capabilities; pathway category predicted class (χ² = 163.6, df = 4, p = 2.5×10⁻³⁴), with carbon-source pathways latent at 217 of 892 (24.3%) versus amino-acid biosynthesis at 48 of 735 (6.5%). [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] A 161-pair, 7-organism classification split 57 (35.4%) Active Dependency, 66 (41.0%) Latent Capability, 24 (14.9%) Incomplete but Important, and 14 (8.7%) Missing — and all 66 Latent Capability pairs became fitness-important under at least one condition type, most often nitrogen limitation, stress, or carbon limitation. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] Conservation does not separate the classes: mean pathway conservation was 0.829 for 755 active dependencies versus 0.869 for 248 latent capabilities, with Mann–Whitney U p = 0.94 for active > latent. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] A 7-organism pilot found 17 of 18 amino-acid pathways complete in all mapped organisms, with *Desulfovibrio vulgaris* lacking a complete serine pathway — a computational call, not a demonstrated auxotrophy. [src: essential_metabolome] See [[concepts/computational-pathway-prediction-validation]].

**Where assays are matched, cross-database agreement is excellent; where they are not, it is thin.** For *Pseudomonas* FW300-N2E3, 17 of 21 testable Web of Microbes metabolites (81%) were fully concordant across matched databases with mean concordance 0.94 and none fully discordant; Fitness Browser agreed 21/21 and GapMind 13/13, but BacDive utilization agreed only 3/7 (43%) against a *Pseudomonas* baseline of 22/80 (27.5%, binomial p = 0.40). [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] Only 21 of 58 produced metabolites could be cross-referenced at all, leaving 37 (64%) untested. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] Direction matters as much as identity: the 2018 Web of Microbes snapshot records 1,338 amplification and 1,155 de novo emergence actions among 10,744 organism observations with no consumption action, and only 69 of 257 compounds (26.8%) had definitive ModelSEED links against 107 (41.6%) formula-only links expanding to 900 candidate molecules. [src: webofmicrobes_explorer] Upstream of modeling entirely, 74 of 83 ENIGMA enrichment compounds (89%) were organism-dark. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] See [[concepts/cross-condition-metabolic-comparability]].

**Optimization objectives blind models to capacity constraints and support functions.** FBA predicted zero NDH-2 flux on standard media because growth optimization routes NADH through the more ATP-efficient Complex I, while measured Complex I growth ratios were 0.37 on quinate, 1.44 on glucose, and 0.49 on acetate. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] On aromatics, FBA captured 1.76× higher Complex I flux (0.55 versus 0.31) yet predicted 0% essentiality, while 10 of 13 Complex I operon subunits produced quinate-specific growth defects, and 30 of 51 quinate-specific genes had no FBA mapping at all. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Nine of 11 aromatic-degradation genes were FBA-discordant (OR = 9.70, q = 0.012), with under-prediction enriched (OR = 12.0, q = 0.004). [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]

## Tensions and Caveats

FBA's reliability has three incompatible headline numbers: 73.8% FBA/TnSeq concordance for 866 genes in ADP1 [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer], only moderate FBA/knockout concordance with a null association inside the TnSeq-dispensable set [src: adp1_triple_essentiality], and 42.5% baseline accuracy with 330 false positives [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. These differ in organism, medium and endpoint and must not be averaged — see [[conflicts/conflict--acinetobacter_adp1_explorer--adp1_triple_essentiality--annotation_gap_discovery--8f009ad9]].

Perfect matched-subset concordance and widespread latency also sit in tension: 21/21 and 13/13 agreement in one organism's selected metabolites against 41.0% Latent Capability in a 161-pair panel [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency, pathway_capability_dependency], plus an unreconciled trehalose count (1 of 6 BacDive strains positive versus 1+/5−) that both sources retain [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]. See [[conflicts/conflict--essential_metabolome--fw300_metabolic_consistency--metabolic_capability_dependency--96fcb005]].

The two latent fractions — 15.8% and 41.0% — are not reconciled, because organism sets, pathways, mappings and thresholds differ [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]; the 15.8% figure itself ranged 4.7%–21.1% across 16 threshold combinations, and the condition-specific median threshold can reclassify pathways by construction [src: metabolic_capability_dependency, pathway_capability_dependency]. Coverage is the binding constraint throughout: only 7 of 48 Fitness Browser organisms had matching GapMind genome data [src: pathway_capability_dependency], only 7 of 45 essential-gene organisms mapped, with *E. coli* K-12 returning 0 GapMind predictions [src: essential_metabolome], and GapMind covers roughly 80 carbon and amino-acid pathways rather than full metabolism [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. Whether NDH-2 compensation explains substrate-specific Complex I dependence is unsettled: organisms with validated NDH-2 showed a *larger* mean Complex I aromatic deficit (−0.297 versus −0.156, p = 0.52) on only 4 NDH-2-lacking organisms [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] — see [[conflicts/conflict--aromatic_catabolism_network--metabolic_capability_dependency--respiratory_chain_wiring]] and [[conflicts/conflict--aromatic_catabolism_network--respiratory_chain_wiring]]. Finally, prevalence is not activity: 0.90 field prevalence for 3-hydroxybenzoic-acid utilizers is an occurrence proxy, not evidence of *in situ* catabolism [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] — see [[conflicts/conflict--enigma_carbon_census_1--essential_metabolome--metabolic_capability_dependency--8ff1555a]].
