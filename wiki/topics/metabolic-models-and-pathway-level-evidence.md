# Metabolic Models and Pathway-Level Evidence

Genome-scale metabolic reconstructions and pathway-completeness predictors are this corpus's main bridge from a genome sequence to a claim about what an organism can metabolize. Two instruments dominate: flux balance analysis (FBA), a constraint-based method predicting feasible metabolic flux distributions and, from them, simulated growth or gene essentiality, and GapMind, a pathway-prediction resource asking whether an organism has the genes for each step of a catabolic or biosynthetic pathway. The member concept pages below, spanning single-strain reconstructions, cross-organism benchmarks and pathway censuses, converge on one finding: a model's agreement with data is bounded by what the reference measures and by whether the model's repair history was independent of the test. Baseline FBA reached 42.5% overall accuracy across 574 organism–carbon-source combinations with 330 false positives [src: annotation_gap_discovery].

## Literature Context

The published genome-scale metabolic model (GEM) literature reports validation accuracies well above this corpus's baseline, but almost always for single, manually curated organisms. EcoCyc-18.0-GEM, built from the *Escherichia coli* K-12 MG1655 EcoCyc database, encompasses 1445 genes, 2286 unique metabolic reactions and 1453 unique metabolites, and reaches 95.2% accuracy predicting gene-knockout growth phenotypes and 80.7% overall accuracy across 431 media conditions — while still logging 70 incorrect essentiality predictions on glucose, 80 on glycerol and 83 incorrect nutrient-utilization predictions [PMID 24974895](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24974895/). Other manually curated reconstructions report 95.83% accuracy, 93.33% sensitivity and 100% specificity for *Streptococcus thermophilus* S-3 (iCH492) [PMID 37814322](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37814322/); an average prediction accuracy over 90% under several growth conditions for *Nitrosomonas europaea* iGC535 [PMID 35108266](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35108266/); and growth and carbon-source utilization predictions aligning with experimental results for *Staphylococcus epidermidis* iSep23 [PMID 40396730](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40396730/). Where curation is lighter, published numbers fall toward this corpus's range: an orthology-mapped GEM of *Lipomyces starkeyi* NRRL Y-11557 (iLst996) validated at 66% accuracy against phenotypic growth assays [PMID 38638323](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38638323/). The corpus's 42.5% baseline accuracy across 574 organism–carbon-source combinations, on models built from automated RAST annotations for 14 organisms, is therefore **consistent** with the field's curation gradient rather than anomalous — the low-curation end of a spectrum the literature has mostly sampled at its top.

Two corpus findings are anticipated by the literature, and one is not. The precision/recall asymmetry — 86.5% recall against 42.5% precision, with 330 false positives — sits alongside an evaluation of four successive *E. coli* GEMs against published mutant fitness data spanning thousands of genes and 25 carbon sources, which demonstrated the utility of the area under a precision-recall curve relative to alternative accuracy metrics [PMID 37888487](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37888487/). An analysis of errors in the latest of those models only (iML1515, not the four-model set) identified isoenzyme gene–protein–reaction mapping as a key source of inaccurate predictions and found vitamins/cofactors likely available to mutants despite being absent from the experimental growth medium [PMID 37888487](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37888487/) — **refining** the corpus's caveats about inserted GPR rules and about medium mismatch, including the hub's point that Web of Microbes rich medium and Fitness Browser minimal medium are not interchangeable. Multistrain reconstruction is likewise established: 183 strain-level *B. subtilis* GEMs defined a species panphenome in which accessory metabolism drives carbon-source versatility [PMID 37108252](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37108252/), and 16 of 25 newly sequenced *Lipomyces* species retained orthologs for more than 97% of iLst996 genes [PMID 38638323](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38638323/) — consistent with the 1,248/1,330 (94%) core reactions across 14 *Acinetobacter baylyi* ADP1 genomes, and with the corpus's own warning that conservation is not validation.

What the candidate literature does not supply is the corpus's central methodological claim: that a gapfilled model cannot validate its own repairs, demonstrated by 23 inserted GPR rules yielding zero wildtype growth in knockout simulations. None of these papers reports that null; the published reconstructions are curated or orthology-mapped rather than conditionally gapfilled against the test set. The corpus's capability-versus-dependency distinction is similarly **novel** here — the literature treats predicted carbon-source utilization as a phenotype to match (up to 28 metabolites nominated as potential novel carbon sources for *B. subtilis* [PMID 37108252](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37108252/)) rather than asking whether a complete pathway is ever fitness-required.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Draft models are permissive before they are wrong.** The cross-organism benchmark decomposes its 42.5% overall accuracy into recall of 86.5% (244 of 282 growth-positive conditions correctly predicted) and precision of 42.5% (244 of 574 growth predictions correct) [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. The models rarely miss real growth; they predict growth that does not occur. Conditional gapfilling — adding reactions to rescue a failed growth prediction — for 38 false-negative cases added 219 reactions, comprising 201 enzymatic, 14 transport and 12 exchange reactions [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. Reconstruction content is itself largely conserved rather than idiosyncratic: an *Acinetobacter baylyi* ADP1 pangenome analysis found 1,330 unique metabolic reactions across 14 genomes, of which 1,248 (94%) were shared by all 14 and classified as core, 62 variable and 20 genome-unique, with gapfilling accounting for 7.7% of reactions on average and 243 missing functions cataloged [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer]. Conserved reaction content does not amount to experimental validation [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer].

**A gapfilled model cannot validate its own repairs.** The annotation-gap study inserted 23 gene–protein–reaction (GPR) rules — formal links between genes and reactions — into SBML models and ran knockout simulations on minimal media with the relevant carbon sources; these produced zero wildtype growth [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. The result could not validate the candidate genes, because the models required the corresponding gapfilled reactions in order to grow at all, so deleting a gene removed a reaction introduced precisely to restore the growth prediction [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. This is the cleanest statement in the corpus of the difference between model repair and model validation: gapfilling repairs a failed prediction, while validation must test against evidence not used to build that repair [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. The report accordingly prioritizes targeted knockout or CRISPRi (gene-suppression) experiments on the 44 high-confidence gene–reaction assignments rather than treating the simulations as biological confirmation [src: annotation_gap_discovery].

**Pathway completeness is capability, not dependency.** Two pathway censuses separate a genomically complete pathway from a pathway the organism measurably needs. Across 161 organism–pathway pairs from 7 model bacteria and 23 GapMind pathways, 57 (35.4%) were Active Dependency, 66 (41.0%) Latent Capability, 24 (14.9%) Incomplete but Important and 14 (8.7%) Missing [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. A broader analysis of 1,695 complete pathway–organism pairs from 48 organisms reported 267 (15.8%) latent capabilities, 881 (51.9%) active dependencies and 547 (32.3%) intermediate, with carbon-source pathways far likelier to be latent (217 of 892, 24.3%) than amino-acid biosynthesis pathways (48 of 735, 6.5%) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Latency is condition-contingent: all 66 aggregate Latent Capability pairs became fitness-important under at least one condition type, most often nitrogen limitation, stress or carbon limitation — though a median-based condition-specific importance threshold can force reclassification by construction [src: pathway_capability_dependency].

**Pathway-level evidence does not resolve to a reaction or a gene.** GapMind covers approximately 80 carbon and amino-acid pathways rather than full metabolism, and its output reports pathway-level step counts, not individual step identities [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. Among 104 GapMind–gapfill pathway pairings, GapMind frequently reported `not_present` or `steps_missing` for carbon sources whose models required gapfilling, but exact concordance was limited for that reason [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. Reaction-level assignment therefore needed triangulation: the full pipeline resolved 96 of 201 gapfilled enzymatic reaction–organism pairs (47.8%), versus 80 (39.8%) without alternative Bakta annotations and 73 (36.3%) without BLAST [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. The unresolved set held 105 pairs (52.2%), including most of the 50 reactions (24.9% of 201) that lacked an EC number — an enzyme-classification identifier — in ModelSEED: only 8 of those 50 (16%) received candidate assignments, against 88 of 151 (58.3%) for EC-annotated reactions [src: annotation_gap_discovery].

**Concordance depends on which reference you consult.** For *Pseudomonas* FW300-N2E3, all 13 metabolites matched to GapMind had complete pathways and all 13 showed growth in Fitness Browser experiments; Fitness Browser comparisons were concordant for 21/21 metabolites, BacDive utilization for 3/7 [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]. Of the 21 testable metabolites, 17/21 (81%) were fully concordant, 4/21 (19%) partially concordant and none fully discordant, with a mean concordance score of 0.94; separately, across 41 individual metabolite–database comparisons, 37 were concordant (90.2%) [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]. Only 21 of 58 exometabolome metabolites (36%) were testable, leaving 37 (64%) untested; and the assays differ physically: Web of Microbes used R2A rich medium, Fitness Browser minimal medium with single carbon or nitrogen sources [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency].

## Tensions and Caveats

The corpus's sharpest disagreement is whether FBA tracks phenotype. A cross-organism benchmark reports 42.5% baseline accuracy driven by 330 false positives [src: annotation_gap_discovery], while an ADP1 analysis reports 73.8% concordance between FBA and TnSeq (transposon-sequencing) essentiality calls [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer]; the two are not measured against the same denominator or endpoint, so neither licenses a general claim — see [[conflicts/conflict--metabolic-model-gapfilling--c50aa829]]. A second, narrower version of the same fight sits within ADP1 itself, where one project reads substantial FBA–TnSeq agreement and another reads only moderate agreement with experimental knockouts plus a null association between FBA class and growth defects among TnSeq-dispensable genes [[conflicts/conflict--metabolic-model-gapfilling--68690ec7]]; relatedly, pangenome status did not explain FBA/knockout discordance, with genes 93–100% core across discordance classes and no significant enrichment (OR = 0.89, p = 0.80) [src: adp1_triple_essentiality].

Perfect matched-subset agreement and widespread latency also collide: 100% concordance for 21 Fitness Browser and 13 GapMind comparisons in one curated strain [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] against 66 (41.0%) Latent Capabilities in a broader classification [src: pathway_capability_dependency], the subject of [[conflicts/conflict--cross-condition-metabolic-comparability--c3fb4a47]]. What predicts latency is itself contested — gene conservation failed to separate active from latent pathways (Mann–Whitney U p = 0.94; rank-biserial r = 0.052), while latent-capability rate tracked pangenome openness (Spearman ρ = 0.69, p = 0.0004, n = 22 clades) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] — see [[conflicts/conflict--metabolic-model-gapfilling--09788ff7]]. Differing latent fractions must not be averaged, and the 15.8% figure ranged from 4.7% to 21.1% across 16 tested threshold combinations [src: metabolic_capability_dependency].

Three further tensions concern scope, not measurement. Whether capability-only and production-only datasets count as comparable evidence is unsettled in [[conflicts/conflict--cross-condition-metabolic-comparability--e6421a5b]]: 17 of 18 amino-acid pathways were complete in all 7 mapped organisms with no statement of condition-specific requirement [src: essential_metabolome], while 19 produced metabolites bridged to Fitness Browser carbon or nitrogen sources with no consumption action recorded [src: webofmicrobes_explorer]. Whether a high concordance score licenses a flux claim or only an endpoint claim is argued in [[conflicts/conflict--metabolic-model-gapfilling--9cf52c50]]; whether community-scale observables constrain models is argued in [[conflicts/conflict--metabolic-model-gapfilling--cf98e361]], where implicated genera appeared in 83/86 genera across 1,719 metagenomes from the National Microbiome Data Collaborative (NMDC) — occurrence, not degradation [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]. And [[conflicts/conflict--cofitness-network-architecture--05940aec]] records FBA predicting no essentiality where mutant phenotypes require the complex, with the compensation explanation failing a cross-species test.

Load-bearing limits: the benchmark dataset held 14 organisms, 12 of them Proteobacteria, on models built from automated RAST annotations with systematic errors [src: annotation_gap_discovery]; only 7 of 45 organisms mapped for the essential-metabolome pilot, and *Escherichia coli* K-12 had 0 GapMind predictions in the queried pangenome collection [src: essential_metabolome]; and only 48 fitness-tested organisms were available among 293,000 genomes with pathway predictions [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Upstream, 74 of 83 enrichment compounds (89%) were organism-dark because utilization determinants were not linkable through queried resources [src: enigma_carbon_census_1].
