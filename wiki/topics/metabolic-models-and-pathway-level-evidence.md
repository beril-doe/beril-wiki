# Metabolic Models and Pathway-Level Evidence

Genome-scale metabolic models and pathway-completeness predictions promise to turn a genome sequence into a statement about what an organism can do. This corpus tests that promise against measured phenotypes — mutant fitness, knockout growth, proteomics, exometabolomics and culture-collection utilization assays — and the recurring result is that the predictions and the measurements are answering different questions. Five member concepts on this hub cover pathway prediction validated by matched growth, the gapfilling machinery and its reliability ceilings, the mismatch between pathway-level and reaction-level evidence, the annotation barrier posed by reactions without enzyme-classification (EC) numbers, and the circularity that can arise when a gapfilled model is asked to validate its own gapfills. The scale of the problem is concrete: baseline flux-balance analysis (FBA) — a constraint-based method for predicting feasible flux distributions and simulated growth — achieved 42.5% overall accuracy across 574 organism–carbon source combinations, with recall 86.5% (244 of 282 growth-positive conditions) and precision 42.5%, producing 330 false positives [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. Against that, pathway predictions matched growth for all 13 matched metabolites in one strain [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]. Both numbers are real; the hub's job is to say what each one licenses.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Pathway completeness is a capability claim, not an activity claim.** For *Pseudomonas* FW300-N2E3, GapMind — a resource that evaluates whether an organism carries the genes for each step of a metabolic pathway — predicted complete pathways for all 13 matched metabolites (lactate, valine, alanine, arginine, aspartate, glutamic acid, glycine, phenylalanine, proline, trehalose, tryptophan, malate, lysine), and all 13 showed growth in Fitness Browser experiments, giving 13/13 agreement [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]. Widen the frame and the picture changes: across 1,695 complete organism–pathway pairs from 48 organisms, 267 (15.8%) were latent capabilities with low aggregate fitness signal, 881 (51.9%) active dependencies and 547 (32.3%) intermediate [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. An independent 161-pair classification over 7 model bacteria and 23 GapMind pathways found 57 (35.4%) Active Dependency, 66 (41.0%) Latent Capability, 24 (14.9%) Incomplete but Important and 14 (8.7%) Missing [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. Latency is category-dependent: carbon-source pathways were latent in 217 of 892 (24.3%) complete pairs versus 48 of 735 (6.5%) for amino-acid biosynthesis [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. See [[concepts/computational-pathway-prediction-validation]].

**Condition choice, not biology, drives much of the disagreement.** All 66 aggregate Latent Capability pairs became fitness-important under at least one condition type — most often nitrogen limitation, stress or carbon limitation — though a median-based condition-specific importance threshold can push pathways across the line by construction [src: pathway_capability_dependency]. Threshold sensitivity is large: the latent fraction ranged from 4.7% to 21.1% across 16 tested threshold combinations [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]. Assays also differ in what they measure: Web of Microbes exometabolomics used R2A rich medium while Fitness Browser used minimal medium with single carbon or nitrogen sources [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]. Essential-gene calls in the essential-metabolome pilot came from RB-TnSeq (random-barcode transposon sequencing) in rich media, where nutrient supplementation can make biosynthetic genes appear non-essential [src: essential_metabolome]. [[concepts/cross-condition-metabolic-comparability]] develops this.

**Pathway-level evidence does not resolve to a reaction or a gene.** Among 104 GapMind–gapfill pathway pairings, GapMind frequently reported `not_present` or `steps_missing` for carbon sources whose ModelSEED models required gapfilling, but exact concordance was limited because the available records gave pathway-level step counts rather than individual missing-step identities [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. The annotation pipeline resolved 96 of 201 gapfilled enzymatic reaction–organism pairs (47.8%), leaving 105 (52.2%) unresolved, and only by combining streams: 96 with the full pipeline versus 86 (42.8%) without EC matching, 80 (39.8%) without Bakta annotations and 73 (36.3%) without BLAST; individual streams alone gave 51 (25.4%), 22 (10.9%) and 70 (34.8%) [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. Gapfilling is non-unique — default ModelSEED gapfilling minimizes added reactions without guaranteeing biological optimality [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. See [[concepts/pathway-versus-reaction-evidence-resolution]].

**Missing EC numbers are a measurable, not cosmetic, barrier.** Of the 201 gapfilled enzymatic reaction–organism pairs, 50 (24.9%) lacked an EC (Enzyme Commission) number in ModelSEED and were designated "dark reactions"; only 8 of those 50 (16%) received candidate assignments versus 88 of 151 (58.3%) for EC-annotated reactions [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. A separate sequence-space gap runs alongside it: only 28.7% of 132,531,501 gene clusters bridged to AlphaFold multiple-sequence-alignment (MSA) depths while 83.8% carried at least one InterProScan domain annotation, and among 38,051,842 gene cluster–UniProt pairs MSA depth tracked domain-hit count at Spearman ρ = 0.7563, with mean domain hits rising from 0.59 at MSA depth < 10 to 10.83 at MSA depth ≥ 10,000 [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. Juxtaposing the two suggests the hypothesis that an EC-less reaction may lack its reaction-to-enzyme key while its candidate proteins also occupy poorly represented sequence space; this is a prioritization hypothesis, not a demonstrated property of the 50 EC-less reactions, and the AlphaFold bridge coverage was itself biased toward better-studied organisms [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]. See [[concepts/ec-less-reaction-annotation]].

**A gapfilled model can be made to validate itself.** Twenty-three gene–protein–reaction (GPR) rules — formal links between genes and metabolic reactions — were inserted into SBML (Systems Biology Markup Language, the exchange format for genome-scale metabolic models) models and tested by knockout simulation on minimal media with the relevant carbon sources; the simulations produced zero wildtype growth, which could not validate the candidates because the models required the gapfilled reactions to grow at all [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. Conditional gapfilling for 38 false-negative cases added 219 reactions — 201 enzymatic, 14 transport, 12 exchange — so growth dependence on those same reactions cannot distinguish a correct assignment from a computationally necessary repair [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. [[concepts/circularity-in-metabolic-model-validation]] and [[concepts/metabolic-model-gapfilling]] carry this forward.

## Tensions and Caveats

The sharpest disagreement is whether completeness proxies activity: [[conflicts/conflict--cross-condition-metabolic-comparability--c3fb4a47]] sets FW300-N2E3's 100% concordance for 21 Fitness Browser and 13 GapMind comparisons [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] against the census finding of widespread latency [src: metabolic_capability_dependency, pathway_capability_dependency]. What predicts latency is itself contested — [[conflicts/conflict--metabolic-model-gapfilling--09788ff7]] notes that conservation did not separate active from latent pathways (mean conservation 0.829 for 755 active, 0.907 for 508 intermediate, 0.869 for 248 latent; Mann–Whitney U p = 0.94, rank-biserial r = 0.052) while pangenome openness correlated with latent rate (Spearman ρ = 0.69, p = 0.0004, n = 22 clades) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]; the differing latent fractions are not resolved because organism sets, pathways, mappings and thresholds differ, and must not be averaged [src: metabolic_capability_dependency].

FBA's verdict depends on the endpoint counted: [[conflicts/conflict--metabolic-model-gapfilling--c50aa829]] contrasts 42.5% cross-organism growth accuracy with 330 false positives [src: annotation_gap_discovery] against 73.8% concordance between FBA and TnSeq — transposon sequencing, which infers gene essentiality from depletion of transposon insertion mutants — in ADP1 [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer]; the two use different denominators and different endpoints. [[conflicts/conflict--metabolic-model-gapfilling--68690ec7]] sets that substantial FBA–TnSeq agreement [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] against moderate agreement with experimental knockouts together with a null association between FBA class and growth defects among TnSeq-dispensable genes [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]. Separately, pangenome status did not explain FBA/knockout discordance: genes were 93–100% core across discordance classes, with no significant enrichment (OR = 0.89, p = 0.80) [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]. [[conflicts/conflict--cofitness-network-architecture--05940aec]] marks a third gap, in which increased model-predicted flux coexisted with missed complex-level essentiality for Complex I, the proton-pumping NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreductase [src: aromatic_catabolism_network].

Coverage is the load-bearing caveat throughout. Only 21 of 58 Web of Microbes metabolites (36%) were cross-referenceable, leaving 37 (64%) untested; BacDive agreed for only 3/7 metabolites against a *Pseudomonas* baseline of 22/80 (27.5%), p = 0.40 [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]. Tryptophan was increased in the exometabolome, had a complete GapMind pathway and 231 significant Fitness Browser genes, yet 0 of 50 *P. fluorescens* strains utilized it as a carbon source — cross-feeding remains a hypothesis, not a demonstrated transfer [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]. Only 7 of 45 organisms mapped in the essential-metabolome pilot, with *E. coli* K-12 returning 0 GapMind predictions [src: essential_metabolome]; GapMind covers roughly 80 carbon and amino-acid pathways, not full metabolism [src: annotation_gap_discovery]. [[conflicts/conflict--cross-condition-metabolic-comparability--e6421a5b]], [[conflicts/conflict--metabolic-model-gapfilling--9cf52c50]] and [[conflicts/conflict--metabolic-model-gapfilling--cf98e361]] frame these as scope rather than measurement disputes. Of 83 ENIGMA enrichment compounds, 74 (89%) were organism-dark [src: enigma_carbon_census_1].
