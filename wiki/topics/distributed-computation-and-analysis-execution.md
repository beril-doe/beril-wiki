# Distributed Computation and Analysis Execution

This topic covers how analyses actually run against the KBase Data Lakehouse: Spark (the distributed query engine behind the Lakehouse), the pandas frames and notebooks where results are examined, and the sessions, credentials and access paths between them. The corpus speaks to it through one source, the central pitfalls digest, which collects operational failures from many projects. Its evidence is practitioner reports of what broke and what fixed it, not controlled benchmarks, so most claims below are well-documented practice rather than quantified findings [src: pitfalls].

## Literature Context

The wider literature treats Spark and cloud execution as a way to scale genomic analysis, and it reports mostly speedups and design guidance. One study found that Spark, used with Jupyter notebooks, reduces user-driven data partitioning and improves portability and reproducibility, and it validated this by reproducing published Alzheimer's Disease Sequencing Project results [PMID 31797624](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31797624/). Spark-based tools report near-linear scalability for clustering billions of reads [PMID 30816928](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30816928/). They also report memory reductions of up to 3x through on-the-fly compression of intermediate data [PMID 31805063](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31805063/). Work on sorted merging of variant call format (VCF) files argues that carefully designed workflow patterns and execution plans are required to overcome bottlenecks on Hadoop, HBase and Spark [PMID 29762754](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29762754/). A bin-based range-join method with balanced partitioning reports up to 4.65x speedup in its Apache Spark add-in over the previously best tool [PMID 37022216](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37022216/). Memory-conscious designs that load only local segments of the genotype matrix, as in REGENIE, are likewise reported to save compute time and memory [PMID 34017140](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34017140/).

On reproducibility and access, reviews and infrastructure papers argue that cloud elasticity, containers and standardized pipelines make results comparable and reusable. ENCODE captures software versions, reference genomes and parameters through Docker and Workflow Description Language (WDL) pipelines [PMID 37503119](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37503119/). Cloud computing is argued to suit large-scale reanalysis, including of privacy-protected data [PMID 29379135](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29379135/). A Google Cloud chapter makes a similar case for containers and cloud storage as supports for reproducibility and record keeping [PMID 31396908](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31396908/).

The corpus findings are consistent with this literature in direction and extend it in kind. The pitfalls digest points the same way on memory footprint and execution plan, and it suggests the hypothesis that table layout is a major cost driver. Its hypothesis that unpartitioned billion-row junction tables drive slow joins matches the partition-focused designs above, although the corpus has not measured the remedy. Its practice of reporting the access path, and of staging Parquet checkpoints, fits the reproducibility emphasis of the ENCODE and cloud papers.

The corpus also covers ground the literature candidates largely do not: practitioner failure modes such as REST timeouts, driver result-size caps, idle-kernel termination, DECIMAL type traps, expired tokens and silent empty results. The candidate papers describe tools, infrastructure and standardized pipelines, and none of them addresses these failures, so the corpus's operational pitfalls are novel relative to them. Its evidence is also weaker, since it comes from anecdotal incident reports rather than benchmarks. The 8% broadcast-hint gain on Smeli is modest beside the large speedups the tool papers report, but those papers use different workloads and are not directly comparable. The literature offers a route to resolving the open questions: controlled comparisons of partitioning and join strategy on the Lakehouse tables, in the style of the benchmarked Spark studies.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Where the work runs shapes what succeeds.** The same analysis can hit different limits depending on the environment. The REST interface returns 504 and 524 timeouts and rejects any query containing `--` with a 400 error, while direct Spark SQL accepts the same text. The digest recommends direct `spark.sql()` for queries over 1M rows or billion-row aggregations [src: pitfalls]. Session construction also differs between JupyterHub notebooks, cluster scripts and local machines, and the wrong import raises `ImportError` or `NameError`. The working claim is that the access path is part of the method and should be reported with it ([[concepts/execution-environment-dependent-access-paths]]) [src: pitfalls]. Direct Spark is not shown to be immune to timeouts [src: pitfalls].

**Table layout is a suspected major cost driver, as a hypothesis.** Most genome junction tables have ~1 billion rows and are unpartitioned, so joins such as `gene_genecluster_junction` with `gene` take 3-5 minutes per species even on Spark [src: pitfalls]. In one profiling run on Smeli, broadcast hints (sending a small table to every worker) gave ~274s against ~300s without, an 8% improvement [src: pitfalls]. Filtering on the partition key made clade-wise extraction fast, whereas `LIMIT/OFFSET` pagination was orders of magnitude slower [src: pitfalls]. A defensive setting that disabled automatic broadcast preceded a 17+ minute hang on a 13.7M × 18K join [src: pitfalls]. The partitioning remedy has not been measured ([[concepts/join-strategy-and-table-partitioning-cost]]) [src: pitfalls].

**Moving results into one process is a recurring failure point.** Calling `.toPandas()` pulls everything to the driver node. One join over 1B-row tables hit `spark.driver.maxResultSize = 1024 MB`, a cap the source says is read-only after session start [src: pitfalls]. Even under the cap, a pandas spatial merge of ~24M rows × 9 columns, a ≈ 9 GB working set, ran out of memory on a 16 GB driver [src: pitfalls]. Reported remedies are staging results as Parquet, batching, and pushing filters into Spark. An algebraic rewrite of a donor-inference step ran in 142s instead of failing [src: pitfalls]. This is the only remedy with a reported time ([[concepts/driver-side-result-collection-limits]]) [src: pitfalls].

**Python-level loops and type mismatches cost time or stop runs.** A row-wise `iterrows()` loop over 27K focal features × 218K contigs ran 30+ minutes without completing, whereas a vectorized merge took ~10 seconds [src: pitfalls]. The digest's claimed "1000× speedup" is an unverified assertion ([[concepts/vectorization-versus-row-wise-iteration]]) [src: pitfalls]. Types also fail across the engine boundary. Spark `DECIMAL` values arrive in pandas as `decimal.Decimal` and break float arithmetic, even for `AVG(CASE WHEN ... THEN 1.0 ELSE 0.0 END)`. Arrow-backed frames from Spark Connect cannot be passed back to `spark.createDataFrame()` ([[concepts/numeric-type-fidelity-across-engine-boundaries]]) [src: pitfalls]. In the documented cases these stop the calculation with an error; silent corruption is untested [src: pitfalls].

**Long jobs outlive their sessions, and failures can be quiet.** JupyterHub kernels with no activity for ~17–25 minutes can be idle-timed out, silently killing Spark cells that take 30+ min. The reported pattern is to convert to a script, run it with `nohup`, and write intermediate Parquet files so `_finalize.py` scripts can resume ([[concepts/long-running-analysis-execution-reliability]]) [src: pitfalls]. Related silent shapes include temporary views destroyed on Spark Connect reconnection, which makes later joins return 0 rows. Cold databases can show a valid `/count` beside empty `/query` results. Permission failures can surface as `S3 access denied` or `403 Forbidden` ([[concepts/silent-failure-modes-in-distributed-queries]]) [src: pitfalls]. The working rule is to verify an empty result independently before treating it as a finding [src: pitfalls].

**Access and compute budgets are reproducibility conditions.** Queries need tenant membership and a valid token. An `ErrorResponse` with error 20040 means the user is not in the group. An expired `KBASE_AUTH_TOKEN` yields 401/403 errors in long sessions. On JupyterHub a fresh token sits in `~/.berdl_kbase_session`, synced every 30 seconds ([[concepts/credential-and-tenant-access-lifecycle]]) [src: pitfalls]. One analysis was approximated to fit hardware. UMAP (a dimension-reduction method) on 83K genomes with cosine distance exceeded 60 minutes without finishing, but after L2-normalization, Euclidean distance and a 20K-subsample fit it took ~7 min in the reported run. For bootstrap uncertainty estimates the digest gives moderate sizes (for example 250-400) as guidance, not as a record of what the models ran. The accuracy cost of these shortcuts is not measured, so settings should be declared ([[concepts/runtime-budgeted-resampling-and-embedding]]) [src: pitfalls].

## Tensions and Caveats

- **Broadcast evidence points in different directions.** Disabling auto-broadcast was blamed for the 17+ minute hang, yet explicit hints gave only an 8% gain for Smeli. No side-by-side measurement reconciles them [src: pitfalls].
- **The result-size cap is described two ways.** One entry says `spark.driver.maxResultSize` cannot be raised via `SET` at runtime, while another suggests raising it in session config. These may refer to different stages, and whether Spark Connect users can set it at session creation is open [src: pitfalls].
- **The 1000× speedup is asserted, not demonstrated.** One incident has no completed row-wise runtime, and the other reports only "seconds instead of minutes" [src: pitfalls].
- **Single-source evidence.** Every claim traces to one digest, often to single projects, with no failure frequencies, token lifetimes or affected-analysis counts. Whether headless `nbconvert` runs avoid idle termination is untested [src: pitfalls].
- **Approximation costs are unmeasured.** The UMAP subsample fit and bootstrap limits to key coefficients are reported as feasible, not as accuracy-checked [src: pitfalls].
