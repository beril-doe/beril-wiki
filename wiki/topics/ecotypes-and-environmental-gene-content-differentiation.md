# Ecotypes and Environmental Gene-Content Differentiation

An **ecotype** is a within-species group of strains that differ systematically in gene content and are presumed to occupy distinct ecological niches. This corpus tests that presumption at two scales: whether genomes cluster into recoverable gene-content groups, and whether gene content tracks the environment a genome came from. The answer is asymmetric. Gene-content structure is easy to find: valid clusters appeared in 12 of 15 sampled species (80%), covering 1,820 genomes across 12 species from 6 phyla [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. Environmental structure is hard to find at genome scale: across 172 species the median partial correlation — the association between environmental distance and gene-content distance after the contribution of phylogenetic distance is removed — was 0.0025, against 0.0143 for the corresponding phylogeny term, with no significant environmental effect in 156 species (90.7%) [src: ecotype_analysis]. The six member concepts below, plus the conflict pages they anchor, work out how both statements can be true.

## Literature Context

The ecotype concept in the candidate literature was built almost entirely from single clades. Marine *Prochlorococcus* splits into high-light and low-light adapted clades whose whole-genome phylogeny — unlike 16S rRNA phylogeny — recovers the phenotypic HL/LL distinction across twelve strains ([PMID 25172453](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25172453/)), and comparison of six HL and six LL strains found distinct compositional, physicochemical and structural traits plus greater variation in orthologous gene repertoire between the two low-light groups ([PMID 20146791](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20146791/)). *Synechococcus* HNLC ecotype CRD1 strains carry an expanded repertoire of Fe transporter, storage and regulatory genes relative to other ecotypes, while the coexisting CRD2 ecotype has a distinct Fe-related gene repertoire described as an independent approach to low-Fe life ([PMID 31840403](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31840403/)). In *Shewanella baltica*, genomic content analysis of 13 strains from two Baltic redox-transition-zone clades found sulfur-respiration genes specific to one clade, while the regulatory divergence was characterized in just two representative strains, OS185 and OS195 ([PMID 29222101](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29222101/)). Against this backdrop, the corpus's recovery of gene-content clusters in 12 of 15 species is consistent with the field; their weak separation (mean silhouette 0.215, median 0.174) is a scale the single-clade literature rarely reports.

The corpus's near-null genome-scale environmental signal — median partial correlation 0.0025, no significant environmental effect in 156 of 172 species (90.7%) — is less anomalous than it first appears. The deep-ocean SAR11 bathytype Ic shared 69% of orthologous clusters with surface genomes and had similar COG distributions, with the authors concluding that adaptation "does not involve large variations in gene content" but subtler amino-acid and genome-size differences; that conclusion rests on four single-cell genomes of 55–86% estimated completeness, and most Ic-specific genes were hypothetical ([PMID 24451205](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24451205/)). SAR11 more broadly shows 48–56% core genome genes, making it among the most conserved free-living bacteria known ([PMID 22991429](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22991429/)) — a reminder that pangenome summary statistics and ecological driver need not covary, consistent with the corpus's openness nulls (rho = -0.05, p = 0.54 for environment; rho = 0.03, p = 0.73 for phylogeny).

The literature's strongest environmental signals sit in particular gene families rather than in whole-genome similarity, which fits the corpus's treatment of locus-specific adaptation as a live hypothesis. *Prochlorococcus* phosphate-acquisition gene content varied across 11 strains in a way explicitly "not congruent with rRNA phylogeny", which those authors read as evidence for genome-wide adaptation to source-ocean P availability, with supporting Sargasso Sea metagenomic data ([PMID 16895994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16895994/)); *Alteromonas* SN2 carries 15 genomic islands attributed to horizontal transfer ([PMID 22563400](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22563400/)); and two Red Sea SAR11 isolates carried 108 (RS39, Ia) and 151 (RS40, Ib) strain-specific genes, of which only 51 and 55 had not been reported before ([PMID 28645159](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28645159/)). Strains of a single soil *Curtobacterium* ecotype from surface leaf litter across one regional climate gradient suggest genetic structure maintained by both isolation by environment and isolation by distance ([PMID 31662456](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31662456/)) — a single-taxon result that mirrors the corpus's embedding distance-decay (0.41 below 100 km to 0.82 at 10K–20K km) while sharpening the tension in [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]]: geography structures environment, yet gene content stays uncoupled at genome scale.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Gene-content ecotypes exist but are weakly separated, and the method partly makes them.** Clusters were obtained by principal component analysis followed by KMeans, searching k = 2–6 and keeping the best silhouette score — a measure of how far observations sit from their own cluster relative to the nearest neighbouring one [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. Species averaged 3.7 ecotypes (range 2–6), with mean silhouette 0.215 and median 0.174, indicating overlapping rather than sharply bounded partitions [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. Separation ranged from *Erwinia amylovora* at 0.468 with 2 ecotypes down to *Staphylococcus simulans* at 0.118 [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. KMeans assumes approximately spherical clusters and a chosen k, and HDBSCAN — which can represent variable-density subpopulations — was unavailable on the cluster [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. [[concepts/ecotype-clustering-validity]] makes the case that these are analyzable partitions, not demonstrated biological populations.

**Where clusters do exist, adaptive functions differentiate more in magnitude than housekeeping functions — but the effects are small.** Across 257 chi-square or Fisher's exact tests over 12 species and 23 COG categories (COG = Clusters of Orthologous Groups, a functional classification of genes), 170 tests (66.1%) were significant after Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction at q < 0.05, and all 12 species had at least one differentiated category [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. Adaptive categories were significant in 67/84 tests (79.8%) versus 33/48 (68.8%) for housekeeping, a 1.16x ratio; the effect-size contrast was sharper, mean 0.0136 versus 0.0064, a 2.13x ratio, one-sided Mann–Whitney U p = 2.53 x 10^-6 [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. Amino-acid metabolism E and defense V were significant in 11/12 species (91.7%) each [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. The largest mean effect sizes, however, fell outside the predefined adaptive set: 0.0392 for S (unknown function) and 0.0337 for L (replication, recombination and repair) [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. [[concepts/adaptive-versus-housekeeping-functional-differentiation]] reads this as non-random differentiation of modest magnitude, with the report itself noting that significance may partly reflect the 1,820-genome sample size [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation].

**Genome-wide, phylogeny beats environment — which is what makes locus-specific adaptation a live hypothesis rather than a conclusion.** Phylogeny dominated gene-content similarity in 60.5% of species versus 39.5% for environment, with significant positive environmental effects in 12 species (7.0%) and significant negative effects in 4 (2.3%) [src: ecotype_analysis]. [[concepts/genome-wide-versus-locus-specific-ecological-adaptation]] treats this as clonal ancestry structuring broad similarity while selection acts at particular loci — but insists the locus-specific reading is a hypothesis suggested by a weak whole-genome signal, not a demonstration that any locus is environmentally selected [src: ecotype_analysis]. Pangenome openness, a single whole-genome summary metric, predicted neither the environment effect (Spearman rho = -0.05, p = 0.54) nor the phylogeny effect (rho = 0.03, p = 0.73); within the limited species set with both pangenome and ecotype results, open versus closed pangenomes did not predict which broad driver dominated gene-content variation [src: pangenome_openness].

**Narrow feature spaces recover environmental structure that whole-genome comparisons miss.** Prophages — phage genomes integrated into a bacterial chromosome — carry gene modules grouped by function such as packaging, lysis or tail morphogenesis. Across 1,773 species, PERMANOVA (permutational multivariate analysis of variance, a distance-based test of whether group centroids differ) gave F = 212.99 for genome size, F = 30.04 for environment and F = 6.17 for phylogeny, each at p = 0.01, with environmental effects persisting within every genome-size quartile at all p < 6.5e-78 [src: prophage_ecology]. Named plant-growth-promoting genes differentiated by habitat, but not all in one direction: among 1,039 soil/rhizosphere species and 10,233 species from other environments, *acdS* prevalence was 15.8% versus 2.6% (odds ratio OR = 7.02, q = 5.1e-62), surviving phylum fixed effects (OR = 6.98, p = 4.8e-61) and a strict rhizosphere/root-nodule filter (OR = 10.6, q = 7.6e-38), while *nifH* was depleted in soil-classified species (12.9% versus 19.7%, OR = 0.60, q = 5.5e-08) and *ipdC* was not significantly enriched (OR = 0.79, q = 0.47) [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]. Soil metal analysis found 2,355 significant COG–metal associations at FDR < 0.05 across 51,748 samples and nine metals [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]. [[concepts/gene-cooccurrence-ecological-guilds]] assembles these into guild claims while insisting co-occurrence is not physical linkage: *pqqC*–*acdS* pairs at OR = 7.24, n = 286, q = 1.2e-83, yet *pqqC* is 81.5% core and *acdS* 70.4% core, compatible with conserved distributions rather than one mobile element [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology].

**The environmental predictor itself is only partly trustworthy.** [[concepts/environment-embedding-geography]] documents that AlphaEarth satellite embeddings covered 83,287 genomes, 28.4% of 293,059; that the embedded subset was 38% human-associated; and that of 83,286 genomes with coordinates only 50,109 (60.2%) were rated Good, with 30,469 (36.6%) in suspicious clusters [src: env_embedding_explorer]. Embeddings behave as ecology should — mean cosine distance rises from 0.41 below 100 km to 0.82 at 10K–20K km [src: env_embedding_explorer] — yet that behaviour does not convert into gene-content signal, which stays near-null genome-wide [src: ecotype_analysis] and is not rescued by restricting the comparison to environmental species [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis].

## Tensions and Caveats

The load-bearing dispute is whether environment matters at all at genome scale, and it fractures into several recorded conflicts. [[conflicts/conflict--ecotype-clustering-validity--c09534c6]] and [[conflicts/conflict--confirmatory-exploratory-ecological-association-discordance--c1d44874]] record that the original analysis places the median partial correlation near zero while the reanalysis places it at 0.081 across 183 species, and that the reanalysis's own quotation of the original figure does not match the original page's number [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis, ecotype_analysis]. The reanalysis attributes the gap to genome sets and downsampling — all embedded genomes, up to 3,505 per species, versus diversity-maximizing downsampling capped at 250 — and preserves only within-method comparisons [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]. Absolute magnitudes are therefore not comparable, and [[conflicts/conflict--environment-embedding-geography--1f216267]] notes this suspends cross-project effect-size comparison in the embedding concept.

What both sides agree on is a null. Environmental species had median partial correlation 0.051 versus 0.084 for human-associated species; the one-sided Environmental > Human-associated test gave U = 1536, p = 0.83 [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]. Continuous fraction analyses also found nothing (rho = -0.085, p = 0.25 for environmental genomes; rho = 0.030, p = 0.69 for human-associated) [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]. This null stays null: clinical sampling bias was confirmed but did not explain the weak relationship [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]. [[conflicts/conflict--ecotype-environment-gene-content--66537c9c]] sharpens the point — environmental samples spread 3.4x across embedding space with distance versus 2.0x for human-associated [src: env_embedding_explorer], yet the steeper gradient yielded no stronger gene-content coupling [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis].

Scope, not biology, may drive the remaining disagreements. [[conflicts/conflict--ecotype-environment-gene-content--744fe541]] and [[conflicts/conflict--ecotype-clustering-validity--f884063a]] note the prophage and metal results use different feature spaces and denominators from the whole-genome test, so a naive reading would mistake feature choice for a biological exception. The prophage result explicitly does not establish discrete ecotypes: individual TerL lineages showed no environment-specific enrichment after FDR correction in 0/500 tests, and module- and lineage-level ecology were described as decoupled [src: prophage_ecology]. [[conflicts/conflict--gene-cooccurrence-ecological-guilds--fdbf2939]] and [[conflicts/conflict--genome-wide-versus-locus-specific-ecological-adaptation--29fec74b]] frame the openness null as a unit-of-analysis problem, not a null about ecological adaptation.

Two readings of weak signal remain unretired in [[conflicts/conflict--genome-wide-versus-locus-specific-ecological-adaptation--3fea06ca]]: locus-specific biology, or poorly resolved environmental variables. Annotation limits cut the same way — approximately 38% of gene clusters had COG annotations, leaving 62% unannotated [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] — and no within-species phylogenetic controls such as core-genome trees were applied [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. Effect sizes can also be sampling artifacts: plant compartment R² fell from a preliminary 0.527 to 0.072 after excluding three genome-rich species per compartment, an 86% loss [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes], a case argued in [[conflicts/conflict--ecotype-environment-gene-content--bea0acfc]] and [[conflicts/conflict--ecotype-environment-gene-content--10a3282f]].
