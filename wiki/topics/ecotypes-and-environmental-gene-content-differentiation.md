# Ecotypes and Environmental Gene-Content Differentiation

An *ecotype* is a population within a named species that is genetically and ecologically distinct — in comparative genomics, usually a cluster of genomes that share accessory gene content and, by hypothesis, a habitat. This corpus attacks the ecotype concept empirically from both ends: it asks whether gene-content clusters exist and are functionally coherent inside species, and separately whether *environment* — as recorded by isolation-source metadata, satellite-derived embeddings, or measured soil chemistry — explains gene content at all once shared ancestry is accounted for. The answers pull apart. Clusters are recurrent and functionally non-random; whole-genome environmental signal is close to nil; and strong environmental structure keeps reappearing, but only inside particular gene subsystems. The corpus is unusual in that the null result and the positive results come from overlapping data, so the disagreement is about scale and feature space rather than about who measured correctly.

## Literature Context

The ecotype concept in microbiology was built on cases where lineage, physiology and habitat align tightly. Marine picocyanobacteria are canonical: *Prochlorococcus* comprises phylogenetically distinct ecotypes differing markedly in photophysiology, allowing growth across light and nutrient gradients within a defined latitudinal belt ([PMID 19487728](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19487728/)). *Synechococcus* clades show the same pattern for temperature — clade II cannot grow below 16 °C, clade I copes below 10 °C, and metagenomic surveys find *ocp* photoprotection genes most prevalent in low-temperature niches and largely lost in tropical clade II ([PMID 34518213](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34518213/)). SAR11 ecotypes partition by water mass and depth in the Arctic Ocean, including an Arctic-restricted subdivision suggesting endemism ([PMID 31501503](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31501503/)), and reverse-ecology analysis of 85 *Deinococcus* genomes defined a cold-adapted clade of eight polar and alpine strains ([PMID 38956546](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38956546/)). Ecotype divergence is also reproducible in the laboratory: *E. coli* populations in the Long-Term Evolution Experiment diverged into stably coexisting ecotypes ([PMID 28509909](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28509909/)), whose fitness landscapes shift with community composition ([PMID 36646697](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36646697/)).

This corpus is consistent with the literature's claim that within-species gene-content clusters exist — clusters were recoverable in 12 of 15 species — but it sharply qualifies the crispness implied by the marine exemplars. Silhouette scores near 0.2 indicate soft boundaries in most taxa, and the significance-versus-effect-size split analysed in [[concepts/adaptive-versus-housekeeping-functional-differentiation]] reframes "ecotype" as a matter of degree rather than of discrete lineages. The published cases are also drawn largely from pelagic and polar systems where one dominant environmental axis (light, temperature, water mass) structures the habitat; across hundreds of species scored against generic environmental descriptors, whole-genome environmental signal essentially disappears relative to phylogeny.

The strongest point of contact — and of tension — concerns the scale at which ecology acts. Oral-microbiome longitudinal metagenomics concluded that genome-wide selection of ecotypes, not gene-level turnover, dominates short-timescale adaptation ([PMID 36214570](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36214570/)). The corpus reaches the opposite emphasis: a genome-wide null alongside strong subsystem-specific signal in prophage modules, soil metal-associated COGs and plant-growth-promoting genes supports the locus-level reading developed in [[concepts/genome-wide-versus-locus-specific-ecological-adaptation]]. That aligns instead with marine work showing recombination-driven divergence concentrated in ecologically diagnostic loci — photosynthesis genes in high-light *Prochlorococcus*, polysaccharide catabolism in *Cellulophaga baltica* ([PMID 29572211](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29572211/)). The corpus does not resolve this; the timescale and habitat differences leave both readings live.

Two corpus results are not addressed by the candidate papers, which is weaker than novelty. The first is the taxonomic-sampling control on plant compartments — an 86% loss of PERMANOVA R² after removing genome-rich species. The nearest candidate, a 383-accession common-garden study attributing rhizosphere composition to switchgrass host genotype ([PMID 35621390](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35621390/)), works on marker-gene community composition rather than genome presence/absence, so an equivalent exclusion test is not directly applicable and its abstract reports no comparable check; the comparison is suggestive, not a demonstrated gap in that literature. The second is that the largest functional effects fall in COG categories S (unknown function) and L rather than in predefined adaptive categories. No candidate paper speaks to this, and the source project itself frames substantial differentiation in unknown-function genes as a recurring theme highlighting annotation gaps — with 62% of clusters unannotated, artefact remains as plausible as discovery.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Gene-content ecotypes are recoverable in most species, but their boundaries are soft.** Applying principal component analysis followed by KMeans clustering, valid gene-content ecotypes were detected in 12 of 15 sampled species (80%), covering 1,820 genomes across 12 species from 6 phyla, averaging 3.7 ecotypes per species with a range of 2–6 [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. Separation was weak: the mean silhouette score — a measure of how much better a genome fits its own cluster than the nearest alternative — was 0.215 and the median 0.174, with *Erwinia amylovora* strongest at 0.468 and *Staphylococcus simulans* weakest at 0.118 [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. KMeans assumes roughly spherical clusters and a pre-chosen cluster count, so the partitions may partly reflect imposed geometry rather than discrete populations; HDBSCAN was unavailable as a comparator [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. [[concepts/ecotype-clustering-validity]] develops this limitation in full.

**Once clusters exist, they differ functionally — and adaptive functions differ more in magnitude than housekeeping ones.** Across 257 chi-square or Fisher's exact tests spanning 12 species and 23 COG categories (Clusters of Orthologous Groups, a coarse functional classification of genes), 170 (66.1%) survived Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction at q < 0.05, and every species with valid clusters had at least one differentiated category [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. Adaptive categories were significant in 67 of 84 tests (79.8%) versus 33 of 48 (68.8%) for housekeeping — only a 1.16x ratio — but their mean effect size was 0.0136 versus 0.0064, a 2.13x ratio, with a one-sided Mann–Whitney U test giving p = 2.53 x 10^-6 [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. Amino-acid metabolism (E) and defense (V) each differentiated in 11/12 species [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. Crucially, the largest mean effects were *not* in the predefined adaptive set: 0.0392 for category S (unknown function) and 0.0337 for L (replication, recombination and repair) [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]. A separate 32-species comparison across 9 phyla and 357,623 genes points the same way at the core-versus-novel level: novel or singleton genes were enriched +10.88% in mobile-element category L and +2.83% in defense V, both with 100% consistency across species [src: cog_analysis]. See [[concepts/adaptive-versus-housekeeping-functional-differentiation]].

**At whole-genome scale, ancestry beats environment — repeatedly and under two different pipelines.** Across 13,381 genomes from 224 species yielding results for 172, the median partial correlation with gene-content similarity was 0.0025 for environment versus 0.0143 for phylogeny; phylogeny dominated in 60.5% of species, a significant positive environmental effect appeared in only 12 species (7.0%), a negative effect in 4 (2.3%), and none in 156 (90.7%) [src: ecotype_analysis]. A systematic genome-level reclassification confirmed rather than rescued the null: environmental species had median partial correlation 0.051 versus 0.084 for human-associated species, with the one-sided Environmental > Human-associated test giving U = 1536, p = 0.83 [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]. Pangenome openness — how much a species' gene repertoire keeps growing as genomes are added — predicted neither driver (Spearman rho = -0.05, p = 0.54 for environment; rho = 0.03, p = 0.73 for phylogeny) [src: pangenome_openness]. [[concepts/genome-wide-versus-locus-specific-ecological-adaptation]] argues that this pattern is best read as ecology acting at loci, not across genomes.

**Environmental structure reappears strongly inside specific subsystems.** Prophage-module composition across 1,773 species showed environment F = 30.04 against phylogeny F = 6.17 (genome size dominant at F = 212.99, p = 0.01 each), with environmental effects significant within *every* genome-size quartile, all p < 6.5e-78 [src: prophage_ecology]. Yet no individual TerL phage lineage showed environment-specific enrichment in 0/500 FDR-corrected tests, so modules and lineages are ecologically decoupled [src: prophage_ecology]. In soil, 51,748 samples across nine metals yielded 2,355 significant COG–metal associations at FDR < 0.05, and a conditional distance-based redundancy analysis (db-RDA — ordination of community dissimilarity on predictors) gave R² = 0.799, p = 0.005 after conditioning on batch and project [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]. Plant-growth-promoting genes show sharp habitat skew: *acdS* at 15.8% prevalence in 1,039 soil/rhizosphere species versus 2.6% elsewhere (OR = 7.02, q = 5.1e-62), *pqqC* 43.8% versus 21.2% (OR = 2.90, q = 2.8e-53), while *nifH* was depleted (12.9% versus 19.7%, OR = 0.60, q = 5.5e-08) [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]. In *Pseudomonas*, carbon-pathway profiles tracked isolation environment among 54 free-living and plant-associated species (999-permutation p = 0.006), but a classifier reached only 0.408 ± 0.169 balanced accuracy against a 0.250 chance baseline [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]. Metal-type diversity predicted niche breadth in phylogenetic generalized least squares (PGLS — regression correcting for shared ancestry) at β = +0.021, SE = 0.0056, p = 1.5x10⁻⁴ [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]. [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]] holds this whole comparison.

**Much apparent ecotype structure is taxonomic sampling in disguise.** In plant compartments, a preliminary 25-marker analysis gave PERMANOVA R² = 0.527, pseudo-F = 235.1, p = 0.001 — which collapsed to R² = 0.072, an 86% loss, after excluding the three genome-rich species per compartment [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]. The refined 17-marker panel gave R² = 0.071 with db-RDA location-only R² = 0.060 (84% of it), 16% attributable to dispersion, and PERMDISP F = 15.6, p = 0.001 [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]. Of 5,671 eggNOG ortholog groups, 5,341 (94.2%) were significant in raw plant-versus-non-plant tests but only 50 survived phylum-level control [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes].

**Co-occurrence marks guilds but not mechanism.** *pqqC* and *acdS* co-occur at OR = 7.24 across n = 286 species, q = 1.2e-83 [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology], yet co-occurring plant-associated genus pairs were slightly *less* metabolically complementary than random pairs (Cohen's d ≈ −0.4, permutation p < 0.001) [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]. Laboratory co-fitness likewise predicted co-occurrence only weakly across 9 organisms (mean delta phi +0.011; across-organism Wilcoxon W = 9, p = 0.13) [src: cofitness_coinheritance]. See [[concepts/gene-cooccurrence-ecological-guilds]].

## Tensions and Caveats

The corpus has no agreed magnitude for the environment–gene-content association. The original analysis reports median partial correlation 0.003 and the reanalysis 0.081, a difference the reanalysis itself characterizes as 27x and attributes to using all embedded genomes — up to 3,505 per species — rather than diversity-maximizing downsampling capped at 250 [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]. These are not commensurable, and must not be averaged: see [[conflicts/conflict--ecotype_analysis--ecotype_env_reanalysis--plant_microbiome_ecotypes--93da56a7]] and [[conflicts/conflict--ecotype_analysis--ecotype_env_reanalysis--env_embedding_explorer--ffd6c359]].

Within a single dataset, environment *dominated* in 39.5% of species while being statistically significant in only 7.0% positive and 2.3% negative — a gap the source flags but does not resolve [src: ecotype_analysis]; see [[conflicts/conflict--ecotype_analysis--ecotype_env_reanalysis--ecotype_functional_differentiation--57f3758f]]. Whether the subsystem signals are genuine exceptions or structured confounding is unsettled — [[conflicts/conflict--ecotype_env_reanalysis--env_embedding_explorer--harvard_forest_warming--8cb3e20e]].

The environmental variable is itself shaky. AlphaEarth embeddings covered 83,287 genomes, 28.4% of 293,059 [src: env_embedding_explorer]; of 83,286 genomes with coordinates, 50,109 (60.2%) were Good, 30,469 (36.6%) suspicious clusters, 2,708 (3.3%) low precision [src: env_embedding_explorer]; per-genome NCBI environment labels were 52.7% unknown (94,957 of 180,025) [src: pitfalls]. Environmental samples show a 3.4x geographic embedding gradient versus 2.0x for human-associated, yet carry *no* stronger gene-content correlation [src: env_embedding_explorer, ecotype_env_reanalysis].

Finally, the ecotype analysis applied no within-species phylogenetic control such as a core-genome tree, and only ~38% of gene clusters carried COG annotations, leaving 62% unannotated [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] — so the functional result is an association, not demonstrated ecological adaptation. See [[conflicts/conflict--bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance--ecotype_analysis--ecotype_functional_differentiation--d8143c37]] and [[conflicts/conflict--ecotype_analysis--ecotype_env_reanalysis--ecotype_functional_differentiation--0a908b43]].
