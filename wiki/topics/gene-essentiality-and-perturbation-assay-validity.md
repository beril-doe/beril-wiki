# Gene Essentiality and Perturbation-Assay Validity

"Essential" is the most load-bearing word in bacterial functional genomics: it decides which genes get annotated as indispensable, which become drug targets, and which are filtered out of screens because no mutant can be made. This corpus is unusual in that it can interrogate the word directly. Several projects place two or more independent perturbation assays on the same genome — random barcode transposon sequencing (RB-TnSeq, which scores each gene by the abundance of its insertion mutants in a pooled competition), complete-gene knockout collections, constraint-based flux balance analysis (FBA, which predicts feasible steady-state fluxes from a stoichiometric model), proteomics, and pangenome conservation — while other projects ask, at the scale of tens of thousands of genes across dozens of bacteria, which genes ever acquire a usable phenotype at all. The result is a coherent argument that essentiality is an *assay outcome under a condition*, and that most of the corpus's apparent biological variation in essentiality is entangled with what could be measured.

## Literature Context

Published work on bacterial essentiality has largely converged on two moves that this corpus inherits and then interrogates. The first is condition contrast: in *Methylobacterium extorquens* PA1, hyper-saturated transposon sequencing against two carbon sources separated 147 genes required only for growth on methanol and 76 only on succinate from 590 required under both, with about 500,000 insertions mapped per condition and a median insertion distance of five base pairs ([PMID 28823675](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28823675/)). The second is cross-validation of a metabolic model against a transposon screen: a curated flux balance analysis model of *Bordetella pertussis*, benchmarked against a TraDIS genome-wide essentiality screen, predicted essentiality with an accuracy of 83% ([PMID 28715411](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28715411/)). Both papers treat the transposon screen as the reference standard against which models are scored.

This corpus is consistent with the condition-dependence result and pushes it further. The methanol-versus-succinate contrast reassigns essentiality with a single change of substrate; the corpus's finding that FBA agreement with complete knockouts shifts between rich and minimal media, and that condition-panel depth determines how many genes ever show a strong effect, is the same phenomenon measured across many more organisms ([[concepts/gene-essentiality]], [[concepts/fitness-condition-coverage-prioritization-bias]]). The phosphoribulokinase result is a particularly apt precedent for the corpus's argument, because a gene from an absent pathway scored essential for regulatory rather than catalytic reasons ([PMID 28823675](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28823675/)) — an essentiality call whose meaning is not recoverable from the annotation. On the model side, the corpus's closest comparable figure falls below the published one rather than contradicting it: 73.8% concordance between FBA and TnSeq in *Acinetobacter baylyi* ADP1 sits under the 83% essentiality accuracy reported for *B. pertussis* ([PMID 28715411](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28715411/)). The corpus's other contested FBA number — 42.5% with 330 false positives — is not comparable to it at all, being the precision of baseline draft-model predictions of carbon-source growth rather than of gene essentiality, and paired in its source with a much higher recall of 86.5%.

Where the corpus goes beyond these papers is in declining to accept the reference standard. Both candidate studies score a model against one transposon screen; neither places two independent perturbation assays on the same genome. The ADP1 comparison of RB-TnSeq calls against a complete-knockout collection, yielding negative Cohen's kappa at every tested threshold, has no counterpart in this literature and undercuts the premise that a published "accuracy against TnSeq" figure measures accuracy against essentiality ([[concepts/essentiality-assay-discordance]]). Relatedly, the near-saturating insertion density reported for the PA1 screen ([PMID 28823675](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28823675/)) is precisely the regime in which the corpus's callability concern would be least severe, which sharpens rather than dissolves its treatment of an 18.6% essential fraction across 221,005 genes as an upper bound confounded by gene length and AT content ([[concepts/transposon-callability-bias]]). That reframing of essentiality as an assay outcome under a condition, rather than a definable genomic set, is the corpus's genuinely novel position relative to these candidates.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Two "essentiality" assays on one genome disagree worse than chance.** In *Acinetobacter baylyi* ADP1, comparing RB-TnSeq essentiality calls with complete-knockout calls across 1,933 genes in rich medium gave only 18 genes (0.9%) called essential by both and 1,411 genes (73.0%) dispensable by both, with two large discordant blocks: 211 genes (10.9%) knockout-essential but RB-TnSeq-dispensable, and 293 genes (15.2%) the reverse [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]. Cohen's kappa — agreement between two binary classifiers corrected for chance, where 0 is chance — was negative at every tested threshold (0.01, 0.025, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20 → −0.139, −0.122, −0.081, −0.024, −0.014), so the discordance is systematic rather than an artifact of one arbitrary cutoff, although the thresholds were not formally corrected for multiple testing [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]. The proposed mechanisms — insertion mutants retaining partial function, read-through transcription, retained domains, polar effects — remain hypotheses in that analysis, not demonstrations [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]. See [[concepts/essentiality-assay-discordance]].

**The information is in the continuous measurement, not the label.** In the same organism, inverted continuous RB-TnSeq fitness ranked knockout-essential genes with AUC (area under the ROC curve; 0.5 is chance) of 0.700 in rich and 0.725 in minimal medium, while the thresholded essentiality fraction gave 0.344 and 0.403 [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]. FBA behaves the same way: against complete knockouts at the lethality boundary it reached F1 = 0.624, kappa = 0.486 over 724 genes in rich media and F1 = 0.673, kappa = 0.493 over 833 genes in minimal media, yet among 478 RB-TnSeq-dispensable genes the FBA class carried no information about quantitative growth defect (χ² = 0.93, p = 0.63; Kruskal–Wallis H = 1.67, p = 0.43) [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]. Changing the prediction target — lethality versus graded impairment — changes every method's apparent accuracy [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]. This is elaborated on [[concepts/gene-essentiality]].

**Conservation is a real but weak correlate, and cannot substitute for perturbation.** Across approximately 194,000 genes from 43 bacteria, essential genes were 82% core (present in essentially all genomes of a species pangenome) versus 66% for always-neutral genes, with fitness breadth associated with core status only weakly (Spearman rho = 0.086, p = 8.1e-230) [src: fitness_effects_conservation]. A 33-organism linkage found 86.1% core for essential versus 81.2% for non-essential genes, median odds ratio 1.56, with 18 of 33 organisms significant after Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction [src: conservation_vs_fitness]. Conservation also fails in the other direction: 3,683 essential-auxiliary genes exist, 38.2% of them hypothetical [src: conservation_vs_fitness], and 24.4% of core genes were beneficial when deleted versus 19.9% of accessory genes [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis, fitness_effects_conservation], with 25,271 genes (17.8%) showing outright trade-offs — fitness below −1 in some conditions and above 1 in others [src: core_gene_tradeoffs].

**Absent data is read as essentiality, and absent data has a shape.** Across 221,005 genes from 48 bacteria, 41,059 (18.6%) were classified essential, but that figure is an upper bound: genes lacking insertions may lack them because they are short, AT-rich, or scaffold-adjacent [src: essential_genome]. Essential genes had median length 675 bp versus 885 bp for non-essential, and 17.8% were under 300 bp — consistent with, though not proof of, length-driven false calls [src: essential_genome]. Organism-level rates spanning 12.2% in Pedo557 to 29.7% in Magneto therefore mix biology with organism-specific callability [src: essential_genome]. [[concepts/transposon-callability-bias]] develops this.

**The genes that enter a collection are not a random sample of the genome.** In the ADP1 deletion collection, 272 of 2,593 RB-TnSeq-dispensable genes (10.5%) had no growth data; the missing genes averaged 813 bp versus 981 bp, were RAST-annotated at 91% versus 100% and KO-annotated at 49% versus 59%, and were core at 76.5% versus 93.3% (p = 1.4×10⁻²⁰) [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes]. The matrix also excludes 499 essential genes and 316 with incomplete data outright [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes]. Condition coverage biases the complementary direction: across 48 organisms, 57,011 of 228,709 genes were dark and 7,787 showed strong effects (|fitness| ≥ 2 in ≥ 1 condition) — a criterion whose satisfaction depends on how many conditions were run, with MR-1 alone carrying 121 historical conditions and contributing 25 of the top 100 candidates [src: functional_dark_matter]. See [[concepts/genetic-perturbation-coverage-bias]] and [[concepts/fitness-condition-coverage-prioritization-bias]].

**The same logic generalizes past genes to compounds.** The ENIGMA carbon census funnel ran 83 compounds → 83 structure-resolved → 54 KEGG-linked → 9 callable → 74 organism-dark (89%), with similar dark fractions for groundwater (53/59, 90%) and necromass (21/24, 88%) [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]. "Organism-dark" marks a resource boundary, not biological absence [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] — the same distinction that separates a non-callable gene from a truly indispensable one ([[concepts/callability-limited-comparative-inference]]).

## Tensions and Caveats

The corpus carries **two incompatible headline core fractions** for essential genes — 82% across ~194,000 genes from 43 bacteria and 86.1% among 148,826 genes from 33 organisms — arising from different datasets, organism filters, and denominators; they must not be averaged [src: fitness_effects_conservation, conservation_vs_fitness]. See [[conflicts/conflict--acinetobacter_adp1_explorer--adp1_triple_essentiality--alphafold_msa_annotation--cc872022]].

The **core denominator itself is unstable**: clades with only 2 genomes give trivially high core fractions, the main *Escherichia coli* clade was excluded for holding too many genomes, and the Keio strain mapped to a small clade at 26.1% coverage [src: conservation_vs_fitness].

Within ADP1, the graded and binary results are **not a contradiction but a target mismatch**: continuous fitness predicts knockout lethality usefully while the thresholded call falls below chance, and the source insists these be kept separate rather than one assay declared definitive [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] ([[conflicts/conflict--adp1_triple_essentiality--conservation_fitness_synthesis--conservation_vs_fitness--3d504d86]]).

**Singleton near-neutrality may be a detection failure**, since poor transposon coverage can mimic true neutrality [src: fitness_effects_conservation], and the ADP1 report explicitly declines to say whether its missing-coverage pattern reflects conservation, mutant-construction difficulty, or misclassified fragments [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes].

**Deep condition coverage is both an asset and a bias**: ten organism–condition experiments address 242 of the top 500 dark genes (45.3%) [src: functional_dark_matter], while in *Desulfovibrio vulgaris* Hildenborough across 757 experiments (337 field, 420 lab) fitness-based predictors of core status were weak (cross-validated AUC 0.517 field-only, 0.531 lab-only, 0.548 combined, rising to 0.645 with gene length) [src: field_vs_lab_fitness].

Finally, **FBA's reported accuracy is itself contested** — 73.8% concordance with TnSeq over 866 ADP1 genes [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] against 42.5% baseline accuracy with 330 false positives elsewhere [src: annotation_gap_discovery].
