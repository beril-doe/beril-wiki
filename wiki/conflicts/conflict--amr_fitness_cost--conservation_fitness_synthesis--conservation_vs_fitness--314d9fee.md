# Core-genome conservation versus measured fitness: one gradient, four non-interchangeable readings

Across this corpus, "how conserved a gene is" and "how much its loss costs" are repeatedly compared — and the comparison comes out differently depending on the gene set, the fitness measure, and the clade sampling used. Three concept pages carry overlapping tensions about it: [[concepts/pangenome-conservation-fitness-decoupling]], [[concepts/core-genome-burden-paradox]], and [[concepts/pangenome-core-boundary-and-clade-size-bias]]. This page collects the four distinct disagreements they record: (1) within the antimicrobial-resistance (AMR) analysis, mechanism predicted conservation status but not baseline fitness cost; (2) the AMR subset shows no core-versus-accessory fitness difference while genome-wide analyses show a positive conservation gradient — at effect sizes that differ between cohorts; (3) conserved genes are nonetheless sometimes *burdensome* in the laboratory, with a functional-category reversal inside that result; and (4) the core boundary that all of these claims rest on is itself shaped by clade sampling and by which genes are observable at all. Two of these are explicitly *not* logical contradictions in the input: the mechanism pairing is "not a statistical contradiction" because conservation status and knockout fitness are different response variables [src: amr_fitness_cost], and the Cell Wall reversal is "not a contradiction of the genome-wide result" [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]. What is genuinely at stake is scope and interchangeability: if these readings are not kept apart, a distribution pattern across genomes gets silently reused as evidence about fitness, or a laboratory fitness cost gets read as evidence about evolutionary retention.

Jargon used below: *core* genes are present in (essentially) all genomes of a sampled species clade, *accessory* genes are not; *essential* genes are those whose disruption is not tolerated — the operational definitions differ between the analyses collected here, and that difference is itself part of disagreement 4, so no single definition is asserted on this page; an *odds ratio* (OR) is the multiplicative change in odds of core membership; *ICA modules* are independent-component-analysis-derived groups of genes with coordinated fitness behaviour; *pangenome openness* is a species-level summary of how much new gene content each added genome contributes; *MWU* is the Mann–Whitney U rank test.

## Evidence Sides

**Side A — Conservation tracks fitness importance, genome-wide and modestly.** A genome-wide analysis found a positive, though weak, conservation gradient from essential or broadly fitness-affecting genes to always-neutral genes, 82% versus 66% core. [src: amr_fitness_cost, fitness_effects_conservation] A 33-organism linkage analysis reproduces the direction with a smaller effect: 86.1% of essential genes classified as core versus 81.2% of non-essential genes, median odds ratio 1.56. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] A module-level analysis is directionally consistent, with module genes 86.0% core versus 81.5% of all genes, i.e. enrichment of conserved, coordinated fitness units. [src: module_conservation] A synthesis likewise supports a modest genome-wide gradient. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

**Side B — For the AMR subset and the baseline measure, conservation and cost are decoupled.** The AMR analysis found virtually identical baseline fitness distributions for core and accessory AMR genes: mean fitness −0.024 for both, p = 0.33. [src: amr_fitness_cost, fitness_effects_conservation] Within the same analysis, mechanism strongly predicted conservation status yet mechanism did not predict baseline fitness cost. [src: amr_fitness_cost] That pairing is not a statistical contradiction — conservation status and knockout fitness are different response variables — but it means a mechanism-associated distribution pattern cannot be treated as evidence for a mechanism-associated fitness burden. [src: amr_fitness_cost] The AMR result supports decoupling for the analyzed resistance subset and baseline measure, while the genome-wide result supports an association with fitness importance when effect breadth and conditional phenotypes are included. [src: amr_fitness_cost, fitness_effects_conservation] The 33-organism integration presents the same directional association with a smaller effect rather than resolving the tension. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] The module result does not resolve the AMR-specific null either, because ICA modules exclude essential genes and summarize module membership rather than AMR baseline means. [src: module_conservation]

**Side C — Conserved genes can be *more* costly, not less.** The synthesis shows that core genes can be more burdensome in the laboratory, so conservation, essentiality, and laboratory cost should not be treated as interchangeable. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] The central form of this is a tension between laboratory burden and evolutionary retention: deletion can improve growth under tested conditions while the gene remains broadly conserved across genomes. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] Inside this side there is a further reversal: core genes were more burdensome in several functional categories, whereas non-core Cell Wall genes were more burdensome. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] That reversal is not a contradiction of the genome-wide result, but it means the paradox cannot be treated as a universal functional rule and must be tested at category and condition resolution. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]

**Side D — The core boundary and the observable gene universe are analysis-dependent.** Clades containing only 2 genomes can produce trivially high core fractions, and the main *Escherichia coli* clade was unavailable because it contained too many genomes. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] The 86.1% versus 81.2% fractions are not directly comparable to the 82% versus 66% fractions from the 43-organism synthesis and independent analysis, because the cohorts and classifications differ. [src: conservation_vs_fitness, conservation_fitness_synthesis, fitness_effects_conservation] These values should not be reconciled silently: they arise from different organism sets, phenotype categories, and integration procedures, so the difference is itself evidence that the estimated conservation boundary is analysis-dependent. [src: conservation_vs_fitness, fitness_effects_conservation] The module analysis adds an observability limit: zero essential genes appeared in modules, because essential genes lack usable transposon-insertion fitness data, so module-level enrichment cannot be compared directly with essential-versus-non-essential core fractions as if they represented the same observable gene universe. [src: module_conservation] Separately, the openness analysis does not contradict the within-clade findings but **refines** their scope: its null correlations concern species-level environment and phylogeny effect sizes, whereas the conservation analyses compare gene categories within sampled clades. [src: pangenome_openness, conservation_vs_fitness, fitness_effects_conservation] Whether openness fails to predict ecological dominance because the metric is too coarse, because relevant adaptation is concentrated in particular functional categories, or because of limited power remains unresolved. [src: pangenome_openness]

## Possible Reconciliations

These are hypotheses, not findings. Except where a bullet restates a cited claim from the input, the reasoning below is this page's own and carries no source attribution.

- **Different response variables (Side A vs Side B).** The input states that conservation status and knockout fitness are formally different outcomes, so a mechanism→conservation association and a mechanism→cost null can both hold. [src: amr_fitness_cost] *Unattributed extension:* the genome-wide gradient may be carried by breadth of fitness effect and conditional phenotypes, which a baseline mean does not encode — the input does note that the genome-wide side includes effect breadth and conditional phenotypes. [src: amr_fitness_cost, fitness_effects_conservation]
- **Scope/subset hypothesis.** *Unattributed reasoning.* The AMR null may be specific to resistance biology, or to the analyzed subset, rather than contradicting a genome-wide trend. [[concepts/pangenome-conservation-fitness-decoupling]] lists candidate causes for the discrepancy — AMR biology, averaging, organism composition, essentiality definitions, module-callability, or pangenome sampling — as editorial reasoning without a source tag, and they are repeated here on the same footing.
- **Averaging hypothesis (Side C).** *Unattributed reasoning.* A per-gene mean over conditions can hide opposite-signed condition-specific effects, so an aggregate core-burden excess and a category-level reversal such as Cell Wall could be two views of the same condition-heterogeneous data.
- **Unmeasured-environment hypothesis (Side C).** The available evidence does not resolve the burden-versus-retention tension by directly measuring natural-environment fitness; it supports the hypothesis that unmeasured environments, ecological interactions, or long-term selection maintain at least some costly core genes. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]
- **Definitional/cohort hypothesis (Side D).** The input states the 86.1%/81.2% and 82%/66% contrasts arise from different organism sets, phenotype categories, and integration procedures. [src: conservation_vs_fitness, conservation_fitness_synthesis, fitness_effects_conservation] *Unattributed extension:* they may therefore be estimates of different quantities rather than conflicting estimates of one quantity.
- **Observability hypothesis (Side D).** Modules contained zero essential genes because essential genes lack usable transposon-insertion fitness data, so the two enrichments are computed over non-overlapping gene universes. [src: module_conservation] *Unattributed extension:* undersampled 2-genome clades may inflate core fractions, and excluding the overlarge main *E. coli* clade may remove exactly the deeply sampled case that would discipline the estimate — the sampling facts themselves are reported. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]

## Resolving Work

*Disagreement 1 — mechanism predicts conservation but not baseline cost.*
- Re-fit mechanism→conservation and mechanism→fitness models on the identical AMR gene set and organism set, reporting both response variables side by side, to test whether the contrast survives matched inputs. [src: amr_fitness_cost]
- Replace the baseline mean with breadth-of-effect and conditional-phenotype summaries as the fitness response, since the genome-wide gradient is defined using effect breadth and conditional phenotypes. [src: amr_fitness_cost, fitness_effects_conservation]
- Stratify the mechanism analysis by mechanism class before pooling, and report the per-class test statistic explicitly, so that a mechanism-specific cost is not averaged away into a pooled null.
- Report the mechanism→cost test and the core-versus-accessory test (mean fitness −0.024 for both, p = 0.33) as separate, separately labelled results, so their statistics are not transferred between claims. [src: amr_fitness_cost, fitness_effects_conservation]

*Disagreement 2 — AMR null versus the genome-wide gradient, and the differing effect sizes.*
- Run matched, condition-specific comparisons: the same conditions, the same conservation caller, and the same essentiality definition applied to both the AMR subset and the genome-wide set. [src: amr_fitness_cost, conservation_vs_fitness]
- Recompute the 82%/66% and 86.1%/81.2% contrasts on the intersection of the 43-organism and 33-organism cohorts, reporting whether the odds ratio stays near the median 1.56, to separate cohort composition from biology. [src: conservation_vs_fitness, conservation_fitness_synthesis, fitness_effects_conservation]
- Hold the phenotype classification fixed while varying only the organism set (and vice versa), to attribute the effect-size gap to classification versus cohort. [src: conservation_vs_fitness, fitness_effects_conservation]
- Repeat the module contrast (86.0% versus 81.5%) with module membership recomputed under an explicit, stated gene universe, so it can be placed on the same axis as essential-versus-non-essential fractions. [src: module_conservation]

*Disagreement 3 — laboratory burden versus evolutionary retention, and the Cell Wall reversal.*
- Test the core-burden claim at category and condition resolution rather than genome-wide, since the reversal appeared specifically for non-core Cell Wall genes. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]
- Identify the specific costly-but-conserved core genes and test them in conditions closer to the organisms' native settings, since the current evidence does not directly measure natural-environment fitness. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]
- Report conservation, essentiality, and laboratory cost as three separate labelled variables per gene, and quantify their pairwise agreement, rather than using them interchangeably. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

*Disagreement 4 — is the core boundary biology or sampling?*
- Recompute core fractions with a minimum-genomes-per-clade threshold and report sensitivity, given that 2-genome clades can produce trivially high core fractions. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]
- Add a tractable subsampling of the excluded main *Escherichia coli* clade (e.g. random genome subsets) to recover the deeply sampled regime that was dropped for containing too many genomes. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]
- Publish, per analysis, the operational essentiality definition used and its stated caveats, then recompute the essential-versus-non-essential core fractions under each definition in turn, since differing essentiality definitions are a candidate cause of the cross-cohort gap.
- Quantify the essential-gene observability gap directly — how many essential genes are unavailable to transposon-insertion fitness data — and propagate it as a stated exclusion whenever module and essentiality enrichments are compared. [src: module_conservation]
- Test whether openness-based nulls change when the metric is computed per functional category rather than per species, to distinguish a too-coarse metric, category-concentrated adaptation, and limited power. [src: pangenome_openness]
