# Essentiality by transposon, by knockout, and by conservation: which assay defines the word?

This page records a cluster of linked disputes carried on [[concepts/essentiality-assay-discordance]] about what counts as evidence that a gene is essential. They matter because every downstream use of the corpus — annotating essential genes, inferring purifying selection, filtering targets — inherits whichever definition is adopted. No disagreement in the source tension text is left out here, but they are not all disagreements of the same kind, and the input is explicit about which is which: a genuine metric-level clash over whether transposon fitness data track complete-knockout lethality well or badly; a **qualification, not a contradiction**, raised within the conservation evidence about whether apparent fitness neutrality is a biological fact or a measurement gap; a **refinement** of how much inferential weight laboratory-measured cost plus conservation can bear as evidence of natural essentiality, where the cited positions agree that the categories are signatures rather than measurements; and a non-comparability between the corpus's two conservation-versus-essentiality analyses. None is settled here; each block reproduces its own project's figures as the tension text gives them, and no block asserts more opposition than the input states. Jargon used below: **RB-TnSeq** (random barcode transposon sequencing) measures the fitness of insertion mutants; **AUC** (area under the ROC curve) summarizes how well a continuous score ranks positives above negatives, with chance-level ranking at the midpoint of the scale; **Cohen's kappa** measures agreement between two binary classifiers corrected for chance, where zero is chance and negative values indicate agreement worse than chance; **core** genes are those present across a species pangenome, **singletons** those found in a single genome.

## Evidence Sides

**Disagreement 1, Side A — continuous transposon fitness does carry information about knockout lethality.** Continuous transposon fitness contains useful information about knockout lethality, supported by AUC values of 0.700 and 0.725 for inverted fitness. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] On this reading the transposon assay is a usable, if imperfect, graded predictor, and the right output of an RB-TnSeq experiment is a score, not a label.

**Disagreement 1, Side B — thresholded transposon essentiality does not agree with knockout calls.** Thresholded RB-TnSeq essentiality shows poor agreement with complete-knockout calls, supported by the 0.05-threshold AUC of 0.344 for essentiality fraction and Cohen's kappa of -0.081. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] Both values fall below chance — an AUC beneath the chance midpoint and a kappa below zero — so on this reading a binary RB-TnSeq essentiality call is not merely noisy under these conditions. The source project itself states that the two conclusions must be kept separate, and that the apparent tension is resolved by distinguishing graded fitness prediction from binary assay concordance rather than treating either assay as a universal definition of essentiality. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]

**Disagreement 2 — a qualification of the graded reading, explicitly not a contradiction of it.** The conservation evidence **supports** the graded-fitness conclusion but introduces a qualification rather than a contradiction: the conservation gradient was statistically robust yet weak, and singleton neutrality may reflect poor transposon coverage rather than true neutrality. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] The consequence is that conservation cannot independently resolve whether an apparently neutral insertion mutant retains function or simply lacks reliable fitness measurement. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] This is a limit stated within one project's own evidence, and it blocks the obvious move of using pangenome status as a tiebreaker between transposon and knockout calls.

**Disagreement 3 — how much weight laboratory cost plus conservation can bear (a refinement, with both cited positions agreeing on the category's status).** The core-gene trade-off evidence **refines** the interpretation of costly conserved genes but does not directly establish natural selection: its Costly + Conserved category is inferred from laboratory burden and conservation, whereas the assay-discordance evidence shows that laboratory measurements can depend on perturbation modality and condition. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] Costly conservation should accordingly be treated as a selection hypothesis requiring ecological validation, not as direct evidence that a gene is essential in nature. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] The synthesis **supports** this tension rather than opposing it: it identifies 28,017 genes as both costly and conserved and 5,526 as costly and dispensable, but explicitly treats these categories as selection signatures rather than direct natural-fitness measurements. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] The live question is therefore not whether the categories are signatures — both cited positions say they are — but how much inferential weight a lab-derived burden measurement can carry toward a claim about nature.

**Disagreement 4, Side A — a higher core fraction for essential genes than for always-neutral genes across a 43-bacteria set.** One analysis reports 82% core for essential genes versus 66% for always-neutral genes across 43 bacteria. [src: fitness_effects_conservation, conservation_vs_fitness]

**Disagreement 4, Side B — a narrower separation between essential and non-essential genes in a separate multi-organism linkage.** A separate 33-organism linkage reports 86.1% versus 81.2% for essential versus non-essential genes. [src: fitness_effects_conservation, conservation_vs_fitness] These results are directionally consistent but differ in category definitions and organism sets; neither resolves the poor binary agreement between insertion and complete-knockout assays. [src: fitness_effects_conservation, conservation_vs_fitness] The two should therefore not be conflated, and neither pair of percentages can stand in for the other as "the" conservation enrichment of essential genes.

## Possible Reconciliations

Each of the following is a **hypothesis**, not a finding in the corpus.

- *Metric-level, not data-level, disagreement (D1).* Both sides of Disagreement 1 may be right if the ranking information in transposon fitness is degraded by thresholding — a graded score with AUC 0.700 and 0.725 can still yield a near-chance or worse-than-chance binary call once a cut is imposed. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] This is the reconciliation the source project itself advances, by separating graded prediction from binary concordance. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]
- *Different phenotypes, same gene (D1).* Insertion mutants and complete knockouts may differ systematically — partial function, polar effects, or condition dependence — so that both assays are internally valid while measuring non-identical things; the corpus states only that laboratory measurements can depend on perturbation modality and condition. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]
- *Coverage, not biology (D2).* If genes with sparse transposon coverage are disproportionately singletons, then "always neutral" is partly a detection category; the possibility that singleton neutrality reflects poor transposon coverage is explicitly raised. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] Under this hypothesis, the robust-yet-weak gradient is an attenuated version of a stronger underlying one.
- *Scope of the claim (D3).* The two positions are compatible if "selection signature" is read as an ecological hypothesis rather than an essentiality claim; both cited sources describe the categories as inferred rather than directly measured. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis, core_gene_tradeoffs] What remains open on this reading is only the ecological validation the trade-off project asks for. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]
- *Category and cohort definitions (D4).* The two conservation contrasts may be measuring different quantities: "always-neutral" is a stricter comparison class than "non-essential," and the 43-bacteria cohort differs from the 33-organism cohort, so the difference between the contrasts could follow from reference class and organism set rather than from conflicting estimates of one underlying quantity. [src: fitness_effects_conservation, conservation_vs_fitness]

## Resolving Work

**Disagreement 1 — graded versus binary transposon essentiality**
- Re-run the transposon-versus-knockout comparison with the continuous fitness score as the only input and report AUC together with calibration, then ask what decision threshold, if any, yields a positive Cohen's kappa against knockout calls; the tension text reports kappa of -0.081 at the 0.05 threshold. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]
- Stratify the discordant genes by insertion density and gene length and test whether the worse-than-chance 0.344 AUC for essentiality fraction concentrates in low-coverage genes. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]
- Repeat the concordance analysis in a second organism with both transposon libraries and complete-knockout collections, to test whether negative kappa is organism-specific or general. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]
- Construct targeted complete knockouts for a sample of genes called dispensable by transposon fitness but essential by knockout, and score viability under the matched medium.

**Disagreement 2 — is neutrality biological or a coverage artifact?**
- Compute per-gene transposon insertion coverage for all singleton genes and test whether mean fitness approaches zero as coverage falls, which would support the coverage-artifact hypothesis the project raises. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- Restrict the conservation gradient analysis to genes above a coverage floor and report whether the gradient strengthens; a gradient that is robust yet weak should sharpen if attenuation is the cause. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- Assay a panel of singleton genes with a non-transposon perturbation (targeted deletion or CRISPRi knockdown) so that neutrality is tested by a modality with no insertion-site bias.
- Quantify how many genes change essentiality class when the coverage floor is imposed, to bound how far this qualification can move the graded-fitness conclusion it otherwise supports. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

**Disagreement 3 — how far a selection signature licenses a natural-essentiality claim**
- Test the 28,017 costly-and-conserved genes for evidence of purifying selection using sequence-based dN/dS across the pangenome, an axis independent of laboratory burden. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]
- Measure fitness of the same gene set in environment-mimicking conditions (soil extract, host-associated, biofilm) and ask whether lab-measured burden reverses — the prediction implied by the ecological-validation requirement. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]
- Compare the 5,526 costly-and-dispensable genes against mobile-element and recent-acquisition annotations to test the ongoing-gene-loss reading of that category. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]
- Check whether membership in the Costly + Conserved category is stable across perturbation modality by recomputing burden from a non-transposon fitness assay, since modality dependence is the stated reason the category cannot stand as direct evidence. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]

**Disagreement 4 — reconciling the two conservation contrasts**
- Recompute both contrasts on the intersection of the 43-bacteria and 33-organism sets with a single harmonized essentiality definition, and report whether 82% versus 66% and 86.1% versus 81.2% converge. [src: fitness_effects_conservation, conservation_vs_fitness]
- Recompute the 43-bacteria contrast using "non-essential" rather than "always neutral" as the comparison class, to quantify how much of the difference between the analyses is definitional. [src: fitness_effects_conservation, conservation_vs_fitness]
- Report per-organism effect sizes for both analyses side by side, so the aggregate percentages can be checked against organism composition rather than compared as single numbers. [src: fitness_effects_conservation, conservation_vs_fitness]
- Test whether either conservation contrast predicts complete-knockout lethality directly, since neither currently resolves the poor binary agreement between insertion and complete-knockout assays. [src: fitness_effects_conservation, conservation_vs_fitness]
