# Prevalence versus activity: what does 0.90 field prevalence license?

The corpus contains one genuine disagreement about what environmental detection is allowed to conclude, and it sits inside a single project before it constrains the others. The ENIGMA carbon census reports 3-hydroxybenzoic-acid utilizers at 0.90 field prevalence at genus resolution — a number large enough to invite ecological reading — while the same project explicitly classifies its environmental atlas as an abundance or occurrence proxy rather than evidence of compound degradation or activity. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The stakes are practical: if prevalence licensed catabolic inference, site selection for enrichment could be reported as a finished answer about *in situ* function (*in situ* = in the environment itself, rather than in laboratory culture); if it does not, prevalence is a shortlist and every compound-specific claim still owes an experiment. Four further projects appear in the source tension text — [[concepts/occurrence-versus-catabolic-activity]] states in each case that the project supports, refines, or at minimum "does not contradict" the boundary — so none of them is an opposing camp. They are recorded below as corroborating and refining evidence on the boundary side, and as scope conditions in the resolving work; this page therefore treats one disagreement and leaves no second disagreement out, because the input asserts none.

## Evidence Sides

**Side A — prevalence carries usable inferential weight, bounded to enrichment accessibility.** A high environmental prevalence can support the practical hypothesis that a taxon is accessible for enrichment. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The census found 3-hydroxybenzoic-acid utilizers at 0.90 field prevalence at genus resolution. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Note what this side does *not* claim: the accessible-for-enrichment reading is explicitly a practical hypothesis about where to obtain organisms, not a statement about who degrades the compound in the field. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

**Side B — occurrence, and genomic potential generally, is not activity.** A high prevalence cannot establish that the taxon catabolizes a particular compound in situ, and the census classifies its environmental atlas as an abundance or occurrence proxy rather than evidence of compound degradation or activity. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Four projects corroborate this side without opposing Side A. The *Pseudomonas* environment-associated carbon profiles are pathway predictions from genomes, not environmental activity measurements; capability profiles may provide a useful intermediate ecological signal, but modest classifier performance and strong subgenus separation limit claims about compound-specific environmental catabolism. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] The essential-metabolome result supports rather than contradicts the boundary: near-complete amino-acid pathway predictions across 7 organisms do not establish biosynthetic activity, and the apparent *D. vulgaris* serine auxotrophy — an inferred inability to synthesize serine — remains unresolved without growth testing; its limited 7-organism pilot and missing *E. coli* coverage in GapMind (the computational tool used to call pathway completeness from genome sequence) caution against treating computational completeness as a universal or experimentally validated property. [src: essential_metabolome] The metabolic-capability study refines the boundary: its latent-capability result was obtained from laboratory fitness conditions and its ecotype associations were observational; all 10 target species formed metabolic clusters, but environment-cluster associations were significant only for *Salmonella enterica* and *Phenylobacterium* sp., with no explicit phylogenetic correction (a statistical adjustment for the fact that related organisms are not independent samples) — showing that genomic capability can carry ecological information while remaining insufficient to establish environmental catabolism. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] At community scale, negative associations between community pathway completeness and ambient amino-acid intensity are compatible with community metabolic interdependence but do not directly demonstrate catabolic uptake, pathway expression, or carbon flux. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

## Possible Reconciliations

These are hypotheses, not findings.

- **Two-claim hypothesis (the resolution the source page itself proposes).** The apparent tension is resolved by treating occurrence as site-selection evidence and catabolism as a separate claim requiring compound-resolved experiments or activity measurements. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Both census statements are then true because they answer different questions, and 0.90 is a strong number about *where to look*.
- **Graded-inference hypothesis.** Genomic capability may carry real ecological information while remaining insufficient to establish environmental catabolism. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] If so, prevalence and capability profiles sit on an evidence ladder below activity rather than in opposition to it, with the *Pseudomonas* intermediate-signal result the same ladder seen from the genome side. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- **Resolution-mismatch hypothesis.** The prevalence figure is at genus resolution while catabolic phenotypes may be strain-variable; subgenus structure dominating the *Pseudomonas* signal is consistent with this. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1, pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]
- **Condition-scope hypothesis.** Laboratory fitness conditions may not include the conditions under which an environmentally prevalent capability is expressed, making laboratory silence and field prevalence compatible. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]

## Resolving Work

- **Compound-resolved activity assay on the high-prevalence case.** Take field material from sites where 3-hydroxybenzoic-acid utilizers reach 0.90 prevalence and run compound-resolved experiments or activity measurements, the evidence type the census names as required. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Question: does measured degradation track prevalence across sites, or is prevalence uninformative about rate?
- **Growth testing of the flagged auxotrophy, on an enlarged panel.** Test *D. vulgaris* on defined media with and without serine, and extend beyond the 7-organism pilot and the missing *E. coli* GapMind coverage before any universality claim. [src: essential_metabolome] Question: does prediction-level completeness survive growth validation outside the pilot set?
- **Phylogenetically corrected re-analysis of ecotype associations.** Re-fit the environment-cluster associations with explicit phylogenetic correction, given that the original had none and only *Salmonella enterica* and *Phenylobacterium* sp. were significant. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] Question: do either of the two significant associations survive control for shared ancestry?
- **Expression or flux measurement at community scale.** Pair community pathway completeness with expression or flux data rather than ambient amino-acid intensity alone, since the observed negative associations cannot demonstrate uptake, expression, or carbon flux. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] Question: does completeness predict realized flux, or only standing metabolite pools?
- **Classifier stratified by subgenus.** Re-evaluate the environment-associated capability classifier within subgenus strata, since strong subgenus separation and modest performance jointly limit compound-specific conclusions. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] Question: is the ecological signal carbon-capability-driven or phylogeny-driven?
