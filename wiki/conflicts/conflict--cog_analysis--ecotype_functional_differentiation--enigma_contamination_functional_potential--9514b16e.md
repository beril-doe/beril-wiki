# Conflict: Does Finer Resolution and Looser Coverage Reveal Functional Structure, or Manufacture It?

Five projects in this corpus bear on one methodological question: when an analysis reports that microbial community function tracks an environmental or host gradient, does that structure survive a change in coverage handling, test specification, taxonomic resolution, composition and genome-size controls, or a curated control panel? The tension as recorded on the source concept page [[concepts/taxonomic-resolution-dependent-functional-inference]] is not a single factual dispute but a family of paired results in which the permissive analysis detects structure and the stricter analysis attenuates or nulls it. It matters because the two members of each pair would license opposite readings of the same body of work — that functional potential responds to the environment, versus that the apparent response is a product of how taxa were mapped, aggregated, scored, or tested. Two of the pairs are internal to a single analysis: in the contamination analysis, the relaxed and strict coverage-adjusted models are two modes of the same model [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]; in the dark-matter analysis, the binomial and Fisher tests are computed over the same 47 testable clusters [src: functional_dark_matter]. The others are cross-project comparisons at differing resolutions and scopes. This page covers five strands: (1) relaxed versus strict coverage-adjusted models; (2) binomial versus Fisher-combined lab–field evidence, and pre-registered versus exploratory trait tests; (3) genus-level nulls versus within-species ecotype differentiation; (4) statistically significant but small compartment effects versus extensive dual-nature marker assignments; and (5) gene-novelty partitioning versus contamination-associated shifts in coarse functional categories. Strands (2) and (4) are recorded in the source text as **refining** the contamination tension rather than opposing it, and strand (5) is recorded there as a scope difference and explicitly *not* a direct contradiction; both framings are preserved below.

## Evidence Sides

**Strand 1, Side A — relaxed coverage adjustment detects an association.** The relaxed coverage-adjusted model yielded FDR q = 0.0462 — FDR (false discovery rate) being the multiple-testing-corrected error rate at which a result is called significant. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

**Strand 1, Side B — strict adjustment and predeclared confirmatory testing do not.** The strict coverage-adjusted model yielded FDR q = 0.130, and both confirmatory genus-level modes were null after predeclared testing. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] The source characterizes this as a methodological tension between a coverage-sensitive exploratory association and a resolution- and specification-robust association, not evidence that the estimates should be averaged. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] The difference could reflect retained abundance, bridge ambiguity, covariate sensitivity, taxonomic aggregation, or a combination of these factors. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

**Strand 2, Side A — pooled and pre-registered evidence supports lab–field concordance (recorded as refining Strand 1, not opposing it).** 29 of 47 testable lab–field clusters were concordant (61.7%), and Fisher's combined probability across 47 individual tests — a procedure that pools per-test p-values into a single statistic — gave p = 0.031. [src: functional_dark_matter] NMDC, the independent metagenomic sample collection used for out-of-sample validation, confirmed all 4 testable pre-registered abiotic predictions. [src: functional_dark_matter]

**Strand 2, Side B — the sign-level test is marginal and the exploratory breadth is suspect.** The one-sided binomial test over the same concordance count gave p = 0.072. [src: functional_dark_matter] The report cautions that 441 of 449 exploratory trait tests reached FDR < 0.05, largely because of compositional coupling. [src: functional_dark_matter] The source's own reading is that these results **refine** rather than resolve the contamination tension: finer-resolution validation can produce coherent directional evidence, while broad or exploratory associations remain sensitive to coverage and compositional structure. [src: functional_dark_matter]

**Strand 3, Side A — finer functional structure exists within species.** The within-species ecotype evidence supports the premise that finer functional structure exists, detecting functional differentiation within species. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]

**Strand 3, Side B — that structure may be phylogenetic, and the genus-level test is null.** The ecotype evidence does not resolve whether the structure is ecological or phylogenetic, because the ecotype analysis lacked within-species phylogenetic controls. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] This limits direct extrapolation from gene-content ecotypes to contamination-associated community functions: the contamination analysis is null or mode-sensitive at genus resolution, whereas the ecotype analysis detects functional differentiation within species. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]

**Strand 4, Side A — refined markers yield extensive apparent structure (recorded as refining Strand 1, not opposing it).** Compartment effects remained statistically significant. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Under a refined marker scheme, 78.7% of 1,115 plant-associated species were classified as dual-nature — carrying both plant-growth-promoting and pathogenic marker signatures. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

**Strand 4, Side B — the effect is small and the classifier fails its controls.** Compartment effects explained only 0.060 of location-only variance, and the earlier large effect was reduced to R² = 0.072 after removal of genome-rich species. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] Categorical assignments misclassified all four neutral controls in a curated 18-organism panel. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The source reads these together as showing that finer or richer marker resolution can produce extensive apparent functional structure while still being sensitive to taxonomic composition, genome size, and classifier definitions — a **refinement** of the contamination tension rather than a countercase to it. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

**Strand 5, Side A — gene-novelty partitioning is consistent across species.** The COG comparison — COG (Clusters of Orthologous Groups) being a coarse functional-category annotation — found consistent core-versus-novel functional partitioning across 32 species. [src: cog_analysis]

**Strand 5, Side B — no robust contamination-associated shift in coarse COG proxies.** The contamination analysis did not find a robust contamination-associated shift in coarse COG proxies at genus resolution. [src: cog_analysis] [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] The source states that this is not a direct contradiction because the studies test different signals: broad evolutionary partitioning by gene novelty versus a site-level ecological association after taxonomic bridging. [src: cog_analysis] [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

## Possible Reconciliations

These are hypotheses, not established explanations; none is demonstrated by the evidence above.

- **Coverage/measurement hypothesis.** The relaxed–strict gap (q = 0.0462 versus q = 0.130) may reflect how much community abundance each mode retains in the functional score, so that the same underlying association is estimated with different attenuation. The source lists retained abundance, bridge ambiguity, covariate sensitivity, taxonomic aggregation, or a combination of these factors as candidate causes. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]
- **Information-use hypothesis.** The binomial p = 0.072 versus Fisher p = 0.031 difference over the same 47 tests may be a property of the tests rather than of the evidence: a sign-level test uses only the direction of each cluster, while a combined-probability test uses each test's p-value. On this hypothesis both figures are correct for their own estimand, and neither settles "concordance" in general. [src: functional_dark_matter]
- **Resolution-scope hypothesis.** If functional variation is organized below the genus, genus-level aggregation could average it away without either result being wrong — consistent with a contamination analysis that is null or mode-sensitive at genus resolution alongside an ecotype analysis that detects differentiation within species. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- **Ancestry hypothesis.** Because the ecotype analysis lacked within-species phylogenetic controls, the within-species differentiation it detects may be phylogenetic rather than ecological, in which case it would not be the finer-grained ecological structure the contamination analysis is looking for. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- **Confounding hypothesis.** Genome size and taxonomic composition, rather than compartment biology, may carry much of the plant-association signal — a reading consistent with the reduction to R² = 0.072 after removal of genome-rich species. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
- **Definitional hypothesis.** A dual-nature assignment covering 78.7% of 1,115 species and the misclassification of all four neutral controls in the 18-organism panel may both follow from where the classifier's category boundary sits, making the disagreement one about definitions rather than about organisms. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
- **Different-estimand hypothesis (COG).** Broad evolutionary partitioning by gene novelty and a site-level ecological association after taxonomic bridging are different signals, so both results can hold at once; the source states the pairing is not a direct contradiction. [src: cog_analysis] [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

## Resolving Work

**Relaxed versus strict coverage-adjusted models (q = 0.0462 versus q = 0.130)**
- Refit the coverage-adjusted model with retained mapped abundance fixed by design (matched or stratified strata) rather than adjusted as a covariate, and ask whether the relaxed–strict gap persists when retained abundance cannot vary between modes. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]
- Re-run the model with bridge ambiguity (clades mapped per genus) as an explicit term, and ask how much of the q-value shift is attributable to multi-clade expansion. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]
- Run a leave-one-covariate-out sweep over the adjustment set to test the covariate-sensitivity candidate directly against the taxonomic-aggregation candidate. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]
- Predeclare a single coverage-adjusted specification as confirmatory in a new sample set, since the exploratory/confirmatory split is what currently generates the interpretive gap rather than a disagreement about the data. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

**Binomial versus Fisher, and pre-registered versus exploratory trait tests**
- Recompute lab–field concordance on the identical 47 testable clusters under both tests plus an effect-size-weighted alternative, reporting all of them beside the 29 of 47 (61.7%) count, so that test choice can be separated from evidence strength. [src: functional_dark_matter]
- Test the exploratory traits under a composition-preserving null, and ask how many of the 441 of 449 hits at FDR < 0.05 survive once compositional coupling is modelled rather than noted. [src: functional_dark_matter]
- Enlarge the pre-registered set beyond the 4 testable abiotic predictions so that confirmatory power approaches exploratory breadth, and report the confirmation rate at the enlarged set. [src: functional_dark_matter]
- Ask whether the clusters driving the Fisher-combined result are the same clusters counted as concordant, which would tell whether the two tests are reading one signal or two. [src: functional_dark_matter]

**Genus-level null versus within-species ecotype differentiation**
- Add the missing within-species phylogenetic controls to the ecotype analysis and ask whether gene-content differentiation survives once shared ancestry within the species is modelled. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]
- Re-run the contamination test at a finer-than-genus resolution and ask whether the confirmatory null is a resolution artifact rather than an absence of association. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]
- Check whether the species showing ecotype differentiation are among the taxa that dominate the contaminated communities, which would make the two analyses commensurable instead of parallel. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]

**Compartment effects, genome size, and marker-scheme definitions**
- Re-estimate compartment variance with genome size entered as a covariate rather than by removing genome-rich species, and ask whether the effect stays near R² = 0.072 or recovers. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
- Recalibrate the dual-nature classifier until the four neutral controls in the 18-organism panel are correctly assigned, then re-derive the 78.7% of 1,115 figure under the recalibrated boundary. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
- Benchmark the refined marker scheme against an independent labelled set, asking whether high dual-nature prevalence is a property of plant-associated genomes or of the marker definition. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
- State explicitly what quantity the 0.060 figure is a fraction of, and re-report compartment effects against a named, common variance denominator so the value is comparable with effect sizes from other analyses. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

**Gene-novelty partitioning versus contamination-associated COG shifts (recorded as a scope difference, not a contradiction)**
- Apply the core-versus-novel COG partitioning used across the 32 species to the contamination study's clade set, and ask whether that partitioning is detectable at all in the bridged communities. [src: cog_analysis] [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]
- Test contamination association against novelty-partitioned COG features instead of coarse COG proxies, to check whether the null is a feature-granularity effect. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] [src: cog_analysis]
- Write out and test the estimand of each analysis separately — evolutionary partitioning by gene novelty versus site-level ecological association after taxonomic bridging — so the pairing is evaluated as two questions rather than one disagreement. [src: cog_analysis] [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential]
