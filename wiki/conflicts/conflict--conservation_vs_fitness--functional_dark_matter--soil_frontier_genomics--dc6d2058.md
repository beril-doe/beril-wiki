# Is the measured dark-matter gap biology, or is it sampling and measurement design?

Four projects in this corpus report quantities intended to index how much microbial biology remains unmapped — a Genomic Discovery Index (GDI) over soil samples, a count of genes still hypothetical after re-annotation, a set of essential genes with no pangenome mapping, and a ranked list of functionally dark candidate systems. Each project also reports, in its own critical assessment, that the same quantity could instead be measuring the design of the survey that produced it: where samples were taken, which organisms have fitness data, which conditions were assayed, and how two component measurements were combined into one ratio. The disagreement is therefore not between projects claiming opposite facts, but between a *discovery-deficit* reading of these indices and a *survey-design* reading, with the corpus currently containing evidence for both and no analysis that separates them. It matters because every downstream use of these indices — targeting sampling campaigns, prioritizing genes for characterization, claiming a biome is genomically under-represented — is valid only under the first reading. This page covers four threads: (1) causal direction of the soil GDI gap, (2) GDI as a composite ratio, (3) residual annotation gap versus total novelty, and (4) taxonomic and condition coverage of experimental dark-matter prioritization. It is a promotion of the tensions recorded on [[concepts/genomic-under-representation]].

## Evidence Sides

**Side A — the indices measure real under-representation and real novelty.**

The GDI pattern is consistent with under-representation of alkaline-soil microbiomes [src: soil_frontier_genomics]. The GDI formulation can identify locations combining high OTU richness (operational taxonomic units, sequence-similarity clusters used as proxy taxa) with low mean genome completeness [src: soil_frontier_genomics]. On the annotation side, a residual gap survives modern re-annotation: among linked dark genes, 6,427 remained hypothetical after Bakta, an automated bacterial genome annotation tool [src: truly_dark_genes]. On the experimental side, essentiality calls identify essential-unmapped genes — genes required for viability that have no pangenome mapping — which may reflect divergent core functions [src: conservation_vs_fitness]. And the prioritization can be extended beyond its original taxonomic base: the extended 50-organism covering set reached 98.7% of ortholog groups across 6 phyla [src: functional_dark_matter].

**Side B — the indices track how the survey was built, not how much biology is missing.**

Reverse causality remains unresolved because lower genome representation could result from fewer 16S samples rather than from assembly or annotation difficulty; the report does not establish whether the observed gap is primarily a sampling gap, a genome-recovery gap, or an annotation gap [src: soil_frontier_genomics]. The index itself is not a pure measure of discovery deficit because richness and completeness are combined in one ratio [src: soil_frontier_genomics]. The annotation residual carries error in both directions at once: 17,479 unlinked genes could not be assessed, which can only leave unexplained genes uncounted, while Bakta can produce false-negative functional calls, which can inflate the 6,427 figure by scoring genes of known function as hypothetical — so the measured residual annotation gap is not interchangeable with the total biological novelty in public genomes [src: truly_dark_genes]. Essentiality calls come from RB-TnSeq — random-barcode transposon sequencing, in which pooled barcoded transposon mutants are tracked by sequencing — under represented library-construction and growth conditions, and pangenome coverage varies among clades, so essential-unmapped genes may instead reflect missing linkage, and the analysis cannot distinguish these explanations universally [src: conservation_vs_fitness]. Finally, the prioritized candidate list is taxonomically narrow: 37 of 48 Fitness Browser organisms were Pseudomonadota, and none of the top 500 candidates came from Archaea, Actinobacteria, or Epsilonproteobacteria [src: functional_dark_matter]. The covering set that broadens phylum coverage does not restore the phenotype evidence, because it lacked Fitness Browser condition profiles for its non-Fitness-Browser organisms [src: functional_dark_matter].

## Possible Reconciliations

These are hypotheses, not findings; none is tested in the corpus as it stands.

- **Scope hypothesis.** Both sides may be right about different scopes: the indices could be valid *relative* rankings within a fixed survey design — which spatial bin, which gene, which organism is more under-mapped than its peers measured the same way — while being invalid as *absolute* estimates of missing biology. Under this hypothesis the soil signal survives as a statement about public databases [src: soil_frontier_genomics] without supporting any claim about the soils themselves.
- **Composite-measurement hypothesis.** The soil disagreement may be arithmetic rather than biological: because richness and completeness are combined in one ratio [src: soil_frontier_genomics], the index can move for either reason, so the causal ambiguity and the ratio confound may be the same problem seen twice, and reporting the components separately could dissolve both at once.
- **Two-sided-error hypothesis.** The annotation numbers may be measurements of different populations with error running in opposite directions rather than competing estimates of one quantity: 6,427 post-Bakta hypothetical genes is what re-annotation could not explain among *linked* genes, 17,479 unlinked genes sit outside the measurement entirely, and Bakta false negatives may sit inside the 6,427 without being novel [src: truly_dark_genes]. Both sides would then be correct about what they measured, and the error would lie in treating the residual as the total — in either direction.
- **Two-populations hypothesis.** Essential-unmapped genes may be a mixture, not a category: some genuinely divergent core functions and some artifacts of incomplete pangenome coverage, in proportions that differ by clade [src: conservation_vs_fitness]. A universal verdict would be wrong under this hypothesis even if both sides' reasoning is sound.
- **Coverage-versus-evidence hypothesis.** Phylum breadth and phenotype evidence may simply be different axes that no current dataset satisfies simultaneously — the covering set buys the first at the cost of the second [src: functional_dark_matter] — in which case the taxonomic skew is an honest limit of available fitness data rather than a flaw in the prioritization method.

## Resolving Work

**Thread 1 — causal direction of the soil GDI gap**
- Regress mean genome completeness on 16S sample count per spatial bin, holding OTU richness fixed, to test whether low completeness tracks sampling effort rather than recovery difficulty [src: soil_frontier_genomics].
- Stratify bins by assembly-difficulty proxies and ask whether the under-representation signal that motivated the alkaline-soil inference persists within strata [src: soil_frontier_genomics].
- Decompose the gap into sampling, genome-recovery, and annotation components and report each separately, since the report does not currently establish which dominates [src: soil_frontier_genomics].

**Thread 2 — GDI as a composite ratio**
- Report OTU richness and mean genome completeness as separate layers alongside GDI and check whether frontier rankings agree across all three [src: soil_frontier_genomics].
- Identify bins where the two components disagree in rank order and treat only the concordant bins as candidate frontiers, since a single ratio cannot distinguish a richness-driven score from a completeness-driven one [src: soil_frontier_genomics].
- Recompute frontier rankings under alternative combination rules — each component alone, and the components rank-standardized before combination — and report how much of the frontier list is invariant to the choice [src: soil_frontier_genomics].
- Test whether the same locations are selected when high richness and low mean completeness are imposed as separate thresholds rather than through one combined value, which would show the ratio is not doing the selection work [src: soil_frontier_genomics].

**Thread 3 — residual annotation gap versus total novelty**
- Extend linkage so the 17,479 unlinked genes can be assessed, then re-count the residual on the full set rather than the linked subset [src: truly_dark_genes].
- Estimate Bakta's false-negative rate on a gold-standard set of experimentally characterized genes and correct the 6,427 residual accordingly [src: truly_dark_genes].
- Report the residual with both of its error directions attached — the unassessed unlinked remainder that it omits, and the false-negative hypothetical calls it may contain — rather than as a novelty estimate [src: truly_dark_genes].

**Thread 4 — coverage of experimental prioritization**
- Score whether essential-unmapped calls concentrate in clades with lower pangenome coverage, which would favor the missing-linkage explanation over divergent core function [src: conservation_vs_fitness].
- Vary library-construction and growth conditions for a subset of organisms and test how many essentiality calls are condition-stable [src: conservation_vs_fitness].
- Re-rank candidates with organism identity permuted or with Pseudomonadota down-weighted, and ask whether the top-500 list still excludes Archaea, Actinobacteria, and Epsilonproteobacteria [src: functional_dark_matter].
- Generate fitness condition profiles for the non-Fitness-Browser members of the 50-organism covering set, so that the 98.7% ortholog-group coverage carries phenotype evidence rather than sequence coverage alone [src: functional_dark_matter].
