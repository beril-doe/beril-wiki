# Conflict: broad ecological AMR signal versus within-lineage, mechanistic, and cross-catalog tests

Across the resistome projects in this corpus, environment-level statistics reliably separate ecological categories, yet nearly every attempt to localize that signal — inside a species, inside a fitness measurement, inside a metal-tolerance phenotype, inside a mobility mechanism, or inside a shared gene catalog — comes back weak, null, or numerically inconsistent with the aggregate claim. This page records five linked disagreements carried on [[concepts/environmental-resistome]]: (1) robust environment-level association versus near-absent within-species environmental structure and no exposure-to-selection test; (2) the broad antimicrobial-resistance (AMR) catalog versus narrow antibiotic-resistance language, together with unreconciled denominators between the discoveries digest and the primary analyses; (3) core/accessory genomic compartment read as intrinsic/acquired versus a null fitness difference; (4) isolate-level metal-tolerance validation versus species-scale cross-resistance and biogeography nulls; and (5) prophage density versus local proximity, and secretion-system co-occurrence read as dissemination. The disagreement matters because the aggregate result is the one most likely to be cited as "environment selects for resistance," while the localizing tests are the ones that would license that causal reading. Nothing here is averaged or adjudicated; each side is reported with the figures its source gives.

## Evidence Sides

**1A. Environment-level association is robust (side: ecological signal is real).** Environment effects explain 2–13% of AMR-composition variance (η² = 0.02–0.13; η², eta-squared, is the fraction of variance attributable to a grouping factor). [src: amr_environmental_resistome] The association persisted after majority-vote classification thresholds and after phylum- and family-level controls, supporting an ecological signal beyond a simple species-label artifact. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]

**1B. The signal does not localize, and no exposure-to-selection test exists (side: association is not demonstrated selection).** Phylogeny, sampling, isolation practice, gene mobility, and annotation coverage may explain part of these effects; clinical exposure could increase AMR, AMR could contribute to clinical isolation, or both could reflect correlated host-associated traits. Majority-vote classification collapses within-species variation. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] Only 20 of 141 testable families (14%) showed significant within-family effects after FDR correction (FDR, false-discovery rate, the expected share of false positives among calls declared significant). Whole-genome ecotype analysis found environmental effects significant and positive in 12 species (7.0%), significant and negative in 4 species (2.3%), and absent in 156 species (90.7%). [src: amr_environmental_resistome, ecotype_analysis] Strain analysis found AMR ecotypes in 19.5% of eligible species, but metadata were sparse and only 2 species passed strict testing criteria. [src: amr_strain_variation] A positive relationship between AMR and phylogenetic distance in 701/1,261 species may reflect lineage-associated acquisition or uneven sampling of lineages across environments; available analyses do not separate these explanations. Geographic coordinates were often missing or imprecise, and partial correlations assume linear relationships between distance matrices, so weak environmental signal cannot distinguish absent adaptation from inadequate environmental representation. [src: amr_strain_variation, ecotype_analysis]

**2A. The catalog is broad (side: "AMR" here includes metal and stress systems).** The AMRFinderPlus catalog detects metal and stress systems; the narrow antibiotic-resistance interpretation is weakened by mercury- and arsenic-resistance families and by the 22.2% Other/Unclassified category. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]

**2B. Denominators and vocabularies disagree across projects (side: the shared numbers are not the same numbers).** The environmental-resistome analysis reports 15,550 unassigned clusters (18.7%), not 18,448 (22.2%); differing denominators and vocabularies must not be silently reconciled. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas, amr_environmental_resistome] The discoveries digest reports efflux at 21% of AMR in human-gut species and 1% in aquatic species, while the environmental-resistome analysis reports 7.0% and 1.1%; the 21% versus 7.0% and related 13% versus 44% core/accessory values require harmonized entity sets, filters, annotations, and denominators. [src: discoveries, amr_environmental_resistome, amr_fitness_cost]

**3A. Genomic compartment tracks mode of resistance (side: core = intrinsic, accessory = acquired).** The atlas links core status to intrinsic examples and accessory status to acquired examples. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]

**3B. Fitness does not distinguish the compartments (side: compartment is not origin or cost).** The fitness analysis found identical mean fitness values of −0.024 for core/intrinsic and accessory/acquired groups (Cohen's d = 0.002, p = 0.33; Cohen's d is a standardized mean difference, with 0.002 being effectively zero). Genomic compartment therefore supports a mode-of-maintenance hypothesis but does not by itself establish resistance origin, mobility, or cost. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas, amr_fitness_cost]

**4A. Isolate-level phenotype supports contamination-associated metal tolerance.** BacDive reports higher metal-tolerance scores among contamination-associated isolates, including Cohen's d = +1.00 for heavy-metal contamination (n = 10). [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

**4B. Species-scale cross-resistance finds nothing.** Cross-resistance analysis found no species-scale correlation between multi-metal tolerance and metal-associated isolation (Spearman rho approximately −0.02, p > 0.8; Spearman rho is a rank correlation), leaving only 20 independent species. [src: metal_cross_resistance]

**4C. Related but non-equivalent habitat-scale results.** Global metagenome-assembled-genome (MAG; a genome reconstructed from a metagenome rather than an isolate) analysis found soil at 5.8% prevalence and OR = 5.05, marine samples at 1.2% and OR = 0.20 (OR, odds ratio, the multiplicative change in odds relative to the reference). [src: metal_resistance_global_biogeography] MicrobeAtlas found metal-type diversity associated with groundwater prevalence (ρ = +0.112, p = 0.0019) but not groundwater-specific fold enrichment (ρ = +0.042, p = 0.242). [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] Soil COG associations (COG, Clusters of Orthologous Groups, a coarse functional gene classification) versus metal-specific interpretation are unresolved because chromium, copper, lead, and zinc co-vary in many industrial soils. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]

**5A. Mobility signal at genome scale is strong.** Prophage density was strongly associated with AMR breadth (rho = 0.572; partial rho = 0.464 after controlling for genome count). [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]

**5B. Mobility signal at local scale is weak, and transfer is not demonstrated.** Local prophage–AMR proximity was modest and heterogeneous, with median species-level OR = 0.85. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization] Type IV secretion system (T4SS; a conjugation-capable secretion apparatus) and carbohydrate-active enzyme (CAZy) evidence supports dissemination as a hypothesis rather than demonstrated transfer: 92 elevated co-occurrences, 77 GT2 horizontal-gene-transfer (HGT) events, 32 normalized high-confidence cross-phylum events, and 10× higher mobile-genetic-element (MGE) density in T4SS-positive genomes, but no CAZy genes on plasmids by ICEfinder and only 12 integrative and mobilizable elements (IMEs) among the top 100 accumulators. [src: t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt]

## Possible Reconciliations

These are hypotheses, not findings.

- **Scale mismatch (disagreement 1).** Environment may structure the resistome mainly through which lineages occupy which habitats, not through within-species adaptation. That would make η² = 0.02–0.13 at species level compatible with 90.7% of species showing no environmental effect, because the variance sits between species rather than inside them. [src: amr_environmental_resistome, ecotype_analysis]
- **Power, not absence (disagreement 1).** With only 2 species passing strict testing criteria and 141 testable families, within-species nulls may be non-detections rather than true nulls; sparse metadata and missing or imprecise geographic coordinates push in the same direction. [src: amr_strain_variation, ecotype_analysis]
- **Definitional divergence (disagreement 2).** If "AMR" in one project means the full catalog including mercury- and arsenic-resistance families and in another means antibiotic-specific families, both the 15,550 (18.7%) / 18,448 (22.2%) pair and the 21% / 7.0% efflux pair could be arithmetically correct over different denominators — a labeling conflict rather than a factual one. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas, amr_environmental_resistome, discoveries]
- **Compartment encodes maintenance, not cost (disagreement 3).** Core and accessory genes may differ in how they are retained while imposing indistinguishable per-gene fitness effects, which would reconcile the intrinsic/acquired mapping with mean fitness of −0.024 in both groups. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas, amr_fitness_cost]
- **Unit-of-analysis difference (disagreement 4).** Tolerance may be a strain-level property that averages away at species level; d = +1.00 over n = 10 contamination-associated isolates and rho ≈ −0.02 over 20 independent species are measurements of different units, and the MAG and MicrobeAtlas results are explicitly non-equivalent habitat-scale quantities. [src: bacdive_metal_validation, metal_cross_resistance, metal_resistance_global_biogeography, microbeatlas_metal_ecology]
- **Co-varying exposure (disagreement 4).** Because chromium, copper, lead, and zinc co-vary in industrial soils, a real metal association and a non-metal-specific soil COG association could both hold. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]
- **Two mobility regimes (disagreement 5).** Prophage density may index a genome's lifetime exposure to mobile elements while current physical adjacency is rare, reconciling partial rho = 0.464 with median OR = 0.85; similarly, T4SS-associated MGE enrichment could mark capacity for transfer while ICEfinder finds no CAZy genes on plasmids. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization, t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt]

## Resolving Work

**Disagreement 1 — association versus selection**
- Run within-species AMR-by-environment tests restricted to species with ≥N genomes per environment and report achieved power, so the 90.7%-absent and 14%-of-families results can be split into true nulls versus non-detections. [src: ecotype_analysis, amr_environmental_resistome]
- Replace majority-vote species labels with per-genome environment labels for the same species set and re-estimate η², testing whether the 2–13% range survives when within-species variation is not collapsed. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]
- Fit a joint model with lineage and sampling effort as covariates on the 701/1,261 phylogenetic-distance result to separate lineage-associated acquisition from uneven lineage sampling. [src: amr_strain_variation]
- Quantify geographic-metadata completeness per species and re-run distance correlations on the coordinate-complete subset only, to test whether weak environmental signal tracks environmental representation. [src: ecotype_analysis, amr_strain_variation]
- Use time-resolved or exposure-annotated isolates to test the direction between clinical exposure and AMR content, the test the corpus currently lacks. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]

**Disagreement 2 — catalog scope and denominators**
- Recompute every environment-level statistic twice, once on the full catalog and once excluding mercury-, arsenic-, and other metal/stress families, and publish both alongside the 22.2% Other/Unclassified share. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]
- Reconcile 15,550 (18.7%) against 18,448 (22.2%) by publishing the exact cluster set, filters, and denominator each figure uses; do not report a merged value. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas, amr_environmental_resistome]
- Re-derive the digest's 21% and 1% efflux figures and the 13% versus 44% core/accessory pair on the harmonized entity set, filters, and annotations used for 7.0% and 1.1%, and report which choice moves the number. [src: discoveries, amr_environmental_resistome, amr_fitness_cost]

**Disagreement 3 — compartment versus fitness**
- Stratify fitness by resistance family and host species rather than by compartment alone, testing whether the d = 0.002, p = 0.33 null hides offsetting family-level effects. [src: amr_fitness_cost, amr_pangenome_atlas]
- Test compartment assignment against independent mobility evidence (plasmid/integron/prophage location) for the same clusters, since compartment currently stands in for mobility without measuring it. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas, prophage_amr_comobilization]
- Measure fitness under matched selective conditions rather than pooled conditions, to ask whether the −0.024/−0.024 equality is condition-averaged rather than intrinsic. [src: amr_fitness_cost]

**Disagreement 4 — metal tolerance across scales**
- Repeat the contamination-association test with isolate counts well beyond n = 10 and report effect size with confidence intervals, so d = +1.00 can be distinguished from small-sample inflation. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- Re-run the multi-metal tolerance versus metal-associated isolation correlation at strain rather than species level, and report how many independent units each version has beyond the current 20. [src: metal_cross_resistance]
- Analyze soil and marine MAG prevalence (5.8%, OR = 5.05; 1.2%, OR = 0.20) and the groundwater ρ = +0.112 / ρ = +0.042 pair under one shared definition of prevalence and enrichment, rather than comparing non-equivalent quantities. [src: metal_resistance_global_biogeography, microbeatlas_metal_ecology]
- Use soils with measured, decorrelated chromium, copper, lead, and zinc concentrations to test whether COG associations are metal-specific or a generic contaminated-soil signature. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]

**Disagreement 5 — mobility evidence**
- Test whether prophage density predicts AMR breadth after controlling for genome size and assembly quality in addition to genome count, extending the partial rho = 0.464 control set. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]
- Model local prophage–AMR proximity hierarchically across species to ask whether median OR = 0.85 masks a minority of species with strong adjacency. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]
- Seek direct transfer evidence — donor/recipient phylogenetic incongruence with element boundaries — for the 77 GT2 HGT events and 32 normalized high-confidence cross-phylum events, since co-occurrence alone cannot demonstrate transfer. [src: t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt]
- Re-scan the top 100 accumulators with mobility callers beyond ICEfinder, given that only 12 IMEs were found and no CAZy genes were called on plasmids. [src: t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt]
