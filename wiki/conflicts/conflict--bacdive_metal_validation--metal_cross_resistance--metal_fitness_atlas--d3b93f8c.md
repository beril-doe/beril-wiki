# Does a genomic metal-gene repertoire predict metal-contaminated isolation environments?

The corpus contains a positive and a null answer to the same question, obtained at different scales. A BacDive-to-pangenome validation reports that strains isolated from heavy-metal contamination carry substantially higher genome-based metal tolerance scores — Cohen's d (a standardized mean difference, in pooled standard deviation units) of +1.00 in n=10 isolates [src: bacdive_metal_validation] — while a gene-resolution cross-resistance study finds no correlation between multi-metal tolerance scores and BacDive metal-environment isolation at Fitness Browser species scale (Spearman rho approximately -0.02, p > 0.8) [src: metal_cross_resistance]. The disagreement matters because the whole inferential chain of [[concepts/within-species-conservation-between-species-functional-divergence]] runs through it: if genome content tracks isolation ecology, normalized metal scores can be used as ecological phenotypes at scale; if it does not, conservation statistics describe gene repertoires only and say nothing about where organisms live. This page also records two subsidiary disagreements that ride on the same question: whether conserved metal-gene content predicts measured fitness at all, and whether an ecological metal-diversity signal survives a habitat-specific test.

## Evidence Sides

**Side A — Positive association at isolate and pangenome scale (BacDive validation).** Heavy-metal contamination isolates showed Cohen's d = +1.00 in n=10 isolates, alongside a pangenome-scale Metal Fitness Atlas result of Cohen's d = +1.0 across 42K strains. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] This side carries its own explicit precision caveat: the n=10 matched isolates produced an observed d=1.00 that barely exceeded the approximately d=0.93 minimum detectable effect size reported for 80% power with n=10 heavy-metal isolates versus approximately 5,000 environmental-baseline strains. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] The contamination gradient therefore supports the layered model, but the strongest estimate requires replication and explicit adjustment for genome size, annotated-cluster count, taxonomy, and sampling source. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] The evidence base is also partial: of 97,334 strains, 55,107 (56.6%) were unmatched, primarily because GTDB (Genome Taxonomy Database) species boundaries differed from LPSN/DSMZ-based naming, so the observed pattern may not represent all described or environmental bacteria. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] BacDive represents culturable, described strains rather than the full diversity of environmental bacteria and may under-represent metal-tolerant extremophiles. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] This incomplete [[concepts/pangenome-integration]] bridge can also change which genome-size and annotation-breadth classes enter the analysis. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

**Side B — Null association at species scale (cross-resistance study).** The cross-resistance study found no correlation between multi-metal tolerance scores and BacDive metal-environment isolation at Fitness Browser species scale (Spearman rho — a rank correlation — of approximately -0.02, p > 0.8); after matching and collapsing strains, it retained 20 independent species and judged the test underpowered. [src: metal_cross_resistance] Its own positive result is gene-level rather than ecological: broadly positive gene-level responses across 28 organisms support the conservation component, but those organisms were not phylogenetically independent. [src: metal_cross_resistance] The gene-level conservation analysis does not directly measure within-species environmental occupancy, so it strengthens the proposed architecture without establishing the causal link left open by the BacDive study. [src: metal_cross_resistance] PGLS (phylogenetic generalized least squares, a regression that models shared ancestry among taxa) or independent contrasts would strengthen the conservation claim. [src: metal_cross_resistance] Critically, these results do not establish whether the discrepancy reflects scale, matching, phenotype definition, or sampling. [src: metal_cross_resistance]

**Side C — Conserved gene content does not predict fitness (Metal Fitness Atlas).** The atlas supports the conservation side through its 87.4% core fraction, but refines the ecological interpretation: the broad metal-important definition (fit < -1 OR n_sick ≥ 1) captures general stress functions as well as metal-specific resistance, and the simple repertoire score failed to predict fitness. [src: metal_fitness_atlas] Core conservation therefore does not by itself validate normalized environmental scores or imply that gene presence predicts metal tolerance. [src: metal_fitness_atlas] This is a distinct disagreement from Sides A and B: it concedes conservation while denying the predictive step that Side A's validation assumes.

**Side D — Ecological association holds on occurrence proxies, with mixed habitat-specific validation (MicrobeAtlas).** Metal-type diversity predicted broader inferred niche breadth after phylogenetic adjustment, yet the analysis used genus-level occurrence proxies rather than direct metal-exposure phenotypes. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] Its groundwater validation was mixed: metal-type diversity correlated with groundwater prevalence (Spearman ρ = +0.112, p = 0.0019), but groundwater-specific fold-enrichment was null (ρ = +0.042, p = 0.242). [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] These findings therefore strengthen the broader-range association without demonstrating a uniquely metal-contamination-driven mechanism. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

## Possible Reconciliations

Each of the following is a hypothesis, not an established finding.

- **Scale and independence hypothesis.** Side A's effect is measured over strains — Cohen's d = +1.0 across 42K strains, and n=10 matched heavy-metal isolates [src: bacdive_metal_validation] — while Side B collapses to 20 independent species and judges the test underpowered [src: metal_cross_resistance]. Both could be right if the association is real but carried largely by within-species and strain-level variation that disappears once species are the unit — the confusion flagged in [[concepts/environmental-resistome]], where species-level conservation and divergence can be obscured when strain-level variation and environmental sampling are treated as interchangeable. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- **Power hypothesis.** A true effect could produce both results if Side A sat at its detection limit, with an observed d=1.00 barely exceeding the approximately d=0.93 minimum detectable effect size for 80% power [src: bacdive_metal_validation], and Side B's 20 independent species had less power still [src: metal_cross_resistance]. Under this hypothesis the null is an absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.
- **Matching hypothesis.** The unmatched fraction — 55,107 of 97,334 strains (56.6%), driven by GTDB-versus-LPSN/DSMZ naming — may make the two studies' matched sets non-comparable in genome-size and annotation-breadth classes. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- **Phenotype-definition hypothesis.** Side A scores genome repertoire [src: bacdive_metal_validation]; Side C shows that the broad metal-important definition (fit < -1 OR n_sick ≥ 1) captures general stress functions as well as metal-specific resistance, and that the simple repertoire score failed to predict fitness [src: metal_fitness_atlas]. The two sides may be measuring general stress capacity versus metal-specific tolerance.
- **Niche-breadth hypothesis.** Side D's pattern — metal-type diversity predicting broader inferred niche breadth, groundwater prevalence positive (Spearman ρ = +0.112, p = 0.0019) but fold-enrichment null (ρ = +0.042, p = 0.242) — is consistent with metal-gene diversity marking generalism rather than contamination adaptation. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] That would explain a strong contamination contrast in curated isolate metadata alongside a weak habitat-specific signal.
- **Sampling hypothesis.** If BacDive's culturable, described strains under-represent metal-tolerant extremophiles, the contamination contrast could be inflated or attenuated relative to species-scale environmental data. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]

## Resolving Work

**On the positive-versus-null association (Sides A and B)**

- Re-run the contamination contrast with the strain unit made explicit: estimate the effect separately within species and between species on the matched BacDive×pangenome set, reporting both, to test whether the signal survives the collapse to 20 independent species. [src: bacdive_metal_validation, metal_cross_resistance]
- Fit the isolation-environment contrast with covariate adjustment for genome size, annotated-cluster count, taxonomy, and sampling source, as the positive side itself specifies, and report whether d=1.00 survives. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- Expand the heavy-metal isolate set beyond n=10 and recompute against the approximately 5,000-strain environmental baseline, so the estimate no longer sits at the approximately d=0.93 minimum detectable effect size. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- Repair the bridge for the 55,107 (56.6%) unmatched strains using genome-level rather than name-level linkage, then test whether the association changes when GTDB-versus-LPSN/DSMZ boundary mismatches no longer filter the sample. [src: bacdive_metal_validation]
- Apply PGLS or independent contrasts to the joint repertoire-versus-isolation-environment test, which the null side names as the analysis that would strengthen the conservation claim and which would also correct the non-independence of its 28 organisms. [src: metal_cross_resistance]

**On whether conserved genes predict tolerance (Side C)**

- Split the metal-important definition into its components (fit < -1 versus n_sick ≥ 1) and re-test prediction separately, to separate general stress functions from metal-specific resistance. [src: metal_fitness_atlas]
- Replace the simple repertoire score, which failed to predict fitness, with a weighted or metal-specific score and re-evaluate against measured fitness on the same organisms. [src: metal_fitness_atlas]
- Test whether the 87.4% core fraction leaves enough accessory variation to carry any ecological signal, by quantifying prediction performance using accessory content only. [src: metal_fitness_atlas]

**On the mixed ecological validation (Side D)**

- Repeat the groundwater test at species rather than genus-level occurrence proxies and report whether the prevalence correlation (Spearman ρ = +0.112, p = 0.0019) and the null fold-enrichment (ρ = +0.042, p = 0.242) converge. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]
- Pair metal-type diversity with direct metal-exposure phenotypes instead of genus-level occurrence proxies, asking whether the niche-breadth association is metal-specific or generalism-driven. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]
- Regress inferred niche breadth and contamination-site isolation on metal-gene diversity in the same model, so that a broader-range effect [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] and a contamination effect [src: bacdive_metal_validation] can be distinguished rather than conflated.
