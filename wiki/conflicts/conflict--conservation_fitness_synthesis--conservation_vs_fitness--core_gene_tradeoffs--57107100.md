# Conflict — What "conserved" means: near-universal ortholog breadth vs graded taxonomic rank vs clade-core membership

This corpus measures "conservation" in ways that cannot be laid on top of one another, and the disagreement runs both between and within projects. One project supplies two of the conflicting measurements itself: an initial eggNOG-based metric that reports near-universal breadth for 99.9% of clusters, and a GTDB r214 re-analysis that resolves conservation into graded taxonomic categories — the project reads the gap between its own two measurements as a tension. [src: functional_dark_matter] A second project scores conservation as membership in a species clade's core genome and finds only a modest association with experimental essentiality. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] A further strand is internal to a single metric: two variants of the same conservation score agree almost perfectly on global ranking yet disagree on most of the genes either would actually nominate for experiment. [src: functional_dark_matter] Three more projects add qualifications that narrow the space without closing it. [src: fitness_effects_conservation, conservation_fitness_synthesis, core_gene_tradeoffs] The stakes are practical rather than semantic — these scores are used to choose which genes get characterized, so a metric that is stable in aggregate but unstable at the top of the list will send two labs to two different bench lists while both cite "conservation." This page collects the metric-calibration tension recorded on [[concepts/comparative-conservation-metric-calibration]]. It does not cover disagreements about dark-gene function assignment or pathway gap-filling, which belong to other pages.

## Evidence Sides

**Side A — Near-universal breadth: the initial eggNOG-based metric.** Scoring clusters against eggNOG-propagated ortholog groups (eggNOG is a database of precomputed orthologous groups, and group membership is propagated across genomes by homology) suggested near-universal breadth for 99.9% of clusters. [src: functional_dark_matter] A score that places 99.9% of clusters in one category does not discriminate among those candidates. [src: functional_dark_matter]

**Side B — Graded taxonomic categories: the GTDB r214 re-analysis, from the same project.** The GTDB r214 analysis (GTDB = Genome Taxonomy Database, release 214, a genome-based bacterial/archaeal taxonomy) resolved root ortholog groups across kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, species, and mobile categories. [src: functional_dark_matter] The project reads this directly as a tension with its own earlier metric: apparent conservation can depend strongly on reference-database composition, ortholog-group propagation, and the taxonomic level used for scoring. [src: functional_dark_matter] Note that the two views are not stated to be scored over the same units or the same denominator, so the comparison is between scoring schemes rather than between two labels on one fixed gene set.

**Side C — Rank agreement vs decision stability, within one metric.** The species-count variant of the metric was highly correlated with the original ranking at the global level, with Spearman ρ = 0.982 (ρ is a rank-correlation coefficient: 1.0 is identical ordering), yet its top-50 and top-100 overlaps were only 62% and 58%, respectively. [src: functional_dark_matter] The conclusion drawn is that rank correlation and decision stability answer different questions and should be reported together when conservation metrics guide experimental selection. [src: functional_dark_matter] This side is not in conflict with A or B so much as it shows that near-identical metrics can still agree on only 62% and 58% of the genes either would nominate at k = 50 and k = 100. [src: functional_dark_matter]

**Side D — Clade-core membership from experimental essentiality.** The Fitness Browser comparison (Fitness Browser = the fitness-experiment data collection linked here to pangenome clusters) introduces a further metric-resolution tension rather than a contradiction: essential genes showed only modest core enrichment (median odds ratio 1.56), while the expanded GTDB analysis produced conservation categories spanning kingdom to species and mobile levels. [src: conservation_vs_fitness, functional_dark_matter] The difference **refines** the interpretation of "conserved": core membership within a species clade and broad taxonomic ortholog breadth are related but not interchangeable measurements. [src: conservation_vs_fitness, functional_dark_matter] A related result pushes further against reading core status as importance: core genes showed heavier fitness-effect tails in both directions, so core status may identify genes with stronger conditional costs and benefits rather than genes with uniformly greater importance. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

**Side E — Qualifications that narrow the space without closing it.** The synthesis **adds a further qualification rather than resolving the metric tension**: module-family breadth did not predict conservation (rho=-0.01, p=0.91), and condition-specific fitness did not establish niche-specific fitness. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] Coordinated architecture and laboratory condition dependence can therefore improve interpretation without making conservation a direct readout of natural ecological importance. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] The core-gene trade-off analysis **reinforces this qualification**: its interpretation of costly conserved genes as maintained by natural selection rests on laboratory fitness measurements and conservation patterns, while laboratory conditions capture only a fraction of natural environments and Fitness Browser conditions are biased toward experimentally convenient settings. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] It also cautions that defining burden as fitness above 1 may capture trade-offs rather than true dispensability, and that a 90% identity threshold for DIAMOND matching (DIAMOND is a fast protein-sequence aligner used here to map genes to clusters) may miss rapidly evolving genes. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]

## Possible Reconciliations

These are hypotheses, not findings; none is established by the cited evidence.

- **Resolution-ceiling hypothesis.** Sides A and B may both be correct measurements of different things: ortholog-group propagation in a broad reference database can assign a cluster to a deeply rooted group on weak homology, saturating the score at "universal," while root ortholog groups placed on a genome-based taxonomy retain rank structure. On this hypothesis the 99.9% figure and the graded categories are a saturated and an unsaturated view of a shared signal, and neither is "wrong" — only one is usable for ranking. [src: functional_dark_matter]

- **Unit-mismatch hypothesis.** Because the two schemes are not stated to score the same units over the same denominator, part of the apparent disagreement between Sides A and B may be a change of unit (clusters vs root ortholog groups) and of coverage rather than a change in measured conservation. [src: functional_dark_matter]

- **Scope-mismatch hypothesis.** Side D may differ from Sides A/B mainly in taxonomic scope: core membership is defined inside a single species clade, whereas ortholog breadth is defined across kingdoms. A gene can be strictly core in its clade and taxonomically narrow, or broadly distributed yet accessory locally; on this hypothesis the median odds ratio of 1.56 reflects a scope mismatch rather than weak biology. [src: conservation_vs_fitness, functional_dark_matter]

- **Conditionality hypothesis.** If core status marks genes with stronger conditional costs *and* benefits rather than uniformly greater importance, then a metric validated against mean fitness will underrate core genes while a metric validated against tail behaviour will overrate them — making the apparent conflict an artifact of which fitness summary statistic is used as ground truth. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

- **Assay-scope hypothesis.** Laboratory conditions capture only a fraction of natural environments and Fitness Browser conditions are biased toward experimentally convenient settings [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]; if so, any metric calibrated against these fitness data may be measuring conservation's correlation with laboratory conditionality rather than with natural ecological importance — consistent with the finding that condition-specific fitness did not establish niche-specific fitness. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

- **Threshold-artifact hypothesis.** Part of the spread between metrics may be introduced by two explicit cut points rather than by biology: defining burden as fitness above 1, and the 90% identity threshold for DIAMOND matching, which may miss rapidly evolving genes and therefore systematically score them as less conserved. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]

## Resolving Work

**On database composition and taxonomic level (Sides A/B):**
- Recompute conservation for one fixed gene set under both schemes — eggNOG-propagated ortholog groups and GTDB r214 root ortholog groups — on a common unit and denominator, and report the joint distribution rather than two marginals, asking how many items called "near-universal" under the first fall below kingdom level under the second. [src: functional_dark_matter]
- Hold the taxonomy fixed and vary only the scoring level (kingdom → species), measuring at which level the 99.9%-universal collapse first appears; this isolates level-of-scoring from database composition. [src: functional_dark_matter]
- Score the `mobile` category separately under both schemes, since mobile elements are the class most likely to be inflated by ortholog propagation. [src: functional_dark_matter]

**On rank correlation vs decision stability (Side C):**
- Report both statistics as a standard pair for every conservation metric variant: global Spearman ρ plus top-k overlap at the k actually used for candidate selection, given that ρ = 0.982 coexisted with 62% and 58% overlap at k = 50 and k = 100. [src: functional_dark_matter]
- Bootstrap the top-50 and top-100 lists across metric variants to estimate how many nominations are stable under resampling, and define a selection rule that only promotes genes appearing in the intersection. [src: functional_dark_matter]
- Test whether the non-overlapping fraction of the top-k is enriched for any particular conservation category, which would convert an apparent instability into an interpretable stratum. [src: functional_dark_matter]

**On core membership vs breadth, and on fitness-based calibration (Sides D/E):**
- Cross-tabulate clade-core/auxiliary status against the GTDB conservation categories for the genes carrying both labels, then recompute the essentiality enrichment within each category to see whether the median odds ratio of 1.56 is uniform or concentrated in one stratum. [src: conservation_vs_fitness, functional_dark_matter]
- Model fitness-effect *tails* (not means) as the response variable against conservation category, directly testing the claim that core genes carry heavier fitness-effect tails in both directions rather than uniformly greater importance. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- Re-run the trade-off analysis under varied cut points — burden thresholds other than fitness above 1, and DIAMOND identity thresholds below 90% — to quantify how much of the costly-conserved-gene set is threshold-dependent and how many rapidly evolving genes are recovered. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]
- Stratify Fitness Browser conditions by how far they depart from the organism's described natural habitat, and ask whether the conservation–fitness relationship strengthens in the more natural subset; this is the concrete test of whether condition-specific fitness can be promoted to niche-specific fitness. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis, core_gene_tradeoffs]
- Re-test module-family breadth against conservation within single conservation categories rather than globally, since a global null (rho=-0.01, p=0.91) can mask offsetting within-stratum relationships. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]
