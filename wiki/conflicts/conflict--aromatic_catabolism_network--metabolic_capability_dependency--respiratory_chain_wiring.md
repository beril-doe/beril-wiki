# NDH-2 compensation: a mechanistic explanation for variable Complex I dependence, or a pattern that does not generalize across species?

Two projects disagree about whether the presence of NDH-2 — a type II NADH dehydrogenase, an alternative NADH-reoxidizing enzyme that works in parallel with Complex I (NDH-1) — explains why Complex I is strongly required on some carbon sources and dispensable on others. One project proposes NDH-2 compensation as the mechanism behind substrate-specific Complex I dependence. The other grouped organisms by NDH-2 presence after filtering and compared their Complex I aromatic deficits, finding the difference pointing opposite to the compensation prediction and not significant, on a small group of NDH-2-lacking organisms. The disagreement matters because it is a test case for the broader question tracked on [[concepts/cross-species-fitness-transferability]]: whether a mechanism inferred in one well-characterized organism can be assumed to operate in relatives that carry the same genes. A third project contributes a related limitation rather than a third side, and is recorded below as a constraint on how either side can be tested, not as evidence for either. This page covers that single disagreement plus the constraint.

## Evidence Sides

**Side A — NDH-2 compensation explains lower Complex I dependence on some substrates (hypothesis).** The aromatic-catabolism analysis proposes that NDH-2 compensation can explain lower Complex I dependence on some substrates. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] The input tension text characterizes this as a substrate- and architecture-based hypothesis — grounded in reasoning about substrates and respiratory-chain wiring rather than in a cross-species test of the compensation prediction. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

**Side B — The cross-species comparison found no compensation pattern.** The cross-species respiratory-chain comparison found no supporting compensation pattern: among organisms retained after filtering, validated NDH-2 organisms had a larger mean Complex I aromatic deficit (-0.297 versus -0.156), with p = 0.52. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] The ordering is opposite to what compensation predicts, and the difference is not significant. The same project flags its own limit: the comparison included only 4 organisms lacking NDH-2 and may therefore be underpowered; the two results should not be reconciled as a universal rule. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] The net effect is that Side B weakens the generalization of Side A across species. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]

**Constraint on testing either side — pathway presence is not pathway dependence.** The metabolic capability analysis introduces a related limitation rather than resolving this tension: pathway-level conservation did not distinguish latent capabilities from active dependencies, with mean conservation of 0.869 for 248 latent capabilities versus 0.829 for 755 active dependencies and Mann–Whitney U p = 0.94 for active > latent (Mann–Whitney U is a rank-based test of whether one group's values tend to exceed another's). [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] Thus, conserved pathway presence alone does not establish conserved fitness dependence, supporting the caution that ortholog transfer cannot substitute for recipient-specific network and condition measurements. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]

## Possible Reconciliations

Each of the following is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated resolution.

- **Power, not absence of effect (measurement).** With only 4 organisms lacking NDH-2, the comparison may lack the sensitivity to detect a real compensation effect, and p = 0.52 would then be uninformative rather than negative — a reading the cross-species project's own underpowering caveat leaves open. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] Under this hypothesis both sides can be right: the mechanism operates, and the test could not see it.
- **Presence versus activity (definitional).** Both sides could hold if scoring an organism as carrying NDH-2 is a poor proxy for NDH-2 actually carrying flux there. The capability-versus-dependency null gives the general form of this failure mode — conserved presence does not establish conserved dependence — and suggests, as a hypothesis, that the same gap applies to NDH-2 calls. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]
- **Scope restriction (scope).** Compensation may hold within the substrate set and architecture where it was proposed [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] while failing as a cross-species rule — which is why the cross-species project declines to reconcile the two results as a universal rule. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]
- **Confounding by deficit magnitude (measurement).** Organisms validated as carrying NDH-2 could differ systematically in other respiratory or ecological properties that drive the larger mean aromatic deficit (-0.297 versus -0.156) independently of NDH-2. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]

## Resolving Work

- Expand the NDH-2-absent group well beyond the 4 organisms available, then re-run the same Complex I aromatic-deficit comparison with a pre-specified power analysis, asking whether the -0.297 versus -0.156 ordering persists or reverses once the smaller group is adequately sampled. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]
- Replace NDH-2 presence calls with direct activity evidence in each compared organism and re-test compensation using activity rather than gene presence as the predictor — the specific gap the capability-versus-dependency null identifies. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring, metabolic_capability_dependency]
- Measure Complex I fitness directly in a recipient organism carrying NDH-2, under the aromatic and non-aromatic conditions at issue, rather than relying on ortholog transfer, which the constraint above argues cannot substitute for recipient-specific network and condition measurements. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network, metabolic_capability_dependency]
- Test the mechanism within a single genetic background by removing NDH-2 and asking whether Complex I dependence rises on the substrates where it is otherwise low — separating the mechanism claim from the cross-species generalization claim. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network, respiratory_chain_wiring]
- Repeat the latent-versus-active conservation comparison (0.869 for 248 latent capabilities versus 0.829 for 755 active dependencies, p = 0.94) restricted to respiratory-chain pathways, to check whether the undifferentiated result also holds for the gene set this conflict turns on. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]
