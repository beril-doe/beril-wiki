# Whole-genome gene content versus prophage modules: a scope disagreement over where environmental structure appears

Two lines of work in this corpus point in different directions about whether bacterial gene content tracks environment. Whole-genome comparisons found weak or usually nonsignificant environment–gene-content relationships, while an analysis restricted to prophage gene modules — clusters of phage-derived genes integrated in bacterial genomes, grouped by function such as packaging, lysis or tail morphogenesis — found an environmental effect after genome-size and family-level comparisons. The disagreement is one of scope rather than direct contradiction: the two analyses do not share the same genomic denominator, so the prophage signal could refine the whole-genome result or merely evade it. It matters for [[concepts/ecotype-clustering-validity]], which withholds validation from gene-content ecotypes: whether a module-level exception is genuine changes what "ecotype" can be claimed to mean.

## Evidence Sides

**Whole-genome gene content: weak or usually nonsignificant environmental relationship.** Whole-genome comparisons found weak or usually nonsignificant environment–gene-content relationships, in contrast to prophage-module composition, which showed an environmental effect after genome-size and family-level comparisons. [src: ecotype_analysis, ecotype_env_reanalysis, prophage_ecology] The whole-genome side is reported in exactly that disjunctive form — weak, or not significant at all — and neither branch is dropped here.

**Prophage-module composition: an environmental effect that survives controls.** Prophage-module composition showed an environmental effect after genome-size and family-level comparisons — that is, after accounting for genome size and after restricting comparisons within taxonomic families — whereas whole-genome comparisons found weak or usually nonsignificant environment–gene-content relationships. [src: ecotype_analysis, ecotype_env_reanalysis, prophage_ecology] The project reporting this signal also states its own limit: the evidence cannot determine whether the difference reflects genuine concentration of ecological adaptation in prophage modules, annotation or sampling differences, or residual confounding, and it therefore does not justify treating prophage-module structure as validated ecotype structure. [src: prophage_ecology]

## Possible Reconciliations

- **Concentration hypothesis.** Ecological adaptation may be genuinely concentrated in mobile, environmentally variable modules, so a whole-genome average dilutes a real signal that survives when the denominator is narrowed. [src: prophage_ecology]
- **Annotation-and-sampling hypothesis.** The difference may arise because prophage modules are annotated and sampled differently from whole gene content, producing an apparent effect without a difference in underlying biology. [src: prophage_ecology]
- **Residual-confounding hypothesis.** Genome-size and family-level controls may not remove all structure shared between environment and lineage, leaving confounding that the module analysis inherits. [src: prophage_ecology]

## Resolving Work

- Apply the prophage-module analysis pipeline unchanged to an equally sized, randomly drawn set of non-prophage gene modules from the same genomes: does the environmental effect persist only in prophage modules?
- Re-run the whole-genome comparison on the prophage-carrying subset with the same genome-size and family-level controls: does the null hold when the controls are matched?
- Test annotation sensitivity by re-deriving prophage modules under an alternative annotation source and re-testing the environmental effect: is the effect annotation-dependent?
- Add explicit phylogenetic controls beyond family-level restriction to the module analysis: does the environmental effect survive when shared ancestry is modelled continuously?
- Assess whether module-level environmental structure is discrete or continuous before any ecotype claim: do modules cluster into separated groups, or vary along a gradient?
