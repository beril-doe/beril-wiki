# Shared Biology or Shared Dispensability — Eleven Unresolved Disagreements About What Fitness Correlations Mean

Almost every claim in this corpus about gene–gene structure rests on the same inferential step: two genes whose fitness profiles move together, or whose presence/absence patterns co-vary, are treated as functionally coupled. The tensions recorded on [[concepts/cofitness-network-architecture]] all attack that step from different directions — some argue the correlation is a co-regulation signal, some that it is shared dispensability, shared stress physiology, an annotation-threshold artifact, a phylogenetic autocorrelation, or a laboratory-only cost with no counterpart in nature. This page records all eleven distinct disagreements present in that Tensions block; none are omitted. They are grouped because they are not independent: a resolution of the null-model design (§1) would change the reading of the metal (§2), defense (§4), and co-inheritance (§8) results, and the measurement-boundary disputes (§5, §7, §11) determine whether the biological disputes are even well-posed. Numbers below are copied exactly from the concept page's tension text with its citations; no figures are imported from the underlying reports, and sentences that reason about *why* two sides are incompatible carry no source tag because they are this page's analysis rather than reported evidence.

## Evidence Sides

### 1. Cofitness enrichment: co-regulation or shared dispensability?

**Side A — the enrichment is biological co-regulation.** The project's cofitness analysis reports enrichment of flagellar motility, chemotaxis, and amino-acid biosynthesis — "cofitness" meaning correlation between two genes' fitness profiles across a panel of growth conditions. Read as co-regulation, the enrichment says that these genes sit under shared transcriptional control, and that a cofitness edge is therefore evidence about regulatory architecture. [src: amr_cofitness_networks]

**Side B — the enrichment is shared dispensability.** The same three categories are among those whose genes carry little or no fitness cost in permissive laboratory conditions. Read this way, the genes correlate because they are jointly *unselected*, not jointly *controlled*, and the cofitness edge is evidence about experimental design rather than about biology. The project's own null model was matched on conservation class rather than on mean fitness, so it does not discriminate between the two readings. [src: amr_cofitness_networks]

**Why the two sides cannot both be assumed.** The sides are not distinguished by the enrichment table — both predict exactly the same list of over-represented categories — which is what makes the disagreement hard. They differ only in the counterfactual: under co-regulation, the correlation should survive a null that holds the genes' typical fitness magnitude fixed and should strengthen in conditions where motility and amino-acid biosynthesis are actually selected; under shared dispensability, the correlation should collapse once mean fitness is matched, because the shared component is the shared absence of selection. Because the reported null controls conservation class instead, the observed enrichment is compatible with either generative story, and every downstream claim built on these neighborhoods — including the network-size analyses in §9 — inherits the ambiguity. The project states the ambiguity rather than resolving it. [src: amr_cofitness_networks]

### 2. Metal cross-resistance: a specific shared mechanism or a general stress response?

**Side A — universal, directionally conserved cross-resistance.** All tested metal pairs were positive, which the project reads as genuine shared metal-handling biology. But no negative controls were included — no metal pair expected to trade off — so universal cross-resistance cannot be distinguished from a general-stress response. [src: metal_cross_resistance]

**Side B — shared stress biology reaching beyond the metals themselves.** The counter-ion study supports shared cellular stress biology and reports 4,304/10,821 shared NaCl–metal records; zinc sulfate had 44.6% overlap despite 0 mM chloride. [src: counter_ion_effects]

**Why the two sides cannot both be assumed.** A design in which every tested pair comes out positive has no internal calibration: a universally positive result is the signature both of a genuinely universal mechanism and of a measurement in which any two perturbations correlate because they share a generic growth-rate or stress axis. Without a pair predicted to be negative, the result cannot falsify the generic-stress alternative. The counter-ion evidence sharpens rather than settles this. Its zinc-sulfate finding removes one specific confound — the shared overlap is not carried by the chloride counter-ion, since the overlap persists at 0 mM chloride — which supports a real shared-response component. [src: counter_ion_effects] But the same finding pushes the shared component further away from metal-specific chemistry and toward a response that sodium chloride also engages, which is precisely the general-stress reading that Side A cannot exclude. The two studies thus agree that something is shared and disagree about whether "shared" licenses the phrase *cross-resistance*.

### 3. Does cross-resistance predict where organisms are found?

**Side A — no organism-scale validation.** Multi-metal tolerance scores did not correlate with BacDive isolation from metal environments at the Fitness Browser species scale (Spearman rho approximately -0.02, p > 0.8); after matching and collapsing strains, only 20 independent species remained, although the limitations report n = 26 at organism scale. [src: metal_cross_resistance]

**Side B — a prior pangenome-scale positive.** The prior Metal Fitness Atlas pangenome-scale result reported Cohen's d = +1.0 across 42K strains. [src: metal_cross_resistance]

**Why the two sides cannot both be assumed.** The contradiction is not between the two numbers but between the two inferences drawn from them. A rho of approximately -0.02 with p > 0.8 is a null: at the Fitness Browser species scale, laboratory metal tolerance carries no detectable information about whether an organism was isolated from a metal-rich environment. That null directly contradicts treating cross-resistance as already validated by isolation environment. It does not contradict the pangenome-scale result itself, which was measured on a different substrate and at a different scale. [src: metal_cross_resistance] The live disagreement is therefore about scope: a claim of the form "metal cross-resistance is ecologically real" is supported at 42K strains and unsupported at 20 species, and the corpus currently contains both statements without a rule for which applies to a given organism-level assertion. A second, internal disagreement compounds this: the analysis-ready sample size is reported as 20 independent species after matching and collapsing strains, and as n = 26 at organism scale in the limitations, so even the power of the null test is stated two ways in the same project. [src: metal_cross_resistance] Until one analysis-ready n is fixed, the strength of the null cannot be quantified, and a null of unknown power cannot be weighed against a large prior effect.

### 4. Anti-phage defense co-occurrence: real syndromes, or detection and phylogeny artifacts?

**Side A — coordinated defense architecture.** Twenty-seven of 28 defense pairings were significant and R-M Type II (restriction–modification) × Gabija was strong, consistent with defense systems assembling into recurrent syndromes. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

**Side B — the prevalence estimate is detection-dependent and the test is not phylogenetically corrected.** CRISPR-Cas prevalence changes from 96% with EggNOG description matching to approximately 55% with Cas1 PF01867 (a Pfam protein-domain model), and the analysis controls for phylum rather than using phylogenetically corrected regression. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

**Why the two sides cannot both be assumed.** Two independent problems stack here. First, presence/absence co-occurrence statistics are computed on binary calls, so a detection method that nearly doubles prevalence redefines the contingency tables from which all 28 tests are drawn; a pairing significant under one calling rule is not guaranteed to be significant under the other, and the corpus does not report the tests recomputed on the stricter domain-based set. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] Second, controlling for phylum treats every genome within a phylum as an independent draw. Defense systems are gained and lost along lineages, so two systems inherited together in a single successful clade will co-occur across the genomes of that clade without any functional relationship between them — an association that phylum-level stratification cannot remove but a phylogenetically corrected regression is designed to absorb. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] Side A's near-uniform significance (27 of 28) is itself the kind of result that non-independence tends to produce, which is why it cannot be read as 27 independent confirmations of coordinated architecture.

### 5. Essential genes and ICA modules: a biological absence or a measurement boundary?

**Side A — essentiality and modularity do not overlap.** Zero essential genes appeared in ICA modules — ICA being independent component analysis, a decomposition of a fitness matrix into co-varying gene modules — while essential-genome analyses independently identify both universally and variably essential gene families. [src: module_conservation; essential_genome]

**Side B — the comparison marks a measurement boundary, not a biological contradiction.** The tension text records this explicitly as a measurement-boundary tension rather than a biological one. [src: module_conservation; essential_genome]

**Side C — at gene-set level the sign reverses.** Truly dark genes show the opposite comparison at the gene-set level — 18.0% essential versus 13.4% for annotation-lag genes — but short genes and insertion bias complicate interpretation. [src: truly_dark_genes]

**Why the two sides cannot both be assumed.** A zero is the strongest possible effect size and the weakest possible evidence when it is entailed by the method. If module membership requires a measured fitness profile, and an essential gene is by definition one that cannot be disrupted to yield a profile, then "zero essential genes in modules" is a restatement of the inclusion criterion rather than a finding about whether essential genes participate in coordinated cellular processes. The corpus contains both the zero and the independently derived essential families, so the two results sit side by side without a stated rule for which questions the zero may be used to answer. [src: module_conservation; essential_genome] Side C complicates the picture in a third way: measured on gene sets rather than modules, essentiality and functional obscurity are positively rather than negatively associated (18.0% versus 13.4%), and the named confounders — short gene length and transposon insertion bias — are the same technical factors that determine whether a gene yields insertions at all. [src: truly_dark_genes] The disagreement is therefore about whether any of these three numbers is a statement about biology or all three are statements about insertion coverage.

### 6. Essentiality: a universal core, or mostly organism-private?

**Side A — a small universal essential core exists.** Fifteen families were essential in all 48 organisms. [src: essential_genome]

**Side B — essentiality is overwhelmingly lineage-specific and often unrecognizable.** 4,799 families were variably essential and 7,084 essential genes had no detectable ortholog in another Fitness Browser organism. Orphan essentials were 49.5% core versus 91.7% for universally essential genes, and 58.7% of orphan essentials were hypothetical versus 8.2%. [src: essential_genome]

**Side C — orphan status may be a method artifact.** Conservative BBH (bidirectional best hit) orthology can miss divergent homologs, so orphan status is not proof of novelty. [src: essential_genome]

**Why the two sides cannot both be assumed.** The three figures pull the concept of "essential gene" in incompatible directions. Fifteen families essential in all 48 organisms describes essentiality as a conserved property of the cell; 4,799 variably essential families and 7,084 orphan essentials describe it as a property of an organism-condition pair, largely private to lineages. [src: essential_genome] The two internal contrasts sharpen the split: orphan essentials are far less likely to be core in their own pangenome than universally essential genes (49.5% versus 91.7%), and far more likely to be annotated as hypothetical (58.7% versus 8.2%). [src: essential_genome] Read one way, that profile describes fast-evolving, genuinely lineage-specific essential functions; read the other way, it describes exactly the population a conservative orthology method fails on — poorly annotated, divergent sequences that BBH declines to match. Side C is not a counter-claim with its own number; it is the observation that the same evidence pattern is produced by genuine novelty and by detection failure, and that the project's method cannot separate them. [src: essential_genome] Any downstream use of the orphan-essential set — as a discovery pool, or as evidence that essentiality is lineage-specific — currently assumes one of these readings without testing it.

### 7. Process inference versus gene-level identity (and how far the dark-gene catalog reaches)

**Side A — fitness-based inference performs poorly at gene-level assignment.** Module-ICA and cofitness voting had <1% strict KEGG KO precision, KEGG KO (KEGG Orthology) being the gene-level functional identifier against which the predictions were scored. [src: fitness_modules]

**Side B — orthology transfer does the gene-level job.** Ortholog transfer achieved 95.8% precision, 91.2% coverage, and 0.934 F1. [src: fitness_modules]

**Side C — hypothesis generation, not assignment.** Functional-dark-matter GapMind (pathway-gap analysis) and domain results, and truly-dark eggNOG or phenotype clues, identify hypotheses rather than direct gene-to-step assignments. [src: functional_dark_matter; truly_dark_genes] The Agora's M26 tree-based donor inference is exploratory rather than donor identification. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

**Side D — the catalog's coverage is taxonomically narrow and partly estimated.** 57,011 dark genes were found, but the Fitness Browser contained 37/48 Pseudomonadota and no Archaea, Actinobacteria, or Epsilonproteobacteria among the top 500 candidates. [src: functional_dark_matter] The truly-dark analysis adds a 17,479-gene pangenome-linkage gap and estimates approximately 2,841 additional truly dark genes among them at the 16.3% linked rate; this is an estimate, not an observed count. [src: truly_dark_genes]

**Why the sides cannot both be assumed.** The gap between <1% and 95.8% precision on the same benchmark is too large to be a tuning difference, and it forces a choice about what fitness correlations are for. If fitness structure is a function-prediction method, the corpus's own benchmark says it is close to useless and orthology should be used instead. [src: fitness_modules] If it is a process-level signal — informative about which cellular process a gene serves without naming the step — then a gene-level benchmark is the wrong instrument, but the corpus has not reported the process-level score that would support that defense. Sides C and D then bound how much of the remaining dark-gene program can be claimed as result: GapMind, domain, eggNOG and phenotype clues are explicitly hypotheses [src: functional_dark_matter; truly_dark_genes], the M26 donor inference is explicitly exploratory [src: gene_function_ecological_agora], the catalog's taxonomic base is dominated by 37/48 Pseudomonadota with three major groups absent from the top 500 [src: functional_dark_matter], and approximately 2,841 of the genes in circulation are an extrapolation at the 16.3% linked rate rather than counted objects. [src: truly_dark_genes]

### 8. Pairwise versus module-level co-inheritance, and the ancestry confound

**Side A — pairwise cofitness barely predicts co-occurrence.** Pairwise cofit pairs had mean delta phi +0.011 across organisms — phi being the co-occurrence coefficient between two genes' presence/absence patterns, and delta phi its excess over matched random pairs — but aggregate delta +0.003 and Wilcoxon p=0.13. [src: cofitness_coinheritance]

**Side B — modules are the stronger unit.** ICA modules had delta phi=+0.053 overall and accessory modules +0.108. [src: cofitness_coinheritance]

**Side C — the module advantage does not generalize.** Korea had no significant modules because all were >90% core with prevalence near 1.0, and accessory-versus-core differences were only near significant (p=0.051). [src: cofitness_coinheritance] Only 5% of mapped modules were accessory, so accessory-module advantages cannot be generalized. [src: module_conservation]

**Side D — coupling is confounded by ancestry.** Near genomes had mean phi=0.102 versus 0.067 for medium-distance genomes, and most organisms lacked a far stratum. [src: cofitness_coinheritance] The metal study likewise requires PGLS (phylogenetic generalized least squares — regression that models shared ancestry as correlated error) or independent contrasts; its organisms were not phylogenetically independent, matrices ranged from 3 to 112 metal experiments, and metal concentrations differed. [src: metal_cross_resistance]

**Why the sides cannot both be assumed.** Side A is close to a null — an aggregate delta of +0.003 at p=0.13 does not establish that cofit genes are co-inherited — while Side B reports an order-of-magnitude larger effect on the same quantity for modules, and a larger one still for the accessory subset. [src: cofitness_coinheritance] Either modules capture a real evolutionary unit that pairs miss, or aggregation is changing the noise properties of the estimate rather than its signal; the corpus reports the contrast but not a test that separates these. Side C then removes most of the ground under the accessory result: the strongest number (+0.108) is measured on about 5% of mapped modules [src: module_conservation], the core-versus-accessory contrast that would justify reading it as a category effect is only near significant at p=0.051, and at least one organism has no accessory modules to test because its modules are >90% core with prevalence near 1.0. [src: cofitness_coinheritance] Side D attacks all of the above from underneath: phi is higher among near genomes (0.102) than medium-distance ones (0.067), so co-occurrence tracks relatedness directly, and with most organisms lacking a far stratum the relatedness gradient cannot be modeled out of the existing data. [src: cofitness_coinheritance] The metal analysis carries the same non-independence, plus heterogeneous matrices of 3 to 112 experiments and differing concentrations, which makes its per-organism estimates unequally precise as well as correlated. [src: metal_cross_resistance]

### 9. Laboratory fitness cost versus selection in nature

**Side A — conservation tracks laboratory burden.** 28,017 genes were costly in the laboratory and conserved in the pangenome versus 5,526 costly and dispensable genes; these are evidence for, not direct measurements of, purifying selection in natural environments. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

**Side B — resistance cost is uniform and unexplained by network context.** Network size does not explain AMR fitness cost: Spearman rho = −0.006, p = 0.87, N = 769; within mechanisms, rho = −0.049 for efflux, +0.038 for enzymatic resistance, and −0.031 for metal resistance, all p > 0.4. Uniform resistance cost was +0.086 and was not explained by neighborhood size. [src: amr_cofitness_networks] Across 801 AMR genes and 25 organisms, pooled knockout fitness shift was +0.086 [+0.074, +0.098]; cost was mechanism-independent (Kruskal–Wallis p=0.89), conservation-independent (p=0.33), and tier-independent (p=0.26). [src: discoveries]

**Side C — but mechanism does matter conditionally.** Efflux genes showed a stronger antibiotic-dependent flip than enzymatic inactivation genes, +0.094 versus -0.001, Mann–Whitney U p=0.007. [src: discoveries]

**Side D — the field test is underpowered.** Only 190/974 species (19.5%) formed at least two AMR ecotypes, and environmental association testing was limited by 52.7% of genomes lacking a classifiable isolation_source. [src: amr_strain_variation]

**Why the sides cannot both be assumed.** Side A's 28,017-versus-5,526 contrast is an association between two measurements — laboratory cost and pangenome conservation — from which selection in nature is inferred, and the project marks the inference as such. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] Side B measures the cost side directly and finds it structurally featureless: a pooled +0.086 [+0.074, +0.098] shift that does not vary with mechanism, conservation, or tier, and a network-size association indistinguishable from zero at N = 769. [src: amr_cofitness_networks; discoveries] A cost that is uniform across exactly the categories that should modulate selection is hard to read as a proxy for natural selection; it is more consistent with a laboratory-condition constant. Side C is the one result that breaks the uniformity, and it breaks it conditionally: under antibiotic exposure the sign separates by mechanism (+0.094 versus -0.001, p=0.007), which shows that the constant is condition-specific rather than intrinsic. [src: discoveries] Side D then blocks the obvious arbitration — testing the association against real environments — because the ecotype structure exists in only 19.5% of species and more than half of genomes carry no classifiable isolation_source. [src: amr_strain_variation]

### 10. Metabolic model versus measured phenotype in ADP1

**Side A — the model says the complex is dispensable.** FBA (flux balance analysis — constraint-based prediction of metabolic fluxes from a stoichiometric network) predicted 0% Complex I essentiality, and 30/51 network genes lacked FBA reaction mappings. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

**Side B — the assay says otherwise.** 10/13 Complex I subunits produced quinate-specific defects. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

**Side C — the proposed rescue fails.** The corrected cross-species test did not support the NDH-2 compensation hypothesis: aromatic deficits were -0.297 with validated NDH-2 versus -0.156 without, p=0.52. [src: discoveries]

**Why the sides cannot both be assumed.** This is the corpus's cleanest model-versus-measurement conflict: a prediction of 0% essentiality and an observation of defects in 10 of 13 subunits cannot both describe the same system under the same condition. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] The disagreement matters beyond ADP1 because FBA predictions are used across this corpus as a stand-in for what a genome "should" require, and a 0% prediction against a 10/13 phenotype sets a lower bound on how far that stand-in can be trusted for energetic rather than stoichiometric requirements. The 30/51 unmapped genes are not a side issue: a majority-unmapped network means the model is being asked about a system it only partially represents, so the prediction's failure may be a scope failure rather than a modeling error — but the corpus cannot currently tell these apart. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Side C removes the leading biological reconciliation. If an alternative NADH dehydrogenase compensated for Complex I loss, the aromatic deficit should be milder where a validated NDH-2 is present; the corrected test found deficits of -0.297 with validated NDH-2 versus -0.156 without, at p=0.52 — a non-significant contrast whose point estimates run opposite to the hypothesis's prediction. [src: discoveries] That is a failure to support, not a refutation, so the compensation route remains open but unevidenced.

### 11. Is a clade-level architecture label rank-invariant, and does it reproduce Alm 2006?

**Side A — labels hold across ranks.** Cyanobacteriia × PSII was STABLE at genus, family, and order despite class-level producer d = +1.50 and consumer d = +0.70. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

**Side B — labels move with aggregation rank.** The Mycobacteriaceae mycolic-acid signal varied with aggregation rank; these findings contradict treating a clade-level architecture label as rank-invariant. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

**Side C — the external quantitative benchmark does not reproduce.** Four framings produced Pearson r values of 0.288, 0.157, 0.100, and 0.105 rather than the reported r ≈ 0.74; consumer-side KO-to-architecture concordance remained r = 0.673, while producer concordance was exploratory at r = 0.093. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

**Why the sides cannot both be assumed.** An architecture label attached to a clade is only meaningful if it names a property of the clade rather than a property of the rank at which the clade was aggregated. The corpus supplies one case where the label is stable across three ranks and one where it is not, in the same analysis framework [src: gene_function_ecological_agora], which means rank-invariance cannot be assumed as a general property and must be reported per clade. Side C compounds this at the level of external validity. Four alternative framings of the same comparison span r = 0.100 to r = 0.288 and none approaches the reported r ≈ 0.74, so the framing choice moves the headline statistic by roughly a factor of three and the corpus has no principled basis for preferring one framing. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] The internal asymmetry is equally unresolved: consumer-side concordance reaches r = 0.673 while producer concordance sits at r = 0.093 and is labeled exploratory [src: gene_function_ecological_agora] — a method that performs on one side of a producer/consumer distinction and not the other is more consistent with a definitional problem on the producer side than with a uniform biological signal.

## Possible Reconciliations

Each item below is a hypothesis, not a finding.

1. **Co-regulation and dispensability are not exclusive.** Regulons that are conditionally off in permissive media are simultaneously co-regulated and jointly dispensable; the enrichment could be real co-regulation that is only *detectable* because dispensability creates fitness variance. Under this hypothesis a mean-fitness-matched null would attenuate but not erase the enrichment. [src: amr_cofitness_networks]
2. **Scope difference between metal-specific and generic stress.** Metal pairs may share a specific ion-handling layer nested inside a broader stress-response layer that NaCl also engages; the zinc sulfate 44.6% overlap at 0 mM chloride is consistent with the broader layer dominating the shared component. [src: counter_ion_effects; metal_cross_resistance]
3. **Scale difference in the environment test.** The null at Fitness Browser species scale (20 independent species) and the Metal Fitness Atlas positive (42K strains) may both be right if the effect is real but small relative to the variance among 20 species, or if isolation-source labels resolve environments only at pangenome scale. [src: metal_cross_resistance]
4. **Definitional difference in defense-system calling.** EggNOG-description and Cas1-domain detection may be measuring different objects — "any CRISPR-associated annotation" versus "a system with an identifiable Cas1" — so 96% and approximately 55% could both be accurate answers to different questions, with the co-occurrence result stable only for the subset detected by both. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]
5. **The essentiality/module disjunction is definitional, not biological.** If fitness-based modules can only contain genes with viable insertion mutants, zero overlap is entailed by the method; the truly-dark 18.0%-versus-13.4% comparison is measured on gene sets rather than modules and so need not conflict. [src: module_conservation; essential_genome; truly_dark_genes]
6. **Orphan essentials as detection failure plus genuine innovation.** The 58.7%-hypothetical enrichment among orphan essentials is equally consistent with fast-evolving genuinely private essentials and with divergent homologs that BBH cannot recover; the two populations may coexist in proportions knowable only after sensitive profile search. [src: essential_genome]
7. **Different information granularity.** Fitness covariance may carry process-level information while orthology carries identity-level information, making <1% KO precision and 95.8% transfer precision compatible measurements of different quantities. [src: fitness_modules]
8. **Aggregation as noise reduction.** Modules may beat pairs because averaging across module members suppresses per-pair measurement noise rather than because modules are the true evolutionary unit; the near-significant accessory-versus-core contrast (p=0.051) and the 5% accessory share leave this open. [src: cofitness_coinheritance; module_conservation]
9. **Laboratory cost as a condition-specific constant.** A uniform +0.086 knockout shift in the laboratory and pangenome conservation can coexist if natural environments supply the conditions under which the same genes pay — the efflux-versus-enzymatic conditional flip (+0.094 versus -0.001) is direct evidence that condition changes sign. [src: discoveries; conservation_fitness_synthesis]
10. **Model-scope mismatch.** With 30/51 network genes unmapped, a 0% Complex I essentiality prediction may fall outside the model's representational scope rather than be wrong about metabolism, if the measured quinate-specific defects in 10/13 subunits reflect requirements not encoded as reactions. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]
11. **Rank-dependence as a property of the clade, not of the method.** Architecture labels may be rank-invariant for clades whose function is uniformly distributed (Cyanobacteriia × PSII, STABLE at genus, family, and order) and rank-dependent for heterogeneous clades (Mycobacteriaceae mycolic acid); an Alm-scale correlation averaged over clades of both kinds could then fall in the 0.100–0.288 range without either the original r ≈ 0.74 or the replication being an error. [src: gene_function_ecological_agora]

## Resolving Work

### 1. Co-regulation versus shared dispensability
- Rebuild the cofitness null matched on **mean fitness and fitness variance** rather than conservation class, and re-test the flagellar/chemotaxis/amino-acid enrichment. [src: amr_cofitness_networks]
- Intersect cofitness neighborhoods with independently derived regulons (operon structure, transcription-factor binding) and ask whether enrichment survives when shared-regulon pairs are removed.
- Stratify the enrichment by condition class: under the dispensability reading it should be strongest in permissive conditions and weaken where motility and amino-acid biosynthesis are selected.
- Re-run the analysis in organisms and conditions where flagellar genes are known to be under selection, as a positive control that a mean-fitness-matched null should still detect.

### 2. Metal cross-resistance versus general stress
- Add explicit **negative-control stressor pairs** — pairs with an expected trade-off — to the metal cross-resistance design; the absence of any negative pair is the crux of the disagreement. [src: metal_cross_resistance]
- Partial out a generic-stress axis (e.g. the first component of a non-metal stress panel) from every metal–metal correlation and report the residual metal-specific correlation.
- Repeat the counter-ion decomposition for additional non-chloride salts to test whether the zinc sulfate result at 0 mM chloride generalizes. [src: counter_ion_effects]
- Compare gene-level overlap sets: which specific genes drive the 4,304/10,821 shared NaCl–metal records versus metal–metal sharing, and are they the same genes? [src: counter_ion_effects]

### 3. Metal tolerance versus isolation environment
- Resolve the sample-size discrepancy first — 20 independent species after matching and collapsing strains versus n = 26 reported at organism scale — and state one analysis-ready n. [src: metal_cross_resistance]
- Power-analyze the Spearman test at that n: what effect size could it have detected at p < 0.05, given the observed rho of approximately -0.02? [src: metal_cross_resistance]
- Re-test the association at pangenome scale by projecting the Fitness Browser tolerance score onto the 42K-strain set, using one isolation-source ontology on both sides. [src: metal_cross_resistance]
- Curate metal-environment labels manually for the matched organisms rather than relying on BacDive free-text isolation fields. [src: metal_cross_resistance]

### 4. Anti-phage defense detection and phylogeny
- Recompute all 28 pairwise co-occurrence tests on the Cas1 PF01867-defined CRISPR-Cas set and report how many of the 27 significant pairings survive. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]
- Replace phylum controls with a phylogenetically corrected model (PGLS or phylogenetic logistic regression) on the species tree. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]
- Benchmark both detection routes against a curated gold standard of manually validated defense loci to establish which of 96% and approximately 55% is closer to truth. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]
- Test whether the R-M Type II × Gabija association holds within single genera, where shared ancestry is nearly constant. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

### 5. Essential genes and modules
- Annotate every module-eligible gene set with the count of genes excluded for lack of insertion mutants, converting the zero overlap into a quantified exclusion. [src: module_conservation]
- Build modules from an essentiality-compatible signal (e.g. knockdown rather than knockout fitness) and ask whether essential genes then form modules. [src: module_conservation; essential_genome]
- Control the 18.0%-versus-13.4% comparison for gene length and insertion density, since short genes and insertion bias are the named confounders. [src: truly_dark_genes]
- Repeat the essential-versus-annotation-lag comparison within length-matched strata. [src: truly_dark_genes]

### 6. Universal versus orphan essentiality
- Re-run orthology for the 7,084 orphan essentials with profile HMM or structure-based search instead of BBH, and report how many remain orphan. [src: essential_genome]
- Test whether the 58.7%-hypothetical enrichment persists after the sensitive re-search, which distinguishes annotation failure from homology failure. [src: essential_genome]
- Check whether the 49.5%-core figure for orphan essentials rises when pangenomes are rebuilt at matched genome counts per species, given the 91.7% comparison for universally essential genes. [src: essential_genome]
- Ask whether the 15 universally essential families and the 4,799 variably essential families differ in module membership, condition breadth, or acquisition depth. [src: essential_genome]

### 7. Process versus identity, and dark-gene coverage
