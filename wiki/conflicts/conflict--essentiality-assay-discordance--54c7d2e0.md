# Costly conserved genes: direct evidence of selection in nature, or a hypothesis awaiting ecological test?

Two lines of work in this corpus agree on the same category of gene — genes that impose a measurable burden in laboratory growth yet remain conserved across a species' pangenome (the full gene complement of a species, split into core genes present in nearly all strains and dispensable genes present in only some) — but disagree about what that category licenses one to conclude. One reading treats the combination of lab cost and conservation as evidence that natural selection maintains the gene because it is essential in environments the lab does not reproduce. The other holds that the category is inferred from laboratory burden and conservation rather than measured in nature, and that assay discordance — the finding that fitness measurements shift with perturbation modality (transposon insertion versus complete deletion) and with assay condition — means the category can only propose selection, not demonstrate it. The disagreement matters because it determines whether these gene sets are reported as findings about nature or as prioritized hypotheses. It arises from [[concepts/essentiality-assay-discordance]].

## Evidence Sides

**Costly conservation as an inferred selection signature about nature.** The core-gene trade-off work defines a Costly + Conserved category and reads it as natural selection maintaining genes despite lab-measured cost. On its own framing, that category is inferred from laboratory burden and conservation rather than from measurement in natural settings. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]

**Costly conservation as a hypothesis requiring ecological validation.** The assay-discordance evidence shows that laboratory measurements can depend on perturbation modality and condition, so the trade-off result **refines** the interpretation of costly conserved genes without directly establishing natural selection. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] On this side, costly conservation should be treated as a selection hypothesis requiring ecological validation, not as direct evidence that a gene is essential in nature. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] The synthesis **supports** this position: it identifies 28,017 genes as both costly and conserved and 5,526 as costly and dispensable, but explicitly treats these categories as selection signatures rather than direct natural-fitness measurements. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

## Possible Reconciliations

- *Hypothesis:* the two sides differ in claim strength rather than in content — the category is a prioritization of candidates for selection, and the disagreement dissolves once the claim is stated as a hypothesis rather than as a measured natural fitness effect.
- *Hypothesis:* because laboratory measurements can depend on perturbation modality and condition [src: core_gene_tradeoffs], those choices determine which genes enter the "costly" column, so what the discordance puts at risk is the category's membership rather than the reading placed on it.
- *Hypothesis:* the ecological validation that the trade-off side itself calls for [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] is the step that would convert the category from a selection signature into a statement about natural fitness, in which case the two sides describe successive stages of one argument rather than rival conclusions.

## Resolving Work

- Re-derive the costly/neutral split under a second perturbation modality (complete deletion rather than transposon insertion) for a subset of the 28,017 costly and conserved genes [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis], and ask how much of the category is modality-dependent.
- Measure fitness for the same genes across an expanded condition panel, and ask whether "costly" is a condition-specific label or stable across conditions.
- Pair the 5,526 costly and dispensable genes [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] with strain-level presence/absence data, and ask whether they show the loss dynamics that a gene-loss interpretation predicts.
- Test the Costly + Conserved genes in a non-laboratory or environment-simulating assay, and ask whether the inferred selection hypothesis [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] survives ecological validation.
