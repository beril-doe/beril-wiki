# Is NDH-2 Compensation a General Rule or an ADP1-Specific Wiring Pattern?

Both projects treat Complex I — the proton-pumping NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreductase (NDH-1) — as dispensable for growth on glucose, and both are compatible with NDH-2, a single-subunit non-pumping NADH dehydrogenase, absorbing the NADH load under those conditions. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network, respiratory_chain_wiring] The disagreement is not about whether compensation happens in *Acinetobacter baylyi* ADP1; it is about **generality and evidentiary standing** — whether the compensation reading can be carried across species as a rule, and what kind of evidence is entitled to assert it. One side infers compensation from fitness values transferred between organisms by orthology; the other, working in a single organism, reaches the same reading for ADP1 but finds that the cross-species test fails and that the key gene has no direct growth data to test with. This matters because the compensation story is the mechanistic bridge that would turn a single-organism wiring observation into a general principle about how branched respiratory chains absorb reducing-equivalent load — see [[concepts/respiratory-capacity-and-nadh-load]]. If the bridge does not hold, the wiring pattern is a species-specific fact about ADP1. This page covers the single disagreement present in the input tension text.

## Evidence Sides

**Side A — Dispensability on simple substrates is read as evidence of NDH-2 compensation, inferred comparatively.** The earlier transferred-fitness interpretation reports Complex I as dispensable on glucose and lactate and treats this as consistent with NDH-2 compensation. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] The inference rests on ortholog-transferred fitness values rather than on a measurement in the organism where the mechanism is proposed. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

**Side B — Compensation is retained as an ADP1-level reading but denied general status, and cannot be directly tested here.** The new ADP1-specific analysis reports a Complex I growth ratio of 1.44 on glucose, predicts zero NDH-2 flux under standard FBA (flux balance analysis — constraint-based prediction of steady-state reaction rates from a genome-scale metabolic model) conditions, and finds no significant cross-species compensation pattern (p = 0.52). [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] Two qualifications are part of this side's own case rather than objections to it. First, the zero-flux prediction is presented by the source as a limitation of FBA itself — growth optimization prefers the ATP-favorable Complex I route, so the model misses the capacity constraints that would force use of an alternative, suboptimal dehydrogenase — not as evidence against compensation in the cell. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] Second, the cross-species null concerns Complex I dependency on *aromatic* substrates specifically, so it does not test compensation on glucose or lactate. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] Critically, NDH-2 in ADP1 is TnSeq-dispensable but absent from the deletion collection and has no growth data, so the central prediction — that NDH-2 compensates on glucose — cannot be directly tested with this dataset. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] That absence of data, not a contrary measurement, is why the tension is unresolved.

These results do not establish that the earlier interpretation is false: they may reflect different fitness metrics, model assumptions, or species-level respiratory architectures. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]

## Possible Reconciliations

These are hypotheses, not findings.

- **Scope, not truth value.** The compensation claim may hold in ADP1 and fail as a cross-species rule, making both sides correct at their own scopes; the tension explicitly leaves open species-level respiratory architectures as an explanation. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]
- **Condition mismatch.** Side A's claim is about glucose and lactate [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] while the failed cross-species test concerns aromatic dependency [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]; a compensation mechanism operating only at low or distributed NADH flux would produce agreement on simple substrates and no signal on aromatics.
- **Metric mismatch.** Transferred ortholog fitness scores and deletion growth ratios need not agree in sign or magnitude for the same underlying phenotype; the tension text allows that the results may reflect different fitness metrics. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]
- **Model-assumption artifact.** Zero predicted NDH-2 flux may be structural rather than biological — a growth-maximizing solution prefers the proton-pumping route whenever available, which the source names as the reason such models miss condition-specific respiratory requirements. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] The informative prediction would be flux in a Complex I knockout model, which the input does not report.
- **Power, not effect.** p = 0.52 is a failure to reject, not evidence of absence; the source's own limitation is that too few organisms *lacking* NDH-2 were available for the comparison to reach significance. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]
- **Third route.** A dehydrogenase other than NDH-2 may absorb part of the load, leaving Side A's phenotype and Side B's cross-species null simultaneously correct.

## Resolving Work

- **Matched deletion assays.** Construct and assay an ADP1 NDH-2 deletion, a Complex I deletion, and the double mutant across glucose, lactate, and an aromatic substrate, asking whether NDH-2 loss becomes costly only in a Complex I background — the signature of compensation, and the gap left by NDH-2's absence from the deletion collection. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]
- **Direct cofactor measurement.** Measure NADH/NAD⁺ ratios across carbon sources in wild type and in each dehydrogenase mutant; compensation predicts a maintained ratio in the Complex I mutant on glucose and a collapsed one where compensation fails. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]
- **Knockout-constrained FBA.** Re-run the flux balance analysis with Complex I flux forced to zero and ask whether NDH-2 flux becomes nonzero, distinguishing "NDH-2 is unused" from "NDH-2 is unused only while Complex I is available" and testing whether the zero-flux result is an optimization artifact. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]
- **Metric harmonization.** Recompute both sides on a single fitness metric for the overlapping organisms and conditions, so that "dispensable on glucose and lactate" [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] and the growth ratio of 1.44 [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] are expressed on the same scale before being compared.
- **Powered, condition-matched cross-species test.** Enlarge the comparison set — especially organisms lacking NDH-2 — and repeat the test on non-aromatic as well as aromatic conditions, reporting effect size and confidence interval rather than a bare p-value, so the p = 0.52 result can be read as either a null effect or an underpowered test. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]
