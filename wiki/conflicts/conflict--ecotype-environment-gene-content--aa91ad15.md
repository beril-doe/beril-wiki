# Core Fixation Versus Mobilome Load in Plant-Associated Genomes

Two measurements from the same corpus pull in opposite directions about how plant-associated bacteria hold their ecologically important genes. One says the functions that matter for plant benefit are fixed in the **core genome** — the gene set present in essentially all members of a species — implying stable vertical inheritance. The other says the same plant-associated lineages carry a heavier **mobilome** — the complement of mobile elements (plasmids, transposons, integrated phage) that move genes between genomes — implying active horizontal turnover. A third line of evidence, on prophages (phage genomes integrated into a bacterial chromosome), partitions the question by gene module rather than by genome. The disagreement matters for [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]] because it determines whether environmental gene-content signal should be read as inherited ecotype structure or as recent, transferable acquisition.

## Evidence Sides

**Beneficial functions are core-fixed.** Beneficial plant-interaction clusters were 64.6% core versus 45.2% for pathogenic clusters, a direction that places beneficial capability in the stably inherited fraction of the genome and pathogenic capability in the flexible fraction. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

**Plant-associated lineages carry more mobile elements.** The same project reports that plant-associated genera had median mobilome burden 3.7 versus 2.8 mobile elements per genome — a genus-level contrast in the opposite direction, toward greater mobility in exactly the lineages whose beneficial genes are core-enriched. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

**Prophage modules split by function, not by genome.** This result is related but not directly contradictory: anti-defense and structural modules were environmentally variable, while packaging, lysis, and regulation were near-universal and more core-like. [src: prophage_ecology] Mobility and core-likeness are therefore reported as properties of gene modules within a mobile element, not of whole genomes.

## Possible Reconciliations

- *Hypothesis:* the two plant-study figures measure different denominators — gene clusters per functional cohort versus mobile elements per genome — so a genome can be both mobile-element-rich and core-fixed in its beneficial repertoire, with no arithmetic conflict. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
- *Hypothesis:* mobile elements are the delivery route and the core genome the destination; beneficial functions arrive horizontally and are then fixed, so high mobilome burden and high core fraction are sequential stages, not alternatives. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
- *Hypothesis:* the prophage module split generalizes — within any mobile element, housekeeping modules behave core-like while interaction-facing modules stay environmentally variable — making "mobile versus core" the wrong unit of analysis. [src: prophage_ecology]

## Resolving Work

- Join per-genome mobile-element calls to per-cluster core/accessory assignments in the plant cohort and ask directly: are beneficial clusters located on or off mobile elements, and does core fraction fall when mobile-borne clusters are excluded? [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
- Repeat the 64.6%-versus-45.2% core-fraction contrast at the genus level rather than the cluster level, to test whether the two plant-study results disagree only because of differing units. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
- Score prophage modules for core/accessory status using the same pangenome partition used for plant-interaction clusters, asking whether near-universal packaging, lysis and regulation modules score as core under an identical threshold. [src: prophage_ecology]
- Stratify the 3.7-versus-2.8 comparison by habitat compartment to test whether mobilome enrichment tracks environment rather than plant association as such. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
- Test whether environmentally variable anti-defense and structural modules co-occur with high-mobilome genera, linking module-level variability to genome-level mobility. [src: prophage_ecology, plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
