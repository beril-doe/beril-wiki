# Broad gene-content null versus subsystem-specific environmental signal

Across this corpus, one reanalysis finds that environment does *not* structure bacterial gene content any better than host-association does, while a dozen other projects report environmental structure in specific functional subsystems, communities and landscapes. The disagreement matters because it decides whether "ecotype" is a general organizing principle of bacterial genomes or a property of particular gene subsystems (prophages, metal-response functions, plant growth-promoting traits) that disappears when gene content is summarized whole-genome. This page records that primary disagreement together with three subsidiary ones that travel with it: whether pangenome openness predicts anything ecological, whether latent metabolic capabilities are as conserved as active dependencies, and whether beneficial plant-interaction functions are core or mobile. Source concept page: [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]].

## Evidence Sides

**D1 · Side A — the species-level null: environment is not special.** The ecotype reanalysis found environmental median partial correlation 0.051 versus human-associated 0.084 (U=1536, p=0.83) — a Mann–Whitney U test comparing, per species, the correlation between environmental distance and gene-content distance after controlling for phylogeny. The direction is the opposite of the ecotype prediction. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] A prior AlphaEarth-based analysis reported median partial correlation 0.0025. [src: env_embedding_explorer; ecotype_env_reanalysis] Two further results sit on this side. The plant compartment analysis found refined plant-marker profiles differing by compartment at PERMANOVA R² = 0.071 (PERMANOVA: permutational multivariate analysis of variance, a test of group differences in a distance matrix) and db-RDA R² = 0.060 (db-RDA: distance-based redundancy analysis, ordination constrained by predictor variables), but the effect fell to R² = 0.072 after removing genome-rich species and was attributed mainly to taxonomic sampling. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes; ecotype_env_reanalysis] And pangenome openness did not predict environment or phylogeny effect sizes (rho = -0.05 and 0.03; p-values 0.54 and 0.73). [src: pangenome_openness]

**D1 · Side B — subsystem-specific environmental signals, of which only the prophage result is reported against genome-size and host-family controls.** Prophage modules showed environment effects after genome-size and host-family controls, with environment F=30.04 versus phylogeny F=6.17 and significant within-quartile tests. [src: prophage_ecology] The remaining results on this side carry no comparable control in the input. The soil-metal analysis found 2,355 significant COG–metal associations (COG: Clusters of Orthologous Groups, a coarse functional annotation of genes) and a conditional db-RDA R² = 0.799. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics] Plant growth-promoting (PGP) trait analysis found soil strongly enriched acdS, pqqC, and hcnC and depleted nifH. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology] MicrobeAtlas found metal type diversity associated with niche breadth. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] In *Pseudomonas*, carbon profiles were associated with environment among 54 free-living and plant-associated species (permutation p = 0.006). [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

**D1 · Side C — signals at other units of analysis.** AlphaEarth shows a 3.4x environmental geographic gradient. [src: env_embedding_explorer] The NMDC community analysis found Soil and Freshwater pathway separation (median PC1 +3.86 versus −6.28). [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] Harvard Forest found treatment × horizon explained 41% of genus-level variance. [src: harvard_forest_warming] These are embedding distances, community pathway profiles and genus abundances — not species-level gene-content similarity.

**D1 · Side B′ — the caveats Side B carries.** The soil-metal result remains vulnerable to co-contamination, conditional-R² interpretation, non-independent tests, effect-size uncertainty, and spatial mismatch; chromium, copper, lead, and zinc co-vary and partial-correlation analyses remain pending, so it does not resolve whether metal type diversity or individual metal concentrations drive functional shifts. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics] The MicrobeAtlas strict prevalence analysis was non-significant at p = 0.092. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology] In *Pseudomonas*, the strongest PCA separation was between *Pseudomonas* s.s. (sensu stricto) and *Pseudomonas_E*, and the classifier achieved only 0.408 +/- 0.169 balanced accuracy — a phylogenetically structured, pathway-specific exception rather than a contradiction of the species-level null. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

**D2 · Side A — openness tracks metabolic capability.** Latent capability rate correlated with openness (ρ = 0.69, p = 0.0004, n = 22) [src: metabolic_capability_dependency], and variable pathway count correlated with openness (partial rho=0.530, p=2.83e-203). [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

**D2 · Side B — openness tracks nothing ecological.** Openness did not predict environment or phylogeny effect sizes (rho = -0.05 and 0.03; p-values 0.54 and 0.73). [src: pangenome_openness]

**D3 · Side A — latent capabilities are no less conserved than active dependencies.** Active mean conservation 0.829 versus latent 0.869, p = 0.94, while latent rate tracked openness. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]

**D3 · Side B — the newer coreness figures.** Core completeness 0.986 versus 0.975 [src: pathway_capability_dependency], and PGP genes were mostly more core than the 46.8% baseline. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]

**D4 · Side A — beneficial plant-interaction functions are core.** Beneficial plant-interaction clusters were 64.6% core versus 45.2% for pathogenic clusters. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]

**D4 · Side B — plant-associated genomes carry more mobile DNA.** Plant-associated genera had median mobilome burden 3.7 versus 2.8 mobile elements per genome (mobilome: the plasmid, transposon and phage-derived fraction of a genome). [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The prophage result is related but not directly contradictory: anti-defense and structural modules were environmentally variable, while packaging, lysis, and regulation were near-universal and more core-like. [src: prophage_ecology]

## Possible Reconciliations

These are hypotheses, not findings.

- **Feature-space mismatch (D1).** The sides use different feature spaces and response variables: broad gene-content similarity versus annotation-defined prophage modules versus metal-associated COG profiles. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis; prophage_ecology; soil_metal_functional_genomics] A hypothesis: a strong signal in a few hundred annotated genes is diluted below detection when averaged over whole-genome similarity, so both sides can be right simultaneously.
- **Unit-of-analysis mismatch (D1, Side C).** Embedding distance, community pathway completeness and genus-level abundance are not species-level gene content; the AlphaEarth comparison in particular measures embedding distance versus gene-content distance, not the same phenomenon. [src: env_embedding_explorer; ecotype_env_reanalysis] Hypothesis: environment sorts *which taxa are present* far more strongly than it sorts *which genes a given species carries*.
- **Taxonomic confounding as the shared explanation (D1).** The plant compartment effect was attributed mainly to taxonomic sampling [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes], and the *Pseudomonas* separation was strongest between *Pseudomonas* s.s. and *Pseudomonas_E* [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]. Hypothesis: several Side-B signals are lineage composition re-expressed as environment, which would fold them back into Side A rather than refuting it.
- **Co-varying predictors (D1, metals).** Chromium, copper, lead, and zinc co-vary and partial-correlation analyses remain pending. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics] Hypothesis: a single contamination axis, not metal identity or metal-type diversity, drives the COG associations — which would also explain why the strict prevalence test was only p = 0.092. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]
- **Openness measures gene turnover, not gene direction (D2).** Hypothesis: openness quantifies *how much* accessory content a species accumulates — which plausibly scales with variable pathway count and latent capability rate — while environment/phylogeny effect sizes quantify *what predicts* that content; a quantity and a direction need not correlate.
- **Different conservation denominators (D3).** Hypothesis: "mean conservation" of a capability, "core completeness" of a pathway and "% core" against a 46.8% baseline are three different statistics over three different gene sets, so 0.829/0.869 (p = 0.94), 0.986/0.975 and the PGP comparison may not be in contact at all.
- **Core function, mobile delivery (D4).** Hypothesis: beneficial plant-interaction functions are ancient and core *within* plant-associated lineages while the elevated mobilome burden (3.7 versus 2.8) reflects independent phage and plasmid load in plant-colonizing habitats — the prophage split between variable anti-defense/structural modules and core-like packaging/lysis/regulation modules is the same pattern in a different subsystem. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes; prophage_ecology]

## Resolving Work

**D1 — broad null versus subsystem signal**
- Re-run the species-level partial-correlation test on *subsystem-restricted* gene sets (prophage modules; metal-associated COGs; the PGP gene panel) using the ecotype reanalysis pipeline and the same species set, asking whether the environmental/human-associated gap moves off 0.051 versus 0.084 when the feature space is narrowed. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis; prophage_ecology; soil_metal_functional_genomics; pgp_pangenome_ecology]
- Apply the genome-size and host-family controls used in the prophage analysis to the soil-metal COG associations, asking how many of the 2,355 significant COG–metal associations survive host-family control. [src: prophage_ecology; soil_metal_functional_genomics]
- Complete the pending partial-correlation analyses separating chromium, copper, lead and zinc, asking whether any single metal retains its COG association once the others are conditioned out. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics]
- Build dedicated functional annotation, matched species, and common environmental metadata across the projects, then re-test whether the subsystem signals are genuine subsystem-level exceptions or structured confounding. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]
- Repeat the Harvard Forest treatment × horizon partition and the NMDC Soil/Freshwater PC1 contrast at species-resolved gene content rather than genus abundance or community pathway completeness, asking whether the 41% and the +3.86/−6.28 separation persist at the unit where the null was measured. [src: harvard_forest_warming; nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology; ecotype_env_reanalysis]

**D2 — openness versus ecological effect size**
- Stratify the openness–effect-size test by COG functional category (mobile-element and defense categories first), asking whether the flat rho = -0.05 / 0.03 hides category-specific structure. [src: pangenome_openness]
- Recompute the openness–latent-capability correlation (ρ = 0.69, p = 0.0004) on the n used by the effect-size test rather than n = 22, asking how much of the contrast is sample size. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency; pangenome_openness]
- Test alternative openness metrics against both response variables in one table, asking whether the disagreement is metric-specific. [src: pangenome_openness; pathway_capability_dependency]

**D3 — latent versus active conservation**
- Recompute active/latent mean conservation (0.829 versus 0.869, p = 0.94) and pathway core completeness (0.986 versus 0.975) on one shared species × pathway matrix, asking whether the two statistics agree when the denominator is fixed. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency; pathway_capability_dependency]
- Place the PGP genes (acdS, pqqC, hcnC, nifH) on that same axis against the 46.8% core baseline, asking whether soil-enriched traits are core-like in the same sense as latent capabilities. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology]
- Power-analyze the p = 0.94 comparison, asking what conservation difference the active/latent test could have detected at its sample size. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency]

**D4 — core function versus mobile delivery**
- Intersect the beneficial plant-interaction clusters (64.6% core) with the mobile elements counted in the mobilome burden (3.7 versus 2.8), asking directly what fraction of beneficial clusters sit on mobile DNA. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
- Repeat the core/accessory split *within* plant-associated genera only, asking whether 64.6% versus 45.2% survives removal of cross-lineage composition effects. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes]
- Classify plant-interaction clusters by the prophage module scheme (near-universal packaging/lysis/regulation versus variable anti-defense/structural), asking whether the core/variable split in plant traits mirrors the prophage one. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes; prophage_ecology]
