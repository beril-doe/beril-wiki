# Environmental signal that is strong within a collection but does not travel across studies or methods

Three projects in this corpus agree that environment leaves *some* imprint on microbial data, and disagree about how large that imprint is and whether it can be trusted outside the setting that produced it. The disagreement has three distinct faces: (A) a within-collection environmental association in metagenome contamination data that is statistically strong but collapses when whole studies are held out [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]; (B) a discordance between a genome-level result in which phylogeny generally dominates environmental similarity and a read-level result in which environment separates sample matrices sharply [src: ecotype_analysis, euk_in_prok_correlates]; and (C) a numerical conflict between two analyses of the *same* ecotype question, whose median environment–gene-content partial correlations differ by a factor the reanalysis itself reports and attributes to method [src: ecotype_analysis, ecotype_env_reanalysis]. All three are recorded as Tensions on [[concepts/classifier-database-compatibility-in-taxonomic-quantification]] and [[concepts/study-batch-confounding-of-environmental-associations]], and none is resolved by the current evidence [src: euk_in_prok_correlates, ecotype_analysis, ecotype_env_reanalysis]. This page covers all three; it does not adjudicate the underlying biology, only states what each side measured.

## Evidence Sides

### Disagreement A — within-collection association vs. transportable inference

**Side A1 — the environmental association is strong and matrix-coherent.** Sample-matrix contrasts in the eukaryotic-contamination analysis were statistically significant, and the environment model reached R²=0.35 under random cross-validation (R², the coefficient of determination: the share of variance in the response that the model accounts for). [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] Within a single study, predictive performance is positive rather than illusory: the within-NEON analysis gives R²=+0.17 ± 0.06. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]

**Side A2 — the association does not transport across studies.** With whole studies held out, the same environment model gives R²=−0.30 — a negative coefficient of determination, i.e. worse than the trivial baseline — and detection AUC=0.56 (AUC, area under the receiver-operating-characteristic curve: a rank-based measure of how well a score separates positives from negatives). [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] The source concept page states plainly that these results are not mutually exclusive: fine-scale environmental structure may be real while the pooled cross-study association is not portable, because study, batch, and unmeasured protocol variables are entangled. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] Even where protocol and batch are held constant, sub-batch confounding from sampling campaigns is not excluded. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] The framing is explicit that this is a tension between within-collection association and transportable inference, not a contradiction between the classifier and the ecological result, and that classifier/database compatibility and study structure are among the factors that must be controlled. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]

### Disagreement B — phylogeny-dominant genomes vs. environment-separated matrices

**Side B1 — phylogeny generally dominates.** The ecotype analysis found that phylogeny generally dominated environmental similarity as a predictor of genome-wide gene-content similarity, reporting a median environment partial correlation (the association between two distance measures after removing the influence of a third) of 0.0025 versus 0.0143 for phylogeny, with no significant environmental effect in 90.7% of species. [src: ecotype_analysis]

**Side B2 — environment separates strongly within matrix.** The contamination analysis detected strong within-matrix, classifier-derived environmental associations. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] The concept page records that these two results cannot be directly reconciled, because they use different responses, data structures, and environmental representations. [src: ecotype_analysis, euk_in_prok_correlates]

### Disagreement C — absolute effect size between the original ecotype analysis and its reanalysis

**Side C1 — near-zero environmental signal.** The original ecotype analysis reports a median environment partial correlation of 0.0025 against 0.0143 for phylogeny, with no significant environmental effect in 90.7% of species, under only 28.4% embedding coverage. [src: ecotype_analysis]

**Side C2 — a much larger absolute signal, same direction of conclusion.** The reanalysis reports an all-species median partial correlation of 0.081 versus 0.003 in the original analysis, and its full-genome extraction produced a reported 27x higher overall median partial correlation than the original downsampled analysis. [src: ecotype_analysis, ecotype_env_reanalysis] The reanalysis nonetheless reproduces the group-level null: environmental species did not show stronger environment–gene-content correlations than human-associated species (p=0.83), despite a confirmed clinical sampling bias. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] The report explicitly identifies the methodological discrepancy as unresolved and states that the absolute correlation difference must not be interpreted as a biological contradiction. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] It attributes the discrepancy to different genome inclusion and downsampling, so absolute effect sizes must not be compared across methods, while its within-method group comparison remains valid. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]

## Possible Reconciliations

These are hypotheses, not findings; none is established by the cited evidence.

- **Scope hypothesis (A).** Both sides of A may be right at different scales: environment predicts within a fixed protocol and campaign (R²=+0.17 ± 0.06) while the pooled cross-study coefficient is carried by study identity, so R²=−0.30 measures portability rather than biology. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] This is the non-exclusive reading the concept page itself offers. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]
- **Different-response hypothesis (B).** The two analyses may both be correct because they answer different questions — read-level contaminant fraction across sample matrices versus genome-wide gene-content similarity within species — with different environmental representations; the concept page states they cannot be directly reconciled on present evidence. [src: ecotype_analysis, euk_in_prok_correlates]
- **Estimator hypothesis (C).** The reported 27x gap may be an estimator property rather than a biological disagreement, since genome inclusion and downsampling differ between the two runs. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] Under this hypothesis the agreement in direction — the p=0.83 group-level null in the reanalysis, and no significant environmental effect in 90.7% of species in the original — is the transferable result, and the magnitudes are not comparable. [src: ecotype_analysis, ecotype_env_reanalysis]
- **Coverage hypothesis (C).** The original analysis's 28.4% embedding coverage may attenuate correlations, so its lower median could be partly a measurement-coverage artifact. [src: ecotype_analysis] This is speculative: the corpus contains no coverage-matched comparison. [src: ecotype_analysis, ecotype_env_reanalysis]
- **What is *not* an acceptable reconciliation.** Averaging the median of 0.0025 and the median of 0.081, or silently preferring the larger or the more recent number, would mix a method difference with an effect size; the reanalysis's own framing forbids cross-method magnitude comparison. [src: ecotype_analysis, ecotype_env_reanalysis]

## Resolving Work

**For A (portability):**
- Rerun the environment model with study-held-out validation after matching studies on protocol and sequencing platform, asking whether detection AUC rises above AUC=0.56 once protocol is balanced rather than merely recorded. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]
- Partition variance within NEON at the sampling-campaign level, testing whether the R²=+0.17 ± 0.06 within-study signal survives campaign-level holdout — the residual sub-batch confounder the concept page flags as unaddressed. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]
- Add a second collection with the same sample matrices sampled under a different study design, and test whether the statistically significant matrix contrasts replicate out of collection, converting the within-collection association into a transportable claim or refuting it. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]

**For B (scale mismatch):**
- Compute both responses on the same samples — read-level contaminant fraction and genome-level gene-content similarity — so the phylogeny-versus-environment comparison and the matrix comparison share a data structure and an environmental representation. [src: ecotype_analysis, euk_in_prok_correlates]
- Repeat the phylogeny-versus-environment partition using the matrix labels of the contamination analysis in place of embedding-derived environmental distance, testing whether the environmental representation rather than the biology drives the discordance between 0.0025 and the strong within-matrix associations. [src: ecotype_analysis, euk_in_prok_correlates]

**For C (magnitude):**
- Rerun the original downsampling protocol and the full-genome extraction on an identical species and genome set, reporting both medians side by side; this is the single analysis that would decide whether the reported 27x gap is entirely methodological. [src: ecotype_analysis, ecotype_env_reanalysis]
- Sweep genome count per species from the downsampled set up to the full set and plot median partial correlation against genome count, testing the reanalysis's stated attribution of the discrepancy to genome inclusion and downsampling. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]
- Subsample the full-genome run to the original analysis's 28.4% embedding coverage, testing the coverage-attenuation hypothesis against the original analysis's stated limitation. [src: ecotype_analysis, ecotype_env_reanalysis]
- Repeat the environmental-versus-human-associated group comparison under both methods; reproducing the p=0.83 null across methods would establish that the group comparison, unlike the absolute magnitude, is method-robust. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]
