# Exploiting Observed Fitness Evidence vs. Correcting for Unequal Condition Coverage

Fitness-based prioritization of uncharacterized ("dark") genes rewards organisms and genes that have already been observed under many experimental conditions. The corpus contains an unresolved disagreement about whether that reward is a feature or an artifact: one line of evidence treats accumulated fitness profiles as the most efficient route to experimental yield, while another treats them as an observation-opportunity bias that should be corrected before ranking. Nested inside this are two further disputes — whether the near-neutrality of rare (singleton) genes is biology or a detection failure, and whether pangenome position (core vs. accessory) supplies a correction term at all, given that its direction reverses by functional category. This page records all three; it does not attempt to settle which prioritization configuration is correct, because no source in the input tension tests coverage-adjusted rankings against unadjusted ones. Source concept page: [[concepts/fitness-condition-coverage-prioritization-bias]].

## Evidence Sides

### Disagreement 1 — Is deep condition coverage a resource to exploit or a bias to correct?

**Side A — exploit the existing evidence.** The prioritization system delivers concrete experimental yield by favoring organisms that already carry many informative fitness profiles: 10 organism–condition experiments achieve coverage of 242 of the top 500 dark genes. [src: functional_dark_matter] On this reading, the accumulated profiles are a genuine asset — they identify where a small, tractable campaign can address the largest block of candidate genes. A within-organism result points the same way: in one organism, fitness importance predicted conservation more consistently than field-versus-lab classification of the assay condition did. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness] Measured fitness importance therefore carries signal that the ecological label of the condition does not, which argues for weighting observed fitness rather than discounting it by where it was observed.

**Side B — the ranking encodes unequal observation.** The same prioritization behavior may underexplore genes from organisms whose condition panels are shallow. [src: functional_dark_matter] Crucially, the report does not establish that the highest-ranked organisms have intrinsically more dark-gene biology, so this distinction must not be inferred from their candidate counts alone. [src: functional_dark_matter] Candidate counts are thus outputs of an unequal sampling design, not measurements of functional darkness. The field-versus-lab result qualifies rather than rescues Side A: showing that fitness importance beats condition-label classification in one organism does not show that unequal condition coverage is harmless. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness] It remains unresolved whether coverage-adjusted rankings would change substantially across organisms with different condition panels and accessory-genome structures. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]

### Disagreement 2 — Is singleton near-neutrality biology or poor transposon coverage?

**Side A — conserved genes carry the condition-specific signal.** Core genes (those present in essentially all members of a pangenome) showed stronger fitness effects in both negative and positive directions, while novel singleton genes (present in only one genome) were near-neutral under tested laboratory conditions. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] Read at face value, this means condition-specific detection is not confined to accessory genes, and that rare novel genes contribute little measurable phenotype — so prioritization that concentrates on well-profiled, conserved biology is not obviously missing the action.

**Side B — the neutrality may be a detection artifact.** The apparent near-neutrality of novel singleton genes may instead reflect poor transposon coverage in RB-TnSeq (randomly barcoded transposon sequencing, which infers gene importance from the abundance of insertion mutants and therefore reports nothing for genes it fails to disrupt). [src: fitness_effects_conservation] Under this reading, singleton flatness is a measurement floor, not a biological result. Neither result establishes how untested ecological conditions would reorder priorities. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

### Disagreement 3 — Does pangenome position supply a usable correction term?

**Side A — core genes bear the higher burden.** Fitness burden was higher for core genes in several functional categories. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] If that held generally, a single core-versus-accessory adjustment could be applied to fitness-based rankings to offset conservation-driven detection differences.

**Side B — the direction reverses by category.** Burden was higher for non-core genes in Cell Wall genes. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] This category-specific reversal cautions against applying a single core-versus-accessory correction to fitness-based rankings. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] The correction term, if one exists, is functional-category-conditional rather than global — which **refines** the coverage-bias tension rather than resolving it. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]

## Possible Reconciliations

These are hypotheses, not findings; none is demonstrated by the input evidence.

- **Scope difference (objective mismatch).** Side A of Disagreement 1 optimizes short-horizon experimental yield — genes addressed per experiment — while Side B optimizes unbiased discovery across the organism panel. Both could be correct under their own objective, and the 242-of-500 set-cover result would then be a statement about campaign efficiency, not about where dark-gene biology is concentrated. [src: functional_dark_matter]
- **Measurement-floor hypothesis.** If singleton near-neutrality is largely a transposon-coverage artifact, Sides A and B of Disagreement 2 are compatible: core genes really do show stronger two-directional effects, *and* singleton effects are unmeasured rather than absent. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] The two claims describe different genes with different detection probabilities.
- **Condition-space hypothesis.** Both the core-gene strength result and the singleton-neutrality result are conditioned on tested laboratory conditions. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] If the untested ecological condition space differentially engages accessory and singleton genes, both sides could hold within the assayed panel while the ranking they imply is unstable outside it.
- **Definitional difference in "burden."** The core/non-core reversal in Cell Wall genes may reflect that the trade-off measured is category-specific in kind, not just in magnitude — cell-envelope genes may be subject to a different selective regime than Protein/RNA-metabolism-type categories. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] Under this hypothesis both directions are real and the error lies only in generalizing either one into a single correction.
- **Predictor-substitution hypothesis.** Fitness importance outperforming field-versus-lab classification in one organism [src: field_vs_lab_fitness] may reflect that condition *labels* are a coarse proxy for condition *coverage*. Both sides of Disagreement 1 could then be right: labels are uninformative, while the depth of the panel behind those labels still biases ranking. [src: functional_dark_matter, field_vs_lab_fitness]

## Resolving Work

**On exploit-vs-correct (Disagreement 1):**
- Recompute the prioritization with an explicit coverage-adjusted fitness axis (e.g. normalizing detected effects by the number and class diversity of conditions assayed per organism) and report rank displacement against the unadjusted ranking — does the identity of the top-ranked organisms survive adjustment, given that intrinsic dark-gene biology was never established for them? [src: functional_dark_matter]
- Run the set-cover experiment-selection procedure under the coverage-adjusted scores and compare achieved coverage against the 242-of-500 figure obtained with 10 organism–condition experiments; the yield cost of de-biasing is the quantity in dispute. [src: functional_dark_matter]
- Hold-out test: mask a deep organism's condition panel down to the depth of a shallow organism, re-rank, and measure how many of its original candidates are lost — a direct estimate of how much rank is bought by coverage alone. [src: functional_dark_matter]
- Assay one shallow-panel organism across the condition classes that drive the top-ranked organism's candidates, and ask whether its dark genes then yield comparable candidate counts. [src: functional_dark_matter]

**On singleton neutrality (Disagreement 2):**
- Stratify the fitness-effect comparison by per-gene transposon insertion density and re-test whether novel singleton genes remain near-neutral among genes with adequate insertion coverage — this separates the measurement-floor hypothesis from true neutrality. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- Extend assays beyond tested laboratory conditions into ecologically motivated conditions and re-measure the core-versus-singleton effect-size contrast, since neither current result establishes how untested conditions would reorder priorities. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- For essential-gene-style cases where transposon mutants are unrecoverable, apply a knockdown modality (e.g. CRISPR interference) to singleton genes and test whether phenotypes appear where transposon data reported neutrality. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

**On the pangenome correction term (Disagreement 3):**
- Fit the burden comparison with a category-by-core-status interaction term across all functional categories and report which categories show reversal beyond the Cell Wall case, rather than assuming a global direction. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]
- Test whether a category-conditional correction changes fitness-based rankings more or less than a single global core-versus-accessory correction; if the two differ materially, the reversal is prioritization-relevant and not merely descriptive. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]
- Replicate the core/non-core burden contrast in organisms with different accessory-genome structures, to test whether the Cell Wall reversal is organism-specific or general. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs, field_vs_lab_fitness]
