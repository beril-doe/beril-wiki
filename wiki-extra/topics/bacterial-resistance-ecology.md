# Bacterial Resistance Ecology

Bacterial resistance ecology examines how antimicrobial-resistance (AMR) and metal-resistance traits are distributed across genomes, strains, lineages, environments, and mobile genetic contexts—and how those traits alter bacterial fitness. This corpus speaks to the topic by combining large pangenome surveys, transposon-fitness experiments, environmental metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs), phenotype databases, and ecological metadata. Its central message is that resistance is neither a uniform species property nor a simple inventory of transferable genes: it is shaped jointly by genomic conservation, conditional selection, shared stress physiology, lineage history, environmental structure, and the scale at which evidence is measured.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Resistance is environmentally structured, but not simply environmentally caused.**  
Across 293K genomes representing 14,723 bacterial species, a uniform AMRFinderPlus analysis identified 83,008 hits, and 53.2% of species carried at least one hit. [src: amr_environmental_resistome, amr_pangenome_atlas] Here, the *resistome* means the collection of resistance-associated genes detected in a population or genome set. The catalog includes stress-response genes as well as classical antibiotic-resistance genes, so its counts describe a broader resistance landscape rather than clinical AMR alone. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas]

AMR is disproportionately found in accessory genomic compartments: 30.3% of AMR genes were core, compared with 46.8% for the surrounding pangenome baseline, while the auxiliary genome was 2.2x enriched for AMR. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] Within species, 51.3% of AMR gene–species records were rare, 41.3% variable, and 7.5% fixed; the median pairwise Jaccard distance was 0.435. [src: amr_strain_variation] This supports a graded distinction between intrinsic resistance—conserved lineage-associated functions—and acquired resistance, which is often accessory or variably inherited, but neither category is absolute. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas, amr_strain_variation] [[concepts/environmental-resistome]]

Environment also correlates with resistome composition. Clinical species had 68% accessory AMR compared with 43% in soil, and the environment effect was significant with Kruskal–Wallis H = 506.0, p = 4×10⁻¹⁰⁷, and η² = 0.036. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] However, these contrasts combine exposure with phylogenetic composition, sampling practices, isolation bias, annotation coverage, and host-associated traits. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] The corpus therefore supports ecological association, not a general claim that environmental exposure directly caused the observed resistance differences. [[concepts/phylogenetic-confounding-of-pangenome-associations]]

**Selection is conditional: resistance can be costly in one context and beneficial in another.**  
Genome-wide transposon fitness data identified 1,352 AMR genes across 43 organisms and analyzed per-organism effects in 25 organisms. [src: amr_fitness_cost] Under non-antibiotic conditions, AMR-gene knockouts had a pooled relative shift of +0.086 [95% CI: +0.074, +0.098], with I² = 54.3% and Q = 52.54, p = 0.0007. [src: amr_fitness_cost] This is a relative perturbation-fitness contrast: AMR knockouts averaged −0.024, while the non-AMR knockout background averaged approximately −0.11. [src: amr_fitness_cost] It does not show that AMR knockouts grew faster than an unperturbed wild type, nor does it directly measure the cost of acquiring or expressing an intact resistance gene. [[concepts/antimicrobial-resistance-fitness-cost]]

Under any-antibiotic exposure, 57.0% of AMR genes showed a flip toward greater importance, with N = 797, mean flip +0.045, and Wilcoxon p = 0.0001. [src: amr_fitness_cost] A *fitness flip* means that a gene is relatively more important under antibiotic exposure than under non-antibiotic conditions; it is not a positive absolute growth rate. Broad-spectrum efflux genes had mean flip +0.094, compared with −0.001 for enzymatic-inactivation genes, with p = 0.007. [src: amr_fitness_cost] Thus, mechanism and exposure jointly determine resistance value.

The validation was weaker when antibiotic class and gene were explicitly matched: among 157 gene–antibiotic pairs, the flip rate was 54.8%, mean flip +0.113, and p = 0.14. [src: amr_fitness_cost] This is an important boundary on generalization: pooled antibiotic conditions support conditional benefit more strongly than the smaller class-matched analysis. [[conflicts/conflict--amr_cofitness_networks--amr_fitness_cost--amr_strain_variation--90a27ef2]] [[concepts/condition-specific-fitness]]

**Metal resistance combines a shared stress layer with chemistry-specific requirements.**  
Across 317 organism–metal-pair observations involving 28 organisms, 98.1% of gene-level fitness correlations were positive and 99.1% were significant at p < 0.05. [src: metal_cross_resistance] No tested metal pair showed systematically negative cross-resistance. [src: metal_cross_resistance] The strongest reported associations included Fe–Zn, mean r = 0.61, and Co–Ni, r = 0.56, whereas Al was comparatively independent, with mean r = 0.34. [src: metal_cross_resistance] This indicates a broadly shared directional response, but the magnitude depends on metal chemistry.

The shared direction may arise from common disruption of protein stability, DNA integrity, membrane function, and cofactor handling, yet the cross-metal study included no non-metal stress controls. [src: metal_cross_resistance] A direct counter-ion comparison found that 4,304 of 10,821 metal-important records, or 39.8%, were also important under NaCl, leaving 60.2% outside that shared-stress overlap. [src: counter_ion_effects] A separate specificity analysis classified 54.9% of 7,609 metal-important records as metal-specific, 38.0% as generally sick, and 7.2% as metal-plus-stress. [src: metal_specificity] The different percentages reflect different thresholds and condition sets rather than interchangeable estimates. [[concepts/metal-cross-resistance]] [[concepts/shared-stress-versus-stressor-specific-fitness]]

Metal-important genes were 87.4% core compared with 76.9% for baseline genes across 22 organisms and 14 metals, while the simple gene-presence repertoire score failed to predict metal fitness. [src: metal_fitness_atlas] This supports a two-layer interpretation: conserved cellular robustness dominates many fitness signals, while specialized efflux, sequestration, and detoxification functions remain mechanistically distinct. [src: metal_fitness_atlas] Composite resistance scores should therefore prioritize hypotheses, not substitute for metal-specific experiments. [[concepts/composite-resistance-score-limitations]]

**Mobile elements and resistance modules connect genomes across ecological scales.**  
The corpus finds strong evidence that resistance is often accessory and co-inherited, but much weaker evidence that any one mobile element is responsible. The strain analysis detected 1,517 resistance islands across 705 species, with mean size 6.2 genes, median size 4 genes, maximum size 43 genes, and mean phi coefficient 0.827. [src: amr_strain_variation] A phi coefficient measures pairwise co-occurrence; it indicates tight co-inheritance, not necessarily physical linkage, co-selection, or functional synergy. Of these islands, 88% contained multiple resistance mechanisms. [src: amr_strain_variation]

Prophage-associated evidence illustrates the scale problem. Among 36,041 AMR instances, 55.7% occurred on contigs carrying strict prophage markers, but only 5.5% were within 5 genes and 10.4% within 10 genes of a marker; the median nearest-marker distance was 34 genes. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization] At a 10-gene threshold, nearby AMR was only modestly more accessory than distal AMR, with OR = 1.10 and a 2.1 percentage point difference; species-level odds ratios had a median of 0.85. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization] Conversely, prophage-marker density correlated strongly with AMR repertoire breadth across 4,770 species, rho = 0.572, and remained associated after controlling for genome count, rho = 0.464. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization] Species-level covariance therefore does not establish local prophage-mediated transfer. [[concepts/resistance-island-coinheritance]] [[concepts/scale-dependent-mobile-element-associations]]

Chromosomal and integrative routes remain plausible in environmental genomes. Among 30,497 high-quality environmental MAGs, 6,652, or 21.8%, carried T4SS or conjugative machinery, and T4SS-positive genomes had 10× higher MGE density. [src: t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt] T4SS–CAZy neighborhoods were enriched in marine sediment, barley rhizosphere, and maize rhizosphere, while 12 IMEs occurred among the top 100 accumulators. [src: t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt] These observations support a chromosomal or integrative transfer model but do not identify the causal route. [[concepts/chromosomal-and-integrative-gene-transfer]]

**Ecology acts through lineages, niches, and sampling structure.**  
Pangenome openness—the degree to which a species’ gene repertoire expands as genomes are sampled—correlated positively with AMR count in 8/10 tested phyla, yet the pooled correlation was nearly zero, rho = 0.006. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas] This is a classic aggregation problem: within-lineage associations can cancel across lineages. Within species, 55.6% of 1,261 tested species showed significant AMR-repertoire association with ANI distance, and 87.8% had positive correlations. [src: amr_strain_variation] [[concepts/phylogenetic-confounding-of-pangenome-associations]]

Geographic metal-resistance hotspots are similarly provisional. Of 30,497 environmental MAGs, 22,356 had usable coordinates, and 2.8% carried at least one metal-resistance type. A 5° grid identified 11 hotspots and 3 coldspots after BH-FDR correction, including an Atacama/Andean cell with prevalence 21.8%, OR = 9.83, q = 7.6×10⁻¹², and n = 101 MAGs. [src: metal_resistance_global_biogeography] The study requires study-origin checks and sampling-effort normalization before interpreting these as regional selection. [[concepts/spatial-sampling-effort-confounding]]

## Tensions and Caveats

The corpus’s major disagreements are best treated as scale and measurement conflicts rather than simple reversals. Broad ecological AMR associations coexist with weak within-species environmental structure: only 20 of 141 testable families showed significant environment effects, while whole-genome ecotype effects were absent in 156 species, or 90.7%. [src: amr_environmental_resistome, ecotype_analysis] This anchors [[conflicts/conflict--amr_environmental_resistome--amr_fitness_cost--amr_pangenome_atlas--76f2bfcc]]: environment may structure communities or species pools without consistently selecting resistance differences within populations.

A second tension concerns system-level markers versus mechanism. Defense markers occurred in 99.8% of 27,690 species-level pangenomes, and 27 of 28 defense-system pairs showed positive co-occurrence, but CRISPR-Cas prevalence ranged from 96% by description matching to approximately 55% by a Cas1 marker. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] Prophage calls may include domesticated remnants or bacterial homologs, and SNIPE’s proposed mechanism remains annotation- and architecture-sensitive. [src: prophage_ecology, snipe_defense_system] These limitations motivate [[conflicts/conflict--phage_defense_arsenal--prophage_amr_comobilization--prophage_ecology--9f1135e8]].

Finally, conservation and fitness do not align universally. The AMR-specific analysis found nearly identical baseline fitness distributions for core and accessory AMR genes, with d = 0.002 and p = 0.33, while broader analyses associate essential or fitness-active genes with higher core representation. [src: amr_fitness_cost, fitness_effects_conservation] Different gene universes, perturbation coverage, condition panels, and definitions prevent treating conservation as a direct proxy for resistance cost or ecological importance. [[conflicts/conflict--amr_fitness_cost--conservation_fitness_synthesis--conservation_vs_fitness--2bd71881]] Composite metal scores face the same problem: BacDive matched 37,368 strains to pangenome species in one analysis and 42,227 in another, while a species-scale metal-environment test was null with rho approximately −0.02 and p > 0.8. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance, bacdive_metal_validation, metal_cross_resistance] [[conflicts/conflict--bacdive_metal_validation--metal_cross_resistance--metal_fitness_atlas--d3b93f8c]]
