# Ecological Genomics Biases

Ecological genomics connects microbial genomes to environments, phenotypes, and ecological interactions, but the corpus shows that this connection is shaped by what is sampled, classified, annotated, and made comparable. Across pangenomes, environmental metadata, phenotype databases, and metagenomes, apparent ecological signals range from robust feature-specific associations to null or non-portable genome-wide effects. The central lesson is not that ecological genomics is unreliable, but that its conclusions must be interpreted as measurements produced by particular reference collections, taxonomic resolutions, functional definitions, and validation designs.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Reference collections define the ecological field of view.** Available genomes are not a neutral sample of microbial diversity. In the AlphaEarth-linked subset, human clinical, gut, and other human-associated genomes together represented 38%, while Soil, Marine, and Freshwater each represented 7%; embeddings covered only 28.4% of 293,059 pangenome genomes. [src: env_embedding_explorer] Among 224 species retained for an environmental reanalysis, 106 (47%) were majority human-associated, 47 (21%) majority environmental, and 71 (32%) Mixed/Other. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] These patterns make cultivation, clinical sampling, and collection history part of the inferential model rather than background metadata. [[concepts/cultivation-collection-bias-in-ecological-genomics]] documents the same issue in phenotype and plant-associated datasets: only 7,995 of 293,059 genomes (2.7%) had plant-associated annotations, and BacDive matching retained only 42,227 of 97,334 strains in one genome-linkage workflow. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes, bacdive_metal_validation]

Environmental metadata are also incomplete and potentially mislocalized. The pitfalls audit found 52.7% unknown per-genome NCBI environment labels, although species-level majority voting reached 91% coverage. [src: pitfalls] For host-associated bacteria, geographic coordinates may describe a collection site rather than the host tissue, body site, or microenvironment actually experienced by the organism. [src: ecotype_analysis] Thus, missingness and spatial embeddings can limit ecological inference even before gene-content comparisons begin. [[concepts/environment-embedding-geography]]

**Sampling depth and analytical choices change the estimated signal.** The ecotype reanalysis used all genomes with embeddings, including up to 3,505 genomes per species, whereas the original analysis used diversity-maximizing downsampling with a maximum of 250 genomes per species. The reanalysis reported a median partial correlation of 0.081 across 183 species versus 0.003 in the original analysis, described as a 27x difference; the studies used different genome sets and distance distributions, so these absolute values are not directly comparable. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] A partial correlation measures association between two variables after accounting for another variable. Within the reanalysis method, environmental species still did not exceed human-associated species: their median was 0.051 versus 0.084, with Environmental > Human-associated giving U=1536 and p=0.83. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] The environmental-genome fraction likewise showed no relationship with correlation strength (rho=-0.085, p=0.25). [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]

These results separate two claims that are often conflated: sampling can alter correlation estimates, but clinical composition alone did not explain the weak environmental comparison. The broader analysis of 172 species found median environment and phylogeny partial correlations of 0.0025 and 0.0143, respectively; phylogeny dominated in 60.5% of species, while significant positive and negative environmental effects occurred in 12 (7.0%) and 4 (2.3%) species, with no significant effect in 156 (90.7%). [src: ecotype_analysis] [[concepts/sampling-depth-and-downsampling-effects]] is the appropriate next step for interpreting these estimates.

**Taxonomic and database resolution determines what can be detected.** Classifiers, pangenome bridges, and annotation databases jointly define the measurable phenotype. In an analysis of 2,759 ReadbasedAnalysis runs from 9 studies, GOTTCHA2 detected eukaryotic reads in 77% of runs, with a median eukaryotic fraction of 2.7%; Kraken2 and Centrifuge produced approximately 0 domain-level Eukaryota signal because their deployed reference databases were prokaryote-restricted. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] A classifier that cannot represent a taxon cannot provide evidence that the taxon is absent. [[concepts/classifier-database-compatibility-in-taxonomic-quantification]]

The same problem appears in functional annotation. Across 48 Fitness Browser organisms, 57,011 of 228,709 genes (24.9%) were classified as dark, while full GTDB r214 pangenome propagation increased dark-gene conservation coverage from 32,791 (57.5%) to 37,997 (66.6%). [src: functional_dark_matter] In the ENIGMA contamination analysis, genus-to-clade ambiguity was substantial: 380 mapped genera were multi-clade genera, and *Pseudomonas* mapped to 433 clades. Strict and relaxed mappings retained a mean mapped abundance fraction of 0.343, whereas a species-proxy mode retained 0.031. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] Finer resolution can reduce ambiguity, but may discard much of the available abundance. [[concepts/taxonomic-resolution-dependent-functional-inference]]

**Whole-genome nulls can coexist with locus-specific ecological structure.** The corpus does not support a simple opposition between “environment matters” and “environment does not matter.” Whole-genome gene-content similarity was usually more strongly structured by phylogeny, but selected pathways and gene modules showed environmental differentiation. Prophage-module composition, for example, was associated with environment after genome-size stratification and family-level comparisons; genome size remained dominant (F=212.99), followed by environment (F=30.04) and phylogeny (F=6.17). [src: prophage_ecology] This indicates that an environmental effect can survive controls in a functional subsystem without implying discrete whole-genome ecotypes. [[concepts/genome-wide-versus-locus-specific-ecological-adaptation]]

The plant-growth-promoting analysis illustrates the same scale distinction. Among soil/rhizosphere species, *acdS* prevalence was 15.8% versus 2.6% elsewhere (OR=7.02, q=5.1e-62), and *pqqC* prevalence was 43.8% versus 21.2% (OR=2.90, q=2.8e-53). [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology] These associations support a gene-level ecological guild, meaning genes or taxa sharing environmental selection or ecological roles, but not physical linkage or metabolic exchange. Co-occurring plant-associated genera were actually slightly less complementary than random pairs (Cohen’s d≈−0.4, permutation p<0.001). [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology, plant_microbiome_ecotypes] [[concepts/gene-cooccurrence-ecological-guilds]]

Other feature-specific results point in the same direction. PHB, a carbon-storage and stress-response polymer, occurred in 21.9% of 27,690 GTDB species and was enriched in variable environments; the environmental-variability test gave chi2=1,656.36, p~0. [src: phb_granule_ecology] Soil-metal analysis found 2,355 significant COG–metal associations at FDR<0.05, where COG means Cluster of Orthologous Groups and FDR means false discovery rate. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics] These are observational associations, not direct demonstrations of mechanism or activity.

**Confirmation is stricter than discovery.** Confirmatory tests often weaken broad ecological claims. In ENIGMA, predeclared genus-level contamination–defense tests were non-significant; the relaxed coverage-adjusted model gave FDR q=0.0462, but the strict model gave q=0.130. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] In plant-associated data, refined compartment separation explained only approximately 6% of functional-marker variance, and the preliminary R²=0.527 fell to 0.072 after excluding three genome-rich species. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] In a separate Oak Ridge comparison, aggregate laboratory metal tolerance was positively but non-significantly associated with the high-uranium/low-uranium abundance ratio (Spearman rho=0.503, p=0.095, n=12 genera), even though five of 11 genera had FDR-significant bidirectional uranium associations. [src: lab_field_ecology]

Feature reuse adds another bias. If genes are used to define clusters and then tested again as cluster outcomes, the analysis risks selection-on-outcome leakage. In one genomic example, PCA and KMeans gene-content clusters were followed by COG testing; 170 of 257 tests (66.1%) were significant after BH-FDR, but the tested representation was biologically related to the grouping representation. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] Independent validation reduced an exploratory 33-candidate list to 3 candidates in a separate microbiome analysis. [src: pitfalls] [[concepts/selection-on-outcome-leakage]] explains why held-out features and independent validation are essential.

## Tensions and Caveats

The promoted conflicts frame the main unresolved issues. [[conflicts/conflict--ecotype_analysis--ecotype_env_reanalysis--euk_in_prok_correlates]] contrasts strong within-collection environmental structure with poor transportability: random-validation prediction reached R²=0.35, but study-held-out prediction reached R²=−0.30 and detection AUC=0.56. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] Study, batch, classifier, and environmental matrix were strongly entangled, so observed differences cannot be assigned cleanly to ecology.

[[conflicts/conflict--ecotype_analysis--ecotype_env_reanalysis--plant_microbiome_ecotypes--93da56a7]] captures the unresolved whole-genome versus localized-structure problem. Environmental correlations, plant-compartment effects, and prophage signals were obtained under different genome universes, taxonomic filters, and feature definitions. The results support feature-specific ecological adaptation, but do not establish a universal genome-wide environmental effect. [src: ecotype_analysis, plant_microbiome_ecotypes, prophage_ecology]

[[conflicts/conflict--cog_analysis--ecotype_functional_differentiation--enigma_contamination_functional_potential--9514b16e]] highlights coverage-sensitive positives versus resolution-robust nulls. Relaxed models and finer functional analyses can recover signal, while strict mappings and confirmatory genus-level tests do not. This is not resolved by selecting one result: the load-bearing limitation is that coverage, aggregation, and feature construction change the estimand itself. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential, functional_dark_matter, cog_analysis]

Finally, [[conflicts/conflict--ecotype_env_reanalysis--enigma_carbon_census_1]] generalizes the same caution to metabolic inference. “Organism-dark” compounds are not necessarily biologically absent: in the ENIGMA carbon census, 83 compounds were structure-resolved, 54 were KEGG-linked, 9 were callable, and 74 were organism-dark. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Callability is therefore a resource-defined boundary, not a direct measure of ecological absence or catabolic inactivity. [[concepts/callability-limited-comparative-inference]]
