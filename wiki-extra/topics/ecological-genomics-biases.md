# Ecological Genomics Biases

Ecological genomics asks how environmental context, spatial setting, host association, and resource availability shape microbial genomes and functions. This corpus shows that the answer depends not only on biology, but also on which organisms are sampled, how environments are encoded, which taxa and genes databases can represent, and whether an association survives phylogenetic, taxonomic, and study-level controls. Across the projects, broad environmental effects are often weak or unstable, while narrower pathway-, locus-, module-, or lineage-specific signals can remain detectable.

## Literature Context

Published work establishes that ecological genomics is shaped by both biological context and the architecture of available data. Meta-pangenomic frameworks explicitly connect pangenomics with metagenomics, highlighting the need to interpret gene-content variation across genomes and communities rather than treating reference genomes as a complete representation of diversity [PMID 32633911](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32633911/). Comparative-metagenomics methodology likewise emphasizes quantitative normalization and careful comparison across unevenly sampled taxa and environments [PMID 27565341](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27565341/). DNA-based abundance estimates can also be distorted by genome size, copy number, extraction, and sequencing effects, making apparent ecological differences partly dependent on the measurement process [PMID 35986714](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35986714/). Recent studies further show that uncultured organisms can contain ecologically relevant functions that are absent from conventional isolate-centered resources, including enzymes from deep-sea microorganisms [PMID 41212644](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41212644/). Together, this literature supports the corpus's emphasis on sampling bias, incomplete annotation, callability, and the distinction between biological absence and failure of representation in [[concepts/callability-limited-comparative-inference]].

The literature also supports a scale-dependent view of ecological adaptation. Microbial community assembly can be strongly associated with host or environmental context, as shown for Rhodobacteraceae associated with the diatom *Asterionellopsis glacialis* [PMID 38809046](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38809046/), while *Bacillus subtilis* exhibits switch-like defensive adaptations to protistan predation [PMID 40991432](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40991432/). Studies of humanized gnotobiotic mice additionally demonstrate that cross-species engraftment can be biased and can produce metabolic divergence, cautioning against treating transferred microbiomes as faithful replicas of their source communities [PMID 41277418](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41277418/). These findings are consistent with this corpus's conclusion that broad whole-genome environmental associations may be weak while pathway-, locus-, module-, or lineage-specific signals remain detectable. The corpus extends that literature by quantifying this contrast across multiple projects: environmental effects are often small after phylogenetic or taxonomic controls, yet prophage modules, plant-growth-promoting genes, carbon-use profiles, and PHB-associated patterns retain narrower signals.

Within the candidate literature, this corpus is most novel in treating analytical design itself as an ecological-genomics result. Its demonstrations that genome inclusion, metadata coverage, taxonomic aggregation, classifier database scope, feature selection, and study-held-out validation can reverse or sharply reduce apparent associations provide an integrated bias audit rather than a single organismal case study. The weak transfer of environmental predictions across studies is therefore a refinement of published warnings about quantitative comparability, not evidence that ecology is irrelevant. At the same time, the corpus is in productive tension with studies that identify clear ecological or functional specialization: it suggests that such positive findings should be interpreted as localized, lineage-structured, or context-dependent signals rather than automatically generalized to genome-wide adaptation. This interpretation also aligns with ecological-genomics work emphasizing organism-specific functional and evolutionary context [PMID 33165775](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33165775/).

## What the Corpus Shows

**The observed genome set is not a neutral sample of microbial diversity.** Available genomes are enriched for organisms that are culturable, described, clinically studied, or accompanied by usable metadata. In the AlphaEarth subset, Human clinical, Human gut, and Human other genomes together comprised 38% of embedded genomes, while Soil, Marine, and Freshwater each contributed 7%; however, AlphaEarth embeddings covered only 28.4% of the 293,059 genomes in the broader pangenome collection. [src: env_embedding_explorer] Environmental metadata were also incomplete: 52.7% of genomes had unknown per-genome NCBI environment labels, although species-level majority-vote labels reached 91% coverage. [src: pitfalls] This makes [[concepts/environment-embedding-geography]] and [[concepts/sampling-depth-and-downsampling-effects]] essential reading before interpreting environment–gene-content associations.

Culture-collection and phenotype resources impose a second selection layer. Of 97,334 BacDive strains, 42,227 matched 6,426 GTDB species in one isolation-environment bridge, while 55,107 remained unmatched; only 25,089 matched strains had isolation-source metadata. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] A related phenotype bridge matched 37,368 strains to 5,647 GTDB species, with feature-specific coverage ranging from 43,378 strains for isolation-source data to 1,980 for acetate utilization. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] These losses can make ecological validation reflect the properties of well-studied lineages rather than the full environmental population. [[concepts/cultivation-collection-bias-in-ecological-genomics]] and [[concepts/phenotype-database-coverage-bias]] develop this point.

**Coverage determines what can be compared and what can be called.** A negative result is not equivalent to biological absence when a classifier, annotation system, or database lacks the relevant taxon or function. In the ENIGMA Carbon Census, 83 compounds were structure-resolved, 54 were linked to KEGG, 9 were initially callable through organism or measured-fitness evidence, and 74 were organism-dark. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] “Organism-dark” denotes a resource-defined failure to connect a compound to an organism-level utilization prediction; it does not demonstrate that the compound is absent, unknown to science, or unusable by microorganisms. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] This is the central caution in [[concepts/callability-limited-comparative-inference]].

The same asymmetry appears in functional annotation. Across 48 Fitness Browser organisms, 24.9% of genes were classified as dark, while propagating annotations through the GTDB r214 pangenome increased dark-gene conservation coverage from 57.5% to 66.6%. [src: functional_dark_matter] In the ecotype-functional analysis, approximately 38% of gene clusters had COG annotations, leaving unannotated functions outside most category-level tests; the unknown-function category itself was significantly differentiated in 11 of 12 analyzed species. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] Thus, [[concepts/taxonomic-resolution-dependent-functional-inference]] and [[concepts/functional-dark-matter]] should be treated as measurement-model pages, not merely annotation housekeeping.

Classifier compatibility creates a related bias in environmental metagenomics. GOTTCHA2 detected eukaryotic reads in 77% of 2,759 NMDC ReadbasedAnalysis runs, whereas Kraken2 and Centrifuge produced approximately 0 domain-level Eukaryota signal in the deployment because their reference databases were prokaryote-restricted. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] The resulting GOTTCHA2 estimate is therefore a relative or ordinal measurement, not a calibrated absolute contamination fraction. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] [[concepts/classifier-database-compatibility-in-taxonomic-quantification]] explains why classifier identity and database scope belong in the measurement model.

**Whole-genome signals often understate localized ecological adaptation.** Across 172 bacterial species, the median partial correlation—association between two variables after accounting for a third—was 0.0025 for environment and 0.0143 for phylogeny; phylogeny dominated in 60.5% of species, while significant positive and negative environmental effects occurred in 7.0% and 2.3% of species, respectively. [src: ecotype_analysis] The environmental reanalysis likewise found no stronger association for environmental than human-associated species: the one-sided comparison gave U=1536 and p=0.83. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] These results support a cautious whole-genome conclusion, not the claim that ecology is irrelevant.

Several projects instead identify signals in selected functions. Prophage-module composition retained environmental structure after genome-size stratification and family-level phylogenetic comparison; genome size was dominant with F=212.99, followed by environment with F=30.04 and phylogeny with F=6.17. [src: prophage_ecology] In plant-growth-promoting genes, acdS prevalence was 15.8% in soil/rhizosphere species versus 2.6% in other environments, while pqqC prevalence was 43.8% versus 21.2%. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology] The pqqC–acdS association had OR = 7.24 and q = 1.2e-83, supporting an ecological guild—genes or organisms sharing environmental selection or roles—without proving physical linkage or metabolic exchange. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology] [[concepts/genome-wide-versus-locus-specific-ecological-adaptation]] and [[concepts/gene-cooccurrence-ecological-guilds]] provide the appropriate interpretation.

Environmental effects can also be pathway-specific and lineage-structured. In *Pseudomonas*, carbon profiles were associated with isolation environment among free-living and plant-associated species, with permutation p = 0.006, but a classifier distinguishing soil, freshwater, plant_surface, and rhizosphere achieved balanced accuracy 0.408 +/- 0.169 against a 0.250 chance baseline. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] PHB, a carbon-storage and stress-associated polymer, occurred in 21.9% of 27,690 GTDB species and was enriched in environmentally variable habitats; after controlling for genome size, the association with niche breadth became much smaller. [src: phb_granule_ecology] These examples support ecological specialization while warning against extrapolating a locus-level signal to genome-wide adaptation.

**Aggregation, study design, and validation determine apparent effect size.** Taxonomic aggregation can discard abundance or merge biologically distinct strains. In the ENIGMA functional bridge, strict and relaxed mapping retained a mean mapped-abundance fraction of 0.343, whereas a species-proxy mode retained 0.031. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] The more restrictive mode reduced ambiguity but also discarded much of the evidence available for functional scoring. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] Similarly, plant-compartment analysis produced a refined PERMANOVA R² of 0.071, while the preliminary R² of 0.527 fell to 0.072 after removing three genome-rich species per compartment. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The apparent effect was therefore strongly sensitive to taxonomic composition.

Exploratory and confirmatory analyses also diverge. Predeclared genus-level ENIGMA tests were non-significant, including rho = 0.0587, p = 0.546, and FDR q = 0.862 in relaxed mapping, while a relaxed coverage-adjusted model produced FDR q = 0.0462 and a strict model produced q = 0.130. [src: enigma_contamination_functional_potential] FDR, or false-discovery rate, controls the expected proportion of false discoveries among declared positives. In a separate feature-reuse example, an exploratory 33-species candidate list fell to 3 candidates under independent within-substudy validation. [src: pitfalls] [[concepts/confirmatory-exploratory-ecological-association-discordance]] and [[concepts/selection-on-outcome-leakage]] describe safeguards such as held-out features and independent validation.

Finally, study-held-out validation distinguishes local structure from portable ecology. For eukaryotic-read fractions, random cross-validation gave environment-model R²=0.35, but GroupKFold—holding out complete studies—gave R²=−0.30 and an out-of-study detection AUC of 0.56. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] The apparent environmental association was therefore not reliably transferable across collections, even though matrix differences were statistically strong. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]

## Tensions and Caveats

The corpus does not resolve whether weak whole-genome environmental associations reflect genuine biological decoupling or measurement limitations. The original and reanalyzed ecotype workflows reported different median correlation scales, 0.003 and 0.081, but changed genome sets, embedding coverage, and sampling procedures prevent treating the difference as a biological effect. [src: ecotype_analysis, ecotype_env_reanalysis] The conflict [[conflicts/conflict--ecotype_analysis--ecotype_env_reanalysis--ecotype_functional_differentiation--57f3758f]] frames the unresolved genome-wide versus locus-specific interpretation.

A second tension concerns positive environmental signals versus lineage and coverage confounding. Phenotype features alone explained 16.3% of variance in one BacDive analysis, taxonomy alone explained 35.4%, and adding phenotype features produced R² = 0.345. [src: bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance] Conversely, selected genes, prophage modules, and metal-associated COGs retain environmental associations after some controls. [src: prophage_ecology, pgp_pangenome_ecology, soil_metal_functional_genomics] The conflict [[conflicts/conflict--bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance--ecotype_analysis--ecotype_functional_differentiation--d8143c37]] cautions that both broad nulls and narrow positives may be valid at different resolutions.

Metal studies show the same scale problem: one species-scale validation found rho approximately -0.02 with p > 0.8, whereas broader atlas and community analyses reported positive metal-related signals. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics] These are not interchangeable endpoints; they measure fitness, occurrence, or residual community functional variation. See [[conflicts/conflict--metal_cross_resistance--metal_fitness_atlas--metal_specificity--6d841267]].
