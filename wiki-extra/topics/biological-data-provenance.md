# Biological Data Provenance

Biological data provenance is the record of where data came from, who maintains it, how it was transformed, which identifiers and evidence types it contains, and whether it remains current and accessible. This corpus treats provenance as part of the measurement model rather than as administrative metadata: a database name can conceal multiple authorities, a successful join can connect incompatible biological entities, and an apparent ecological or functional signal can reflect classifier, study, or sampling structure. The central question is therefore not merely whether data can be found, but whether a scientist can reconstruct and defend the path from source record to biological claim.

## Literature Context

Published data-infrastructure work consistently treats provenance, standardization, and discoverability as prerequisites for reuse rather than as downstream documentation. The ENCODE Uniform Analysis Pipelines formalize reproducible processing across large genomic collections, while the ENCODE portal presents data through standardized metadata and an organized access layer [PMID 37503119](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37503119/) [PMID 37066421](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37066421/) [PMID 26527727](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26527727/). Comparable principles appear in ecological informatics: Soranno et al. describe a multi-scaled geospatial and temporal database assembled from disparate sources to promote open science and reuse [PMID 26140212](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26140212/). Semantic management systems such as SemPoD likewise frame explicit representation of biological data and relationships as necessary for translational analysis [PMID 23282161](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23282161/). More recent ecosystem proposals extend this lifecycle perspective to NMR data, structural modeling, and membrane-protein annotation by emphasizing auditable workflows, integrated resources, and benchmark-ready outputs [PMID 41903834](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41903834/) [PMID 42037134](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42037134/) [PMID 42626122](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42626122/) [PMID 42037134](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42037134/).

The BERIL corpus is consistent with this literature in treating provenance as part of the analytical substrate. Its emphasis on live schema inspection, lineage artifacts, explicit missing-value semantics, and reproducible execution extends the uniform-pipeline and data-lifecycle logic from individual resources to a heterogeneous lakehouse. The corpus also operationalizes a concern implicit in disparate-source ecology databases: discovery must expose scale, authority, temporal currency, and ownership. The [[concepts/provenance-aware-resource-discovery]] analysis shows why names alone are inadequate, while [[concepts/data-landscape-ownership-and-coverage-bias]] makes visible how agency and tenant concentration can shape apparent biological coverage.

The corpus extends the literature most sharply by distinguishing a potential schema connection from a realized biological integration. Its 536 cross-tenant bridges, corrected identifier route for the Fitness Browser integration, and unresolved high-leverage joins show that standardized access does not guarantee shared identifier semantics, matching records, or interpretable evidence. This is a novel emphasis relative to the candidate infrastructure papers: provenance is tested at the claim level, not merely recorded at the dataset or workflow level. The corpus further identifies tensions that broad lifecycle frameworks can obscure, including classifier-database effects, pooled-run statistical units, occurrence versus catabolic activity, and production versus measured dependency. Thus, the corpus does not reject harmonized portals or auditable pipelines; it argues that they are necessary but insufficient. A join may be executable yet biologically misleading, and a reproducible workflow may faithfully reproduce a classifier- or sampling-dependent signal. These findings motivate the corpus’s evidence-state framework in [[concepts/cross-tenant-data-bridging]] and its separation of measurement types in [[concepts/occurrence-versus-catabolic-activity]].

## What the Corpus Shows

**Discovery must expose authority, scale, and currency together.**  
Resource discovery is unreliable when it depends on names alone. An audit resolved 20 database names containing `nmdc` to 7 maintained resources across three tenants and six provenance classes, including genuine NMDC resources, NCBI and Pfam re-hosts, KBase-derived products, and a NEON namesake collision. [src: nmdc_context_audit] The genuine NMDC biosample universe contained 16,640 samples, whereas the co-hosted NCBI mirror contained 51,711,888 biosamples, creating an approximately 3,000× scale trap if resource role and object counts are not shown at discovery time. [src: nmdc_context_audit] This is the practical rationale for [[concepts/provenance-aware-resource-discovery]]: authority, tenant, scale, access surface, and freshness should be visible before analysis begins.

Currency is equally important. The Web of Microbes resource used in one integration was a 2018 export accessed through the Wayback Machine, containing 37 organisms and 589 metabolites; 332 metabolites, or 56.4%, were unidentified, and only 20 experimental organisms were represented. [src: webofmicrobes_explorer] The snapshot can support historical exploration, but it cannot silently stand in for a current metabolomics service or for broader organism coverage. [src: webofmicrobes_explorer] Similarly, live BERDL discovery should inspect current Iceberg namespaces and schemas rather than relying on archived SQL or historical inventories. [src: pitfalls]

**Ownership and documentation shape what appears to be available.**  
The BERDL atlas inventories 1,740 deduplicated tables across 119 databases, 17 tenants, and 10 agencies or programs. [src: berdl_data_atlas] DOE accounts for approximately 78% of tables, while six biological topics are more than 75% single-owner; mobile_phage is 96% PhageFoundry-owned and pangenome is 79% KBase-owned. [src: berdl_data_atlas] These concentrations mean that apparent topic coverage can reflect funding, curation, and sampling history rather than a balanced collection of independent observations. [src: berdl_data_atlas] Provenance analysis should therefore record ownership and agency alongside biological topic, not treat the catalog as an owner-neutral pool.

Observed reuse is also partly an observability measurement. A README-based audit found that 51 of 66 BERIL projects, or 77%, spanned multiple tenants, with KBase appearing in 53/66 projects and KEScience in 35/66. [src: berdl_data_atlas] Because the audit searched README files, reuse documented only in notebooks, research plans, or query histories may be missing; the reported breadth is therefore a lower bound. [src: berdl_data_atlas] This motivates [[concepts/cross-tenant-data-bridging]] and cautions against interpreting “zero documented use” as proof that a bridge was never used.

**A schema link is only the first provenance state.**  
The atlas identifies 536 unordered cross-tenant bridges from 29 canonical join keys, including `sample_id`, `genome_id`, `ncbi_taxon_id`, `feature_id`, and `ec_number`. [src: berdl_data_atlas] These are potential connections, not evidence that values share an identifier namespace, biological meaning, or overlapping records. [src: berdl_data_atlas] The distinction is formalized in [[concepts/cross-tenant-data-bridging]] and [[concepts/cross-tenant-data-bridging]].

The validated UC1 bridge illustrates the required progression. A proposed `protein_id` route was corrected after live SQL probing showed that Fitness Browser exposed the composite `orgId`, `locusId` key. [src: berdl_data_atlas] The corrected path joined gene-fitness records to SwissProt best hits and then to AlphaFold entries, producing a cohort of 55,454 genes across 48 organisms with both fitness data and an AlphaFold model. [src: berdl_data_atlas] By contrast, four other high-leverage bridges—between ENIGMA and PhageFoundry, KBase and refdata, NMDC and PROTECT, and NMDC and refdata—remained unvalidated in the study. [src: berdl_data_atlas] A defensible bridge should document semantic definitions, identifier transformations, match and duplicate rates, provenance, and representative biological records before interpretation.

**Evidence types must not be collapsed after joining.**  
A technically executable join does not make its endpoints biologically equivalent. The ENIGMA carbon census began with 83 structure-resolved compounds, 54 KEGG-linked compounds, and only 9 callable compounds; 74/83, or 89%, remained organism-dark under the project’s definitions. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Environmental occurrence of candidate genera therefore could not substitute for compound-resolved catabolic evidence: the atlas detected organisms in 1,719 NMDC metagenomes and 302 Planet Microbe runs, but no environmental dataset measured use of the census compounds. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] This distinction is developed in [[concepts/occurrence-versus-catabolic-activity]] and [[concepts/callability-limited-comparative-inference]].

The same principle applies to metabolite-to-gene inference. The Web of Microbes exploration found 19 produced metabolites also represented in Fitness Browser carbon- or nitrogen-source experiments, but the 2018 snapshot lacked organism consumption actions. [src: webofmicrobes_explorer] Production and growth-related records can therefore motivate a testable hypothesis without establishing uptake, pathway flux, or ecological utilization. [src: webofmicrobes_explorer] In another matched integration, 17/21 metabolites were fully concordant across Web of Microbes, Fitness Browser, BacDive, and GapMind, but concordance in a selected matched subset still does not establish condition-specific dependence. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]

**Measurement provenance includes classifiers, statistical units, and execution behavior.**  
A classifier-derived abundance is jointly determined by reads, classifier, reference database, and reporting scheme. In the NMDC eukaryotic-read analysis, GOTTCHA2 detected eukaryotic reads in 77% of 2,759 runs, while Kraken2 and Centrifuge produced approximately 0 domain-level Eukaryota signal because their deployed databases were prokaryote-restricted. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] The near-zero outputs therefore cannot be interpreted as evidence that eukaryotic reads were absent. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] Cross-study analyses must preserve classifier and database identity as provenance fields, as argued in [[concepts/classifier-database-compatibility-in-taxonomic-quantification]].

Statistical provenance also begins with the observed data grain. Of the 2,759 runs, 1,067 were pooled from multiple biosamples, so the analysis used `workflow_run_id` rather than treating each biosample as an independent observation. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] This avoids pseudoreplication—artificially inflating sample size by duplicating one run-level measurement—but representative-biosample labeling can still introduce metadata noise. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] These limitations are detailed in [[concepts/pooled-run-pseudoreplication-and-metadata-label-noise]].

Finally, computational execution is part of reproducibility. Large BERDL tables require live schema inspection, explicit lineage artifacts such as `lineage.yaml`, and missing-value dictionaries that distinguish pending data from true NULL values. [src: discoveries] Joins touching the 2.5B-row UniProt identifier table require broadcast-join safeguards, and notebook workflows may need cached CSV intermediates rather than direct `toPandas()` conversion. [src: discoveries] A dataset that is theoretically available but cannot be joined, interpreted, or rerun is not yet a realized evidence source.

## Tensions and Caveats

The corpus’s main disagreement is between broad connectivity and evidential validity. [[conflicts/conflict--berdl_data_atlas--cofitness_coinheritance--ecotype_env_reanalysis]] contrasts 536 schema-level bridges and apparently actionable cross-collection associations with the fact that only UC1 was sample-executed, while other integrations retained ambiguous identifiers, indirect prophage inference, or weak external validation. [src: berdl_data_atlas, webofmicrobes_explorer, prophage_ecology] The resolution is not to reject large-scale integration, but to label its evidence state: discovered, value-overlapping, executable, validated, or biologically interpretable.

A second tension concerns environmental signals. Random cross-validation of eukaryotic read fractions produced R²=0.35 for environment, but study-held-out GroupKFold produced R²=−0.30 and an out-of-study detection AUC of 0.56. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] Within one approximately constant NEON protocol, local vegetation and geography remained associated with the response, but missing extraction, filtration, host-depletion, and library-preparation metadata prevent clean causal interpretation. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] This is anchored by [[conflicts/conflict--env_embedding_explorer--euk_in_prok_correlates--gene_function_ecological_agora]]: provenance-compatible validation can preserve a within-study signal while showing that cross-study portability is unresolved.

A third tension concerns agreement among models and assays. [[conflicts/conflict--essential_metabolome--fw300_metabolic_consistency--metabolic_capability_dependency]] records 100% concordance in a selected 21-comparison subset alongside broader analyses in which 57/161 complete pathway pairs were active dependencies and 66/161 were complete but latent. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency, discoveries] Completeness, annotation agreement, production, and measured requirement are distinct evidence types; provenance must retain those distinctions rather than promoting concordance into proof of function.
