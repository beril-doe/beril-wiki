# Biological Data Interoperability

Biological data interoperability is the ability to discover, connect, and interpret data held in different databases, tenants, projects, and evidence systems without confusing technical connectivity with biological comparability. This corpus speaks directly to the topic because it spans shared identifiers, cross-tenant data bridges, provenance audits, metabolite–fitness integration, environmental metadata, literature links, and phenotype resources. Taken together, the projects show that interoperability is not a single engineering property: it is a staged process extending from resource discovery and schema matching through identifier validation, evidence alignment, and biologically defensible interpretation.

## Literature Context

Published work generally frames biological data interoperability as a combination of shared semantics, explicit metadata, provenance, and reproducible computational infrastructure rather than as simple database connectivity. Reviews and methodological papers have emphasized the need to represent microbial interactions and omics data in FAIR (findable, accessible, interoperable, and reusable) forms, with models that preserve biological context across sources [PMID 36005399](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36005399/) [PMID 33401308](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33401308/). Cross-organism systems such as InterMine demonstrate the value of common schemas and queryable relationships for comparative analysis [PMID 26097192](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26097192/), while KBase workflows show how integrated analysis environments can support reproducible extraction and interpretation of metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs) [PMID 36376589](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36376589/). Related work in bioimaging, biomarker modeling, and computational toxicology likewise treats data models, minimum data sets, and documentation standards as prerequisites for meaningful reuse [PMID 36853922](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36853922/) [PMID 35925813](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35925813/) [PMID 31404555](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31404555/) [PMID 33720747](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33720747/).

The corpus is consistent with this literature but makes the requirements operational at an unusually broad, cross-tenant scale. Its distinction between candidate bridges and realized integrations extends the usual FAIR and schema-centered framing: the BERDL inventory exposes a large connection surface, yet documented reuse is concentrated and several proposed bridges have no demonstrated use. Its [[concepts/provenance-aware-resource-discovery]] theme further sharpens published recommendations by showing that identical or similar resource names can conceal different authorities, mirrors, derivatives, scales, and freshness. This is consistent with work on centralized and federated data sharing, which identifies governance, access, and provenance as central design problems rather than merely technical obstacles [PMID 38836701](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38836701/). The corpus extends that perspective by treating tenant placement, ownership concentration, documentation practices, and historical snapshots as measurable determinants of what interoperability appears to enable.

The corpus also extends the literature from record linkage to endpoint validation. Its [[concepts/potential-versus-realized-data-integration]] framework shows that a shared field name is insufficient without namespace checks, key probing, overlap measurement, duplicate assessment, and biological review. More importantly, the FW300 comparison demonstrates that production, utilization, pathway capability, and fitness effects can agree, partially agree, or remain biologically non-equivalent even after successful joins. That emphasis is compatible with FAIR interaction-representation principles [PMID 36005399](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36005399/), but the explicit separation of technical join validity from biological interpretation is a distinctive contribution of this corpus. Relative to the candidate literature, its quantitative audit of potential versus realized bridges, provenance collisions, and research-use observability is novel; its warning that broad technical integration does not establish biological comparability is also in productive tension with infrastructure narratives that can make successful connectivity appear synonymous with validated reuse [PMID 33401308](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33401308/) [PMID 36376589](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36376589/).

## What the Corpus Shows

**The available connection surface is broad, but potential is not realized integration.** The BERDL inventory contains 1,740 deduplicated tables across 119 databases and 17 tenants, with 536 unordered cross-tenant bridges defined by 29 canonical keys. [src: berdl_data_atlas] These keys include `sample_id`, `genome_id`, `ncbi_taxon_id`, `feature_id`, and `ec_number`, spanning sample, genome, taxonomy, annotation, pathway, biochemical, protein, phage, literature, and workspace relationships. [src: berdl_data_atlas] A schema-level bridge means that datasets expose fields that could be related; it does not mean that their values refer to the same biological objects.

Documented reuse is substantial but concentrated. Fifty-one of 66 audited projects, or 77%, spanned multiple tenants; KBase appeared in 53/66 projects, or 80%, and KEScience in 35/66, or 53%. [src: berdl_data_atlas] The realized KBase × KEScience bridge accounted for 36 cross-tenant projects, mostly pangenome-by-fitness joins through `genome_id` and `ncbi_taxon_id`. [src: berdl_data_atlas] By contrast, five proposed high-leverage bridges had zero realized use at audit time, including ENIGMA–PhageFoundry, NMDC–PROTECT, and NMDC–refdata connections. [src: berdl_data_atlas] The distinction between candidate connectivity and demonstrated reuse is central to [[concepts/potential-versus-realized-data-integration]].

**Interoperability begins with provenance, not naming.** Resource labels and tenant locations are insufficient because the same apparent namespace can contain distinct authorities and derivatives. An audit of 20 database names containing `nmdc` resolved them to seven maintained resources across three tenants and six provenance classes. [src: nmdc_context_audit] These included genuine NMDC resources, NCBI and Pfam re-hosts, Arkin and NMDC-derived KBase products, and `kbase.nmdc_neon`, which is a NEON namesake collision rather than an NMDC resource. [src: nmdc_context_audit] The genuine NMDC biosample universe contained 16,640 samples, whereas a co-hosted NCBI mirror contained 51,711,888 biosamples, creating an approximately 3,000× scale trap if resource role is ignored. [src: nmdc_context_audit]

The same principle applies to historical or derived collections. The 2018 Web of Microbes snapshot contains 37 organisms and 589 metabolites, with 332, or 56.4%, unidentified; it was accessed through the Wayback Machine rather than treated as a current service. [src: webofmicrobes_explorer] PaperBLAST contains 12.4 million rows across 14 tables linking sequences and genes to literature, annotations, structures, and full-text snippets, but those links do not by themselves establish functional authority. [src: paperblast_explorer] [[concepts/provenance-aware-resource-discovery]] therefore argues that discovery records should expose authority, scale, tenant, access surface, freshness, and analytical limitations together.

**A valid join requires value-space and identifier validation.** A value space is the set of actual identifiers and values represented by a dataset, together with their semantics, namespace, transformations, and provenance. The corpus repeatedly shows why a shared column name is only a starting point: `genome_id` can represent different identifier systems in KBase, NCBI, and metagenome-assembled genome pipelines. [src: berdl_data_atlas] The validated UC1 bridge illustrates the required workflow. Fitness Browser did not expose the anticipated `protein_id`; SQL probing showed that the usable key was the composite `orgId`, `locusId`, which was then joined to SwissProt accessions and AlphaFold entries. [src: berdl_data_atlas]

That correction produced a cohort of 55,454 genes across 48 organisms with both fitness data and an AlphaFold model, including 22,303 distinct AlphaFold models. [src: berdl_data_atlas] The result supports a sequence of schema inspection, key probing, overlap measurement, duplicate assessment, and biological record review rather than assuming that an executable SQL query is a valid scientific join. [[concepts/potential-versus-realized-data-integration]] extends this requirement to identifier namespaces, transformation rules, match rates, and cross-tenant deduplication.

**Biological interoperability must align endpoints, not merely records.** Cross-collection integration becomes scientifically useful only when the linked measurements answer compatible questions. A metabolite detected in one resource, for example, is not automatically a consumed nutrient, a fitness determinant, or a gene-essentiality signal. The Web of Microbes exploration identified 19 produced metabolites for *Pseudomonas* FW300-N2E3 that also appeared in Fitness Browser carbon- or nitrogen-source experiments: five were de novo products and 14 were amplified metabolites. [src: webofmicrobes_explorer] However, the snapshot lacked organism-consumption actions, so production could not be interpreted as utilization or as evidence of gene essentiality. [src: webofmicrobes_explorer]

A more constrained four-resource comparison joined Web of Microbes exometabolomics, Fitness Browser gene fitness, BacDive utilization phenotypes, and GapMind predictions for the same organism. Of 21 testable metabolites, 17/21, or 81%, were fully concordant and 4/21, or 19%, partially concordant; none were fully discordant, and mean concordance was 0.94. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] Yet BacDive utilization was 3/7, and tryptophan was produced and associated with significant fitness effects even though 0/50 *P. fluorescens* strains used it as a carbon source. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] Interoperability therefore requires explicit endpoint labels—production, utilization, capability, dependency, flux, and fitness are not interchangeable.

The same issue appears in environmental and genomic integration. An ecotype reanalysis joined environment classifications, AlphaEarth embeddings, ANI distances, and gene-cluster memberships, but environmental species did not show stronger partial correlations than human-associated species (U=1536, p=0.83). [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] Conversely, prophage work joined gene modules, phylogeny, environmental metadata, contig localization, and NMDC samples across 27,702 bacterial species, 4,005,537 gene clusters, and 6,365 NMDC samples, while acknowledging that NMDC prophage burden was inferred indirectly and not independently validated for prophage genes. [src: prophage_ecology] A technically successful bridge can thus support a null result or a qualified association rather than a mechanistic conclusion.

**Ownership and documentation shape what interoperability appears to be.** Data availability is unevenly distributed across owners: DOE accounts for approximately 78% of BERDL tables, while six topics are more than 75% single-owner, including mobile phage at 96% PhageFoundry ownership and pangenome at 79% KBase ownership. [src: berdl_data_atlas] Among audited projects, ENIGMA contained 36% of tables but appeared in six projects, whereas PhageFoundry contained 14% and appeared in five. [src: berdl_data_atlas] Such distributions affect which biological systems and methods are easiest to connect.

Observed reuse is also a documentation measurement. The BERDL audit mined README files, so the reported 51 multi-tenant projects and 77% rate are lower bounds; notebook source, research plans, and query histories may contain additional integrations. [src: berdl_data_atlas] This limitation is developed in [[concepts/research-use-observability-bias]]. Finally, interoperability does not guarantee balanced knowledge transfer: PaperBLAST reports an organism-level literature Gini coefficient of 0.967, and 9.2% of 50%-identity protein families, corresponding to 31,653 families, had zero papers. [src: paperblast_explorer] A connected resource graph may still carry severe research-attention and functional-annotation inequality.

## Tensions and Caveats

The principal disagreement is whether broad technical integration should be treated as biological validation. [[conflicts/conflict--berdl_data_atlas--cofitness_coinheritance--ecotype_env_reanalysis]] captures this tension: large bridge inventories and strong associations coexist with ambiguous authority, incomplete endpoint semantics, and untested use cases. Only UC1 was sample-executed in the atlas; UC2–UC5 remained unvalidated. [src: berdl_data_atlas]

A second tension concerns model or pathway agreement versus active metabolism. [[conflicts/conflict--acinetobacter_adp1_explorer--adp1_triple_essentiality--annotation_gap_discovery]] is relevant because concordant predictions can support capability while failing to establish condition-specific flux or dependency. The FW300 comparison found strong concordance in its tested set, but tryptophan remained a counterexample in which production, pathway completeness, and fitness evidence did not establish carbon utilization. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]

Environmental interoperability has a related limitation. [[conflicts/conflict--ecotype_env_reanalysis--env_embedding_explorer--harvard_forest_warming]] contrasts broad environmental nulls with subsystem-specific signals. The ecotype result was null at the reported scale, while prophage integration found environmental effects after controls; these findings need not conflict if they operate in different feature spaces, cohorts, or levels of biological organization. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] [src: prophage_ecology]

Finally, [[conflicts/conflict--nmdc_context_audit--paperblast_explorer--pitfalls]] highlights that successful queries and apparent integration can conceal provenance ambiguity, interface failures, incidental literature mentions, or stale snapshots. Counts obtained through Iceberg metadata were usable for audited NMDC tables, while REST `/count` and `/schema` were unreliable for some large-table workflows. [src: nmdc_context_audit] [src: pitfalls]
