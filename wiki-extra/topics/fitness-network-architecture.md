# Fitness Network Architecture

Fitness network architecture is the organization of gene–phenotype relationships across conditions, organisms, genomic neighborhoods, and pangenomes. In this corpus, fitness is not arranged as a simple list of independently acting genes: resistance determinants, metabolic enzymes, cofactors, respiratory complexes, regulatory genes, and unknown-function genes can participate in shared condition-dependent modules. The corpus speaks to this topic through genome-wide RB-TnSeq measurements, cofitness networks, independent component analysis (ICA), pangenome conservation, and pathway-specific case studies, while also showing where these methods cannot distinguish biological coupling from shared dispensability, ancestry, or laboratory context.

## Literature Context

The methodological foundation for this corpus is the use of transposon sequencing (Tn-seq), which enables parallel measurement of mutant fitness across many genes and conditions and can be adapted to genetic-interaction analyses. [PMID 19767758](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19767758/) Earlier work has also framed bacterial fitness as a property of network architecture rather than isolated gene effects: network hubs can influence evolvability, while alternative or “underground” metabolic routes can shape evolutionary potential even when they are not part of the primary pathway. [PMID 37147799](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37147799/) [PMID 25071190](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25071190/) Studies of bacterial interspecies hybrids further show that genetic architecture and fitness can be reorganized across genomic backgrounds, supporting the view that phenotype depends on interactions among genes and cellular context. [PMID 33247724](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33247724/)

The corpus is consistent with this literature in treating fitness as modular, condition-dependent, and physiologically distributed. Its RB-TnSeq and cofitness results extend the Tn-seq framework from individual fitness measurements to cross-condition modules, conserved module families, and large support networks. The finding that modules can connect resistance, metabolism, cofactors, respiration, regulation, and unknown-function genes is a broader empirical realization of network-level coupling and underground-metabolism principles. The ADP1 quinate example further extends prior pathway-centered thinking by showing that a functional system can be genomically dispersed while remaining physiologically integrated. This interpretation is compatible with work emphasizing computational representations of metabolic circuits and their design constraints. [PMID 38662439](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38662439/) It also provides a systems-level context for studies of Acinetobacter-specific regulation, although the corpus's cofitness assignments should remain hypotheses rather than definitive regulatory annotations. [PMID 40387328](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40387328/) [PMID 39026751](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39026751/)

The corpus goes beyond the candidate literature by quantifying conserved fitness modules across 32 organisms, contrasting pairwise cofitness with module-level pangenome co-occurrence, and separating baseline AMR knockout burden from antibiotic-condition benefit. These results suggest that multi-gene architecture is more informative for inheritance than isolated pairwise similarity, a distinction not established by the cited network studies. They also expose an important tension: large cofitness neighborhoods may reflect shared dispensability rather than mechanistic coupling, and modeled essentiality can disagree with measured growth phenotypes. Computational essentiality work similarly underscores the value—and limits—of combining genome-scale models with learned predictors. [PMID 38448436](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38448436/) Finally, the corpus's gene-specific ecological associations are compatible with literature emphasizing complex root–soil–microbe interactions, but its weak whole-genome environmental effects sharpen that claim into a testable hypothesis about ecological guilds rather than uniform genome-wide adaptation. [PMID 28514442](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28514442/)

## What the Corpus Shows

**Fitness architecture is modular, but modules are phenotypic rather than automatically regulatory.** Cofitness—the similarity of genes’ fitness profiles across experimental conditions—reveals coordinated responses across genes that may be far apart on the chromosome. Across 32 organisms, ICA identified **1,116** stable modules; **94.2%** showed elevated within-module cofitness, with mean within-module \(|r|=0.34\) compared with **0.12** in the background and **22.7×** genomic-adjacency enrichment. [src: fitness_modules] The adjacency signal indicates that some modules reflect operons or local gene organization, but the enrichment does not reduce cofitness to physical proximity. [src: fitness_modules]

AMR genes illustrate the scale of this architecture. Among **801** AMR genes with fitness data, **192 (24%)** were assigned to ICA modules, and **769/801 (96%)** had at least one extra-operon cofitness partner at \(|r|>0.3\). [src: amr_cofitness_networks] Mean AMR support-network size was **233 genes** at \(|r|>0.3\), **110** at \(|r|>0.4\), and **71** at \(|r|>0.5\). [src: amr_cofitness_networks] These large neighborhoods are best interpreted as condition-associated phenotypic systems: a gene can correlate with another because both become important under the same stress or both become dispensable under the same laboratory conditions. [src: amr_cofitness_networks]

The recovered architecture is also conserved at a broad level. Of **209** AMR gene–module assignments, **208 (99%)** belonged to cross-organism conserved module families. [src: amr_cofitness_networks] Across the wider module collection, **156** families spanned at least two organisms, **28** spanned five or more, and one spanned **21 of 32** organisms. [src: discoveries] This supports recurring process-level organization, but not the claim that every gene in an aligned module has an identical molecular function. [src: fitness_modules]

**Condition dependence links baseline burden to context-specific benefit.** Fitness networks are shaped by the environment in which perturbations are measured. Under non-antibiotic conditions, AMR-gene knockouts had a pooled relative shift of **+0.086 [95% CI: +0.074, +0.098]** compared with non-AMR knockouts across **25** organisms. [src: amr_fitness_cost] This is a relative RB-TnSeq contrast, not evidence that AMR knockouts had positive absolute growth: their mean absolute fitness was **−0.024**, and testing against zero gave **p = 0.999**. [src: amr_fitness_cost]

The same resistance genes could become advantageous in a different condition. Across antibiotic experiments, **57.0%** of AMR genes showed a fitness flip toward greater importance, based on **N = 797**, with mean flip **+0.045** and Wilcoxon **p = 0.0001**. [src: amr_fitness_cost] The architecture was mechanism-sensitive under exposure: broad-spectrum efflux genes had mean flip **+0.094**, while enzymatic-inactivation genes had **−0.001** (**p = 0.007**). [src: amr_fitness_cost] Yet mechanism did not determine baseline cost: efflux, enzymatic inactivation, metal resistance, and unknown mechanisms did not differ significantly (**H = 0.65, p = 0.89**). [src: amr_fitness_cost]

This decoupling is central. A gene’s network position may encode conditional protection without implying a large constitutive burden, and pangenome distribution need not follow the same logic as laboratory knockout cost. Mechanism strongly predicted conservation status (**χ² = 69.3, p = 1.4×10⁻¹³**), while core and accessory AMR genes had virtually identical mean fitness distributions of **−0.024** (**p = 0.33**). [src: amr_fitness_cost]

**Physiological coupling crosses genomic boundaries.** The *Acinetobacter baylyi* ADP1 quinate-support network provides a concrete example of distributed architecture. It contained **51 genes**, of which **44/51 (86%)** belonged to four subsystems: **8** aromatic-pathway genes, **21** Complex I genes, **7** iron-acquisition genes, and **2** PQQ-biosynthesis genes; it also included **6** transcriptional regulators and **7** unassigned genes. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] These functions are coupled because quinate catabolism requires PQQ-dependent dehydrogenation, iron-dependent aromatic-ring cleavage, TCA-cycle oxidation, and respiratory reoxidation of NADH. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

The genes were not organized as one physical locus. Complex I occupied **714–729 kb**, the pca/qui pathway **1,709–1,724 kb**, PQQ biosynthesis was at **2,461 kb**, and iron-acquisition genes were distributed across **4 loci**. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Only **9** genomic clusters contained at least two quinate-specific genes, with an overall nearest-neighbor ratio of **0.89 observed/expected**. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Thus, network architecture can be physiologically integrated while remaining genomically dispersed.

Respiratory genes made the coupling especially visible. Complex I accounted for **21/51 (41%)** of the support network, and flux-balance analysis predicted **1.76×** higher Complex I flux on aromatic substrates, with fluxes of **0.55** versus **0.31**. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Experimentally, **10/13** Complex I operon subunits produced quinate-specific growth defects. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Cofitness also assigned previously uncharacterized proteins such as ACIAD3137 and ACIAD2176 to the Complex I-associated system at \(r>0.98\), generating testable hypotheses rather than definitive annotations. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

**Multi-gene structure is more informative than isolated pairwise links for inheritance.** Laboratory cofitness alone was a weak predictor of pangenome co-occurrence. Across **9** organisms and **2,253,491** cofit pairs, the mean delta phi was **+0.011**, with aggregate delta **+0.003**; the across-organism Wilcoxon test was not significant (**W=9, p=0.13**). [src: cofitness_coinheritance] By contrast, across **195** ICA modules in **6** organisms, within-module co-occurrence exceeded a prevalence-matched null by delta phi **+0.053**; **51/195 (26%)** were significant at \(p<0.05\), and **21/195 (11%)** remained significant at \(q<0.05\). [src: cofitness_coinheritance]

The strongest module-level inheritance signal occurred in accessory modules: mean delta phi was **+0.108**, compared with **+0.059** for core modules and **+0.031** for mixed modules. [src: cofitness_coinheritance] However, the accessory-versus-core contrast only trended toward significance (**p=0.051**). [src: cofitness_coinheritance] More broadly, fitness modules were predominantly core: module genes were **86.0%** core versus **81.5%** for all genes, and the median module was **93.4%** core. [src: module_conservation] The architecture therefore includes both conserved physiological programs and more mobile, potentially co-inherited accessory units.

**Ecological guilds extend the architecture beyond laboratory fitness.** Pangenome co-occurrence can reveal recurring ecological combinations even when physical linkage is unknown. In plant-growth-promoting bacteria, eight of ten focal-gene pairs were significant after correction; the strongest was pqqC–acdS with OR **7.24**, \(n=286\), and \(q=1.2e-83\). [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology] The association was accompanied by environmental differentiation: acdS prevalence was **15.8%** in soil/rhizosphere species versus **2.6%** elsewhere, with OR **7.02**. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology] Yet whole-genome environmental effects were generally weak: across **172** species, the median partial correlation for environment was **0.0025**, and no significant environmental effect occurred in **156 (90.7%)**. [src: ecotype_analysis] This contrast supports a gene- or module-specific ecological architecture rather than a uniform environmental restructuring of entire genomes.

## Tensions and Caveats

The central interpretive tension is whether cofitness reflects mechanistic coupling or shared dispensability. AMR neighborhoods were enriched for flagellar, chemotaxis, and amino-acid-biosynthesis functions, but the original null matched conservation class rather than mean fitness. [src: amr_cofitness_networks] Because genes with similar condition-dependent dispensability can correlate without shared regulation, these enrichments require a fitness-matched null, ideally retaining conservation matching while also matching the mean-fitness distribution. [src: amr_cofitness_networks] This unresolved issue is anchored in [[conflicts/conflict--amr_cofitness_networks--amr_strain_variation--aromatic_catabolism_network]].

A second tension concerns inheritance versus physical linkage. Only **0.7%** of cofit pairs were adjacent within five genes, while ICA modules showed a stronger co-occurrence signal than pairs. [src: cofitness_coinheritance] This supports multi-gene ecological or selective units, but co-occurrence does not establish shared operons, islands, or transfer events. The prevalence ceiling is a further limitation: in Korea, **95.2%** of cofit pairs had undefined phi because both genes were present in **100% of 72 genomes**. [src: cofitness_coinheritance] These issues are developed in [[conflicts/conflict--amr_cofitness_networks--amr_fitness_cost--amr_pangenome_atlas]] and [[conflicts/conflict--amr_cofitness_networks--amr_strain_variation--aromatic_catabolism_network]].

Finally, modeled support can disagree with phenotype. FBA predicted **0%** essentiality for Complex I despite defects in **10/13** Complex I subunits, while another ADP1-specific analysis reported a Complex I growth ratio of **1.44** on glucose and zero predicted NDH-2 flux. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] These results may reflect different metrics, conditions, and model assumptions rather than a single contradiction; the issue is documented in [[conflicts/conflict--aromatic_catabolism_network--respiratory_chain_wiring]] and [[conflicts/conflict--acinetobacter_adp1_explorer--adp1_triple_essentiality--annotation_gap_discovery]]. More generally, ICA module membership is process-level evidence: changing component thresholds, experiment depth, and annotation thresholds changes module size, coverage, and apparent functional yield. [src: fitness_modules]
