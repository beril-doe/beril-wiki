# Ecological Genomic Inference

Ecological genomic inference asks how environmental context, geography, lineage, gene content, and measured phenotypes can be connected without confusing genomic potential with realized ecological function. This corpus speaks directly to that problem through comparative pangenomics, environmental embeddings, metagenomic occurrence, functional annotation, laboratory fitness, and field observations. Its central message is conditional: ecological structure is detectable, often at particular loci, pathways, modules, or lineages, but broad genome-wide and cross-study conclusions are highly sensitive to representation, taxonomic resolution, database coverage, validation design, and the definition of the biological outcome.

## Literature Context

Published comparative-genomic work provides a strong basis for treating ancestry, population structure, and gene flow as primary controls on observed genomic similarity. Studies of the human microbiome frame microbial populations as structured evolutionary systems in which recombination, transmission, and local demographic history shape gene-content variation rather than environmental labels alone [PMID 31780057](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31780057/). Genomic epidemiology likewise shows how whole-genome variation can reconstruct transmission and evolutionary dynamics in pathogen populations [PMID 32407507](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32407507/), while work on bacterial recombination emphasizes that lateral exchange can redistribute adaptive traits across otherwise divergent lineages [PMID 37755994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37755994/). Deep phylogenetic studies further demonstrate that the choice of evolutionary framework and taxonomic sampling strongly affects conclusions about ancestral states and ecological transitions [PMID 31430481](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31430481/); recent reconstruction of early eukaryotic history similarly illustrates how lineage history constrains interpretation of genomic innovation [PMID 39585925](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39585925/).

This literature is consistent with the corpus’s finding that phylogeny generally explains broad bacterial gene-content similarity better than environmental similarity, while ecological differentiation can remain detectable in particular functional compartments. The prophage literature is especially aligned with the corpus: prophage genomic analyses have connected gene content with phage-cycle regulation and prophage inactivation, showing why mobile genetic modules may carry ecological information not visible in genome-wide comparisons [PMID 39132840](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39132840/). Research on viral genetic diversity likewise treats ecological and evolutionary processes as interacting determinants of genome composition rather than as separable causes [PMID 31430481](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31430481/). The corpus extends these ideas beyond transmission-focused or single-lineage systems by quantifying how environmental associations change with genomic scale: soil-metal COG associations, prophage modules, PGP gene co-occurrence, and *Pseudomonas* carbon profiles provide comparative evidence for locus-, pathway-, and module-specific ecological structure. Its explicit comparison of pooled association with study-held-out prediction also sharpens a concern implicit in genomic epidemiology: a detectable contrast need not be transportable across sampling designs, databases, or studies [PMID 32407507](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32407507/).

The corpus is also consistent with experimental literature cautioning that genomic potential does not automatically represent realized function. Gnotobiotic-mouse work demonstrates that cross-species engraftment can be biased and can produce metabolic divergence from the donor community, directly illustrating the importance of host context and validation when inferring activity from genomic content [PMID 41277418](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41277418/). Against that background, the corpus’s separation of occurrence, pathway completeness, laboratory fitness, and field abundance is a useful extension, particularly in its quantified laboratory–field concordance and its warning that exploratory associations may be inflated by compositional coupling. The more novel contribution is therefore methodological and integrative: it makes representation, classifier database coverage, metadata provenance, and validation transportability explicit parts of ecological genomic inference, rather than treating them as secondary technical limitations. These results motivate [[concepts/environmental-embedding-ecological-validity]], [[concepts/occurrence-versus-catabolic-activity]], and [[concepts/lab-field-fitness-concordance]] as linked components of the same inference problem.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Whole-genome ancestry usually dominates, but does not erase ecological differentiation.** Across 13,381 genomes from 224 species, with correlation results for 172 species, phylogeny generally explained bacterial gene-content similarity better than environmental similarity. The median partial correlation was 0.0025 for environment versus 0.0143 for phylogeny; phylogeny dominated in 60.5% of species and environment in 39.5%. Significant environmental effects were positive in 12 species (7.0%), negative in 4 species (2.3%), and absent in 156 species (90.7%). [src: ecotype_analysis] A partial correlation measures association between two variables after accounting for a third variable. These results support treating ancestry as the default explanation for broad gene-content similarity, while leaving room for ecological adaptation concentrated in restricted genomic regions. [[concepts/environmental-embedding-ecological-validity]] [[concepts/genome-wide-versus-locus-specific-ecological-adaptation]]

A reanalysis confirms the comparison-level null rather than showing that clinical sampling bias explains it. Among 224 classified species, 106 (47%) were majority human-associated, 47 (21%) majority environmental, and 71 (32%) Mixed/Other. Environmental species had median partial correlation 0.051 versus 0.084 for human-associated species; the one-sided Environmental > Human-associated test gave U=1536 and p=0.83. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] However, the reanalysis used a different genome universe and sampling strategy: its all-species median was 0.081, compared with 0.003 in the original analysis, and the reports caution that absolute values are not directly comparable. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] [[concepts/sampling-depth-and-downsampling-effects]]

**Ecological signals are often locus-, pathway-, or module-specific.** The weak whole-genome result does not imply that environmental selection is absent. In a soil analysis spanning 51,748 samples and nine metals, 2,355 significant COG–metal associations were detected at FDR < 0.05; COG means Cluster of Orthologous Groups functional category, and FDR means false discovery rate. ABC and RND transporters and biosynthesis genes dominated top hits. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics] A conditional distance-based redundancy analysis (db-RDA), a multivariate method relating community profiles to predictors, yielded R² = 0.799 and p = 0.005 after conditioning on batch and project effects. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics] This is a residual-variance result, not an unconditional estimate of the total environmental effect, and co-contaminating metals leave metal specificity unresolved. [[concepts/ecotype-environment-gene-content]]

Other targeted systems show the same pattern. Prophage-module composition was associated with genome size, environment, and family-level phylogeny; genome size was dominant with F=212.99, followed by environment with F=30.04 and phylogeny with F=6.17. Environmental effects remained significant within every genome-size quartile, with all p < 6.5e-78. [src: prophage_ecology] In *Pseudomonas*, carbon profiles were associated with isolation environment among free-living and plant-associated species at permutation p = 0.006, although a five-fold Random Forest classifier reached balanced accuracy 0.408 +/- 0.169 against a 0.250 chance baseline. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] These findings support ecological information in selected functional spaces, not a universal environment-determined genome architecture.

Gene co-occurrence can identify ecological guilds, meaning recurring sets of genes associated with a shared niche or ecological role, without proving physical linkage or joint activity. Across 27,702 species, eight of 10 focal PGP gene pairs were significant after BH-FDR correction; the strongest positive association was pqqC–acdS with OR = 7.24, n = 286 co-occurring species, and q = 1.2e-83. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology] Soil/rhizosphere prevalence was also elevated for acdS, pqqC, and hcnC, while nifH was depleted. [src: pgp_pangenome_ecology] Yet co-occurring plant-associated genus pairs were slightly less complementary than random pairs, with Cohen’s d ≈ −0.4 and permutation p < 0.001. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] The corpus therefore favors shared selection as one interpretation of guild structure, not automatic claims of metabolic cooperation. [[concepts/gene-cooccurrence-ecological-guilds]]

**Environmental measurements are proxies whose validity depends on scale and provenance.** AlphaEarth embeddings captured geographic structure: mean cosine distance increased from 0.41 for pairs less than 100 km apart to 0.82 for pairs in the 10K–20K km bin, with environmental samples showing a stronger nearby-to-intercontinental gradient than human-associated samples. [src: env_embedding_explorer] But the embeddings covered only 28.4% of 293,059 genomes, and environmental metadata were uneven: isolation_source covered 91.6% of embedded genomes, host 63.7%, env_broad_scale 41.8%, and env_medium 37.8%. A keyword workflow captured 71% of genomes with an isolation-source label, leaving 17% in Other and 12.5% Unknown. [src: env_embedding_explorer] Geographic coordinates may describe a collection site rather than the organism’s relevant host, tissue, chemical exposure, or microenvironment. [src: ecotype_analysis]

This distinction is crucial when apparent environmental prediction fails to transport. In 2,759 NMDC ReadbasedAnalysis runs from nine studies, GOTTCHA2 detected eukaryotic reads in 77% of runs, with median fraction 2.7% and mean 13.3%; Kraken2 and Centrifuge produced approximately 0 domain-level Eukaryota signal because their deployed databases were prokaryote-restricted. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] Classifier database compatibility therefore forms part of the measurement model: an unrepresented taxon cannot be treated as absent. Although random cross-validation gave environment-model R²=0.35, study-held-out GroupKFold validation gave R²=−0.30 and detection AUC=0.56. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] [[concepts/classifier-database-compatibility-in-taxonomic-quantification]] [[concepts/study-batch-confounding-of-environmental-associations]]

**Genomic potential is not realized ecological activity.** The ENIGMA Carbon Census illustrates an evidence funnel from chemical identity to organism-level inference. Of 83 compounds, all were structure-resolved, 54 were KEGG-linked, and only 9 were initially callable through isolate or measured-fitness evidence, leaving 74 organism-dark compounds. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] “Organism-dark” means not linkable through the queried resources; it does not mean biologically absent or scientifically unknown. The dark set included 33 compounds with no reaction in queried genomes and 29 fully orphan compounds lacking a KEGG link. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] [[concepts/callability-limited-comparative-inference]]

Environmental occurrence remains useful for prioritization but does not establish catabolism. The atlas detected 83 of 86 implicated genera in 1,719 NMDC metagenomes, and high-prevalence taxa can guide enrichment selection. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Nevertheless, occurrence is not substrate uptake, pathway expression, growth, or carbon flux. Similarly, GapMind pathway completeness was associated with some ambient metabolite intensities, including leucine r = −0.390, q = 0.022, n = 62, and arginine r = −0.297, q = 0.049, n = 80, but the predictor measured genomic potential rather than in situ catabolism. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] *Pseudomonas* carbon profiles likewise lacked direct activity measurements. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] [[concepts/occurrence-versus-catabolic-activity]]

Laboratory–field comparisons support conditional concordance. Among 151 accessory dark-gene clusters across 31 species, 10 of 137 environmental-category tests were significant at FDR < 0.05, and a preregistered lab–field mapping found 29 of 47 testable clusters concordant, or 61.7%. [src: functional_dark_matter] Independent NMDC validation confirmed all four testable preregistered abiotic predictions, but 441 of 449 exploratory tests also passed FDR < 0.05, plausibly because of compositional coupling. [src: functional_dark_matter] In Oak Ridge groundwater, aggregate laboratory metal tolerance was positively but non-significantly associated with high-uranium/low-uranium abundance ratio, rho = 0.503, p = 0.095, n = 12 genera, while five of 11 genera had FDR-significant associations in both directions. [src: lab_field_ecology] [[concepts/lab-field-fitness-concordance]]

**Representation determines what can be inferred.** Cultivation and collection bias affect both genotype–phenotype linkage and ecological labels. Of 97,334 BacDive strains, 42,227 (43.4%) matched 6,426 GTDB species, while 55,107 (56.6%) remained unmatched; only 25,089 matched strains had isolation-source metadata. [src: bacdive_metal_validation] In plant-associated genomics, only 7,995 of 293,059 genomes (2.7%) had plant-associated annotations, and endophytes comprised 29 species. [src: plant_microbiome_ecotypes] MicrobeAtlas further found that sequenced-genome coverage correlated with inferred metal-type diversity, r = 0.35, p = 2.6×10⁻¹⁹, making inferred niche breadth partly a sampling-effort proxy. [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]

These limitations also affect apparent ecotypes. PCA followed by KMeans identified gene-content ecotypes in 12 of 15 species, but mean silhouette score was 0.215 and median 0.174, indicating generally weak separation; KMeans assumes approximately spherical clusters. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] Embedding-based UMAP and DBSCAN similarly produced visually coherent structure whose interpretation depended on parameters, label harmonization, and taxonomic composition. [src: env_embedding_explorer] [[concepts/ecotype-clustering-validity]] [[concepts/embedding-cluster-interpretation-limits]]

## Tensions and Caveats

The corpus does not resolve whether ecological structure is broad or mainly localized. [[conflicts/conflict--ecotype_analysis--ecotype_env_reanalysis--ecotype_functional_differentiation]] Whole-genome analyses favor weak or ancestry-dominated effects, while soil metals, prophage modules, PGP genes, plant markers, and *Pseudomonas* carbon pathways show narrower signals. These are not necessarily contradictory estimands: a locus-specific response can be diluted in whole-genome similarity. [src: ecotype_analysis, soil_metal_functional_genomics, prophage_ecology, pgp_pangenome_ecology]

A second tension concerns observed association versus transportable inference. [[conflicts/conflict--ecotype_analysis--euk_in_prok_correlates]] Significant matrix contrasts and geographic embedding gradients coexist with poor study-held-out prediction. Classifier databases, study nesting, batch, and missing protocol metadata prevent treating pooled environmental differences as portable biological rules. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]

A third is the boundary between capability and activity. [[conflicts/conflict--enigma_carbon_census_1--nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology--pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]] Callable compounds, pathway completeness, co-occurrence, and environmental prevalence can prioritize experiments, but none alone demonstrates compound-resolved in situ catabolism. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1, nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology, pseudomonas_carbon_ecology]

Finally, evidence callability and cultivation bias can create asymmetric tests. [[conflicts/conflict--ecotype_env_reanalysis--enigma_carbon_census_1]] A null contrast may reflect weak biology, misaligned environmental predictors, missing genomes, unrepresented taxa, or uncallable functions. Conversely, positive adjusted associations may be exploratory, composition-driven, or sensitive to taxonomic bridge choices. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis, enigma_contamination_functional_potential, functional_dark_matter]
