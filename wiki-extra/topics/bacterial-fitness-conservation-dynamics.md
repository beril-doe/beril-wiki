# Bacterial Fitness-Conservation Dynamics

Bacterial fitness–conservation dynamics describe how laboratory measurements of gene importance relate to pangenome retention across lineages. The corpus shows a reproducible but modest connection: genes that strongly affect growth are more often conserved, yet conservation, essentiality, laboratory burden, ecological value, and functional annotation remain distinct properties. This makes the topic useful for prioritizing experiments, provided that condition coverage, perturbation callability, pangenome sampling, and environmental scope are treated as part of the evidence rather than as background details.

## Literature Context

Published work has shown that gene importance is not adequately captured by binary essential/non-essential labels. In *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*, Bosch et al. used genome-wide gene-expression tuning to measure the consequences of graded depletion rather than relying only on complete loss-of-function calls. Their study revealed diverse vulnerabilities: genes can differ substantially in the expression reduction required to impair growth, and genes that appear dispensable under one perturbation regime may still impose important physiological constraints when their expression is reduced. This work establishes that bacterial fitness is continuous, perturbation-dependent, and strongly shaped by the operating range of gene expression. [PMID 34297925](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34297925/)

The corpus is consistent with that framework in several respects. Its comparison of insertion-based and complete-knockout assays shows that binary essentiality calls can disagree sharply, whereas continuous fitness values retain predictive information; this directly reinforces the expression-tuning literature’s emphasis on graded vulnerability. The corpus also finds widespread condition dependence, including genes whose deletion effects reverse across environments, and it treats laboratory burden as distinct from evolutionary retention. These findings extend the literature from within-organism perturbation landscapes to a cross-organism comparison of fitness with pangenome conservation. The observed enrichment of essential or strongly fitness-affecting genes among core genes therefore supports the expectation that constrained functions are more often retained, while the modest effect sizes show that conservation is not a substitute for measuring vulnerability.

Several corpus results go beyond what can be inferred from the candidate study. The analyses quantify how conservation metrics, clade sampling, condition-panel depth, perturbation callability, and annotation quality alter candidate prioritization across dozens of bacteria. They also identify conserved genes that remain hypothetical and fitness-covarying modules that are enriched in the core genome, suggesting that evolutionary retention and physiological coupling can help prioritize unknown functions. These are extensions rather than direct replications of the *M. tuberculosis* result. At the same time, the corpus introduces a broader tension: genes can be costly in laboratory assays yet remain conserved, or costly and dispensable while enriched for mobile-element signals. Such patterns are compatible with the literature’s warning that apparent dispensability depends on perturbation context, but they cannot be explained by intrinsic expression vulnerability alone. They require ecological history, horizontal transfer, and pangenome sampling to be modeled explicitly through concepts such as [[concepts/laboratory-fitness-versus-natural-selection]], [[concepts/condition-dependent-gene-tradeoffs]], and [[concepts/pangenome-core-boundary-and-clade-size-bias]].

## What the Corpus Shows

**Conservation is informative, but not a definition of importance.** Across 194,216 protein-coding genes from 43 bacteria, essential genes were 82% core, whereas genes that were always neutral in tested experiments were 66% core. Fitness breadth had only a weak association with core status (Spearman rho=0.086, p=8.1e-230), despite the large dataset. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis, fitness_effects_conservation] A separate integration of 148,826 genes from 33 organisms found essential genes 86.1% core versus 81.2% for non-essential genes, with a median odds ratio of 1.56; 18 of 33 organisms showed significant enrichment after Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] These results support conservation as a prioritization signal, not as a standalone proxy for gene function or biological indispensability. The distinction is central to [[concepts/comparative-conservation-metric-calibration]] and [[concepts/pangenome-conservation-fitness-decoupling]].

Metric choice can also change practical decisions. In one dark-gene analysis, 99.9% of clusters received the same near-universal eggNOG breadth classification, making that category weakly discriminative. Replacing it with species-count scoring produced Spearman ρ = 0.982 with the original ranking, but top-50 and top-100 overlaps were only 62% and 58%. [src: functional_dark_matter] A larger GTDB r214 analysis expanded conservation coverage from 32,791 (57.5%) to 37,997 (66.6%) dark genes and recovered 5,206 additional dark genes. [src: functional_dark_matter] Thus, a globally stable ranking can still yield unstable experimental shortlists, while broader taxonomic references can reveal structure hidden by coarse metrics.

**Core genes are active, conditional, and sometimes costly.** The corpus directly challenges the idea that conserved genes are uniformly beneficial or inert in laboratory culture. Across the synthesis, 24.4% of core genes had positive fitness when deleted, compared with 19.9% of accessory genes; positive deletion fitness means that the mutant grew better under the tested conditions. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] The function-resolved analysis found that core genes were more burdensome in Protein Metabolism, Motility, and RNA Metabolism, while Cell Wall genes showed the reverse pattern, with non-core genes more burdensome. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]

Condition-dependent fitness means that a disruption effect changes with the environment. The trade-off analysis identified 25,271 genes, or 17.8% of those examined, with fitness below -1 in some conditions and above 1 in others. These genes were 1.29 times more likely to be core than non-core genes (odds ratio 1.29, p=1.2e-44). [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] Strong condition-specific phenotypes were also more common among core genes: 77.3% of genes with such annotations were core versus 70.3% without them (OR=1.78, p=1.8e-97). [src: fitness_effects_conservation] The implication is not that every core gene is broadly required, but that conserved functions often participate in networks whose value depends on substrate, stress, physiology, or community context. See [[concepts/condition-dependent-gene-tradeoffs]], [[concepts/condition-specific-fitness]], and [[concepts/core-genome-burden-paradox]].

**Laboratory burden and evolutionary retention measure different time scales.** The joint matrix identified 28,017 genes that were costly in laboratory assays and conserved in the pangenome, compared with 5,526 genes that were costly and dispensable. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis, core_gene_tradeoffs] The first group is consistent with retention because laboratory conditions omit many environments in which a gene may provide value; the second is a candidate class for gene loss. Neither category directly measures natural selection, because RB-TnSeq fitness is measured under particular assay conditions whereas pangenome conservation integrates evolutionary histories across environments. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

The costly-and-dispensable class has a distinctive, though indirect, signature. Among 142,190 genes from 43 bacteria, these genes were 7.45 times more likely than costly-and-conserved genes to contain mobile-element keywords (OR=7.45, p=4.6e-71). [src: costly_dispensable_genes] They also had lower annotation rates, with SEED annotations in 50.8% versus 74.9% of costly-and-conserved genes and KEGG annotations in 20.0% versus 42.7%. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] This supports a model in which some burdensome accessory material is recently acquired, mobile, fragmentary, or vulnerable to loss, while cautioning that mobile-element keywords do not prove active transfer or future deletion. [[concepts/costly-dispensable-gene-loss]] and [[concepts/horizontal-gene-transfer-driven-innovation]] develop this interpretation.

**Fitness is strongly condition-dependent, and observation is uneven.** In *Acinetobacter baylyi* ADP1, deletion phenotypes were measured across 8 carbon sources; mean growth ratios ranged from 0.41 on urea to 1.36 on quinate, and 625 genes, or 31% of the complete matrix, had condition-specificity scores ≥ 1.0. [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes] In a separate comparison of insertion and complete-knockout assays, only 18 genes (0.9%) were called essential by both methods among 1,933 genes. At the RB-TnSeq threshold of 0.05, Cohen’s kappa was -0.081, while continuous inverted fitness achieved AUC values of 0.700 in rich medium and 0.725 in minimal medium. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] Continuous effects therefore preserve information that binary essential/non-essential calls can discard; this is the focus of [[concepts/essentiality-assay-discordance]] and [[concepts/gene-essentiality]].

Condition coverage also changes what can be discovered. The Fitness Browser collection contained 48 organisms, with MR-1 represented by 121 historical conditions. Organisms with deeper panels have more opportunities to reveal condition-specific phenotypes and can therefore be favored by prioritization systems. [src: functional_dark_matter] In *Desulfovibrio vulgaris* Hildenborough, field fitness alone, laboratory fitness alone, and their combination produced cross-validated AUC values of 0.517, 0.531, and 0.548, respectively; adding gene length raised AUC to 0.645. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness] This indicates that ecological labels alone are weak predictors of conservation and that genomic features and assay coverage must be modeled explicitly. [[concepts/fitness-condition-coverage-prioritization-bias]], [[concepts/fitness-importance-versus-ecological-context]], and [[concepts/genetic-perturbation-coverage-bias]] provide the methodological detail.

**Conserved functions can remain unknown and organized into modules.** Core status does not guarantee functional characterization. Among core clusters bridged to AlphaFold alignments, median MSA depth was 15,308 compared with 5,299 for auxiliary-plus-singleton clusters. Yet 415,603 distinct core clusters had MSA depth below 10; 68.9% were annotated as hypothetical, while only 137 had an EC annotation and 346 mapped to KEGG. [src: alphafold_msa_annotation] Conservation can therefore identify an important unknown without supplying a molecular mechanism.

Fitness covariance adds process-level structure. ICA, or independent component analysis, identified 1,116 modules across 32 organisms; module genes were 86.0% core versus an 81.5% baseline, with odds ratio 1.46 and p=1.6e-87. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis, module_conservation] However, module membership is threshold- and depth-dependent, and module-level cofitness does not establish precise gene function. In held-out benchmarking, ortholog transfer achieved 95.8% strict precision and 91.2% coverage, whereas Module-ICA achieved less than 1% strict KEGG KO precision and 23.3% coverage. [src: fitness_modules] Read [[concepts/fitness-module-detection-sensitivity]], [[concepts/fitness-matched-null-models]], and [[concepts/core-gene-annotation-paradox]] before treating a conserved module as a functional annotation.

## Tensions and Caveats

The central conflict is not whether conservation and fitness are related, but how far that relationship can be generalized. [[conflicts/conflict--conservation_fitness_synthesis--conservation_vs_fitness--core_gene_tradeoffs--57107100]] records disagreement among conservation metrics, while [[conflicts/conflict--conservation_fitness_synthesis--conservation_vs_fitness--core_gene_tradeoffs--c0b42874]] anchors the core-gene burden paradox. The 82%-versus-66% gradient and the 86.1%-versus-81.2% comparison come from different cohorts and phenotype definitions; they should not be averaged or treated as a single effect estimate. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis, conservation_vs_fitness, fitness_effects_conservation]

Core boundaries are also sampling-conditioned. Clades containing only 2 genomes can produce trivially high core fractions, while incomplete joins and excluded clades can alter which genes enter the comparison. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] Essentiality calls add technical uncertainty: missing transposon insertions may reflect short genes, low-complexity regions, or scaffold edges, and RB-TnSeq typically measures growth in a restricted condition set. [src: essential_genome, conservation_vs_fitness] These issues mean that apparent neutrality can reflect missing measurements, whereas apparent essentiality can reflect callability or assay context.

Finally, laboratory fitness is not natural selection. A costly conserved gene may be maintained by host, soil, biofilm, stress, competition, or other environments absent from the assay. Conversely, a costly dispensable gene may be mobile DNA that is useful only intermittently. [[conflicts/conflict--conservation_fitness_synthesis--conservation_vs_fitness--field_vs_lab_fitness--e8524ac8] documents this proxy problem, and [[conflicts/conflict--conservation_vs_fitness--core_gene_tradeoffs--fitness_effects_conservation--99d5abbe]] highlights the unresolved role of clade sampling and gene observability.
