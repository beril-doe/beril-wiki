# Microbial Metabolic Dependency

Microbial metabolic dependency describes when an organism relies on externally supplied compounds, neighboring organisms, or condition-specific environmental resources because its own biosynthetic or utilization capacity is incomplete, dispensable, or insufficient under particular conditions. Across the BERIL corpus, dependency is not a binary property inferred from a missing gene or complete pathway: it emerges from the interaction among genome content, pathway architecture, measured fitness, metabolite production, cultivation conditions, and community context. [src: essential_metabolome, pathway_capability_dependency, metabolic_capability_dependency]

## Literature Context

Published work supports a view of microbial dependency as an environmentally contingent and evolutionarily malleable property rather than a simple consequence of pathway absence. Experimental evolution has shown that bacteria can acquire metabolic dependencies, demonstrating that loss of biosynthetic capacity can emerge from selection and genomic change rather than merely reflecting an ancestral defect. [PMID 27814362](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27814362/) In gut communities, complex-carbohydrate degradation is distributed across organisms with complementary capabilities, while physiological measurements of the intestinal environment show that nutrient availability, host conditions, and spatial context vary substantially across the gut. [PMID 22572875](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22572875/) [PMID 37165188](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37165188/) Studies of ethanolamine utilization and specific interspecies metabolism further show that substrate use depends on regulatory, ecological, and community context, not solely on whether a nominal pathway can be annotated in a genome. [PMID 29463652](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29463652/) [PMID 31196984](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31196984/)

The corpus is consistent with this literature in separating metabolic capability from demonstrated independence. Its distinction between complete pathways, latent capability, active dependency, and incomplete-but-important pathways operationalizes a principle implicit in experimental evolution and gut-microbiome studies: a pathway may be present yet conditionally important, or incomplete yet functionally rescued by alternative routes or community resources. The corpus extends that literature by quantifying this distinction across hundreds to thousands of organism–pathway pairs and by showing that latent status changes with condition type and classification threshold. Its findings on community metabolomics are also consistent with published evidence that microbial products and host-associated metabolites vary with environmental state, diet, physiology, and disease-related perturbation. [PMID 37165188](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37165188/) [PMID 38963458](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38963458/) [PMID 39531110](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39531110/) However, the corpus's negative relationships between community pathway completeness and ambient amino-acid intensity extend these observations toward a testable community-level dependency model, while appropriately stopping short of claiming demonstrated metabolite transfer.

Several corpus results are more novel or sharpen unresolved issues in the literature. The FW300-N2E3 comparison provides unusually direct pathway-prediction and growth concordance, but its tryptophan production–utilization mismatch emphasizes that extracellular accumulation is not equivalent to self-use or confirmed cross-feeding. This interpretation is compatible with work showing that microbial metabolite production can be redirected by pH and host physiology, and that specific bacterial pathways can alter host-relevant compounds. [PMID 41730870](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41730870/) [PMID 31196984](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31196984/) The corpus further contributes a genome-scale link between latent metabolic capacity and pangenome openness, specialist-dominated carbon utilization, and recipient-specific respiratory constraints such as NADH load. These analyses extend the candidate literature by connecting dependency to genome dynamics and network architecture, but remain correlational or model-dependent; direct exchange experiments, matched perturbations, and condition-resolved measurements are needed to establish causality.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Pathway completeness indicates capability, not independence.**  
The corpus repeatedly separates predicted metabolic capability from demonstrated growth requirement. GapMind, a pathway-completeness method, classified amino-acid pathways as complete or likely complete, but these calls are computational evidence rather than direct biochemical or growth measurements. [src: essential_metabolome] In a seven-organism pilot, 17 of 18 amino-acid biosynthesis pathways were complete in all seven organisms; six organisms had 18/18 complete pathways, while *Desulfovibrio vulgaris* had 17/18. [src: essential_metabolome] The apparent *D. vulgaris* serine gap therefore suggests possible serine dependence, but could also reflect a divergent enzyme, a non-canonical route, or incomplete annotation. [src: essential_metabolome] See [[concepts/biosynthetic-prototrophy-and-auxotrophy]] and [[concepts/pathway-versus-reaction-evidence-resolution]].

The larger capability–dependency analyses make the distinction explicit. Among 161 organism–pathway pairs from seven model bacteria, 57 (35.4%) were Active Dependency, 66 (41.0%) were Latent Capability, 24 (14.9%) were Incomplete but Important, and 14 (8.7%) were Missing. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] In this classification, a latent capability is a complete pathway whose genes show little fitness cost under aggregate tested conditions; an Incomplete but Important pathway contains genes with fitness evidence despite an incomplete computational call. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] Across 1,695 complete pathway–organism pairs from 48 organisms, 267 (15.8%) were latent, 547 (32.3%) intermediate, and 881 (51.9%) active. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] Thus, pathway presence supports the possibility of synthesis or utilization, but does not establish nutrient independence or condition-independent dispensability.

**Dependency is strongly conditional on environment and assay.**  
All 66 Latent Capability pairs in the 161-pair analysis became fitness-important under at least one condition type, particularly nitrogen limitation, stress, or carbon limitation. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] This supports a conditional-dependency model: a biosynthetic route may be dispensable in a rich or permissive medium but important when an external nutrient is scarce or when an alternative pathway is stressed. The result remains partly threshold-sensitive because importance was assessed using median-based thresholds within condition subsets. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

The broader analysis also found that carbon-source utilization pathways were latent in 217 of 892 complete pairs (24.3%), compared with 48 of 735 amino-acid biosynthesis pairs (6.5%). [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] Complete amino-acid pathways were therefore less often fitness-neutral than complete carbon-utilization pathways in that dataset, although these classes summarize measured fitness rather than directly proving prototrophy or auxotrophy. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] The latent fraction varied from 4.7% to 21.1% across 16 threshold combinations, demonstrating that the magnitude of apparent dependency depends on classification choices. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] [[concepts/cross-condition-metabolic-comparability]] provides the most useful guide to comparing these assays.

Matched experimental evidence can support capability without resolving dependency. For *Pseudomonas* FW300-N2E3, GapMind predicted complete pathways for all 13 matched metabolites, and all 13 also showed growth in Fitness Browser experiments, yielding 13/13 agreement. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] However, the comparison covered only 13 metabolites and did not establish that completeness predicts growth under every medium or environmental condition. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] The distinction is central to [[concepts/computational-pathway-prediction-validation]].

**Community context is compatible with metabolite sharing, but does not prove exchange.**  
Community-scale metabolomics provides evidence consistent with Black Queen-like dynamics, in which organisms lose costly functions while relying on compounds supplied by other community members. Among 13 testable amino-acid pathways, 11 showed the predicted negative relationship between community pathway completeness and ambient amino-acid intensity; the sign test was p = 0.011. [src: discoveries, nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] Leucine showed r=-0.390, q=0.022, n=62, and arginine showed r=-0.297, q=0.049, n=80; these were the two associations surviving the reported false-discovery-rate correction. [src: discoveries, nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

The pattern was not universal. Tyrosine was an anti-Black-Queen outlier with r=+0.419, while isoleucine showed no significant association, with r=-0.057, q=0.823, n=18. [src: discoveries, nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] These correlations are therefore compatible with community provisioning but do not identify producers, recipients, transport, or direct metabolite exchange. The dataset was also dominated by one study: 125 of 131 samples (95%) came from a single study, and all 62 leucine samples came from it. [src: discoveries, nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] Read [[concepts/community-metabolic-interdependence]] next for the community-level interpretation and its boundaries.

**Production can create an ecological resource without implying self-use.**  
The FW300-N2E3 exometabolome contained 58 produced or increased metabolites: 27 emerged and 31 increased. Only 21 of 58 could be cross-referenced against another database. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] Tryptophan provides the strongest production–utilization contrast: it increased extracellularly, had a complete GapMind biosynthesis prediction, and was associated with 231 significant Fitness Browser genes, yet 0 out of 50 *Pseudomonas fluorescens* strains in BacDive utilized it as a carbon source. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] This supports overflow, signaling, or cross-feeding as hypotheses, rather than demonstrating any one mechanism or a confirmed transfer event.

The distinction matters because exometabolomics measures emergence or accumulation, whereas growth assays measure whether a compound supports growth. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] A producer may release a metabolite without catabolizing it, and a community member may use it even when the producer does not. The Web of Microbes snapshot records production-related actions but no organism consumption actions, so production-to-fitness links cannot establish reciprocal uptake. [src: webofmicrobes_explorer] See [[concepts/metabolic-overflow-and-ecological-secretion]].

**Dependency is shaped by specialization, genome dynamics, and network architecture.**  
Carbon-utilization capacity is concentrated rather than evenly distributed. Among 3,109 genomes carrying at least one of eight callable capacities, 675 carried exactly one, while only 18 carried at least three; the maximum was four. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] This pattern supports specialist-dominated utilization, with broader-capacity generalists uncommon in the surveyed set. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Within particular genera, however, individual capacities could be highly prevalent, including approximately 100% capacity for 3-hydroxybenzoic acid in Castellaniella and Hylemonella and 100% capacity for phthalic acid in Paenarthrobacter. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

Pathway variation also tracks genome fluidity. Variable metabolic pathways correlated with pangenome openness at rho=0.327 and p=7.2e-71, increasing to partial rho=0.530 and p=2.83e-203 after controlling for genome count. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] Latent-capability rate correlated with openness at Spearman rho=0.69, p=0.0004, n=22 clades. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] These associations support a relationship between metabolic repertoire variation and genome dynamics, but do not establish whether openness causes latent capacity, whether community context selects both, or whether sampling drives both observations. [[concepts/metabolic-capacity-specialization]] and [[concepts/pangenome-openness-determinants]] develop this point.

Finally, dependency may reflect network capacity rather than the identity of a substrate. In *Acinetobacter baylyi* ADP1, Complex I fitness effects initially appeared associated with aromatic growth, but the largest transferred defects occurred on acetate (-1.55) and succinate (-1.39), non-aromatic substrates that generate high NADH flux. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Complex I flux was predicted to be 0.55 on aromatic substrates versus 0.31 on comparison conditions, or 1.76× higher, while direct phenotypes showed quinate-specific defects for 10/13 operon subunits. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] This suggests that respiratory dependency can arise when reducing-equivalent production exceeds alternative capacity. [[concepts/respiratory-capacity-and-nadh-load]] explains why ortholog transfer and flux models must be interpreted against recipient-specific architecture.

## Tensions and Caveats

The principal tension is between matched concordance and context-dependent pathway use, documented in [[conflicts/conflict--essential_metabolome--fw300_metabolic_consistency--metabolic_capability_dependency]]. The FW300-N2E3 subset showed 100% agreement for 13 GapMind comparisons and 21 Fitness Browser comparisons, whereas broader analyses found 41.0% Latent Capability in the 161-pair set and 15.8% latent status among 1,695 complete pairs. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency, pathway_capability_dependency, metabolic_capability_dependency] These findings need not conflict: they concern different organisms, matched subsets, pathway definitions, conditions, and thresholds. [src: essential_metabolome, fw300_metabolic_consistency]

A second tension concerns whether computational models establish function. [[conflicts/conflict--acinetobacter_adp1_explorer--adp1_triple_essentiality--annotation_gap_discovery]] contrasts supportive model concordance with baseline FBA accuracy of 42.5% across 574 organism–carbon-source combinations. [src: annotation_gap_discovery] The annotation-gap pipeline resolved 96 of 201 gapfilled reaction–organism pairs (47.8%), leaving 105 unresolved. [src: annotation_gap_discovery] A pathway gap may therefore represent absent biology, a non-canonical route, annotation failure, or a reaction outside the pathway resource.

A third tension concerns respiratory compensation. [[conflicts/conflict--aromatic_catabolism_network--respiratory_chain_wiring]] contrasts the earlier interpretation that Complex I was dispensable on glucose and lactate with an ADP1-specific growth ratio of 1.44 on glucose, zero predicted NDH-2 flux, and no significant cross-species compensation pattern (p = 0.52). [src: aromatic_catabolism_network, respiratory_chain_wiring] Species-specific architecture, model objectives, and condition definitions could explain the difference; direct matched deletion and redox experiments are still needed.

Finally, community evidence is correlational and coverage-limited. Only approximately 2% of compounds in the NMDC metabolomics analysis had KEGG identifiers, three amino-acid pathways were untestable because relevant compounds were absent, and metabolite matching risked leucine/isoleucine collisions. [src: discoveries] Consequently, the corpus supports dependency as a layered, testable hypothesis—not as a conclusion from pathway absence, metabolite presence, or conservation alone.
