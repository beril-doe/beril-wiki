# Fitness-Conservation Genomics

Fitness-conservation genomics examines how experimentally measured effects of disrupting bacterial genes relate to their evolutionary retention across pangenomes. The corpus speaks directly to this question by combining random barcode transposon sequencing (RB-TnSeq), a pooled perturbation method that estimates mutant growth effects, with orthology, pangenome, functional-annotation, and structural-coverage analyses. Across these projects, conservation is informative but not decisive: it enriches for essential and repeatedly important genes, yet conserved genes can be conditionally burdensome, poorly characterized, or essential only in particular contexts. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis, fitness_effects_conservation, conservation_vs_fitness]

## Literature Context

Published comparative-genomics work establishes that bacterial genomes are shaped by both vertically inherited backbones and horizontally acquired, variably retained DNA. Reviews of horizontal gene transfer describe gene exchange as a source of adaptive innovation, while methodological work emphasizes the need to distinguish lateral acquisition from vertical inheritance using phylogenetic and comparative evidence. [PMID 34773098](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34773098/) [PMID 22407713](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22407713/) Prophage, transposon, plasmid, and other mobile-element sequences are consequently expected to be unevenly distributed across strains and to contribute disproportionately to accessory genomes. [PMID 12794192](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12794192/) Organism-focused studies likewise show that pangenome structure can reflect ecology, pathogenicity, host association, and recent gene exchange rather than a simple hierarchy of universally useful versus dispensable genes. [PMID 15035020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15035020/) [PMID 31845645](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31845645/) [PMID 31120895](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31120895/)

This corpus is consistent with that literature in finding that broadly conserved genes are enriched for experimentally important functions, while mobile-element-associated genes are preferentially accessory or narrowly distributed. Its costly-dispensable class, including genes annotated with transposase, integrase, phage, insertion-sequence, recombinase, and prophage terms, therefore extends established genomic expectations into a direct perturbation framework: it links pangenome distribution to measured laboratory burden rather than inferring fitness from sequence history alone. The result is also compatible with comparative studies showing that strain-level diversity and accessory content can be central to bacterial adaptation. [PMID 34773098](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34773098/) [PMID 12794192](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12794192/) [PMID 35838756](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35838756/) The corpus further extends pangenome literature by quantifying how conservation relates to RB-TnSeq fitness breadth, condition-dependent trade-offs, and coordinated fitness modules across dozens of bacteria, rather than treating “core” status as a proxy for invariant benefit.

Several findings sharpen limitations already implicit in comparative genomics. Genome catalogs and improved annotation strategies expand the detectable diversity of microbial lineages, but conservation estimates remain dependent on taxon sampling, orthology definitions, database composition, and phylogenomic framework. [PMID 24316578](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24316578/) [PMID 29277865](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29277865/) [PMID 40081368](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40081368/) The corpus makes this dependence experimentally consequential: conservation metrics can agree globally while producing different top-ranked genes, and apparent neutrality can reflect incomplete perturbation or condition coverage. Its observation that many core clusters remain hypothetical also sits in tension with the assumption that conserved sequence is necessarily well characterized. Finally, the corpus’s “core-genome burden paradox”—where conserved genes can improve growth when disrupted—goes beyond cataloging genome conservation and challenges any direct equivalence between evolutionary retention and benefit in a simplified laboratory environment. That interpretation is a hypothesis about ecological or historical selection, not a direct demonstration of natural selection.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Conservation carries a real but modest fitness signal.** Across 194,216 protein-coding genes from 43 bacteria, essential genes were 82% core, whereas genes neutral in every tested experiment were 66% core. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] A related analysis found that genes affecting 20 or more experiments were 79% core, compared with 66% for genes affecting no experiments; fitness breadth had only a weak association with core status, with Spearman rho=0.086 and p=8.1e-230. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] In a separate linkage of 33 organisms, essential genes were 86.1% core versus 81.2% for non-essential genes, with a median odds ratio of 1.56; 18 of 33 organisms showed significant enrichment after Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate correction. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] These results support conservation as a useful prioritization feature, not as a standalone proxy for biological importance. The distinction is central to [[concepts/fitness-importance-versus-ecological-context]] and [[concepts/laboratory-fitness-versus-natural-selection]].

The magnitude of the signal also depends on how conservation is measured. An initial eggNOG-based analysis assigned 30,721 of 30,756 dark-gene clusters, or 99.9%, to universal breadth. [src: functional_dark_matter] Replacing that score with a species-count variant gave Spearman ρ = 0.982, but top-50 and top-100 ranking overlaps were only 62% and 58%. [src: functional_dark_matter] A larger GTDB r214 analysis expanded coverage from 32,791 to 37,997 dark genes and resolved conservation across kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, species, and mobile categories. [src: functional_dark_matter] Thus, global rank agreement can coexist with instability at experimental decision thresholds; [[concepts/comparative-conservation-metric-calibration]] is the appropriate next step.

**Core genes are active, conditional, and sometimes costly.** The corpus overturns a simple picture in which conserved core genes are uniformly beneficial or indispensable in laboratory growth. Positive deletion fitness means that a mutant grows better after disruption, indicating a burden imposed by the intact gene under the assay conditions. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] By this definition, 24.4% of core genes were beneficial when deleted, compared with 19.9% of accessory genes. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] Core genes also had heavier fitness-effect tails in both negative and positive directions. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

Condition-dependent fitness means that a gene’s effect changes across environments. The corpus identified 25,271 true trade-off genes, representing 17.8% of genes examined; these genes had fitness below -1 in some conditions and above 1 in others. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] Trade-off genes were 1.29 times more likely to be core than non-core genes, with odds ratio 1.29 and p=1.2e-44. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] Strong condition-specific phenotypes were also more common among core genes: 77.3% of genes with such annotations were core versus 70.3% without them, with OR=1.78 and p=1.8e-97. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

The burden pattern is function-specific. Core genes were more burdensome in Protein Metabolism, Motility, and RNA Metabolism, with core-minus-non-core differences of +6.2, +7.8, and +12.9 percentage points, respectively. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] Cell Wall genes showed the reverse pattern, with non-core genes more burdensome and a difference of -14.1 percentage points. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] These findings define the [[concepts/core-genome-burden-paradox]]: evolutionary retention may reflect benefits in environments not represented by laboratory assays, while the same genes impose costs in simplified media.

**The conserved and dispensable quadrants imply different evolutionary hypotheses.** Combining laboratory burden with pangenome status produced 28,017 genes that were costly in the laboratory and conserved, alongside 5,526 genes that were costly and dispensable. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis, core_gene_tradeoffs] The first group is consistent with genes maintained by selection in environments broader than the laboratory; the second is a candidate class for ongoing gene loss. These are selection signatures, not direct measurements of natural selection. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

The costly-dispensable group has a strong mobile-DNA signature. Mobile-element keywords—including transposase, integrase, phage, insertion sequence, recombinase, and prophage—were 7.45 times more likely in costly-dispensable than costly-conserved genes, with OR=7.45 and p=4.6e-71. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] The SEED category “Phages, Prophages, Transposable elements, Plasmids” was enriched 11.7-fold, with FDR=1.3e-17. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] These genes also had lower annotation rates, 44.5% orphan status, a median ortholog breadth of 15 organisms, and a 24.2% singleton fraction; the corresponding costly-conserved group had annotation rates of 74.9%, orphan status of 13.1%, median breadth of 31 organisms, and no singletons. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] The evidence supports [[concepts/costly-dispensable-gene-loss]] and [[concepts/costly-dispensable-gene-loss]], while leaving open whether each individual gene is transient, functional, or degraded.

**Conservation does not equal characterization.** Core classification records broad retention within a sampled pangenome; it does not guarantee a known molecular function. Among gene clusters bridged to AlphaFold, core clusters had median multiple-sequence-alignment (MSA) depth of 15,308, compared with 5,299 for auxiliary-plus-singleton clusters. [src: alphafold_msa_annotation] Even so, 415,603 distinct core clusters had MSA depth below 10; their mean depth was 4.57 and median depth was 4.0. [src: alphafold_msa_annotation] Of these low-MSA core clusters, 68.9% were annotated as hypothetical, while only 3.8% of all bridged core genes were hypothetical. [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]

This creates a particularly valuable discovery class: genes retained broadly but poorly represented in characterized sequence space. The fitness data reinforce that conservation and annotation are different axes. Essential-auxiliary genes were 38.2% hypothetical, and essential-unmapped genes were 44.7% hypothetical. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] Conserved genes can therefore be experimentally active without having a settled function, as developed in [[concepts/core-gene-annotation-paradox]].

**The unit of conservation can be larger than a gene, but observation is uneven.** Independent component analysis (ICA), a method for identifying coordinated fitness-response patterns, identified 1,116 fitness modules across 32 organisms. These modules contained 86% core genes versus an 81.5% baseline, with odds ratio 1.46 and p=1.6e-87. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] Among 974 mapped modules with at least 3 genes, 577, or 59%, were more than 90% core, while 48, or 5%, were less than 50% core. [src: module_conservation] This supports coordinated conservation, but module membership does not establish molecular mechanism or ecological specialization.

Both fitness observation and core assignment are coverage-dependent. The Fitness Browser collection contained 48 organisms, with MR-1 having 121 historical conditions; organisms with deeper condition panels have more opportunities to reveal condition-specific effects. [src: functional_dark_matter] In *Desulfovibrio vulgaris* Hildenborough, field fitness alone predicted core status with cross-validated AUC 0.517, laboratory fitness alone with 0.531, and combined fitness with 0.548; adding gene length raised AUC to 0.645. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness] This result makes [[concepts/fitness-condition-coverage-prioritization-bias]] and [[concepts/genetic-perturbation-coverage-bias]] essential safeguards against treating unobserved phenotypes as neutrality.

## Tensions and Caveats

The central disagreement is whether conservation–essentiality enrichment is primarily biological or sampling-dependent. [[conflicts/conflict--conservation_vs_fitness--core_gene_tradeoffs--fitness_effects_conservation]] reports the positive evidence—86.1% versus 81.2% core, and 82% versus 66% in a broader cohort—but also notes that clades containing only 2 genomes can produce trivially high core fractions. Different organism sets and essentiality definitions prevent direct reconciliation of these estimates. [src: conservation_vs_fitness, conservation_fitness_synthesis, fitness_effects_conservation]

A second tension concerns perturbation coverage. In the ADP1 deletion collection, 93.3% of present dispensable genes were core compared with 76.5% of missing dispensable genes; missing genes were also shorter and less well annotated. [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes] This could indicate that conserved genes are more recoverable, but technical construction difficulty, fragment misclassification, incomplete transposon coverage, and annotation quality may produce the same pattern. [[conflicts/conflict--adp1_deletion_phenotypes--core_gene_tradeoffs--costly_dispensable_genes]] Novel singleton genes appeared near-neutral in tested assays, yet poor transposon coverage may explain part of that result. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

The laboratory-versus-natural-selection conflict is load-bearing. RB-TnSeq measures disruption effects under specified growth conditions, whereas pangenome conservation integrates retention across environments and evolutionary histories. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] Consequently, the 28,017 costly-conserved genes are consistent with natural selection maintaining context-dependent functions, but do not directly demonstrate purifying selection. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] [[conflicts/conflict--conservation_fitness_synthesis--conservation_vs_fitness--field_vs_lab_fitness]] details this scope difference.

Finally, AMR provides a genuine decoupling case. Core and accessory AMR genes both had mean fitness of −0.024, with Cohen’s d = 0.002 and p = 0.33, even though resistance mechanism strongly predicted pangenome distribution. [src: amr_fitness_cost] This does not negate the genome-wide association; it shows that conservation, baseline knockout cost, and conditional fitness can diverge by gene class. [[conflicts/conflict--amr_fitness_cost--conservation_fitness_synthesis--conservation_vs_fitness]]
