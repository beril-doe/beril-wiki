# Fitness Module Inference

Fitness module inference uses patterns of mutant growth across many experimental conditions to identify genes that behave as coordinated units. In this corpus, independent component analysis (ICA)—a statistical decomposition method for separating recurring signals in fitness profiles—is applied to RB-TnSeq (randomly barcoded transposon sequencing) data, pangenomes, annotations, and comparative-genomic evidence. The resulting modules help connect gene disruption phenotypes to biological processes, conserved genome architecture, and experimental priorities, while also exposing where cofitness reflects shared dispensability or assay design rather than direct regulation. [src: fitness_modules, conservation_fitness_synthesis, amr_cofitness_networks]

## Literature Context

Published work supports the general premise that biological responses are organized into interacting modules rather than isolated gene effects. Modeling studies have proposed that evolving biological networks can combine scale-free connectivity with hierarchical modularity and disassortative structure, providing a theoretical basis for recurring, partially insulated functional units. [PMID 17300817](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17300817/) Experimental evolution in *E. coli* likewise showed that adaptation to ethanol tolerance involved regulatory and metabolic rewiring, indicating that phenotypic change can be distributed across coordinated network responses rather than attributable to a single locus. [PMID 20531407](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20531407/) More recent work describes adaptation as proceeding through a small number of phenotypic modules, while orthologous-gene coevolution networks have been used to recover higher-order cellular and genomic structure. [PMID 42615478](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42615478/) [PMID 35507651](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35507651/)

The corpus is consistent with this literature in treating coordinated profiles as informative about process-level organization. Its recovery of 1,116 reproducible ICA modules across 32 organisms, strong within-module cofitness enrichment, and substantial genomic-adjacency enrichment provides a microbial, perturbation-based counterpart to network modularity and coevolution analyses. It extends prior work in three important ways: across-organism module families make conservation an explicit dimension of module analysis; pangenome comparisons separate core from accessory module structure; and held-out benchmarking directly tests whether modules support gene-level functional annotation. The low strict precision of Module-ICA for specific KEGG assignments, contrasted with stronger ortholog-transfer performance, qualifies the broader literature’s network-level interpretation: modules are persuasive evidence for shared phenotypic context, but not necessarily for shared molecular function. [[concepts/fitness-module-detection-sensitivity]]

Chemical-genetic interaction studies and machine-learning analyses of gene-fitness data further establish that perturbation matrices can reveal drug mechanisms, genetic architecture, and disease-associated functional networks. [PMID 41659671](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41659671/) [PMID 35044082](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35044082/) Other systems-level studies show that gene networks can embed persistent causal or regulatory changes within broader environmentally elicited responses, rather than mapping one-to-one onto direct regulation. [PMID 37607232](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37607232/) The corpus extends this principle by explicitly testing the limits of cofitness: extra-operon partners, broad support networks, and functional enrichments do not establish coregulation, and network breadth did not predict AMR fitness cost. Its combination of cross-organism reproducibility, conservation structure, and null-model cautions is therefore novel relative to the candidate literature. The unresolved issue is not whether modules exist, but which components reflect regulation, physical linkage, shared dispensability, ancestry, or assay design—precisely the gap addressed by [[concepts/cofitness-network-architecture]] and [[concepts/fitness-matched-null-models]].

## What the Corpus Shows

**Modules recover reproducible, cross-organism fitness structure.**  
Across 32 organisms, the fitness-module analysis identified 1,116 stable modules, with each organism represented by at least 100 experiments. [src: fitness_modules] Using an absolute ICA component-weight threshold of \(|weight| \geq 0.3\) and a maximum of 50 genes per module, 94.2% of modules had significantly elevated within-module cofitness, with mean absolute correlation of 0.34 inside modules versus 0.12 in the background—a 2.8× enrichment. [src: fitness_modules] Genomic adjacency was also enriched 22.7×, indicating that operon or neighborhood organization contributes to the signal, but does not reduce module inference to physical proximity alone. [src: fitness_modules]

The architecture is visible across resistance and non-resistance genes. Among 801 antimicrobial-resistance (AMR) genes with fitness data, 192 (24%) were assigned to ICA modules, and 208/209 (99%) AMR gene-module assignments belonged to cross-organism conserved families. [src: amr_cofitness_networks] AMR-containing modules had a median of 46 genes, compared with 27 in non-AMR modules, with Mann–Whitney \(p = 1.7\times10^{-8}\). [src: amr_cofitness_networks] Cross-organism alignment found 156 module families spanning at least 2 organisms, 28 spanning 5 or more, 7 spanning 10 or more, and one spanning 21 of 32 organisms. [src: fitness_modules]

These results support using modules as recurring units of condition-associated physiology. They do not establish that all members have the same molecular function: held-out benchmarking gave ortholog transfer 95.8% strict precision, 91.2% coverage, and 0.934 F1, whereas Module-ICA achieved less than 1% strict precision and 23.3% coverage for specific KEGG KO assignments. [src: fitness_modules] Thus, a module is stronger evidence for shared process-level context than for a gene-level annotation. [[concepts/fitness-module-detection-sensitivity]]

**Conservation is organized at the module level, but not uniformly.**  
The conservation synthesis analyzed 194,216 protein-coding genes across 43 bacteria, while the module-conservation analysis found that module genes were 86.0% core compared with 81.5% for all genes, corresponding to OR = 1.46 and \(p = 1.6\times10^{-87}\). [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis, module_conservation] Here, “core” means broadly retained across the relevant pangenome; “accessory” means present in a more restricted subset. Among 974 modules with at least 3 mapped genes, 577 (59%) were more than 90% core, 349 (36%) were mixed, and 48 (5%) were accessory; the median module was 93.4% core. [src: module_conservation]

This pattern places fitness architecture mainly within conserved genomic structure, while preserving a biologically important accessory component. Module-family breadth did not predict core fraction: Spearman \(\rho=-0.01\), \(p=0.914\). [src: module_conservation] Accessory modules nevertheless showed the strongest pangenome co-inheritance signal in a separate analysis. Across 195 ICA modules in 6 organisms, within-module co-occurrence had mean \(\phi=0.229\), compared with 0.177 for a prevalence-matched null, for \(\Delta\phi=+0.053\); 21/195 modules remained significant at FDR \(q<0.05\). [src: cofitness_coinheritance]

The module signal was stronger than the pairwise signal. Across 2,253,491 cofit pairs in 9 organisms, mean \(\Delta\phi\) was +0.011 across organisms, but the aggregate effect was +0.003 and the across-organism Wilcoxon test was not significant (\(W=9\), \(p=0.13\)). [src: cofitness_coinheritance] This suggests that coordinated multi-gene structure can capture inheritance or functional coupling that isolated pairwise cofitness does not reliably recover. It does not determine whether the coupling arises from regulation, physical linkage, selection, or another shared mechanism. [[concepts/module-level-coinheritance]]

**Fitness, conservation, and burden describe different dimensions of gene value.**  
The corpus does not equate conservation with universal laboratory benefit. Essential genes were 82% core, whereas genes always neutral in the available experiments were still 66% core. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] Moreover, 24.4% of core genes showed positive fitness when deleted, compared with 19.9% of accessory genes; positive deletion fitness means that the mutant grew better than the reference strain under the tested conditions. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

Core genes were 1.78× more likely than accessory genes to have strong condition-specific phenotypes and 1.29× more likely to represent trade-offs that were important in some conditions but burdensome in others. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] The joint conservation–fitness matrix classified 28,017 genes as costly in the laboratory yet conserved in the pangenome, and 5,526 as costly and dispensable. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] These classes support treating laboratory fitness and evolutionary retention as complementary measurements rather than interchangeable definitions of importance. [[concepts/core-genome-burden-paradox]]

The functional structure is also nonuniform. Motility and chemotaxis genes showed a +7.8 percentage-point core-burden excess, RNA metabolism a +12.9 percentage-point excess, and protein metabolism a +6.2 percentage-point excess, whereas non-core cell-wall genes were more burdensome than core cell-wall genes. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] These patterns are consistent with condition-dependent energetic and physiological trade-offs, but the experiments did not directly measure fitness in soil, biofilms, host tissue, or other natural environments. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] [[concepts/laboratory-fitness-versus-natural-selection]]

**Condition space determines what modules can be inferred.**  
Experimental depth is a measurement constraint, not merely a technical detail. Organisms with fewer than approximately 100 experiments produced weaker modules, although experiment count alone was insufficient: Caulo had 198 experiments but only 2.9× correlation enrichment. [src: fitness_modules] The analysis also imposed a 40% component cap to avoid FastICA convergence failures, which may cause modules to be missed in organisms with few experiments. [src: fitness_modules]

Threshold choices materially changed the result. The initial D’Agostino K-squared membership rule produced modules of 100–280 genes with 59% enrichment and 1–17× correlation enrichment; the absolute-weight rule produced smaller, more coherent modules with 94% enrichment and 2.8× correlation enrichment. [src: fitness_modules] Annotation choices similarly altered apparent biological yield: lowering the enrichment-overlap threshold from 3 to 2 and adding Pfam domains increased the module annotation rate from 8% to 80%, expanded annotated modules from 92 to 890, and unlocked 7.6× more function predictions. [src: fitness_modules] These settings affect what is detectable and annotatable, so module absence should not automatically be interpreted as biological absence. [[concepts/fitness-module-detection-sensitivity]] [[concepts/fitness-module-detection-sensitivity]]

Uneven condition coverage also biases downstream prioritization. In the functional-dark-matter analysis, 57,011 of 228,709 genes across 48 organisms were dark genes, and 7,787 had strong fitness effects, defined as absolute fitness of at least 2 in at least one condition. [src: functional_dark_matter] MR-1 had 121 historical conditions, and organisms with deeper coverage had more opportunities to reveal condition-specific phenotypes. [src: functional_dark_matter] The framework ranked 17,344 phenotype-bearing dark genes; 82/100 top candidates had hypotheses supported by at least 3 evidence types, but only 64% of the original fitness-active top 50 remained under conservation-dominant or drop-tractability settings. [src: functional_dark_matter] [[concepts/fitness-condition-coverage-prioritization-bias]]

**Cofitness is structured evidence, not direct regulatory proof.**  
Cofitness is similarity in genes’ fitness profiles across conditions; it indicates shared phenotypic response, not necessarily shared transcriptional control. Among 801 AMR genes, 769 (96%) had at least one extra-operon cofitness partner at \(|r|>0.3\), and mean support-network sizes were 233 genes at \(|r|>0.3\), 110 at \(|r|>0.4\), and 71 at \(|r|>0.5\). [src: amr_cofitness_networks] Yet support-network breadth did not predict AMR fitness cost: Spearman \(\rho=-0.006\), \(p=0.87\), across 769 genes. [src: amr_cofitness_networks]

Functional enrichments are therefore hypotheses requiring appropriate controls. Flagellum-dependent motility had mean OR 4.7 across 5 organisms, flagellum assembly had mean OR 5.3 across 5, and histidine and tryptophan biosynthesis each had mean OR 5.3 across 3 organisms. [src: amr_cofitness_networks] Shaken liquid culture and supplemented media can make motility or biosynthesis jointly dispensable, generating correlated profiles without direct co-regulation. [src: amr_cofitness_networks] The existing permutation matched conservation class but not mean fitness, motivating a fitness-matched null that also matches the \(-0.05\) to \(+0.05\) mean-fitness range. [src: amr_cofitness_networks] [[concepts/cofitness-network-architecture]] [[concepts/fitness-matched-null-models]]

## Tensions and Caveats

The main interpretive disagreement is whether conserved, essential, or module-associated genes should be expected to show consistent importance. The corpus reports strong core enrichment for essential and costly genes, but also finds persistent hypothetical genes with an essential fraction of 18.0% versus 13.4% for annotation-lag genes, and only 859 of 17,222 ortholog families universally essential while 4,799 were variably essential. [src: truly_dark_genes] Insertion-based ICA cannot observe essential genes because viable insertions are absent, so module conservation and essentiality are partly measurements of different gene populations. [src: module_conservation, conservation_fitness_synthesis] This disagreement is anchored in [[conflicts/conflict--acinetobacter_adp1_explorer--adp1_triple_essentiality--alphafold_msa_annotation]].

A second tension concerns whether cofitness and co-inheritance establish biological coupling or merely reflect shared stress, ancestry, prevalence, and laboratory context. Pairwise co-occurrence was weak overall, near genomes had higher \(\phi\) than medium-distance genomes (0.102 versus 0.067), and Korea had 95.2% NaN pairwise \(\phi\) values because many genes were present in all 72 genomes. [src: cofitness_coinheritance] Metal-pair analyses lacked negative controls, while multi-metal tolerance did not correlate with metal-associated isolation data (\(\rho\) approximately −0.02, \(p>0.8\)). [src: metal_cross_resistance] These limitations motivate [[conflicts/conflict--amr_cofitness_networks--amr_strain_variation--aromatic_catabolism_network]].

Finally, conservation and environmental structure do not resolve one another consistently. Latent complete pathways had mean conservation 0.869 versus 0.829 for active dependencies, while a separate 7-organism analysis found core completeness of 0.986 for active dependencies versus 0.975 for latent capabilities.  Such differences may reflect cohort, pathway, organism, or definition changes rather than a single contradiction. Laboratory fitness remains an imperfect proxy for natural selection, and condition-specific effects do not establish niche-specific fitness. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] This broader context is captured in [[conflicts/conflict--aromatic_catabolism_network--conservation_fitness_synthesis--discoveries]].
