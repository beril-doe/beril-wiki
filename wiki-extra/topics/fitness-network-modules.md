# Fitness Network Modules

Fitness network modules are groups of genes whose disruption produces coordinated patterns across experimental conditions. In this corpus, they provide a systems-level view between individual gene effects and whole-genome conservation: modules connect resistance, metabolism, respiration, stress response, phage defense, and genes of unknown function into condition-dependent functional architectures. The corpus is especially useful because it combines Fitness Browser and RB-TnSeq measurements, independent component analysis (ICA), pangenomes, genomic neighborhoods, and pathway models—while repeatedly testing whether apparent coordination reflects biochemical coupling, shared dispensability, inheritance, or methodological structure. [src: fitness_modules, conservation_fitness_synthesis, cofitness_coinheritance]

## What the Corpus Shows

**Modules capture coordinated phenotypes, not automatically shared regulation.** Cofitness—the similarity of two genes’ fitness profiles across experimental conditions—identifies genes that respond similarly when perturbed. It therefore supports a shared phenotypic response, but does not by itself demonstrate that the genes share transcriptional regulation, a transcription factor, or a signaling pathway. Genes may cofit because they are both dispensable in one condition and important in another. [src: amr_cofitness_networks] ICA extends this pairwise idea by identifying coordinated multi-gene components, while density-based clustering and thresholding are used to distinguish coherent modules from weak background associations. [src: fitness_modules, discoveries]

Across 32 bacteria, robust ICA and DBSCAN produced 17–52 modules per organism, and 94.2% of modules had significantly elevated within-module cofitness. Within-module mean absolute correlation was 0.34 versus 0.12 in the background, while genomic adjacency was enriched 22.7-fold. [src: discoveries, fitness_modules] The adjacency result shows that modules often recover operon-scale organization, but the broader cofitness analyses show that the signal is not reducible to physical proximity. [src: fitness_modules, cofitness_coinheritance] Cross-organism alignment found 156 module families spanning at least 2 organisms, 28 spanning 5+ organisms, and one spanning 21 of 32 organisms. [src: discoveries] These conserved families make modules useful as comparative units, although their membership and interpretation remain condition- and organism-dependent. [src: module_conservation, essential_genome]

**Distributed genes can form one functional system.** The clearest example is the *Acinetobacter baylyi* ADP1 quinate-support network, where genes involved in aromatic catabolism, respiration, iron acquisition, PQQ cofactor biosynthesis, and regulation jointly support growth on quinate. The 51-gene network assigned 44 genes to four functional subsystems: aromatic-pathway genes, Complex I genes, iron-acquisition genes, and PQQ-biosynthesis genes. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] These loci occupy distinct chromosomal regions: the Complex I operon, the pca/qui pathway, PQQ biosynthesis, and iron-acquisition genes are separated rather than assembled as one cross-category neighborhood. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

The biochemical interpretation is specific rather than merely correlational. Quinate catabolism produces metabolites entering the TCA cycle; quinate dehydrogenase requires PQQ, protocatechuate 3,4-dioxygenase requires non-heme Fe²⁺, and respiratory machinery must reoxidize NADH generated during oxidation. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Ten of 13 Complex I operon subunits independently produced quinate-specific growth defects, supporting functional coupling between aromatic catabolism and respiration despite genomic separation. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] This makes [[concepts/genomic-dispersal-functional-coupling]] a useful complement to [[concepts/cofitness-network-architecture]]: a module can represent a biochemical support network rather than a single locus.

The AMR analyses point to the same architecture at broader scale. Among 801 AMR genes with fitness data, 192 (24%) were assigned to ICA modules, and AMR-containing modules had a median of 46 genes compared with 27 in non-AMR modules. [src: amr_cofitness_networks] Of 209 AMR gene-module assignments, 208 (99%) belonged to cross-organism conserved families. [src: amr_cofitness_networks] The result is not restricted to resistance mechanisms: support neighborhoods were enriched for flagellar motility, chemotaxis, and amino-acid biosynthesis, suggesting that resistance phenotypes can be embedded in larger cellular systems. [src: amr_cofitness_networks]

**Multi-gene modules predict inheritance better than isolated links.** A pangenome is the collection of genes observed across genomes in a defined group; co-inheritance asks whether genes tend to occur together across those genomes. Pairwise cofitness was only weakly related to pangenome co-occurrence, whereas ICA modules showed a stronger signal. Across 9 organisms, the mean pairwise cofitness effect was delta phi +0.011, with 7 of 9 organisms positive and 8 of 9 significant at p<0.05; however, the aggregate delta was +0.003 and the across-organism Wilcoxon test was not significant, with p=0.13. [src: cofitness_coinheritance] This variation argues against treating one pairwise statistic as a universal measure of evolutionary coupling.

At module level, 195 ICA modules across 6 organisms had mean within-module phi=0.229 versus a prevalence-matched null mean of 0.177, giving delta=+0.053. [src: cofitness_coinheritance] A total of 51/195 modules were significant at p<0.05, and 21/195 remained significant at q<0.05 after false discovery rate (FDR) correction. [src: cofitness_coinheritance] The stronger module signal supports [[concepts/module-level-coinheritance]]: coordinated sets can capture inheritance structure that pairwise edges miss, particularly when individual genes are nearly universal or when phylogenetic structure dominates.

**Conservation supports modules, but does not define their biological importance.** Module genes were 86.0% core compared with 81.5% for all genes, indicating enrichment in the conserved portion of the sampled pangenomes. [src: module_conservation] This is consistent with a broader, weaker relationship between fitness importance and conservation: one integration found essential genes to be 86.1% core versus 81.2% for non-essential genes, with a median odds ratio of 1.56. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] Another synthesis reported 82% core among essential genes versus 66% among always-neutral genes, showing that the magnitude of the association depends on organism set and category definition. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

The correct interpretation is therefore enrichment, not equivalence. Core status, essentiality, laboratory burden, and module membership measure different properties. Core genes can show both stronger beneficial and deleterious deletion effects, and module genes are not necessarily uniformly essential or beneficial. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] Essential genes were absent from the module-conservation comparison because essential genes lack usable transposon-insertion fitness data, making module analyses conditional on assay callability. [src: module_conservation]

**Modules open a route into functional dark matter and defense systems.** Functional dark matter—genes with limited or no experimentally established function—can be prioritized through module membership, neighborhoods, conservation, and measured phenotypes. The corpus identifies dark genes in ICA modules and uses nearby annotated genes as guilt-by-association evidence, but explicitly treats these assignments as hypotheses rather than direct functional validation. [src: functional_dark_matter] Ortholog transfer is substantially more reliable for gene-level functional prediction than module-based KO assignment: Module-ICA and cofitness voting had less than 1% strict KEGG KO precision, whereas ortholog transfer achieved 95.8% precision and 91.2% coverage. [src: discoveries]

This distinction matters scientifically. A module may robustly identify a process—such as stress adaptation, transport, respiration, or defense—without identifying the precise molecular activity of every member. [src: functional_dark_matter, fitness_modules] The phage-defense analysis adds a complementary architecture in which coordinated anti-phage systems, accessory acquisition, and prophage-associated arms-race signals can be studied as distributed defense arsenals rather than isolated genes. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] Similarly, metal-fitness work finds broad cross-metal coordination, but requires stress-specific controls before interpreting every shared response as a dedicated resistance mechanism. [src: metal_cross_resistance, counter_ion_effects]

## Tensions and Caveats

The central interpretive conflict is whether fitness-network signals represent biological coordination or measurement structure. [[conflicts/conflict--amr_cofitness_networks--amr_strain_variation--aromatic_catabolism_network--d0b6ec8b]] Cofitness can reflect shared dispensability, common stress responses, ancestry, or condition selection rather than direct regulation. The AMR support-network analysis used conservation-matched nulls, but those controls do not resolve shared dispensability versus co-regulation. [src: amr_cofitness_networks] Metal analyses found positive cross-metal correlations, yet no negative controls were included to distinguish universal stress responses from metal-specific biology. [src: metal_cross_resistance]

Inheritance signals are also constrained by prevalence and phylogeny. Most Fitness Browser genes mapped to core clusters above 95% prevalence, where phi approaches zero because there is little presence/absence variation. [src: cofitness_coinheritance] Near genomes had higher mean phi than medium-distance genomes, with mean values of 0.102 versus 0.067, while most organisms lacked a far-distance stratum. [src: cofitness_coinheritance] Thus, module-level co-inheritance is informative, but raw pairwise associations should be interpreted alongside prevalence, phylogenetic distance, and the amount of computable data.

Conservation does not settle essentiality or natural fitness. [[conflicts/conflict--acinetobacter_adp1_explorer--adp1_triple_essentiality--alphafold_msa_annotation--cc872022]] Continuous fitness contains information about knockout lethality, but thresholded RB-TnSeq essentiality can disagree with complete-knockout calls.  Laboratory deletion effects also need not reflect long-term selection across environments, and conserved genes can remain burdensome under tested laboratory conditions. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] These limitations are especially important when modules are used to infer ecological function.

Finally, pathway models can underrepresent module biology. In the ADP1 aromatic network, flux-balance analysis predicted 0% essentiality for the Complex I-linked genes despite 1.76× higher Complex I flux on aromatic substrates and growth defects for 10/13 Complex I subunits. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] [[conflicts/conflict--aromatic_catabolism_network--respiratory_chain_wiring]] The result cautions that predicted capability, flux, growth dependence, and observed fitness are distinct endpoints. Model scope, annotation gaps, alternative routes, and condition choice can all change the apparent module.
