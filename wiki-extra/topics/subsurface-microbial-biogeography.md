# Subsurface Microbial Biogeography

Subsurface microbial biogeography concerns how microbial communities, functional potential, and metabolic activity vary across depth, hydrogeological position, groundwater flow paths, and attached versus planktonic habitats. This corpus speaks to the topic at several scales: a 3×3 well grid at the SSO site, paired groundwater–sediment samples, and comparative genomes from deep clay, shallow clay, and soil cohorts. Together, the projects support a view in which subsurface geography is structured and biologically meaningful, while showing that inferred metabolism remains sensitive to sampling compartment, phylogenetic composition, marker validity, and missing geochemical measurements. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology, bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory, clay_confined_subsurface]

## Literature Context

Published work generally portrays subsurface microbial biogeography as the outcome of interacting physical transport, geological heterogeneity, environmental selection, and dispersal limitation rather than simple geographic distance alone. In deep-subsurface aquifers, geological activity can structure microbiomes through advection, linking community composition to movement along groundwater flow paths. [PMID 35696589](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35696589/) Studies of petroleum reservoirs likewise identify environmental selection and biogeography as joint controls on subsurface microbiomes, while work on the deep biosphere describes a geological-process-mediated microbial dispersal loop. [PMID 36786580](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36786580/) These findings are consistent with the corpus observation that hydrogeological zone explained more sediment-community variance than well identity, and with its detection of significant distance–decay across a six-meter well grid. The strong persistence of well-specific groundwater structure across two dates further extends this literature by showing short-term spatial retention at the scale of a managed sampling grid, although it does not by itself demonstrate advective causation.

The literature also indicates that subsurface and other extreme environments can combine deterministic habitat filtering with local stochasticity and historical contingency. Globally separated acidic cave biofilms can exhibit convergent community assembly, whereas seamount communities show contributions from both stochastic and deterministic processes. [PMID 36602326](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36602326/) Dispersal rates across bacterial and archaeal lineages are also highly variable, providing a mechanistic basis for why physically connected environments may nevertheless retain distinct communities. [PMID 37512802](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37512802/) [PMID 34282284](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34282284/) The corpus is therefore consistent with a structured-biogeography model, but it adds a comparatively fine-grained distinction between groundwater and attached sediment: paired samples from the same wells remained compositionally different, with groundwater enriched in taxa interpreted as oxidative or plume-associated and sediment enriched in anaerobic taxa. That habitat partitioning is a useful extension because it cautions against treating groundwater as a direct proxy for the rock- or sediment-attached microbiome.

The corpus’s genomic results place subsurface specialization in a more qualified position than a broad adaptation narrative. Its larger deep-clay *Bacillota_B* genomes and 547 enriched orthologous groups are compatible with published evidence that geological and environmental conditions can shape subsurface distributions and genomic composition, including genomic and environmental controls on *Castellaniella* biogeography. [PMID 38671539](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38671539/) However, the within-lineage analysis found that sulfate-reduction-associated signals, rather than the entire Wood–Ljungdahl and hydrogenase toolkit, provided the strongest support for specialization. This refinement is not directly tested by the cited biogeographic studies and is potentially novel at the level of this corpus. Conversely, its proposed oxidative-to-fermentative redox sequence remains more tentative than the established community patterns: taxonomic functional inference and absent geochemical measurements cannot establish the hypothesized plume or redox zones. The corpus thus extends literature emphasizing geological control by resolving meter-scale and compartment-specific structure, while remaining in tension with any interpretation that equates community geography alone with measured metabolic geography.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Depth and hydrogeological position organize community composition.** In the SSO sediment dataset, hydrogeological zone explained 27.5% of community variance, with F = 4.05 and p = 0.0001, whereas well identity explained 19.2% and was not significant, with F = 0.80 and p = 0.979. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Samples from the same well but different depths had median Bray–Curtis dissimilarity of 0.977, while samples from the same depth zone in different wells had median dissimilarity of 0.835. Bray–Curtis dissimilarity measures compositional difference based on taxon abundances. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The analyzed depth classes were VZ with 33 samples, VSZ with 25, SZ1 with 54, and SZ2 with 45. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

These patterns indicate that vertical hydrogeological position can be more informative than well label for explaining sediment community structure. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Ten of 12 dominant phyla had significant depth associations at p < 0.05: Chloroflexi, Patescibacteria, Myxococcota, and Spirochaetota were shallow-enriched, while Firmicutes, WPS-2, Bacteroidota, and Proteobacteria were deep-enriched. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The positive depth association for Firmicutes was Spearman ρ = +0.76, linking the SSO community pattern to the deeper representation of Bacillota-related lineages discussed in [[concepts/subsurface-bacillota-specialization]]. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

**Spatial structure occurs at meter scale and persists over short intervals.** Across 9 wells arranged in a 3×3 grid spanning approximately 6 m, the SSO sediment dataset contained 23,458 amplicon sequence variants (ASVs), which are sequence-resolved 16S rRNA features, and 37 sediment core samples aggregated per well. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Community similarity declined significantly with geographic distance: a Mantel test, which correlates ecological and geographic distance matrices, gave Spearman ρ = 0.323, p = 0.029, with 9,999 permutations. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The U3–M6–L7 pairs formed the strongest similarity corridor, with Bray–Curtis dissimilarities of 0.558, 0.615, and 0.646, respectively, and negative distance residuals for all three pairs. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The groundwater data show that this spatial organization is not simply a transient artifact of one sampling date. Across 5 wells sampled on September 9 and September 18, 2024, well identity explained 49.9% of community variance, with p = 0.001, whereas sampling date explained 0.8%, with p = 0.998. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The median Bray–Curtis dissimilarity was 0.917 between spatial samples and 0.351 between temporal samples, and the two date-specific distance matrices retained the same well-to-well structure, with Mantel Spearman ρ = 0.867 and p = 0.001. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] This supports short-term persistence of spatial community structure, as detailed in [[concepts/spatial-structure-versus-short-term-temporal-stability]], but it does not establish seasonal or long-term stability. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

**Groundwater and sediment are distinct subsurface habitats.** Paired groundwater and sediment samples collected at the same wells had median Bray–Curtis dissimilarity of 0.424, with within-well values ranging from 0.364–0.450 across 5 wells. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Groundwater was enriched in taxa associated in the report with plume or oxidative processes, including *Rhodanobacter* at 3.62% versus 1.23% in sediment, *Gallionella* at 0.14% versus 0.01%, and *Sideroxydans* at 0.06% versus 0.01%. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Sediment was enriched in *Anaeromyxobacter* at 1.24% versus 0.03% in groundwater, *Arcobacter* at 0.54% versus 0.00%, and *Ca. Methanoperedens* at 0.42% versus 0.00%. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

Thus, groundwater cannot be treated as a simple sample of the attached sediment microbiota. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The contrast is consistent with groundwater enrichment in denitrifiers and iron oxidizers and sediment enrichment in anaerobic taxa, but these assignments were inferred from taxonomy rather than measured directly in the sampled populations. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The distinction between attached and planktonic habitats is developed in [[concepts/subsurface-hydrogeological-zonation]] and is important when interpreting redox gradients or comparing genome collections from porewater and rock-associated sources.

**Community geography suggests, but does not prove, redox geography.** The SSO analysis used taxon-to-trait inference to identify possible process distributions. Functional inference covered 22 classes at 78% coverage and 65 annotated genera at 21% coverage. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Denitrification ranged from 1.9–7.7% and peaked at M5 at 7.7%, iron oxidation peaked at U3 at 2.8%, iron reduction peaked at U1 at 2.3%, sulfur oxidation and methanotrophy both peaked at M4 at 1.9%, and fermentation peaked at L9 at 5.3%. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

The proposed spatial sequence is oxidative activity near U3, denitrification near M5, and fermentation near L9 along an O₂ → NO₃⁻ → Fe(III) → SO₄²⁻ → fermentation progression. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] M5 is hypothesized to be a plume mixing zone, while M6 is hypothesized to be an anaerobic dead zone. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] These are prioritization hypotheses rather than demonstrated geochemical zones: direct measurements of oxygen, nitrate, iron, sulfate, carbon, and related variables were unavailable in the analyzed SSO dataset. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] The inferential chain and its limitations are summarized in [[concepts/microbiome-inferred-redox-gradients]] and [[concepts/subsurface-hydrogeological-zonation]].

**Genomic specialization is real at cohort level but not uniformly ecological.** Deep-clay Bacillota_B were compared with soil-baseline Bacillota_B using accessory gene content, genome size, and functional markers. The analysis identified 547 significantly enriched eggNOG orthologous groups (OGs), where OGs are clusters of evolutionarily related genes, at q<0.05. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] Deep-clay genomes had mean genome sizes of 4,110,038 bp versus 3,046,124 bp in soil-baseline genomes, with Cohen’s d=+1.39 and Mann–Whitney p=0.025. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] Mean eggNOG OG counts were 2,630 versus 2,106, with Cohen’s d=+1.30 and p=0.022. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

However, the clay-confined analysis found that the combined Wood–Ljungdahl, group 1 [NiFe]-hydrogenase, and dissimilatory sulfate-reduction toolkit was not uniformly independent of lineage. Within Bacillota_B, Wood–Ljungdahl and [NiFe]-hydrogenase signals were not significant, whereas sulfate reduction occurred in 5/5 deep genomes versus 4/19 baseline genomes, with OR = ∞, p = 0.003, and p_BH = 0.044. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] The strongest interpretation is therefore cohort-level specialization with a particularly robust sulfate-reduction component, not proof that every enriched function is a general adaptation to subsurface life. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] The broader issue is developed in [[concepts/subsurface-bacillota-specialization]].

**Functional geography depends on valid markers and sampled compartments.** The deep-clay cohort showed a strong sulfate-reduction-associated signal relative to a rock-attached null: 5/9 genomes were positive versus 0.018 expected among 9, with binomial p = 4.0×10⁻¹². [src: clay_confined_subsurface] In contrast, the original iron-reduction comparison used K07811, K17324, and K17323, which corresponded to TMAO reductase, glycerol ABC ATP-binding, and glycerol ABC permease rather than canonical iron-reduction markers. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] A corrected detector combined PFAM PF02085, PFAM PF22678, and a CXXCH heme-binding motif threshold of ≥4. PFAM is a protein-family profile resource, while CXXCH is a sequence motif associated with c-type heme binding. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

Under that corrected detector, positivity was 55.6% in anchor_deep, 40.0% in anchor_shallow, and 40.9% in soil_baseline; all pairwise Fisher tests were non-significant, with p values of 0.46, 0.49, and 1.0. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] This removes support for the original shallow-enrichment narrative. Marker validation, rather than inherited annotation labels, must precede ecological claims; see [[concepts/functional-marker-validation]] and [[concepts/multi-heme-cytochrome-detection]].

## Tensions and Caveats

The central conflict is whether broad deep-clay Bacillota_B differences represent recurrent subsurface specialization or primarily lineage-clumped sampling and metric choice. [[conflicts/conflict--bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory--clay_confined_subsurface--enigma_sso_asv_ecology]] The 547 enriched OGs and larger genomes support broad cohort differentiation, but only sulfate reduction remained significant after within-phylum control, and the deep cohort contained 9 genomes before quality filtering. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory, clay_confined_subsurface] The SSO evidence cannot independently resolve this genomic question because its functional assignments were based on 16S taxonomy, with genus-level annotation covering only 21% of reads. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology]

A second conflict concerns the retained respiratory label: one source calls it “sulfite reduction,” while the clay report calls it dissimilatory “sulfate reduction.” Both report 5/9 positives and the same null scale, but the underlying marker definitions have not been shown to be identical. [[conflicts/conflict--bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory--clay_confined_subsurface]] [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory, clay_confined_subsurface]

The SSO spatial model is also limited by sampling design. Sediment was collected during February–March 2023, whereas groundwater was sampled in September 2024, creating an 18-month offset. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Groundwater ASV data covered only 5 of 9 wells and excluded inferred hotspot wells M5 and U3. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Although 221 geochemistry sample tubes were registered in CORAL, the relevant measurements had not been loaded into the analyzed dataset. [src: enigma_sso_asv_ecology] Consequently, the observed spatial and habitat patterns are stronger evidence than the proposed plume path or redox ladder.
