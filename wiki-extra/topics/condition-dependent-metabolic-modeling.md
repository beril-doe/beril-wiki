# Condition-Dependent Metabolic Modeling

Condition-dependent metabolic modeling asks what an organism can do, what it must do, and which genes become important under a specified medium, substrate, electron-donor load, or ecological context. The BERIL corpus speaks directly to this problem because it compares flux-balance analysis (FBA), pathway-completeness prediction, mutant fitness, knockout lethality, metabolite production, and genome annotations across contrasting conditions. Its central lesson is that metabolic capability is not equivalent to active flux or essentiality: each inference depends on the condition, assay endpoint, organismal network, and quality of the evidence used to construct the model. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer, adp1_triple_essentiality, essential_metabolome, fw300_metabolic_consistency]

## Literature Context

Published work increasingly treats metabolism as a condition-dependent, constrained system rather than as a fixed list of genome-encoded capabilities. Early computational studies developed systematic condition-dependent annotation of metabolic genes and showed that gene function can be assigned differently depending on the environmental state being modeled ([PMID 17895423](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17895423/)). Genome-scale modeling in yeast likewise connected condition-specific regulation to pathway activity, while whole-cell modeling showed that compartment-specific proteome constraints can drive distinct metabolic strategies ([PMID 23631471](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23631471/); [PMID 35145105](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35145105/)). More recent approaches have coupled constraint-based models to machine learning for predicting in vivo protein abundances and have extended condition-aware modeling to microbial communities using metatranscriptomic guidance ([PMID 37563787](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37563787/); [PMID 36814842](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36814842/)). Protocol work on TRIMER similarly formalized condition-dependent prediction of metabolite yields, reinforcing that predicted outputs must be tied to specified substrates, media, and objectives ([PMID 35243375](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35243375/)).

The corpus is strongly consistent with this literature, but supplies unusually direct cross-endpoint evidence for the consequences of conditioning. Its ADP1 results show that essentiality, FBA flux classes, and mutant fitness change substantially between rich, minimal, and carbon-source-specific conditions, while the FW300-N2E3 comparison demonstrates agreement between pathway-completeness calls and matched growth phenotypes only for a defined subset. This supports the modeling principle that capability, flux, growth support, and dependency are distinct predictions. It also extends prior efforts to reconcile metabolic reconstructions with high-throughput essentiality data, which identified reconstruction and interpretation issues as central to explaining model–experiment discrepancies ([PMID 30973869](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30973869/)). In particular, the corpus makes the endpoint distinction concrete: FBA can predict increased respiratory demand while assigning zero essentiality, and pathway completeness can coexist with failure to use a metabolite as a carbon source.

The corpus further extends the literature by quantifying how inferred network structure affects validation: most ADP1 growth-phenotype predictions required at least one gapfilled reaction, and false negatives were associated with greater gap counts. That result sharpens the general warning from condition-dependent annotation and reconstruction studies that model coverage is not equivalent to experimentally established function ([PMID 17895423](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17895423/); [PMID 30973869](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30973869/)). Its Complex I analysis also proposes a more specific mechanistic refinement: respiratory dependence may track NADH-generation load and available alternative capacity, not simply whether a substrate is aromatic. This is compatible with work showing that metabolic constraints shape antibiotic-resistance evolution ([PMID 28265005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28265005/)), but the corpus tests the principle through condition-matched fitness and flux evidence in a non-resistance metabolic setting. The resulting tension is therefore not with condition-dependent modeling itself, but with any interpretation that treats conserved pathway presence, model agreement, or a single-condition essentiality call as universal evidence of active or indispensable metabolism.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Condition changes the biological question being modeled.** A metabolic model does not predict a single condition-free property of a gene. In ADP1, 499 genes were essential on minimal media compared with 346 on LB, and FBA flux classes changed for 177 of 866 genes between rich and minimal media. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] Across eight carbon sources, 333 of 478 genes showed condition-specific growth defects, whereas only 10 showed defects across all eight conditions; mean pairwise defect correlation was 0.38, ranging from -0.03 to 1.0. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] These results make the medium part of the prediction target: a model tested on one nutrient regime should not automatically be treated as validated in another. [[concepts/metabolic-model-gapfilling]] and [[concepts/essentiality-prediction-task-definition]] develop this distinction.

The distinction also applies to continuous phenotypes. Continuous mutant fitness preserves ranking information that can be lost when outcomes are reduced to essential versus dispensable. In the ADP1 analysis, inverted fitness predicted knockout essentiality with AUC = 0.700 in rich media and AUC = 0.725 in minimal media, while an essentiality-fraction predictor produced AUC = 0.344 and AUC = 0.403, respectively. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] Here, AUC means Area Under the ROC Curve, a threshold-independent measure of classification performance. The appropriate model evaluation therefore depends on whether the intended output is lethality, growth impairment, or quantitative condition-specific fitness. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality]

**Pathway capacity is evidence of potential, not proof of dependency.** GapMind, a pathway-completeness method, evaluates whether annotated genes support the steps of a metabolic pathway. In a seven-organism pilot, 17 of 18 amino-acid pathways were complete in all seven organisms, and six organisms had all 18 pathways complete; *Desulfovibrio vulgaris* had 17/18. [src: essential_metabolome] The missing serine pathway suggests possible serine auxotrophy, meaning dependence on externally supplied serine, but does not establish it because divergent enzymes, non-canonical routes, or incomplete annotation could create an apparent gap. [src: essential_metabolome]

The same limitation appears at larger cohort scale. In the quality-filtered deep-clay cultured cohort, mean amino-acid pathway completeness was 15.50/18 versus 17.14/18 in the baseline, with p = 0.009 and Cohen’s d = −0.84; the result contradicted the expectation that the cultured deep cohort was unusually self-sufficient. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] Yet this does not exclude highly self-sufficient uncultured lineages, because the cohort contains only cultured representatives and the score summarizes 18 pathways. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] [[concepts/biosynthetic-prototrophy-and-auxotrophy]] and [[concepts/biosynthetic-self-sufficiency-and-cultivation]] show why pathway calls should be treated as hypotheses about capability rather than direct measurements of growth physiology.

**Independent phenotypes can support predictions, but only within matched endpoints and conditions.** In the FW300-N2E3 analysis, GapMind predicted complete pathways for 13 matched metabolites, and all 13 also showed growth in Fitness Browser experiments, giving 13/13 agreement. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] This provides organism-level support for pathway-completeness predictions in that matched subset, but it does not demonstrate that the predictions work under every medium or identify which individual pathway steps generated the growth phenotype. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]

The corpus repeatedly separates production, utilization, and dependency. Web of Microbes records metabolite production or changes, whereas growth assays test whether a substrate supports growth; production can reflect overflow metabolism, biosynthetic byproduct release, or secretion. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] Tryptophan illustrates the distinction: it was increased in Web of Microbes, had a complete GapMind pathway, and was associated with growth involving 231 significant Fitness Browser genes, yet 0 of 50 *Pseudomonas fluorescens* strains in BacDive used it as a carbon source. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] Thus, a model may correctly identify biosynthetic capacity without establishing extracellular utilization or physiological requirement.

**Flux demand and gene essentiality can diverge when alternative routes exist.** FBA is a constraint-based method that predicts steady-state flux distributions, generally under a growth-optimization objective. In ADP1 quinate catabolism, it predicted 1.76× higher Complex I flux on aromatic substrates than on the comparison condition, with fluxes of 0.55 versus 0.31, but predicted 0% essentiality for Complex I. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Experimentally, 10/13 Complex I operon subunits produced quinate-specific growth defects. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] The model therefore captured increased respiratory demand while missing a complex-level bottleneck.

The likely biological issue is network capacity rather than pathway presence alone. Complex I, or NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreductase, reoxidizes reducing equivalents generated during substrate oxidation, while NDH-2 is a non-pumping alternative NADH dehydrogenase. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Transferred fitness data showed Complex I orthologs had mean fitness of -1.35 on aromatic conditions versus -0.77 on comparison conditions, with p < 0.0001, but the largest defects occurred on acetate (-1.55) and succinate (-1.39), both non-aromatic substrates that can generate high NADH flux. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] This supports the hypothesis that respiratory dependence tracks NADH load as well as substrate identity. [[concepts/respiratory-capacity-and-nadh-load]] explains why a condition-aware model needs capacity constraints and organism-specific respiratory architecture.

**Gapfilling expands model coverage while increasing evidential dependence.** Gapfilling adds inferred reactions or functions to enable a model to simulate growth when genomic evidence is incomplete. Across 14 ADP1 genomes, 1,330 unique metabolic reactions were assessed: 1,248, or 94%, were shared across all genomes, while 62 were variable and 20 were genome-unique. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] Nevertheless, gapfilling accounted for 7.7% of reactions on average, and 243 missing functions were cataloged. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer]

This inferred layer materially affects predictions: 105,376 of 121,519 growth phenotype predictions, or 87%, required at least one gapfilled reaction. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] False negatives had higher mean gap counts than correct predictions, indicating that extensive inference can be associated with unreliable phenotype calls. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] The issue is not that gapfilling is intrinsically invalid; rather, its confidence should be carried into condition-specific validation. [[concepts/metabolic-model-gapfilling]] collects examples from annotation, pathway, fitness, and environmental datasets.

## Tensions and Caveats

The corpus contains a direct disagreement over whether model agreement and pathway conservation demonstrate active, condition-specific metabolism: [[conflicts/conflict--acinetobacter_adp1_explorer--adp1_triple_essentiality--annotation_gap_discovery]]. FBA and GapMind show substantial agreement with selected phenotypes, including 73.8% FBA/TnSeq concordance in an 866-gene overlap and 13/13 GapMind–Fitness Browser agreement in FW300-N2E3. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer, fw300_metabolic_consistency] However, FBA class was not associated with growth-defect status among TnSeq-dispensable genes, and the broader FW300 comparison found only 21/58 metabolites testable against another database. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality, fw300_metabolic_consistency] The reconciliation is endpoint-specific: capability, production, utilization, flux, and dependency are different claims.

A second tension concerns whether essentiality follows conserved genomic content or condition-specific network function: [[conflicts/conflict--acinetobacter_adp1_explorer--adp1_triple_essentiality--alphafold_msa_annotation]]. In ADP1, essential genes were 86.1% core versus 81.2% of non-essential genes, supporting a conservation signal. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] Yet condition-specific defects, respiratory compensation, and pathway gaps show why core status cannot substitute for a medium-specific mechanistic test. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality, aromatic_catabolism_network, essential_metabolome]

Cross-species transfer adds another load-bearing limitation. Ortholog-transferred fitness can reveal a conserved direction of pressure, but recipient organisms may differ in respiratory-chain composition, bypasses, and capacity. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] The transferred Complex I signal is therefore weaker evidence for a universal aromatic-substrate rule than direct matched-condition measurements in one organism. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Finally, GapMind pathway calls in the seven-organism pilot are computational and based on a restricted, incompletely mapped sample; only 7 organisms were mapped successfully from an underlying collection of 45. [src: essential_metabolome]
