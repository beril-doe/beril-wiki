# Environmental Genomics Confounding

Environmental genomics confounding occurs when an apparent relationship between microbial genomes and environmental context is partly—or primarily—produced by sampling composition, study batch, metadata quality, taxonomic representation, laboratory processing, or analytical choices. This corpus speaks directly to the problem because it compares environmental embeddings, pangenomes, metagenomic read fractions, resistance genes, functional traits, and geographic patterns across heterogeneous collections. Its central message is not that environmental signals are absent, but that their biological interpretation depends on whether environment has been separated from the processes that determine what was sampled, how it was measured, and how it was represented.

## Literature Context

Published microbiome research has long established that apparent biological signatures can be produced or distorted by the way samples are collected, processed, classified, and analyzed. Quince et al. describe shotgun metagenomics as a pipeline in which sampling design, DNA extraction, sequencing, taxonomic profiling, and statistical analysis each affect the final result, making technical comparability a prerequisite for ecological interpretation. [PMID 28898207](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28898207/) Salter et al. demonstrated that reagent and laboratory contamination can substantially alter sequence-based microbiome profiles, particularly when contaminant biomass is large relative to the biological signal. [PMID 25387460](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25387460/) Eisenhofer et al. likewise emphasize that low-biomass studies require explicit contamination controls and careful interpretation because negative or weak biological findings may reflect detection limits and laboratory background. [PMID 30497919](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30497919/) The corpus is therefore consistent with the literature when it treats classifier databases, missing protocols, pooled samples, metadata completeness, and sampling composition as part of the measurement system rather than as secondary nuisances.

Clinical microbiome studies further show that environmental or disease associations are often entangled with correlated exposures. Forslund et al. found that metformin treatment contributed substantially to gut-microbiome differences attributed to type 2 diabetes. [PMID 26633628](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26633628/) Yap et al. reported that dietary preferences mediated part of the association between autism and the gut microbiome. [PMID 34767757](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34767757/) Reviews and multi-omics studies of NAFLD and cardiometabolic disease similarly stress the need to separate microbial signatures from metabolic state, treatment, diet, and other host variables. [PMID 32152478](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32152478/) [PMID 35177860](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35177860/) This context supports the corpus's distinction between pooled association and portable environmental prediction: its held-out-study failure for eukaryotic read fraction, weak whole-genome environment correlations, and sensitivity to metadata and taxonomic composition extend these concerns from human cohorts to heterogeneous environmental-genomics collections. The corpus also extends prior methodological warnings by quantifying how environmental labels, coordinates, database scope, and study composition jointly constrain inference across thousands of genomes and metagenomic runs.

A notable result is that the corpus does not reduce confounding to an absence of biology. Its strong matrix differences in eukaryotic detection, environmental contrasts in AMR burden, and conditional agreement between laboratory fitness and environmental distributions are compatible with literature showing that biologically meaningful signals can coexist with treatment, diet, disease, and technical effects. They are also more cautious than many observational interpretations because cross-study validation and within-lineage controls are treated as decisive tests of portability. In this respect, the corpus's most distinctive contribution is a systematic demonstration that geographic structure, environmental association, and functional enrichment need not imply genome-wide adaptation or causal selection. Its emphasis on database representational limits and study-held-out validation is especially important for environmental genomics, where the candidate literature is dominated by host-associated microbiomes and low-biomass contamination problems rather than broad cross-biome genomic comparisons.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Environmental signal is often detectable, but portability is the decisive test.** Across the corpus, environmental variables frequently associate with genomic or metagenomic measurements. In a collection of 2,759 NMDC ReadbasedAnalysis runs from 9 studies, an environment-only model achieved random cross-validation R²=0.35 for eukaryotic read fraction, compared with R²=0.24 for `study_id` alone. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] However, when complete studies were held out using GroupKFold—a validation design that tests transfer to unseen studies—the environment model achieved R²=−0.30 and detection AUC=0.56, approximately chance. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] Adding sequencing metadata did not improve the held-out result, which indicates that a strong pooled association need not be a portable environmental rule. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]

The same distinction appears in genomic comparisons. AlphaEarth embeddings showed a geographic distance gradient, with environmental samples increasing from mean cosine distance 0.27 below 100 km to 0.90 above 10,000 km, while human-associated samples increased from 0.37 to 0.75 across the same broad scale. [src: env_embedding_explorer] Yet the ecotype analysis found a median partial correlation of only 0.0025 between environmental similarity and gene-content similarity, compared with 0.0143 for phylogeny; phylogeny dominated in 60.5% of species, while environment dominated in 39.5%. [src: ecotype_analysis] These results suggest that spatial structure can be real without being equivalent to genome-wide ecological adaptation.

**Sampling composition is a primary confounder.** The AlphaEarth subset contained 83,287 genomes, or 28.4% of 293,059 genomes in the KBase pangenome database, and 38% of the embedded genomes were human-associated. [src: env_embedding_explorer] Human clinical, human-gut, and human-other genomes numbered 16,390, 13,466, and 1,669, respectively, whereas soil, marine, and freshwater genomes numbered 6,073, 5,850, and 5,840. [src: env_embedding_explorer] Consequently, a pooled “environmental” comparison may also be a comparison of clinical versus environmental sampling campaigns, taxonomic composition, geography, and metadata practices.

Clinical sampling bias is demonstrable but does not explain every weak ecological result. Among 224 species retained in an AlphaEarth reanalysis, 106 species (47%) were majority human-associated, 47 (21%) majority environmental, and 71 (32%) Mixed/Other. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] Environmental species had median partial correlation 0.051, whereas human-associated species had median 0.084; a one-sided Mann–Whitney test for Environmental > Human-associated gave U=1536 and p=0.83. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] The fraction of environmental genomes per species also showed no relationship with partial-correlation strength, with rho=-0.085 and p=0.25. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis] Thus, clinical overrepresentation is a real limitation, but removing or quantifying it did not reveal a substantially stronger whole-genome environment signal in this analysis. [src: ecotype_env_reanalysis]

**Metadata harmonization changes the measurement rather than merely cleaning it.** Environmental categories were often inferred from heterogeneous free-text fields. A keyword workflow mapped 5,774 unique `isolation_source` values into 12 broad categories, captured 71% of genomes with an isolation-source label, and left 17% in Other and 12.5% in Unknown. [src: env_embedding_explorer] Structured fields were less ambiguous but less complete: `env_broad_scale` covered 41.8% of genomes, `env_local_scale` 37.9%, and `env_medium` 37.8%. [src: env_embedding_explorer] These operational categories therefore encode both biological context and the availability and interpretation of metadata.

Coordinate data have a similar problem. Among 83,286 genomes with latitude and longitude, 50,109 (60.2%) were classified as Good, 30,469 (36.6%) as Suspicious cluster, and 2,708 (3.3%) as Low precision because they used integer degrees. [src: env_embedding_explorer] A shared coordinate may represent a genuine field site, an institutional address, or an approximate placeholder; it may also describe the collection location rather than the organism’s actual microenvironment. [src: env_embedding_explorer] This is especially consequential for host-associated bacteria, for which a geographic coordinate may not represent the host tissue, body site, or chemical exposure experienced by the organism. [src: ecotype_analysis]

Species-level labels introduce another resolution problem. In the AMR collection, 280,337 genomes had environmental metadata, 93.5% were classified per genome, and species-level majority-vote labels covered 95% of species, or 13,981 species. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] The environment–AMR association persisted across majority-vote thresholds of 50%, 60%, 75%, and 90%, with η² = 0.044–0.056. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] That robustness supports a broad association, but majority voting can still collapse genomes from distinct environments into one species label and cannot establish genome-level acquisition or selection.

**Measurement systems and reference databases are part of the confounding structure.** Taxonomic abundance is jointly determined by reads, classifier, reference database, taxonomic scope, and reporting behavior. In the NMDC analysis, GOTTCHA2 detected eukaryotic reads in 77% of 2,759 runs, with a median eukaryotic fraction of 2.7%, a mean of 13.3%, and 20% of runs exceeding 20%. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] By contrast, Kraken2 and Centrifuge produced approximately 0 domain-level Eukaryota signal because their deployed databases were prokaryote-restricted. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] A near-zero result from a database that cannot represent a taxon is not evidence that the taxon was absent; GOTTCHA2 values were therefore interpreted as relative or ordinal rather than calibrated absolute contamination. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]

The apparent matrix pattern was nevertheless strong: eukaryotic detection was 99.5% in aquatic freshwater samples, 55.7% in terrestrial soil, and 100% in plant-root samples; the matrix comparison gave H=77.8 and p=1.3×10⁻¹⁷. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] These findings may reflect biology, but the corpus requires classifier and database compatibility before treating them as directly comparable environmental measurements. This principle extends to functional and resistance inference: downstream conclusions inherit the representational coverage and annotation behavior of upstream databases. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]

**Pooled observations and missing protocols leave residual uncertainty.** Of 2,759 NMDC runs, 1,067 were pooled from multiple biosamples. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] The analysis correctly used `workflow_run_id` as the statistical unit rather than treating each constituent biosample as independent, avoiding pseudoreplication. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] However, each pooled run inherited environmental metadata from a representative biosample selected by `MIN(biosample_id)`, which can add label noise when pooled biosamples differ. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates]

The same analysis lacked populated metadata for DNA-extraction kit, size fractionation or filtration, host-depletion method, and library preparation. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] Eukaryotic fractions differed among matrices, but unmeasured laboratory processing could not be separated from environmental effects. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] Sequencing depth was negatively associated with eukaryotic fraction, with Spearman ρ=−0.29, p=5.2×10⁻⁷, and n=292, but those measurements came from a handful of non-soil studies while the dominant NEON soil study recorded no depth. [src: euk_in_prok_correlates] The depth association is therefore suggestive rather than a controlled protocol effect.

**Specific functions can retain environmental structure when whole-genome signals are weak.** The corpus does not support reducing all environmental inference to a single genome-wide correlation. In the AMR analysis, clinical-source species had a median of 5 AMR gene clusters versus 2 for soil, aquatic, and host-associated species; the environment effect was H=781.9, p=9.4×10⁻¹⁶⁷, and η²=0.056. [src: amr_environmental_resistome] Five of six major phyla showed significant within-phylum environment effects, while 20 of 141 testable families (14%) showed significant within-family effects after FDR correction. [src: amr_environmental_resistome]

Similarly, laboratory fitness sometimes predicted environmental distributions. Of 47 testable dark-gene fitness mappings, 29 were concordant, or 61.7%; independent NMDC analyses confirmed all 4 testable preregistered abiotic predictions. [src: functional_dark_matter] Yet 441 of 449 exploratory tests also reached FDR < 0.05, largely attributed to compositional coupling, and only 10 of 137 clusters tested for environmental-category enrichment were significant at FDR < 0.05. [src: functional_dark_matter] The defensible conclusion is conditional concordance, not universal laboratory-to-field causation.

## Tensions and Caveats

The principal disagreement is captured by [[conflicts/conflict--ecotype_env_reanalysis--env_embedding_explorer--harvard_forest_warming]]: broad gene-content analyses report weak or null environmental effects, while particular functions, lineages, compartments, and environmental variables show detectable structure. The corpus therefore supports testing functional subsets rather than treating a genome-wide null as evidence that environment is biologically irrelevant. [src: ecotype_analysis, plant_microbiome_ecotypes, soil_metal_functional_genomics]

A second tension is between environmental association and robust causal prediction, described in [[conflicts/conflict--env_embedding_explorer--euk_in_prok_correlates--gene_function_ecological_agora]]. Geographic embeddings, soil-metal models, and selected functional analyses show structure, but AlphaEarth covers only 28.4% of genomes, environmental labels are incomplete, and cross-study eukaryotic prediction fails under held-out validation. [src: env_embedding_explorer, soil_metal_functional_genomics, euk_in_prok_correlates]

The resistance literature adds a further caveat in [[conflicts/conflict--metal_cross_resistance--metal_fitness_atlas--metal_specificity]]. Environmental AMR contrasts can be large, but species-scale and groundwater validations are not uniformly positive, and the reported outcomes measure different phenomena. [src: amr_environmental_resistome, microbeatlas_metal_ecology, metal_resistance_global_biogeography] Likewise, geographic hotspots should not be treated as selection maps until study origin, biome composition, and sampling effort are controlled. The global metal-resistance analysis identified 11 hotspots and 3 coldspots, but explicitly required effort correction and study-origin checks. [src: metal_resistance_global_biogeography]

Finally, [[conflicts/conflict--berdl_data_atlas--cofitness_coinheritance--ecotype_env_reanalysis]] emphasizes that technically successful joins are not equivalent to validated biological evidence. Cross-collection associations can be useful for hypothesis generation, but uncertain provenance, incomplete metadata, taxonomic imbalance, and weak external validation limit causal interpretation.
