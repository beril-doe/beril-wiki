# Bacterial Genome Evolution

Bacterial genome evolution in this corpus is the study of how conserved cellular functions coexist with rapidly changing accessory DNA, and how inheritance, horizontal gene transfer (HGT), selection, ecology, and measurement shape the observed pangenome. The corpus speaks to this topic through cross-species functional comparisons, gene-level fitness and conservation analyses, ecological gene-content tests, and focused studies of subsurface specialization. Together, these projects support a structured view of genome evolution: bacterial genomes are not uniformly variable, but neither are core and accessory categories sufficient on their own to explain persistence, loss, or adaptation.

## Literature Context

Published work generally treats bacterial genome evolution as the interaction of a conserved backbone with a flexible accessory component whose distribution is shaped by recombination, mobile DNA, ecology, and selection. Plasmids are not merely passive repositories of accessory genes: their mobility depends on transfer capacity, host range, and ecological opportunity, providing a mechanistic basis for horizontal gene transfer (HGT) as a source of genomic innovation. [PMID 20805406](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20805406/) Pangenome ecology similarly frames gene-content variation as a population-level trait, in which demographic structure, environmental distributions, and lineage history jointly influence which genes are retained or exchanged. [PMID 32633912](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32633912/) Comparative studies of *Elizabethkingia anophelis*, *Shewanella algae*, *Clostridium tertium*, and symbiotic *Photobacterium* further show that substantial within-species diversity and genome plasticity can coexist with lineage-specific conserved functions. [PMID 39472795](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39472795/) [PMID 35143386](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35143386/) [PMID 40796304](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40796304/) [PMID 38112751](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38112751/)

The corpus is strongly consistent with this literature, but gives the general model quantitative and functional resolution. Its enrichment of novel genes in mobility, defense, and unknown-function categories, together with the concentration of mobile-element terms among costly but dispensable genes, supports the established view of a mobile accessory genome rather than a uniformly evolving chromosome. Its finding that resistance genes can be partitioned into core and accessory fractions also accords with culture-enriched human gut data showing that resistance repertoires contain both broadly shared and variably distributed components. [PMID 30953542](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30953542/) The corpus extends this literature by directly connecting pangenome membership to measured fitness: accessory genes may be burdensome under laboratory conditions yet retain condition-specific phenotypes, making persistence interpretable as an ecological trade-off rather than simple neutrality. Its module-level analysis likewise moves beyond gene-by-gene classification to test whether functional systems show structured conservation.

The corpus also refines, and in places complicates, ecological accounts of genome evolution. Its weak genome-wide environmental signal is compatible with pangenome ecology and with work emphasizing phylogenetic or biogeographical constraints on HGT, including the finding that transfer opportunities can be limited by lineage and geography. [PMID 32633912](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32633912/) [PMID 41404872](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41404872/) However, the corpus's subsystem-level prophage and soil-metal associations, alongside the contrast between genome-wide and locus-specific tests, make explicit that ecological adaptation may be invisible in whole-genome similarity. The deep-clay result—larger, more functionally equipped genomes rather than streamlining—extends comparative plasticity studies and is potentially in tension with any expectation that specialization should generally reduce genome size. Finally, the corpus's warnings about identifier mapping, assembly/completeness effects, and insertion-based essentiality add a methodological contribution: pangenome conclusions depend not only on biological processes, but also on reconstruction accuracy and the limits of the assays used to measure conservation and fitness. Tools such as PEPPAN address the technical problem of accurately defining core and pan-genomes, but do not by themselves resolve these biological and measurement-level ambiguities. [PMID 33055096](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33055096/)

## What the Corpus Shows

**A conserved cellular core coexists with a mobile, defense-rich accessory genome.**  
Across 32 species, 9 phyla, and 357,623 genes, novel or singleton genes were enriched in COG L, the category containing replication, recombination, and mobile-element functions, by +10.88%, with 100% consistency across species. They were also enriched in COG V, defense mechanisms, by +2.83%, and COG S, unknown function, by +1.64%. [src: cog_analysis] By contrast, core genes were depleted relative to novel or singleton genes in translation (COG J, -4.65%), nucleotide metabolism (COG F, -2.09%), coenzyme metabolism (COG H, -2.06%), amino-acid metabolism (COG E, -1.81%), and energy production (COG C, -1.75%). [src: cog_analysis]

This pattern motivates the [[concepts/two-speed-bacterial-genome]] model: a relatively conserved metabolic and housekeeping core operates alongside an accessory component enriched for mobility, defense, and poorly characterized functions. The COG analysis interprets HGT—the movement of genetic material between lineages—as a major innovation mechanism, but the data classify genes by novelty and function rather than directly reconstructing transfer events. [src: cog_analysis] Thus, mobile-element enrichment supports HGT as a hypothesis, not proof that every novel gene was transferred. [src: cog_analysis] The same distinction is important for [[concepts/horizontal-gene-transfer-driven-innovation]], where functional novelty is associated with mobile elements but requires gene-tree, synteny, or genomic-context evidence for transfer-resolved conclusions.

**Accessory DNA can be adaptive, burdensome, and evolutionarily unstable.**  
The strongest direct evidence for unstable accessory material comes from the costly+dispensable class: 5,526 genes among 142,190 genes from 43 bacteria were costly in laboratory fitness assays but not broadly conserved in the pangenome. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] These genes were 7.45 times more likely than costly+conserved genes to contain mobile-element keywords, including transposase, integrase, phage, insertion sequence, recombinase, or prophage terms (OR=7.45, p=4.6e-71). [src: costly_dispensable_genes] The SEED category “Phages, Prophages, Transposable elements, Plasmids” was enriched 11.7-fold in this group (FDR=1.3e-17). [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

The same genes had weaker evidence of deep conservation: 50.8% had SEED annotations versus 74.9% of costly+conserved genes, 20.0% had KEGG annotations versus 42.7%, and 44.5% were orphans lacking an ortholog group versus 13.1%. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] Their median ortholog breadth was 15 organisms versus 31, and their median length was 615 bp versus 765 bp. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] These features are consistent with recent acquisition, fragmented mobile DNA, or poorly characterized accessory functions, but short length and annotation absence do not establish degradation in any individual gene. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

The burden is not equivalent to uselessness. A condition-specific phenotype was recorded for 14.1% of costly+dispensable genes, compared with 2.7% of neutral+dispensable genes. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] This supports [[concepts/condition-specific-fitness]]: a gene can impose a cost in tested laboratory conditions yet persist if it provides episodic benefits in environments, hosts, or communities not represented by those assays. [src: costly_dispensable_genes] The corpus therefore distinguishes laboratory burden from long-term evolutionary loss rather than treating either as a direct proxy for the other.

**Genome evolution can involve expansion as well as streamlining.**  
The deep-clay Bacillota_B comparison challenges the expectation that ecological specialization necessarily produces small, streamlined genomes. Ten deep-clay genomes had a mean genome size of 4,110,038 bp versus 3,046,124 bp for 62 soil-baseline genomes, with Cohen’s d=+1.39 and p=0.025. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] CheckM-rescaled genome sizes were 4,323,230 bp versus 3,233,715 bp, with Cohen’s d=+1.37 and p=0.013. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] Mean eggNOG orthologous-group counts were 2,630 versus 2,106, with Cohen’s d=+1.30 and p=0.022; CheckM-rescaled counts were 2,771 versus 2,233, with Cohen’s d=+1.32 and p=0.009. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory]

The difference was not explained by an apparent completeness imbalance: mean CheckM completeness was 94.7% for anchors and 94.3% for baseline genomes, with p=0.93. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] Among 14,109 Firmicutes-level eggNOG groups, 547 were significantly enriched after false-discovery-rate correction, fold-difference filtering, and minimum-positive-count criteria. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] Enriched functions included anaerobic respiration, sporulation and revival, mineral attachment or exopolysaccharide production, anaerobic regulation, and osmoadaptation. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] The report interprets this as consistent with self-sufficiency and broader persistence capacity in deep clay, while noting that the cohort contains only 10 anchors and is phylogenetically clumped. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] See [[concepts/genome-expansion-versus-streamlining]] for the comparison and its lineage-confounding limits.

**Ecology often acts through subsets of the genome rather than whole-genome turnover.**  
Across 172 bacterial species, the median partial correlation between environmental similarity and gene-content similarity was 0.0025, compared with 0.0143 for phylogeny; partial correlation measures an association between two variables while accounting for another. [src: ecotype_analysis] Phylogeny dominated the gene-content signal in 60.5% of species, while environment dominated in 39.5%; significant positive environmental effects occurred in 12 species (7.0%), negative effects in 4 (2.3%), and no significant effect in 156 (90.7%). [src: ecotype_analysis]

These results support vertical inheritance—descent from ancestors—as the stronger general predictor of genome-wide gene-content similarity, while leaving open the possibility that selection and HGT act at specific loci or functional systems. [src: ecotype_analysis] This is the central distinction in [[concepts/genome-wide-versus-locus-specific-ecological-adaptation]]: a weak whole-genome environmental signal does not demonstrate ecological irrelevance, because signal can be diluted when only a small subset of genes responds.

The broader corpus contains subsystem-level examples consistent with that possibility. Prophage-module composition showed environmental effects after genome-size stratification, with environment F=30.04 versus family-level phylogeny F=6.17. [src: prophage_ecology] Soil-metal analysis reported 2,355 significant COG–metal associations and conditional db-RDA R² = 0.799. [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics] However, environmental embeddings covered only 28.4% of genomes, geographic metadata were often incomplete or imprecise, and the ecotype analysis did not directly test whether particular COG categories had stronger environmental associations. [src: ecotype_analysis] These results make locus-specific adaptation a well-supported direction for analysis, not a completed demonstration.

**Integration is necessary because no single genomic signal has one interpretation.**  
The corpus repeatedly joins pangenome membership to fitness, functional annotation, ecology, and phenotype. The ADP1 bridge mapped all 4,891 BERDL clusters to 4,081 unique ADP1 clusters and achieved a 100% gene-level match across 43,754 genes, while explicitly warning that gene-level completeness does not prove cluster identity. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] A broader Fitness Browser integration contained approximately 194,000 genes from 43 bacteria with RB-TnSeq, essentiality, condition-specific phenotypes, and pangenome mappings. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] RB-TnSeq is random-barcode transposon sequencing, an insertion-based method for estimating gene fitness effects.

At module scale, 1,116 fitness modules were 86.0% core versus 81.5% for all genes; among 974 modules with at least 3 mapped genes, 577 (59%) were >90% core, 349 (36%) mixed, and 48 (5%) <50% core. [src: module_conservation] These results support structured conservation, but they do not make module membership equivalent to evolutionary history or mechanism. The integration logic is therefore central to [[concepts/pangenome-integration]]: identifiers and provenance must be preserved, and a successful computational join must not be mistaken for biological validation.

## Tensions and Caveats

The most important tension is whether essentiality generally tracks conservation. [[conflicts/conflict--acinetobacter_adp1_explorer--adp1_triple_essentiality--alphafold_msa_annotation]] summarizes evidence that essential genes are 86.1% core versus 81.2% for non-essential genes, alongside results showing lineage-restricted or pathway-dependent essentiality. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer, conservation_vs_fitness] Only 859 of 17,222 ortholog families were universally essential, while 4,799 were variably essential and 11,564 were never essential. [src: discoveries] Insertion-based assays can miss essential genes because essential genes are not recoverable as viable insertions, so measured essentiality is an upper bound conditioned on library construction and growth conditions. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]

A second tension concerns whether gene-content associations establish biological coupling. [[conflicts/conflict--amr_cofitness_networks--amr_strain_variation--aromatic_catabolism_network]] notes that ICA modules had delta phi=+0.053 overall and +0.108 for accessory modules, but pairwise cofitness aggregation was delta +0.003 with Wilcoxon p=0.13. [src: cofitness_coinheritance] Near genomes also had higher mean phi than medium-distance genomes, and most organisms lacked a far-distance comparison. [src: cofitness_coinheritance] Shared stress, ancestry, and laboratory conditions can therefore produce signals resembling functional linkage.

Environmental interpretation is similarly conditional. [[conflicts/conflict--ecotype_env_reanalysis--env_embedding_explorer--harvard_forest_warming]] contrasts broad environmental nulls with subsystem-specific signals: the prior AlphaEarth analysis reported median environmental partial correlation 0.0025, whereas prophage and soil-metal analyses detected stronger system-level associations. [src: ecotype_analysis, prophage_ecology, soil_metal_functional_genomics] Genome size, phylogenetic clustering, uneven metadata, annotation quality, and environmental representation remain load-bearing limitations.

Finally, [[conflicts/conflict--phage_defense_arsenal--prophage_amr_comobilization--prophage_ecology]] cautions that marker and co-occurrence evidence is not mechanistic proof. Only 10.4% of AMR instances were within 10 genes of a strict prophage marker, and genome size remained dominant in the prophage analysis (F=212.99). [src: prophage_amr_comobilization, prophage_ecology] Broad accessory-genome patterns are therefore strongest as comparative evidence and hypothesis generators unless supported by transfer-resolved, experimentally validated mechanisms.
