# Bacterial Gene Fitness Conservation

Bacterial gene fitness conservation concerns how measured effects of disrupting a gene relate to its retention across genomes. The corpus shows a reproducible but modest connection: genes that are essential or strongly conditionally important are, on average, more likely to be pangenome-core, yet conservation is neither equivalent to essentiality nor evidence of uniform benefit. Instead, retention reflects fitness across environments and evolutionary histories that laboratory assays only partially sample, while experimental callability, pangenome boundaries, and annotation quality shape what can be observed. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis, fitness_effects_conservation, conservation_vs_fitness]

## Literature Context

Published work supports the view that gene importance is not a binary, context-free property. Bosch et al. used genome-wide expression tuning in *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* to show that graded changes in gene expression can expose diverse vulnerabilities, including dependencies that may be missed by a simple essential/non-essential classification. Their study therefore frames fitness as a quantitative and condition-sensitive phenotype rather than a fixed label attached to a gene. [PMID 34297925](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34297925/) Higuera-Llantén et al. likewise combined comparative genomics with functional analysis of natural-transformation-related genes in *Piscirickettsia salmonis*, illustrating how the distribution of a gene family across genomes must be interpreted alongside organism-specific biology and experimental validation. [PMID 41636501](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41636501/)

The corpus is consistent with both studies in treating conservation and perturbation as related but non-identical evidence. Its finding that essential genes are enriched in the pangenome core, while many always-neutral genes are also core, agrees with the literature’s emphasis on partial predictive value rather than equivalence between retention and indispensability. The corpus’s condition-specific phenotypes and trade-offs further extend the vulnerability framework of Bosch et al. from expression perturbation within one pathogen to cross-organism comparisons of deletion fitness. In particular, the observation that conserved genes can improve laboratory growth when disrupted is compatible with the possibility that their benefits are environmental or conditional, rather than universally visible in a simplified assay. [[concepts/core-genome-burden-paradox]] [[concepts/condition-dependent-gene-tradeoffs]]

The broader corpus also extends the comparative-genomic perspective represented by the *P. salmonis* study. It tests conservation–fitness relationships across dozens of bacteria, quantifies their modest effect sizes, and shows that coordinated fitness modules can be more conserved than individual genes. These cross-project results are novel relative to the two candidate papers in their scale and explicit calibration of core status, module membership, assay coverage, and annotation uncertainty. They also introduce a potential tension: the corpus finds broad conservation of some genes despite positive deletion fitness in the laboratory, whereas a gene-centered functional dissection can readily encourage interpretation of retained genes through their demonstrated cellular roles. The apparent tension is not a direct contradiction, because neither study establishes that laboratory fitness alone determines long-term genomic retention. Instead, the corpus makes explicit a generalization suggested by the literature: conservation is a useful prioritization signal, but its biological meaning depends on ecological context, lineage history, perturbation modality, and the boundaries used to define the pangenome. [[concepts/comparative-conservation-metric-calibration]] [[concepts/laboratory-fitness-versus-natural-selection]]

## What the Corpus Shows

**Conservation carries a real but weak fitness signal.** A pangenome-core gene is one retained across the sampled genomes of a species or clade; an auxiliary gene is more variably distributed. Across 194,216 protein-coding genes from 43 bacteria, essential genes were 82% core, whereas genes neutral in every tested experiment were 66% core. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] The same analysis found only a weak association between fitness breadth and core status, despite strong statistical support (Spearman rho=0.086, p=8.1e-230). [src: fitness_effects_conservation] A separate 33-organism integration estimated 86.1% core among essential genes versus 81.2% among non-essential genes, with a median odds ratio of 1.56; 18 of 33 organisms showed significant enrichment after false-discovery-rate correction. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] These estimates should be read as directionally consistent, not interchangeable: they use different cohorts, mappings, and essentiality definitions. The practical conclusion is that conservation can prioritize candidates, but cannot by itself identify which genes are biologically indispensable. [[concepts/comparative-conservation-metric-calibration]] [[concepts/gene-essentiality]]

**Core genes are active, conditional, and sometimes costly.** Laboratory fitness is commonly measured by comparing the growth of a mutant with that of a reference strain under specified conditions. A positive deletion-fitness value means that the deletion mutant grows better, implying a burden associated with retaining the intact gene in that assay. Across the synthesis, 24.4% of core genes showed positive fitness when deleted, compared with 19.9% of accessory genes. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] Core genes were also 1.78x more likely to have strong condition-specific phenotypes and 1.29x more likely to be trade-offs—genes with fitness below -1 in some conditions and above 1 in others. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis, core_gene_tradeoffs] The trade-off analysis identified 25,271 such genes, representing 17.8% of those examined, and found them 1.29 times more likely to be core than non-core genes (odds ratio 1.29, p=1.2e-44). [src: core_gene_tradeoffs]

This is not a uniform “core genes are costly” rule. Burden was higher for core genes in Protein Metabolism, Motility, and RNA Metabolism, with differences of +6.2, +7.8, and +12.9 percentage points, respectively, but the direction reversed for Cell Wall genes, where non-core genes were more burdensome by -14.1 percentage points. [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] The corpus therefore supports a condition- and function-dependent model: conservation may preserve genes whose advantages emerge in environments absent from the laboratory, while the same genes can reduce growth in a simplified medium. The joint matrix contained 28,017 genes that were costly in the laboratory and conserved, compared with 5,526 that were costly and dispensable. These categories are evidence for possible retention or loss under selection, not direct measurements of natural selection. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis, core_gene_tradeoffs] [[concepts/condition-dependent-gene-tradeoffs]] [[concepts/core-genome-burden-paradox]] [[concepts/laboratory-fitness-versus-natural-selection]]

**Fitness effects are organized into conserved functional units.** Cofitness is similarity between genes’ fitness profiles across conditions; it indicates shared phenotypic response, not necessarily shared transcriptional regulation. [src: amr_cofitness_networks] Independent component analysis (ICA), a decomposition method for extracting coordinated patterns from multivariate fitness data, identified 1,116 stable modules across 32 organisms. Module genes were 86% core versus an 81.5% genome-wide baseline, with odds ratio 1.46 and p=1.6e-87; 59% of modules were more than 90% core. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] A related module analysis found 577 of 974 mapped modules (59%) above 90% core, 349 (36%) between 50% and 90%, and 48 (5%) below 50%. [src: module_conservation]

These results suggest that conservation can operate at the level of coordinated processes rather than only individual genes. However, the enrichment is modest in absolute terms and is conditional on module construction. Using an absolute component-weight threshold of |weight| >= 0.3 and a maximum of 50 genes produced modules with 94% enrichment and 2.8x within-module cofitness enrichment; a different membership rule produced larger, less coherent modules. [src: fitness_modules] Genomic adjacency was enriched 22.7x, showing that operon organization contributes to the signal. [src: fitness_modules] Thus, conserved modules are useful hypotheses about process-level organization, not proof that every member has the same function or fitness importance. [[concepts/cofitness-network-architecture]] [[concepts/fitness-module-detection-sensitivity]]

**Conservation also separates genome functions, but not absolutely.** Across 32 species, 9 phyla, and 357,623 genes, novel or singleton genes were enriched in COG L (replication, recombination, and mobile elements) by +10.88%, and in COG V (defense) by +2.83%, with 100% consistency across species for both signals. They were also enriched in COG S (unknown function) by +1.64%, with 69% consistency. [src: cog_analysis] Core genes were relatively depleted in translation J by -4.65%, nucleotide metabolism F by -2.09%, coenzyme metabolism H by -2.06%, amino acid metabolism E by -1.81%, and energy production C by -1.75%. [src: cog_analysis] This supports a conserved metabolic and informational backbone alongside a more variable accessory genome enriched for defense, mobility, and poorly characterized functions.

Within-species ecotypes—groups distinguished by accessory gene content—also differed more strongly in adaptive categories than in housekeeping categories. Adaptive categories had mean effect size 0.0136 versus 0.0064 for housekeeping categories, a 2.13x ratio, with p = 2.53 x 10^-6; nonetheless, housekeeping categories were significant in 68.8% of tests. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] The largest mean effects were in COG S (0.0392) and COG L (0.0337), outside the predefined adaptive set. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] Conservation therefore marks broad functional constraint, but does not create a clean adaptive–housekeeping boundary. [[concepts/two-speed-bacterial-genome]] [[concepts/adaptive-versus-housekeeping-functional-differentiation]]

**Unknown genes expose the difference between retention and knowledge.** Core status usually brings greater sequence representation: core clusters bridged to AlphaFold had median multiple-sequence-alignment depth of 15,308, versus 5,299 for auxiliary-plus-singleton clusters. [src: alphafold_msa_annotation] Yet 415,603 distinct core clusters had MSA depth below 10; their mean depth was 4.57, median depth 4.0, and 68.9% were annotated as hypothetical proteins. [src: alphafold_msa_annotation] Conservation can therefore identify genes retained across genomes even when their molecular roles remain unknown.

Fitness data reinforce, rather than remove, this distinction. Essential genes are more often core, but always-neutral genes are still 66% core, and core genes can have strong positive, negative, or condition-specific effects. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis, fitness_effects_conservation] Conversely, apparent neutrality among singleton genes may reflect poor transposon coverage rather than biological inactivity. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] Conservation is thus a prioritization axis for unknown genes, not a substitute for functional validation. [[concepts/core-gene-annotation-paradox]] [[concepts/comparative-conservation-metric-calibration]]

## Tensions and Caveats

The central disagreement is whether conservation–essentiality estimates describe a general biological law or a scope-dependent association. The promoted conflict [[conflicts/conflict--acinetobacter_adp1_explorer--adp1_triple_essentiality--alphafold_msa_annotation]] highlights that core fractions, knockout phenotypes, model predictions, and structural annotation do not measure the same property. The 82%-versus-66% and 86.1%-versus-81.2% estimates support enrichment, but their different cohorts and definitions prevent direct reconciliation. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis, fitness_effects_conservation, conservation_vs_fitness]

The burden paradox is equally load-bearing: genes can be retained broadly while benefiting deletion mutants in laboratory conditions. [[conflicts/conflict--conservation_fitness_synthesis--conservation_vs_fitness--core_gene_tradeoffs]] Laboratory assays do not directly measure selection in soil, biofilms, host tissue, or other natural settings, and the 28,017 Costly + Conserved genes remain a selection hypothesis rather than proof of purifying selection. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis, core_gene_tradeoffs]

Measurement modality can reverse conclusions. In one comparison of 1,933 genes, only 18 (0.9%) were called essential by both RB-TnSeq and complete-knockout assays; 211 (10.9%) were knockout-essential but RB-TnSeq-dispensable, while 293 (15.2%) showed the reverse pattern. At the 0.05 threshold, Cohen’s kappa was -0.081, whereas continuous inverted fitness gave AUC values of 0.700 in rich medium and 0.725 in minimal medium. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] [[conflicts/conflict--adp1_triple_essentiality--core_gene_tradeoffs--fitness_effects_conservation]] Continuous fitness should therefore not be collapsed prematurely into binary essentiality.

Finally, both perturbation and conservation are coverage-dependent. Small clades can produce trivially high core fractions, and genes without usable transposon insertions may be falsely classified as essential because of sequence composition, short length, or scaffold position. [src: conservation_vs_fitness, essential_genome] Unequal condition panels also favor organisms with more opportunities to reveal conditional effects. [src: functional_dark_matter] These biases mean that absence of a measured fitness effect is weaker evidence than a directly replicated effect. [[concepts/pangenome-core-boundary-and-clade-size-bias]] [[concepts/transposon-callability-bias]] [[concepts/fitness-condition-coverage-prioritization-bias]]
