# Comparative Metabolic Dependencies

Comparative metabolic dependency asks when a genome-encoded pathway represents true nutritional independence, a condition-specific requirement, a latent capability, or a function supplied by other organisms. The BERIL corpus speaks directly to this question by aligning pathway predictions, mutant-fitness measurements, growth phenotypes, metabolic models, exometabolomics, pangenomes, and environmental surveys. Across these evidence types, the central result is not a single dependency map but an evidence hierarchy: genomic capacity is informative, yet dependency is conditional on organism, substrate, respiratory architecture, assay, and community context.

## What the Corpus Shows

**Pathway completeness is capability evidence, not dependency evidence.**  
The strongest comparative baseline comes from the [[concepts/biosynthetic-prototrophy-and-auxotrophy]] analyses. In a seven-organism pilot, 17 of 18 amino-acid biosynthesis pathways were complete in all organisms, and six organisms had 18/18 complete pathways. *Desulfovibrio vulgaris* was the exception, with a predicted serine-pathway gap and 17/18 complete pathways. [src: essential_metabolome] These results support broad amino-acid biosynthetic capacity in the sampled bacteria, but the serine result remains a hypothesis about possible auxotrophy because GapMind predictions can miss divergent enzymes, non-canonical pathways, or genes absent from annotations. [src: essential_metabolome]

Matched experimental evidence can strengthen a capability call. For *Pseudomonas* FW300-N2E3, all 13 metabolites with complete GapMind pathways also showed growth in Fitness Browser experiments, giving 13/13 agreement. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] However, the broader comparison shows why this should not be generalized into pathway independence. Among 1,695 complete pathway–organism pairs from 48 organisms, 267 (15.8%) were latent, 547 (32.3%) intermediate, and 881 (51.9%) active according to fitness-based criteria. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] A separate 161-pair analysis classified 57 (35.4%) as Active Dependency, 66 (41.0%) as Latent Capability, 24 (14.9%) as Incomplete but Important, and 14 (8.7%) as Missing. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] Thus, a complete pathway can be fitness-neutral under tested conditions, while an incomplete pathway can contain genes that matter for growth.

The distinction is especially clear across pathway classes. Carbon-source utilization pathways were latent in 217 of 892 cases (24.3%), compared with 48 of 735 amino-acid biosynthesis cases (6.5%). [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] The latent fraction varied from 4.7% to 21.1% across 16 threshold combinations, but the existence of a non-trivial latent class was retained. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] This supports [[concepts/cross-condition-metabolic-comparability]]: “can synthesize,” “can grow on,” and “requires the pathway” are related but distinct endpoints.

**Dependency is revealed by condition space and physiological load.**  
The [[concepts/condition-space-dimensionality]] evidence shows that dependency is not a single organismal property. In an *Acinetobacter baylyi* ADP1 deletion matrix covering 2,034 genes and 8 carbon sources, five principal components captured 82% of the variance; the first explained 36.7%, while the second explained 12.7% and separated urea-associated nitrogen metabolism from carbon metabolism. [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes] Pairwise condition correlations were moderate at best, with a median of 0.25 across 28 pairs, and 333 of 478 TnSeq-dispensable genes (70%) showed condition-specific defects. [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes, adp1_triple_essentiality]

The broader pathway analyses support the same structure: all 66 aggregate Latent Capability pairs in the 161-pair study became fitness-important under at least one condition type, particularly nitrogen limitation, stress, or carbon limitation. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] This result is partly threshold-sensitive because the condition-specific median threshold can induce reclassification, but it remains consistent with the idea that latent capability is often conditional rather than permanently dispensable. [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

Respiratory metabolism provides a mechanistic example. In ADP1, Complex I, the proton-pumping NADH dehydrogenase, was required on quinate but dispensable in some transferred-fitness comparisons on glucose and lactate. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network, respiratory_chain_wiring] The largest Complex I defects in the transferred dataset occurred on acetate (-1.55) and succinate (-1.39), both non-aromatic substrates that generate substantial NADH through the tricarboxylic-acid cycle. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Flux-balance analysis, or FBA, predicted 1.76× higher Complex I flux on aromatic substrates than on the comparison condition, with fluxes of 0.55 versus 0.31, but predicted 0% Complex I essentiality. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] The comparison indicates that substrate identity is an imperfect proxy for dependency; reducing-equivalent load, alternative respiratory routes, and network capacity may matter more.

**Comparative transfer requires recipient-specific network context.**  
Ortholog transfer can detect conserved pressure but cannot be treated as a universal dependency map. The transferred Fitness Browser dataset contained 12,241 entries covering 2,005 genes and 13 conditions, and Complex I orthologs had mean fitness of -1.35 on aromatic conditions versus -0.77 on comparison conditions. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network] Yet the strongest individual effects occurred on non-aromatic substrates, weakening a simple “aromatic pathway requires Complex I” interpretation. [src: aromatic_catabolism_network]

The ADP1-specific wiring analysis further complicates the compensation model. It reported a Complex I growth ratio of 0.37 on quinate, 1.44 on glucose, and 0.49 on acetate; ACIAD3522 had a growth ratio of 0.013 on acetate. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] NDH-2, an alternative non-pumping NADH dehydrogenase, was present but had no growth data, and FBA predicted zero NDH-2 flux under standard conditions. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] Across species, validated NDH-2 occurred in 5 of 14 organisms, and the predicted compensation pattern was not supported, with p = 0.52. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring] These results motivate [[concepts/cross-species-fitness-transferability]]: ortholog function depends on alternative routes, stoichiometric context, and recipient architecture.

**Dependency is shaped by genome dynamics and specialization.**  
Metabolic variation is not distributed uniformly across genomes. The ENIGMA Carbon Census found a mean of approximately 1.19 callable carbon capacities per genome, a median of 1, and a maximum of 4 among genomes carrying at least one capacity; 675 of 3,109 genomes carried exactly one of the eight capacities, whereas only 18 carried at least three. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] This supports a specialist-dominated view of carbon utilization, with capacity concentrated in particular lineages rather than broadly spread across the genome collection. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Within some genera, however, individual capacities were highly prevalent, including approximately 100% 3-hydroxybenzoic-acid capacity in Castellaniella and Hylemonella and 100% phthalic-acid capacity in Paenarthrobacter. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

Pangenome analyses connect this specialization to genome openness. Variable metabolic pathway counts correlated with openness at rho=0.327 and p=7.2e-71, increasing to partial rho=0.530 and p=2.83e-203 after controlling for genome count. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] At the clade level, latent-capability rate correlated with pangenome openness at Spearman rho=0.69, p=0.0004, n=22 clades. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] These associations are compatible with accessory pathway turnover, ecological sharing, or community dependence, but do not establish causality or metabolite exchange.

The cultivation boundary is important here. Quality-filtered deep-clay cultured genomes had mean amino-acid pathway completeness of 15.50/18 versus 17.14/18 in the soil baseline, with p=0.009 and Cohen’s d=-0.84. [src: clay_confined_subsurface] By contrast, deep-clay Bacillota_B genomes were larger than the soil baseline, with mean genome sizes of 4,110,038 bp versus 3,046,124 bp and mean orthologous-group counts of 2,630 versus 2,106. [src: bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory] Genome expansion therefore indicates one form of functional capacity, while the 18-pathway completeness metric measures another. This is the central issue in [[conflicts/conflict--bacillota_b_subsurface_accessory--clay_confined_subsurface--conservation_fitness_synthesis--912a2415]].

**Community evidence supports interdependence, but not direct exchange.**  
Community metabolomics provides evidence consistent with Black Queen-like dynamics, in which organisms lose costly functions while relying on community-produced metabolites. Across 220 NMDC samples and 80 GapMind pathways, 11 of 13 testable amino-acid pathways showed negative correlations between community pathway completeness and ambient amino-acid intensity, with sign-test p=0.011. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] Leucine had r=-0.390, q=0.022, n=62, and arginine had r=-0.297, q=0.049, n=80; tyrosine was an outlier with r=+0.419. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology] The pattern is pathway-specific and compatible with provisioning, but does not identify producers, recipients, uptake, or flux.

The FW300-N2E3 integration illustrates why production and utilization must remain separate. Of 58 exometabolites, 21 could be cross-referenced; 17/21 (81%) were fully concordant across matched databases, while BacDive utilization agreed for only 3/7 comparisons. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] Tryptophan increased extracellularly, had a complete biosynthesis prediction, and was associated with 231 significant Fitness Browser genes, yet 0 of 50 *Pseudomonas fluorescens* strains utilized it as a carbon source. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency] This supports possible overflow, signaling, or cross-feeding, not demonstrated transfer. Environmental occurrence is likewise insufficient: the ENIGMA atlas detected 83 of 86 implicated genera in 1,719 NMDC metagenomes, but did not measure compound degradation or uptake. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]

## Tensions and Caveats

The corpus contains genuine endpoint disagreements rather than one settled dependency definition. The conflict [[conflicts/conflict--essential_metabolome--fw300_metabolic_consistency--metabolic_capability_dependency--96fcb005]] contrasts 13/13 matched concordance with broader latent and intermediate pathway classes. The difference is plausibly explained by selected matched conditions versus heterogeneous organism–pathway comparisons, but it prevents treating either result as universal.

Model predictions also diverge from phenotypes. FBA showed 42.5% baseline accuracy across 574 organism–carbon-source combinations and produced 330 false positives, while another ADP1 analysis reported 73.8% FBA/TnSeq concordance for 866 genes. [src: annotation_gap_discovery, acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] The conflict [[conflicts/conflict--acinetobacter_adp1_explorer--adp1_triple_essentiality--annotation_gap_discovery--8f009ad9]] anchors this limitation: agreement depends on organism, model formulation, endpoint, and evidence resolution.

Conservation is also an unreliable substitute for dependency. In the broad analysis, latent pathways had mean conservation 0.869 versus 0.829 for active dependencies, with p=0.94 for active greater than latent; the smaller matched analysis found mean core completeness of 0.986 for Active Dependencies versus 0.975 for Latent Capabilities. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency, pathway_capability_dependency] These differing results motivate [[conflicts/conflict--conservation_fitness_synthesis--conservation_vs_fitness--core_gene_tradeoffs--57107100]] and caution against inferring physiological dependence from core status alone.

Finally, pathway-level evidence cannot uniquely identify reaction-level genes. Of 201 gapfilled reaction–organism pairs, only 96 (47.8%) received confidence-scored candidate genes, leaving 105 unresolved. [src: annotation_gap_discovery] GapMind completeness, SEED pathway membership, conservation, and fitness constrain hypotheses but do not replace step-level biochemical, genetic, or flux validation. [src: annotation_gap_discovery, metabolic_capability_dependency]
