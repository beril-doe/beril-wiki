# Markus de Raad

ORCID: [0000-0001-8263-9198](https://orcid.org/0000-0001-8263-9198)

## Profile

Markus de Raad’s project on lignin enrichment and ecological memory investigated how lignin, labile-carbon co-supplementation, and sequential passaging reshape bacterial and fungal communities using 16S V3–V4 and ITS2 amplicon data from 21 samples across seven groups. [[summaries/lignin_community_enrichment__REPORT]] Lignin acted as a strong bacterial selective filter: Shannon diversity declined from 6.46 to 3.16, observed OTUs from 1,594 to 163, and treatment explained 97.9% of bacterial community variance (R²=0.979, p=0.001), while Pseudomonas and Acinetobacter together exceeded 64% of reads after enrichment. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Labile carbon produced a distinct assemblage, increasing Acinetobacter to 41.7% and Aeromonas to 20.2% while reducing Pseudomonas to 23.0%. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]

A recurring focus is how environmental history and resource context determine microbial community structure rather than simply selecting for a fixed set of degraders. Round-1 history explained 58.9% of Round-2 bacterial variance (F=14.31, R²=0.589, p=0.002), compared with 32.7% for the current carbon source, and communities retained a reported memory index of approximately 0.50 despite identical subsequent conditions. [src: lignin_community_enrichment] The project also highlights domain-specific reproducibility: fungal diversity collapsed under enrichment, but fungal replicate distances reached 0.99–1.00 in several Round-2 groups, and the Round-2 fungal history effect was not statistically detectable (R²=0.142, p=0.090). [src: lignin_community_enrichment] Taken together, the corpus suggests that their research interests center on microbial succession, ecological memory, resource-dependent selection, and the contrasting stability and reproducibility of bacterial versus fungal responses to lignin-associated environments. [src: lignin_community_enrichment]
