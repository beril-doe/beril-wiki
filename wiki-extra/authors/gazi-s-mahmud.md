# Gazi S. Mahmud

## Profile

The project [[summaries/alphafold_msa_annotation__REPORT]] investigated AlphaFold multiple-sequence-alignment (MSA) depth as a proxy for functional annotation richness across bacterial pangenome gene clusters, joining pangenome, Bakta, InterProScan, and AlphaFold datasets. Of 132,531,501 gene clusters, 38,804,903 (29.3%) had a real UniProt accession and 38,051,842 (28.7%) bridged to AlphaFold MSA-depth data, while 70.7% lacked a usable AlphaFold bridge. Among bridged clusters, core genes had a median MSA depth of 15,308 versus 5,299 for auxiliary+singleton genes and 5,527 for auxiliary non-singleton genes; MSA depth and domain-hit count were strongly associated (Spearman ρ = 0.7563). [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]

The project’s concrete contribution was to distinguish annotation-gap layers rather than treating the bacterial annotation gap as a single phenomenon: accessory and singleton genes had higher hypothetical-protein rates, while a separate subset of 415,603 core clusters had MSA depth below 10 and a 68.9% hypothetical rate, with fewer than 0.1% EC- or KEGG-annotated. [src: alphafold_msa_annotation] Across this work, the projects center on using large-scale comparative genomics and structural databases to quantify how conservation, sequence-space representation, pangenome class, and functional annotation interact, while identifying poorly characterized proteins for follow-up structural investigation. The emphasis on low-MSA-depth core proteins suggests an interest in prioritizing conserved but structurally isolated proteins for experimental characterization; this is a prioritization hypothesis rather than a validated experimental finding. [src: alphafold_msa_annotation]
