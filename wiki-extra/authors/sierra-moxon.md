# Sierra Moxon

ORCID: [0000-0002-8719-7760](https://orcid.org/0000-0002-8719-7760)

## Profile

Sierra Moxon’s project investigated the relationship between genomic metabolic capability and experimentally measured metabolic dependency across 1,695 complete pathway–organism pairs from 48 organisms, integrating GapMind pathway predictions, Fitness Browser gene-fitness data, and SEED subsystem annotations. It classified 267 pairs (15.8%) as latent capabilities, 547 (32.3%) as intermediate, and 881 (51.9%) as active dependencies; carbon-source utilization pathways were latent in 217 of 892 cases (24.3%), compared with 48 of 735 amino-acid biosynthesis pathways (6.5%). The study further found that latent capability rate correlated positively with pangenome openness across 22 species clades (Spearman ρ = 0.69, p = 0.0004), while pathway-level conservation did not distinguish latent capabilities from active dependencies (Mann–Whitney U p = 0.94). [metabolic_capability_dependency__REPORT](../../wiki/summaries/metabolic_capability_dependency__REPORT.md) [^metabolic_capability_dependency]

Across this work, their projects center on the distinction between what microbial genomes can encode and what organisms require under experimental conditions, and on how genome dynamics and environment shape that distinction. The same project identified metabolic clustering in all 10 examined species, with silhouette scores ranging from 0.349 in *PALSA-747* sp. to 0.894 in *Salmonella enterica*, and found significant associations between metabolic clusters and isolation environment in *Salmonella enterica* (χ² = 1570.2, df = 25, p < 0.0001) and *Phenylobacterium* sp. (χ² = 12.2, df = 1, p = 0.0005). These recurring emphases suggest research interests in latent metabolic capabilities, dependency and essentiality, pangenome openness, and environment-linked metabolic ecotypes, including how community context may shape genome evolution in ways consistent with a Black Queen framework.
