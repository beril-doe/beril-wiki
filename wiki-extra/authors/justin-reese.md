# Justin Reese

ORCID: [0000-0002-2170-2250](https://orcid.org/0000-0002-2170-2250)

## Contributions

[[summaries/ecotype_functional_differentiation__REPORT]] clustered bacterial genomes from the KBase Data Lakehouse pangenome database and identified valid gene-content ecotypes in 12 of 15 sampled species (80%), assigning 1,820 genomes across 12 species that averaged 3.7 ecotypes each. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] The project compared ecotype COG (Clusters of Orthologous Groups) profiles across 257 tests and found that 170 (66.1%) were significant after BH-FDR (Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate) correction, with all 12 species showing at least one significantly differentiated category. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation] It found larger mean effect sizes for adaptive categories than for housekeeping categories (0.0136 versus 0.0064; one-sided Mann–Whitney U p = 2.53 × 10⁻⁶), while categories S (unknown function) and L (replication, recombination, and repair) had the largest mean effect sizes at 0.0392 and 0.0337. [src: ecotype_functional_differentiation]

[[summaries/phage_defense_arsenal__REPORT]] analyzed seven anti-phage defense-system families across the KBase Data Lakehouse pangenome and detected markers in 27,626 of 27,690 species-level pangenomes (99.8%), yielding 930,573 marker hits across 825,476 unique gene clusters. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] The project found that defense-system count correlated with prophage-cluster burden (Spearman ρ = 0.609; n = 7,323), with the partial correlation remaining ρ = 0.301 after adjustment for genome size and phylum. [src: phage_defense_arsenal] It also found significant positive co-occurrence for 27 of 28 defense-system pairs, including an R-M Type II × Gabija association with 2,429 observed co-occurrences, null mean 1,555, z = 46.1, and odds ratio = 24.0. [src: phage_defense_arsenal]

[[summaries/prophage_amr_comobilization__REPORT]] compared antimicrobial-resistance (AMR) genes with prophage markers and found that 14,669 of 27,702 species (52.9%) carried both marker types, while 20,073 of 36,041 sampled AMR gene instances (55.7%) occurred on contigs carrying strict prophage markers. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization] The project found a positive association between prophage-marker density and AMR repertoire breadth across 4,770 species (Spearman ρ = 0.572, p < 10⁻³⁰⁰), with a log-log regression slope of 0.823 and R² = 0.30. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization] It found that AMR genes within 10 genes of a prophage marker were 67.6% accessory versus 65.5% for distal AMR genes, corresponding to an odds ratio of 1.10 and a 2.1 percentage-point difference, but the proposed fitness-cost comparison could not be tested because the KBase Data Lakehouse Fitness Browser contained RB-TnSeq (random barcode transposon sequencing) data for only 48 model organisms. [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]
