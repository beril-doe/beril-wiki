# Justin Reese

ORCID: [0000-0002-2170-2250](https://orcid.org/0000-0002-2170-2250)

## Profile

Their projects investigate how bacterial pangenome variation relates to functional differentiation, phage defense, prophage content, and antimicrobial-resistance (AMR) gene distribution. In the ecotype analysis, PCA and KMeans identified valid gene-content ecotypes in 12 of 15 species (80%), assigning 1,820 genomes across 12 species; 170 of 257 COG-category tests (66.1%) were significant after Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction, and adaptive categories had a mean effect size of 0.0136 versus 0.0064 for housekeeping categories. [ecotype_functional_differentiation__REPORT](../../wiki/summaries/ecotype_functional_differentiation__REPORT.md) [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] The phage-defense project detected defense markers in 27,626 of 27,690 species-level pangenomes (99.8%), found a partial Spearman correlation of ρ = 0.301 between defense-system count and prophage burden after controlling for genome size and phylum, and observed significant positive co-occurrence for 27 of 28 defense-system pairs. [phage_defense_arsenal__REPORT](../../wiki/summaries/phage_defense_arsenal__REPORT.md) [^phage_defense_arsenal]

Their prophage–AMR project found that 20,073 of 36,041 AMR gene instances (55.7%) occurred on contigs carrying strict prophage markers, while only 1,991 (5.5%) were within five genes of such a marker; at the species level, prophage-marker density correlated with AMR repertoire breadth at Spearman ρ = 0.572 across 4,770 species, with the association remaining at ρ = 0.464 after controlling for genome count. [prophage_amr_comobilization__REPORT](../../wiki/summaries/prophage_amr_comobilization__REPORT.md) [^prophage_amr_comobilization] The attempted comparison of fitness costs was not testable because the fitness browser contained RB-TnSeq data for only 48 model organisms with poor overlap with the analyzed pangenome species. [prophage_amr_comobilization__REPORT](../../wiki/summaries/prophage_amr_comobilization__REPORT.md) [^prophage_amr_comobilization]

Across the corpus, their projects center on large-scale, quantitative analysis of accessory gene content and mobile genetic elements, with recurring attention to defense syndromes, prophage associations, functional consequences, and the limits imposed by detection methods, sampling, annotation, and phylogenetic confounding. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] [^phage_defense_arsenal] [^prophage_amr_comobilization] These themes suggest research interests in connecting bacterial genome diversity to ecological or adaptive function and in testing whether mobile elements—including defense loci, prophages, and AMR genes—co-occur, spread, or structure variation across species, while distinguishing measured associations from evidence of mechanism. [^ecotype_functional_differentiation] [^phage_defense_arsenal] [^prophage_amr_comobilization]
