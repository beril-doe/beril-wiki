# Paramvir S. Dehal

ORCID: [0000-0001-5810-2497](https://orcid.org/0000-0001-5810-2497)

## Contributions

The [[summaries/acinetobacter_adp1_explorer__REPORT]] project integrated genome features, TnSeq essentiality, flux-balance analysis (FBA), mutant growth, proteomics, pangenome classifications, and functional annotations for *Acinetobacter baylyi* ADP1 and 13 related genomes, covering 5,852 genes across a 15-table, 461,522-row database. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] The [[summaries/adp1_triple_essentiality__REPORT]] project compared FBA, RB-TnSeq, complete-gene knockout phenotypes, growth fitness, and proteomics, finding moderate FBA concordance with knockout lethality but no association between FBA class and quantitative growth defects among 478 TnSeq-dispensable genes; proteomic expression predicted knockout essentiality with ROC AUC = 0.743. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] The [[summaries/adp1_deletion_phenotypes__REPORT]] and [[summaries/aromatic_catabolism_network__REPORT]] projects analyzed condition-specific ADP1 growth phenotypes and quinate catabolism, identifying 625 condition-specific genes and a 51-gene quinate-support network in which 21 genes belonged to respiratory Complex I. [src: adp1_deletion_phenotypes, aromatic_catabolism_network] The [[summaries/respiratory_chain_wiring__REPORT]] project mapped carbon-source-specific respiratory configurations and reported Complex I growth ratios of 0.37 on quinate, 1.44 on glucose, and 0.49 on acetate. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]

The [[summaries/conservation_vs_fitness__REPORT]] project linked Fitness Browser genes to pangenome clusters across 33 organisms and found that essential genes were 86.1% core compared with 81.2% for non-essential genes. [src: conservation_vs_fitness] The [[summaries/fitness_effects_conservation__REPORT]] and [[summaries/conservation_fitness_synthesis__REPORT]] projects analyzed approximately 194,000 genes from 43 bacteria and found a conservation gradient from 82% core among essential genes to 66% among always-neutral genes, while core genes were beneficial when deleted in 24.4% of cases compared with 19.9% for auxiliary genes. [src: fitness_effects_conservation, conservation_fitness_synthesis] The [[summaries/fitness_modules__REPORT]] and [[summaries/module_conservation__REPORT]] projects used independent component analysis (ICA) to identify 1,116 fitness modules across 32 organisms and found that module genes were 86.0% core versus an 81.5% all-gene baseline; the [[summaries/cofitness_coinheritance__REPORT]] project found that ICA modules had mean co-occurrence delta phi = +0.053, compared with +0.003 for aggregate cofit pairs. [src: fitness_modules, module_conservation, cofitness_coinheritance] The [[summaries/costly_dispensable_genes__REPORT]] project characterized 5,526 costly-and-dispensable genes and found that they were 7.45 times more likely than costly-and-conserved genes to contain mobile-element keywords. [src: costly_dispensable_genes]

The [[summaries/amr_pangenome_atlas__REPORT]] project analyzed 27,690 pangenome species and found that AMR genes were 30.3% core versus 46.8% for the pangenome baseline, while the [[summaries/amr_fitness_cost__REPORT]] project analyzed 1,352 AMR genes across 43 organisms and estimated a pooled non-antibiotic fitness shift of +0.086 across 25 organisms. [src: amr_pangenome_atlas, amr_fitness_cost] The [[summaries/amr_environmental_resistome__REPORT]] and [[summaries/amr_strain_variation__REPORT]] projects found that clinical species had a median of 5 AMR gene clusters versus 2 in soil, aquatic, and host-associated species, and identified 1,517 resistance islands across 705 species with mean pairwise co-occurrence phi = 0.827. [src: amr_environmental_resistome, amr_strain_variation] The [[summaries/metal_fitness_atlas__REPORT]] and [[summaries/metal_cross_resistance__REPORT]] projects analyzed metal-associated fitness across 31–37 organisms and found that metal-important genes were 87.4% core versus 76.9% for baseline genes, while 98.1% of 317 organism–metal-pair observations showed positive gene-level cross-resistance correlations. [src: metal_fitness_atlas, metal_cross_resistance] The [[summaries/lab_field_ecology__REPORT]], [[summaries/bacdive_metal_validation__REPORT]], and [[summaries/bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance__REPORT]] projects compared laboratory metal-fitness predictions with field, isolation, and phenotype data, finding five significant genus–uranium associations at Oak Ridge, a Cohen’s d = +1.00 difference for 10 heavy-metal contamination isolates, and a full-model R² = 0.633 for predicting metal tolerance from taxonomy, phenotypes, and metal-resistance gene counts. [src: lab_field_ecology, bacdive_metal_validation, bacdive_phenotype_metal_tolerance]
