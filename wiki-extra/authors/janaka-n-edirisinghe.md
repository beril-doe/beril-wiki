# Janaka N. Edirisinghe

ORCID: [0000-0003-2493-234X](https://orcid.org/0000-0003-2493-234X)

## Profile

The available project, [[summaries/annotation_gap_discovery__REPORT]], investigated how metabolic-model gapfilling, Fitness Browser phenotypes, pangenome conservation, GapMind pathway evidence, and BLAST homology could be integrated to identify genes underlying missing enzymatic annotations across 14 organisms and 18 carbon sources. Using flux-balance analysis (FBA), a constraint-based method for predicting metabolic flux and growth, the study assigned candidate genes to 96 of 201 gapfilled reaction–organism pairs (47.8%), including 44 high-confidence, 19 medium-confidence, and 33 low-confidence assignments; 105 pairs (52.2%) remained unresolved. [src: annotation_gap_discovery] The full evidence pipeline resolved 13 percentage points more cases than BLAST alone, while no individual evidence stream exceeded 35% resolution. [src: annotation_gap_discovery]

Recurring themes are evidence triangulation, the use of phenotype and comparative-genomic data to improve functional annotation, and the limits imposed by poorly characterized or phylogenetically divergent biology. Resolution ranged from 71.4% in *Klebsiella michiganensis* to 20.0% in *Bacteroides thetaiotaomicron*, and only 8 of 50 dark reactions lacking ModelSEED EC numbers were resolved, compared with 88 of 151 reactions with known EC numbers. [src: annotation_gap_discovery] These findings suggest that their projects center on computational microbial metabolism, genome annotation-gap discovery, and combining experimental fitness data with sequence and pathway evidence to prioritize testable gene–function assignments, especially for reactions that resist standard homology-based annotation. [src: annotation_gap_discovery]
