# Dileep Kishore

## Profile

The corpus project investigated how genomic metabolic capability relates to experimentally observed dependency by crossing GapMind pathway-completeness predictions with random-barcode transposon sequencing (RB-TnSeq) fitness data from the Fitness Browser. It classified 161 organism–pathway combinations across 7 model bacteria: 57 (35.4%) were Active Dependency, 66 (41.0%) Latent Capability, 24 (14.9%) Incomplete but Important, and 14 (8.7%) Missing. All 66 Latent Capability pairs became fitness-important under at least one tested condition type, especially nitrogen limitation, stress, or carbon limitation, while Active Dependencies had mean core gene completeness of 0.986 versus 0.975 for Latent Capabilities. The project also analyzed approximately 293,000 genomes spanning 2,810 GTDB species and found that variable pathway count correlated with pangenome openness, with raw Spearman rho=0.327 (p=7.2e-71) and partial Spearman rho=0.530 after controlling for genome count (p=2.83e-203); the signal was positive in 13 of 18 genera and significant in 5. Its core-versus-all-gene comparisons further identified accessory-gene contributions to amino acid biosynthesis, including a completeness gap of 0.146 for leucine and valine biosynthesis. These findings suggest that their projects center on condition-dependent gene essentiality, the distinction between genomic potential and realized metabolic need, and the relationship between pathway variation, accessory genes, and pangenome openness.
