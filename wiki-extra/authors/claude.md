# Claude

## Profile

The project represented here investigated whether prophage-associated genomic features are linked to antimicrobial-resistance (AMR) gene distribution across the GTDB pangenome, using gene-neighborhood co-localization, species-level repertoire comparisons, and an attempted fitness-cost comparison ([[summaries/prophage_amr_comobilization__REPORT]]). It identified 83,008 AMR gene clusters and 3,465,244 broad prophage-marker clusters; among 36,041 AMR gene instances, 55.7% occurred on contigs carrying strict prophage markers, while 10.4% were within 10 genes of one. The strongest result was a species-level association between prophage-marker density and AMR repertoire breadth across 4,770 species (Spearman rho=0.572, p<10^-300; log-log slope=0.823; R²=0.30), whereas the proximity association was modest: AMR genes within 10 genes of a prophage marker were 67.6% accessory versus 65.5% for distal genes (odds ratio=1.10; one-sided p=0.005). [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]

Across this work, the recurring themes are microbial gene mobility, the genomic context of AMR, cross-species comparative analysis, and careful separation of association from demonstrated transfer mechanisms. The findings suggest that the author's research interests center on testing whether prophages and other mobile genomic features help explain resistance-gene acquisition and repertoire breadth, while evaluating how such patterns vary across phylogenetic groups and genomic scales. The project also highlights interest in connecting comparative genomics to experimental fitness data, although the proposed comparison could not be tested because the BERDL fitness browser contained RB-TnSeq data for only 48 model organisms with poor overlap with the analyzed GTDB species.
