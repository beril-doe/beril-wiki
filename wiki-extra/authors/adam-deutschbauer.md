# Adam Deutschbauer

ORCID: [0000-0003-2728-7622](https://orcid.org/0000-0003-2728-7622)

## Profile

The available project investigated bacterial fitness under metal stress at cross-species scale, integrating 559 experiments across 31 organisms and 16 metals with 383,349 gene × metal fitness records, pangenome conservation analysis, conserved ortholog-family discovery, module analysis, and genome-scale prediction. Its central finding was that metal-important genes were predominantly core-genome genes: across 22 organisms and 14 metals, 87.4% were core versus 76.9% of baseline genes (OR=2.08, p=4.3e-162). This supports a core-genome robustness model in which metal stress affects broadly conserved cellular functions, while still distinguishing those functions from specialized metal-resistance systems. [[summaries/metal_fitness_atlas__REPORT]] [src: metal_fitness_atlas]

A recurring emphasis is on comparing metal-specific fitness effects with genomic conservation and on separating generalizable patterns from organism- or metal-specific exceptions. Essential-metal tolerance genes showed stronger core enrichment than toxic-metal genes, with mean core-fraction deltas of +0.148 and +0.081, respectively (U=39, p=0.015); manganese had a core fraction of 1.000 among 30 important genes, whereas cadmium had a delta of -0.010 (p=0.92). The project also identified 1,182 ortholog groups with metal phenotypes conserved across at least two organisms and 601 across at least three, with OG00128 spanning 17 organisms and nine metals. Taken together, these projects center on using large comparative fitness atlases to identify conserved cellular vulnerabilities, test how metal function relates to genome conservation, and prioritize broadly recurring gene families as candidates for mechanistic follow-up.
