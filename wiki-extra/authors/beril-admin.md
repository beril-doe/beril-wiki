## Profile

Beril Admin’s project investigated an integrated SQLite dataset for *Acinetobacter baylyi* ADP1 and 13 related genomes, combining genome features, TnSeq essentiality, flux balance analysis (FBA), mutant growth fitness, proteomics, pangenome classification, functional annotations, metabolic reactions, and growth phenotypes across 15 tables, 461,522 rows, and 135 MB of data. [[summaries/acinetobacter_adp1_explorer__REPORT]] [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] The project found that no single gene was represented across all six major data modalities, while essentiality, pangenome status, and proteomics showed substantial pairwise overlap; it also documented 7 engineered ADP1 strains and proteomic measurements for 2,383 genes, with high cross-strain correlation consistent with targeted rather than global effects. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer]

A concrete contribution was a bridge between ADP1 mmseqs2-style pangenome cluster IDs and BERDL centroid gene IDs: all 4,891 BERDL clusters mapped to 4,081 unique ADP1 clusters, producing a 100% gene-level match across 43,754 genes. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] The project also quantified disagreement and condition dependence in functional predictions: 639 of 866 genes with both FBA and TnSeq calls were concordant, essentiality calls covered 499 genes on minimal media versus 346 on LB, and FBA flux classes changed for 177 of 866 genes between rich and minimal media. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] Across these findings, the project centers on making heterogeneous microbial datasets interoperable, testing how genetic essentiality and metabolism depend on growth conditions, and identifying condition-specific gene functions; these themes suggest interests in multi-omics integration, pangenome-to-function mapping, metabolic-model refinement, and experimentally grounded analysis of microbial fitness. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer]
