# Aindrila Mukhopadhyay

ORCID: [0000-0002-6513-7425](https://orcid.org/0000-0002-6513-7425)

## Profile

The project linked here investigated whether counter ions—especially chloride delivered by metal salts—confound genome-wide metal-fitness measurements. Across 71 NaCl experiments, 10,821 metal-important gene records, 86 organism × metal pairs, 14 metals, and 19 organisms, it found that 4,304 records (39.8%) overlapped with NaCl-important genes; after excluding a *Synechococcus elongatus* outlier, overlap remained 36.7% (3,739/10,183). [[summaries/counter_ion_effects__REPORT]] [src: counter_ion_effects]

The analysis found no evidence that chloride dose was the primary driver of the shared signal: zinc sulfate delivered 0 mM chloride yet had 44.6% NaCl overlap, compared with 41.3% for cobalt, 41.0% for copper, and 39.3% for nickel. It instead interpreted the overlap as reflecting shared cellular-stress biology involving cell-envelope integrity, DNA repair, and ion homeostasis, while noting that NaCl also supplies sodium and osmotic stress. [[summaries/counter_ion_effects__REPORT]] [src: counter_ion_effects]

Across the corpus, these results suggest that the projects center on separating general stress responses from metal-specific genetic requirements and testing the robustness of comparative fitness resources: after shared-stress genes were removed, core-genome enrichment was preserved for 12 of 14 metals, with corrected enrichment increasing for seven metals, including molybdenum from +0.132 to +0.145 and uranium from +0.031 to +0.040. The research interests suggested by these projects therefore include comparative microbial fitness, metal toxicity and homeostasis, confounding-factor control, and analysis of how broadly shared stress programs shape genome-wide phenotypes; the mechanistic interpretations remain extrapolations from fitness-profile patterns rather than direct biochemical tests.
