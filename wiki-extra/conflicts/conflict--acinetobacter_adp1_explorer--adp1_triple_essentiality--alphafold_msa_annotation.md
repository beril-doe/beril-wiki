# Essentiality Tracks Conservation—Except When It Does Not

The corpus contains a disagreement over whether essentiality is generally a property of conserved, core genes or whether essential genes can be lineage-restricted, pathway-dependent, and invisible to some measurements. The tension matters because core enrichment is observed at the gene level, while pathway, metabolic, insertion-based, and pangenome analyses often produce weaker, reversed, or apparently contradictory patterns. These results concern [[concepts/gene-essentiality]], but they may reflect different populations, definitions, organisms, growth conditions, and measurement capabilities rather than one biological relationship.

## Evidence Sides

**Core conservation is associated with essentiality and strong fitness effects.** ADP1 analyses associate essentiality with core status and richer annotation, while essential genes were 86.1% core versus 81.2% for non-essential genes. [src: acinetobacter_adp1_explorer] [src: conservation_vs_fitness] Core enrichment also appears among fitness-defined groups: 24.4% of positive-fitness core genes versus 19.9% of accessory genes, and broad metal-important genes were 87.4% core versus 76.9% baseline. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] [src: core_gene_tradeoffs] [src: metal_fitness_atlas] Among metal-specific categories, metal+stress genes were 94.3% core, while general sick genes were 90.2% core. [src: metal_specificity]

**Essentiality and conservation can separate at the gene, pathway, or module level.** Persistent hypothetical genes were less core and had narrower ortholog breadth than annotation-lag genes, yet had a higher essential fraction, 18.0% versus 13.4%. [src: truly_dark_genes] Latent complete pathways had mean conservation 0.869 versus 0.829 for active dependencies, with p=0.94. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] In only 7 matched model organisms, Active Dependencies instead had mean core completeness 0.986 versus 0.975 for Latent Capabilities. [src: pathway_capability_dependency] Only 859 of 17,222 ortholog families were universally essential, while 4,799 were variably essential and 11,564 never essential. [src: discoveries]

**Some apparent disagreements arise because the assays cannot observe the same biology.** Zero essential genes appeared in modules because essential genes are invisible to insertion-based ICA, although 86.0% of module genes were core versus 81.5% of all genes. [src: module_conservation] Module-ICA had <1% strict KO precision despite strong cofitness, versus 95.8% for ortholog transfer. [src: fitness_modules] Essentiality is an upper bound because genes without fitness data may lack insertions because they are short, lie in low-complexity regions, or occur at scaffold edges; essentiality was measured under a single growth condition represented by RB-TnSeq library construction. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]

**Metabolic models do not consistently recover experimental essentiality.** ADP1 showed moderate FBA–knockout concordance, whereas annotation-gap analysis found 42.5% baseline FBA accuracy across 574 organism–carbon-source combinations; FBA lacked mappings for 30/51 aromatic-network genes and predicted 0% Complex I essentiality despite 1.76× higher aromatic flux. [src: adp1_triple_essentiality] [src: annotation_gap_discovery] [src: discoveries]

## Possible Reconciliations

- **Hypothesis — population and coverage effects:** the 415,603 core clusters with MSA depth <10 and 68.9% hypothetical are not the same population as ADP1 essentiality analyses. [src: alphafold_msa_annotation] Clades with only 2 genomes can have trivially high core fractions, and pangenome coverage varies among clades. [src: conservation_vs_fitness]
- **Hypothesis — definition and threshold effects:** broad metal-important genes, DvH heavy-metal genes, metal-specific genes, and metal+stress genes use different definitions, thresholds, organism sets, and stress histories; DvH heavy-metal genes were 71.2% core and not significantly enriched after correction, while metal-specific genes were 84.8% core pooled. [src: metal_fitness_atlas] [src: field_vs_lab_fitness] [src: metal_specificity]
- **Hypothesis — pathway completeness is not gene essentiality:** complete pathways may be latent or replaceable, whereas essentiality can depend on a particular organism, condition, or flux requirement. Complex I was required on quinate despite theoretical quinate production of 0.57 NADH per carbon, but dispensable on glucose at 1.50 NADH per carbon. [src: respiratory_chain_wiring]

## Resolving Work

- Recompute core fractions and essentiality using identical clade inclusion, genome-coverage thresholds, and ortholog definitions; test whether the 86.1% versus 81.2% difference persists without small clades and uneven coverage.
- Stratify essentiality by gene length, low-complexity status, scaffold position, insertion density, and growth condition; test how much the upper-bound definition changes after correcting for missing-observation bias.
- Compare pathway conservation, core completeness, and gene-level essentiality in the same organisms and pathways, including the 7 matched model organisms; test whether latent pathways are complete but nonessential because of redundancy.
- Refit FBA with curated gene–reaction mappings, alternative objectives, and measured flux constraints; test the 30/51 aromatic-network mapping gap and the 0% Complex I prediction against knockout data.
- Build strain-resolved joins using assembly accessions, NCBI-taxid-backed reconciliation, and GTDB-version-aware synonymy; test whether cross-dataset predictor comparisons survive correction of short names, cohort synonyms, and locus-tag mismatches.
