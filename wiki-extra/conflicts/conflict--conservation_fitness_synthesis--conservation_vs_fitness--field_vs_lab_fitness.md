# Laboratory Fitness Versus Evolutionary Retention: What Makes a Gene Core?

[[concepts/laboratory-fitness-versus-natural-selection]] records a disagreement between laboratory measurements of deletion burden and evolutionary patterns of gene retention. Laboratory assays identify substantial fitness effects for some genes, while conservation and pangenome analyses suggest that core status reflects selection across broader contexts. The tension matters because it determines whether apparent contradictions reflect invalid measurements, environmental mismatch, assay coverage, or differences in cohort and definition.

## Evidence Sides

**Laboratory burden and evolutionary conservation**  
24.4% of core genes were burdensome when deleted in the laboratory, yet core genes were more conserved than always-neutral genes, which were 66% core. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] The synthesis interprets this as an environmental mismatch: laboratory conditions measure a restricted component of fitness, while pangenome conservation integrates selection across broader evolutionary contexts. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

**Condition-specific phenotypes versus niche-specific fitness**  
Core genes were 1.78x more likely to show strong condition-specific phenotypes, but the report did not find that condition-specific fitness was equivalent to niche-specific fitness. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] Environmental experiments or validated environmental proxies are therefore needed rather than further interpretation of laboratory measurements alone. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

**Core and auxiliary effects versus singleton neutrality**  
Core genes were more likely than auxiliary genes to show both beneficial deletion effects and strong fitness effects overall, while singleton genes appeared largely neutral in the tested assays. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] This supports the interpretation that assay coverage and network context shape observed fitness, but it does not establish whether singleton neutrality reflects true dispensability or missing transposon measurements. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

**Different ecological categories and core status**  
Field-stress genes were more core than heavy-metal-important genes, even though both categories were treated as ecologically relevant. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness] The report hypothesizes that specific metal-resistance mechanisms may be accessory while uranium- and mercury-related responses may involve more fundamental stress pathways; this is not a direct mobile-element or natural-selection measurement. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]

**Different estimates of essential-core enrichment**  
The broader synthesis estimated 82% essential-core, while the 33-organism conservation_vs_fitness linkage estimated 86.1%. Both indicate enrichment, but their different cohorts and definitions prevent direct numerical reconciliation. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] [src: conservation_vs_fitness]

## Possible Reconciliations

- **Hypothesis — environmental scope:** Laboratory deletion assays may capture fitness under restricted conditions, whereas conservation integrates selection across broader evolutionary contexts. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]
- **Hypothesis — phenotype versus niche:** Strong condition-specific phenotypes may not be equivalent to niche-specific fitness; environmental dependence could require direct environmental testing or validated proxies. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]
- **Hypothesis — network and assay coverage:** Singleton neutrality may reflect missing transposon measurements or limited assay coverage rather than true dispensability. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- **Hypothesis — category and mechanism differences:** The field-stress/heavy-metal contrast may reflect biology, condition classification, or assay coverage. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]
- **Hypothesis — cohort and definition effects:** The 82% and 86.1% estimates may differ because the analyses used different cohorts and definitions. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] [src: conservation_vs_fitness]

## Resolving Work

- Measure deletion and transposon fitness for the same core, auxiliary, and singleton genes across laboratory conditions and environmentally representative conditions; test whether singleton neutrality persists.
- Compare condition-specific phenotypes with validated environmental proxies and direct niche exposures; test whether strong condition-specific effects predict niche-specific fitness.
- Reanalyze field-stress and heavy-metal genes using matched condition definitions and equal assay coverage; test whether their core-status difference is biological or methodological.
- Harmonize the cohorts and essential-core definitions used in the 82% and 86.1% analyses; test whether the estimates converge under identical inclusion rules.
- Integrate laboratory fitness, environmental fitness, and pangenome conservation in a common model; test which component best explains core status.
