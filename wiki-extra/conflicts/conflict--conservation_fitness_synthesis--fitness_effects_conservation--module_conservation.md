# Do Conserved Modules Really Predict Fitness Importance?

The disagreement concerns how strongly evolutionary conservation should be interpreted as evidence that genes are functionally important in coordinated modules. The [[concepts/fitness-module-detection-sensitivity]] analysis reports substantial enrichment of conservation among module genes, whereas the genome-wide fitness-conservation analysis finds only a weak association between fitness importance and conservation and warns that measurement limitations can make novel genes appear neutral. The synthesis therefore supports module-level enrichment while qualifying whether it demonstrates uniform fitness benefit, environmental necessity, or independence from detection thresholds. [src: module_conservation] [src: fitness_effects_conservation] [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

## Evidence Sides

**Module conservation indicates coordinated evolutionary retention.** The module-conservation analysis found an 86.0% core fraction for module genes versus 81.5% for all genes. [src: module_conservation] The new synthesis reports 86% core module genes against an 81.5% baseline across 1,116 modules. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] This side treats the higher core fraction as evidence that module membership is associated with evolutionary retention, potentially reflecting coordinated functional importance. [src: module_conservation] [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

**Genome-wide conservation has only limited predictive value for fitness importance.** The genome-wide fitness-conservation analysis found only a weak association between fitness importance and conservation across approximately 194,000 genes. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] It also emphasized that novel singleton genes may appear neutral because of poor transposon coverage. [src: fitness_effects_conservation] The synthesis further documents that core genes may be laboratory-burdensome, and concludes that module conservation does not establish that modules are uniformly fitness-beneficial, environmentally essential, or independent of threshold and callability constraints. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

## Possible Reconciliations

- **Measurement-estimand hypothesis:** The 86.0% versus 81.5% comparison is conditional on detected, non-essential module members and selected module thresholds, while the genome-wide analysis spans broader fitness categories and approximately 194,000 genes. [src: module_conservation] [src: fitness_effects_conservation] The two analyses may therefore measure different relationships rather than contradicting one another.
- **Callability hypothesis:** Poor transposon coverage may preferentially make novel singleton genes appear neutral, weakening the genome-wide fitness-conservation gradient without eliminating enrichment among well-detected module genes. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- **Scope hypothesis:** Modules may be evolutionarily retained as coordinated systems even when individual genes do not show uniformly strong fitness effects in laboratory assays. This would be consistent with the synthesis’s finding that core genes may be laboratory-burdensome. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]
- **Threshold hypothesis:** The observed module enrichment may depend partly on module-construction and conservation-call thresholds rather than solely on biological fitness importance. [src: module_conservation] [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]

## Resolving Work

- Reanalyze the same approximately 194,000 genes using identical conservation, fitness, essentiality, and module-membership definitions; test whether the 86.0% versus 81.5% contrast persists under a common estimand.
- Stratify module and non-module genes by transposon coverage, singleton status, and detectability; estimate whether correcting for poor coverage removes or reduces the weak genome-wide association.
- Perform sensitivity analyses across module thresholds and conservation-call thresholds; determine whether the 86% core fraction and enrichment across 1,116 modules are robust to alternative cutoffs.
- Compare laboratory fitness effects with environmental or condition-specific assays for core module genes; test whether laboratory-burdensome genes show conservation despite weak or directionally different laboratory fitness effects.
