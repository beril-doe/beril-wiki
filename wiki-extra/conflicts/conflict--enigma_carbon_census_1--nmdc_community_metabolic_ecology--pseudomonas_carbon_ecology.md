# Environmental Prevalence Versus In Situ Catabolism

The disagreement in [[concepts/occurrence-versus-catabolic-activity]] concerns what environmental prevalence can legitimately establish. One interpretation treats repeated detection of a taxon, especially at high prevalence, as evidence that it is practically accessible for enrichment and therefore a useful guide for selecting organisms or sites. The opposing boundary is that prevalence, genomic capability, and community-level pathway patterns do not by themselves show that a taxon catabolizes a particular compound in situ. The distinction matters because site-selection evidence can be valuable without being mistaken for direct evidence of degradation, uptake, expression, or carbon flux.

## Evidence Sides

**Occurrence and capability profiles as ecological evidence**

The census found 3-hydroxybenzoic-acid utilizers at 0.90 field prevalence at genus resolution, supporting the practical hypothesis that a taxon with high environmental prevalence may be accessible for enrichment. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The *Pseudomonas* report further indicates that environment-associated carbon profiles derived from genomes may provide a useful intermediate ecological signal. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] On this side, occurrence and predicted capability are treated as informative evidence for prioritizing taxa, environments, and enrichment strategies, even when they are not direct activity measurements.

**Occurrence and prediction do not establish compound-specific activity**

The census explicitly classifies the environmental atlas as an abundance or occurrence proxy rather than evidence of compound degradation or activity. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] Thus, the finding of 3-hydroxybenzoic-acid utilizers at 0.90 field prevalence at genus resolution cannot establish that the taxon catabolizes that compound in situ. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1] The *Pseudomonas* profiles are pathway predictions from genomes, not environmental activity measurements; modest classifier performance and strong subgenus separation limit claims about compound-specific environmental catabolism. [src: pseudomonas_carbon_ecology] Similarly, negative associations between community pathway completeness and ambient amino-acid intensity are compatible with community metabolic interdependence but do not directly demonstrate catabolic uptake, pathway expression, or carbon flux. [src: nmdc_community_metabolic_ecology]

## Possible Reconciliations

- **Hypothesis — evidence hierarchy:** Occurrence can support site selection and enrichment design, while compound-resolved experiments or activity measurements are required for an in situ catabolism claim. [src: enigma_carbon_census_1]
- **Hypothesis — measurement difference:** The census measures environmental occurrence, the *Pseudomonas* report predicts genomic capability, and the NMDC analysis evaluates community-level associations; these measurements answer different questions rather than directly contradicting one another.
- **Hypothesis — scope difference:** A taxon may be present and genetically capable of using a compound without expressing that capability under the sampled environmental conditions.
- **Hypothesis — ecological interdependence:** Community pathway completeness and ambient amino-acid intensity may reflect cross-feeding or metabolic interdependence, allowing pathway presence without proving direct uptake or flux.

## Resolving Work

- Pair genus-resolved prevalence with compound-resolved enrichment experiments to test whether taxa detected at 0.90 field prevalence actually grow on 3-hydroxybenzoic acid.
- Measure compound disappearance and labeled-carbon incorporation in environmental samples to distinguish catabolic uptake from mere occurrence.
- Use transcriptomics, proteomics, or other activity measurements targeting the predicted pathways to test whether genomic capability is expressed in situ.
- Compare pathway predictions with direct flux measurements across environments to determine whether classifier performance and subgenus separation limit compound-specific inference.
- Test whether the NMDC community associations predict uptake, expression, or carbon flux by combining ambient amino-acid measurements with time-resolved community activity data.
