# Ecological AMR association versus unresolved within-species causality

[[concepts/environmental-resistome]] documents a disagreement between broad ecological evidence that environment is associated with AMR composition and narrower analyses that find limited, heterogeneous, or non-causal within-species structure. The same problem appears in the interpretation of resistance catalogs, metal tolerance, genomic compartment, and mobile-element evidence: large-scale patterns support ecological or dissemination hypotheses, but measurement choices and confounding prevent them from establishing adaptation, resistance origin, or transfer.

## Evidence Sides

**Broad ecological and functional signals support environmental structure.** Environment effects explain 2–13% of AMR-composition variance (η² = 0.02–0.13), and the environment–AMR association persisted after majority-vote thresholds and phylum- and family-level controls [src: amr_environmental_resistome]. The positive relationship between AMR and phylogenetic distance occurred in 701/1,261 species [src: amr_strain_variation, ecotype_analysis]. Soil had 5.8% metal-resistance prevalence and OR=5.05, while marine samples had 1.2% and OR=0.20; metal-type diversity was associated with groundwater prevalence (ρ = +0.112, p = 0.0019) [src: metal_resistance_global_biogeography, microbeatlas_metal_ecology]. BacDive also found higher metal-tolerance scores among contamination-associated isolates, including Cohen’s d = +1.00 for heavy-metal contamination (n = 10) [src: bacdive_metal_validation]. Prophage density was strongly associated with AMR breadth (rho=0.572; partial rho=0.464 after controlling for genome count) [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. T4SS-positive genomes had 10× higher MGE density, alongside 92 elevated co-occurrences, 77 GT2 HGT events, and 32 normalized high-confidence cross-phylum events [src: t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt].

**Within-species, causal, and interpretive evidence remains weak or inconsistent.** Only 20 of 141 testable families (14%) showed significant within-family effects after FDR correction; whole-genome ecotype analysis found effects significant and positive in 12 species (7.0%), significant and negative in 4 species (2.3%), and absent in 156 species (90.7%) [src: amr_environmental_resistome, ecotype_analysis]. AMR ecotypes occurred in 19.5% of eligible species, but only 2 species passed strict testing criteria [src: amr_strain_variation]. Cross-resistance found no species-scale correlation between multi-metal tolerance and metal-associated isolation (Spearman rho approximately −0.02, p > 0.8), with only 20 independent species [src: metal_cross_resistance]. Groundwater-specific fold enrichment was not associated with metal-type diversity (ρ = +0.042, p = 0.242) [src: microbeatlas_metal_ecology]. Local prophage proximity was modest and heterogeneous, with median species-level OR=0.85 [src: prophage_amr_comobilization]. The catalog includes metal and stress systems, 15,550 unassigned clusters (18.7%), and a 22.2% Other/Unclassified category in another analysis [src: amr_pangenome_atlas, amr_environmental_resistome]. Core/intrinsic and accessory/acquired groups had identical mean fitness values of −0.024 (Cohen’s d = 0.002, p = 0.33) [src: amr_pangenome_atlas, amr_fitness_cost].

## Possible Reconciliations

- **Hypothesis—scale:** Between-environment composition differences may be real while most species lack strong within-species ecological differentiation.
- **Hypothesis—measurement:** Missing or imprecise geographic coordinates, sparse metadata, majority-vote labels, and differing denominators could attenuate or reshape effects.
- **Hypothesis—definition:** Broad AMRFinderPlus categories may capture metal and stress systems, whereas narrow “antibiotic resistance” analyses measure a different entity set.
- **Hypothesis—confounding:** Phylogeny, sampling, isolation, gene mobility, and correlated host-associated traits may jointly generate observed associations without direct exposure-to-selection causality.

## Resolving Work

- Harmonize entity sets, annotations, filters, and denominators to test the 21% versus 7.0% efflux estimates and the 13% versus 44% core/accessory values [src: discoveries, amr_environmental_resistome, amr_fitness_cost].
- Analyze dense longitudinal isolate collections with precise exposure, location, and treatment metadata to test whether exposure precedes within-lineage AMR change.
- Fit phylogenetically and geographically explicit models with nonlinear distance terms to distinguish adaptation from lineage distribution and sampling.
- Resolve co-varying chromium, copper, lead, and zinc exposures using multimetal measurements and isolate-level genotypes [src: soil_metal_functional_genomics].
- Validate transfer claims with long-read assemblies and experimentally confirmed plasmid, ICE, or IME movement, given that ICEfinder found no CAZy genes on plasmids and only 12 IMEs among the top 100 accumulators [src: t4ss_cazy_environmental_hgt].
