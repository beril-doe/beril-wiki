# Matched Concordance vs. Context-Dependent Pathway Use

The disagreement concerns whether pathway completeness and cross-database agreement can serve as evidence that a metabolic capability is functionally used. The [[concepts/cross-condition-metabolic-comparability]] evidence supports strong concordance in a selected matched subset, while broader analyses classify complete pathways as active, latent, or intermediate under particular organisms and conditions. The essential-metabolome and Web of Microbes results add assay- and scope-dependent limitations: completeness or production alone does not establish requirement, uptake, or ecological use.

## Evidence Sides

**Matched cross-database comparisons support complete concordance.** The FW300-N2E3 matched subset shows 100% concordance for the 21 Fitness Browser and 13 GapMind comparisons. This is evidence that the selected, cross-database matched observations agree across those comparisons. [src: fw300_metabolic_consistency]

**Broader pathway-level analysis distinguishes completeness from functional dependence.** The broader metabolic-capability analysis finds that complete pathways can be latent or intermediate under measured fitness criteria. The 161-pair analysis classifies 57 (35.4%) complete-and-important Active Dependencies and 66 (41.0%) complete-but-latent capabilities, rather than treating pathway completeness as a uniform proxy for use. [src: metabolic_capability_dependency] [src: pathway_capability_dependency]

**The essential-metabolome pilot shows that complete pathways need not establish condition-specific requirement.** It found 17 of 18 amino-acid pathways complete in all 7 mapped organisms, but used rich-media RB-TnSeq and computational GapMind calls that cannot determine whether those pathways are required under defined conditions. [src: essential_metabolome]

**Web of Microbes supports a production bridge but not reciprocal-use conclusions.** WoM provides a direct production-to-Fitness Browser bridge for 19 metabolites, but its 2018 snapshot has no organism consumption action, while the existing comparisons include utilization or single-substrate growth evidence. Thus production and fitness concordance can be genuine for aligned observations without demonstrating reciprocal uptake or ecological use. [src: webofmicrobes_explorer]

## Possible Reconciliations

- **Hypothesis — selection scope:** The 100% concordance may be valid for the selected FW300-N2E3 matched subset, while the 161-pair analysis applies across organisms and conditions with more heterogeneous evidence.
- **Hypothesis — definition of “use”:** “Complete,” “important,” “latent,” and “intermediate” may describe different biological or operational states, so pathway completeness can agree with database annotations without implying measured dependence.
- **Hypothesis — assay and medium scope:** Rich-media RB-TnSeq and computational GapMind calls may detect capability or annotation completeness, whereas defined-condition fitness assays test requirement under a particular environment.
- **Hypothesis — direction of evidence:** Production-to-fitness links and consumption or single-substrate growth evidence may measure different directions of metabolite exchange; concordance in one direction does not establish reciprocal uptake.

## Resolving Work

- Repeat matched Fitness Browser–GapMind comparisons across more organisms and pathways, testing whether concordance remains high outside the selected FW300-N2E3 subset.
- Reassess the 161 pathway-organism pairs under explicitly aligned media and fitness criteria, asking whether the 57 (35.4%) Active Dependencies and 66 (41.0%) latent capabilities change classification.
- Measure amino-acid requirements with defined-media RB-TnSeq or targeted growth assays, testing whether the 17 of 18 complete pathways are required under conditions where each metabolite is controlled.
- Add direct consumption measurements to the 19-metabolite Web of Microbes bridge and curate compounds with identifier-based matching, testing whether production links correspond to organism uptake.
