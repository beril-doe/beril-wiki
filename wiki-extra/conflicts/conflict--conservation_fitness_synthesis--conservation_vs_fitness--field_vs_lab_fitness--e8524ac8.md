# Laboratory Fitness Versus Natural Selection: What Makes Core Genes Persist?

The disagreement concerns how laboratory fitness measurements relate to evolutionary retention across environments. Laboratory deletions identify burdens under restricted assay conditions, whereas conservation and pangenome patterns reflect selection across broader contexts. The tension matters because core status can indicate long-term evolutionary importance even when a gene’s deletion appears neutral or burdensome only under particular conditions. The source discussion is documented on [[concepts/laboratory-fitness-versus-natural-selection]].

## Evidence Sides

**Laboratory burden and condition-specific fitness**

In laboratory assays, 24.4% of core genes were burdensome when deleted, while core genes were 1.78x more likely to show strong condition-specific phenotypes. However, the report did not find that condition-specific fitness was equivalent to niche-specific fitness. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] The new analysis further found that core genes were more likely than auxiliary genes to show both beneficial deletion effects and strong fitness effects overall, while singleton genes appeared largely neutral in the tested assays. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]

**Evolutionary retention and ecological context**

Core genes were more conserved than always-neutral genes, even though always-neutral genes were 66% core. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] A broader synthesis estimated that 82% of core genes were essential, while the 33-organism conservation_vs_fitness linkage estimated 86.1%. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] [src: conservation_vs_fitness] The DvH results also found that field-stress genes were more core than heavy-metal-important genes, although both categories were treated as ecologically relevant. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]

## Possible Reconciliations

- **Hypothesis — environmental mismatch:** Laboratory conditions may measure only a restricted component of fitness, while pangenome conservation integrates selection across broader evolutionary contexts. This would allow a gene to appear neutral or burdensome in the laboratory while remaining evolutionarily retained. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]
- **Hypothesis — condition and niche are different levels of analysis:** Strong condition-specific phenotypes may not represent niche-specific fitness. Resolving the distinction would require environmental experiments or validated environmental proxies. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis]
- **Hypothesis — assay coverage and network context:** The apparent neutrality of singleton genes may reflect missing transposon measurements rather than true dispensability, while core-gene effects may be amplified by network context. [src: fitness_effects_conservation]
- **Hypothesis — ecological category or pathway differences:** Specific metal-resistance mechanisms may be accessory, whereas uranium- and mercury-related responses may involve more fundamental stress pathways. The observed difference could instead reflect condition classification or assay coverage. [src: field_vs_lab_fitness]
- **Hypothesis — scope and definition:** The 82% and 86.1% essential-core estimates may both be valid for their respective cohorts and definitions, without being directly numerically reconcilable. [src: conservation_fitness_synthesis] [src: conservation_vs_fitness]

## Resolving Work

- Measure the same genes with deletion and transposon assays across laboratory, field-stress, and metal-related conditions; test whether singleton neutrality persists when assay coverage expands.
- Compare condition-specific laboratory phenotypes with validated environmental proxies and direct environmental experiments; test whether strong condition-specific effects predict niche-specific fitness.
- Reanalyze field-stress and heavy-metal categories using matched gene definitions, exposure levels, and assay coverage; test whether their different core enrichment reflects biology or classification.
- Recompute essential-core estimates on identical organism cohorts using identical core, essentiality, and conservation definitions; test whether 82% and 86.1% converge.
- Integrate fitness effects with phylogenetic conservation and gene-network context across environments; test whether evolutionary retention is better explained by broad environmental selection than by laboratory burden alone.
