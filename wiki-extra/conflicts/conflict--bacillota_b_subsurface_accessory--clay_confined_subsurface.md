# Sulfite Reduction or Dissimilatory Sulfate Reduction — Terminology or Biological Difference?

Both sources report 5/9 positives and the same null scale, but one calls the signal “sulfite reduction” while the other calls it dissimilatory “sulfate reduction.” The numerical agreement could suggest continuity between the findings, yet the non-identical terminology may reflect different marker definitions, biological processes, or reporting conventions. Resolving this matters because silently treating sulfite and sulfate reduction as interchangeable could misclassify the underlying metabolism and distort comparisons across projects.

## Possible Reconciliations

- **Hypothesis — substrate or pathway scope:** “Sulfite reduction” may refer to a narrower terminal step or substrate-specific assay, while “dissimilatory sulfate reduction” may refer to the broader pathway in which sulfate is reduced through sulfite as an intermediate. The labels could therefore describe related but differently scoped biological claims.
- **Hypothesis — marker-definition difference:** The two projects may have used different marker genes, protein annotations, or signal thresholds. A marker associated with sulfite reduction could have been interpreted as evidence for the broader sulfate-reduction pathway in the clay report.
- **Hypothesis — reporting terminology:** The underlying marker definitions may be equivalent, with the disagreement arising only from taxonomic, biochemical, or editorial naming conventions.
- **Hypothesis — shared positives but different identities:** The matching 5/9 positives and null scale may indicate comparable detection outcomes without establishing that the detected process was defined identically in both studies.
